For years, the BC PNP Tech Pilot was one of the best-known fast tracks to permanent residence in Canada. Software developers, data scientists and engineers in British Columbia watched for weekly tech draws with low minimum scores. Then the program changed. The final BC Provincial Nominee Program (BC PNP) draw for priority technology occupations took place on December 3, 2024, and in 2026 tech workers compete under a very different system.

If you searched for the BC PNP Tech Pilot in 2026, this guide gives you the honest picture: what ended, what replaced it, how tech professionals can still qualify, and how to give your registration the best chance in a highly competitive year.

Quick Summary

There is no dedicated BC PNP tech stream in 2026. The last tech-specific draw was held on December 3, 2024. Tech workers now apply through the Skills Immigration Skilled Worker stream, usually with the Express Entry BC option, and compete for invitations under BC’s “Innovate” priority, which targets high-earning or high-scoring candidates with permanent job offers. BC received 6,254 nominations for 2026 after a 1,000-space increase in August.

What Was the BC PNP Tech Pilot?

The Tech Pilot launched in 2017 to help British Columbia employers attract and keep workers in technology occupations. It was not a separate stream. Instead, it gave candidates in eligible tech occupations access to regular, targeted invitation rounds within BC’s Skills Immigration and Express Entry BC streams. In 2021, BC made the pilot permanent and later called it the BC PNP Tech program.

For a long time, these tech draws offered some of the most predictable invitation patterns of any provincial program. That predictability ended with the December 2024 draw.

What Changed for 2026?

In 2026, BC organized its selection around three strategic priorities, often described as “Care, Build and Innovate”:

PriorityWho it targets
CareHealthcare and related occupations, such as nurses, pharmacists, psychologists, dentists, veterinarians, veterinary technicians, early childhood educators and French-speaking K-12 teachers
BuildNine skilled trades requiring SkilledTradesBC certification or apprenticeship, including electricians, plumbers, welders, carpenters and heavy-duty equipment mechanics
InnovateHigh-earning and/or high-scoring candidates with permanent job offers, with a goal of 35% of nominations going to locations outside Metro Vancouver

Tech professionals now compete mainly through the “Innovate” priority, which BC calls High Economic Impact selection. This means your job offer‘s wage and your registration score matter more than your occupation title.

BC also closed several pathways. The Entry Level and Semi-Skilled stream and the International Graduate and International Post-Graduate streams are no longer available for new applicants.

How Many Nominations Does BC Have in 2026?

British Columbia initially received 5,254 provincial nominations for 2026, well below the 9,000 it requested. On August 18, 2026, BC received 1,000 additional spaces, bringing its total to 6,254. Demand remains far higher than supply. In May 2026, BC reported nearly 10,000 active registrations with job offers, more than twice the number of nominations it had available at the time.

Who Can Apply as a Tech Worker in 2026?

Most tech professionals apply through Skills Immigration: Skilled Worker. Core requirements include:

  • A full-time, indeterminate (permanent) job offer from an eligible B.C. employer in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation.
  • At least two years of full-time, directly related work experience.
  • Language ability of at least Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 4, although higher scores improve your registration score.
  • A wage consistent with B.C. wage rates for the occupation.
  • An employer that meets BC PNP requirements and completes the Employer Declaration Form.

If you also qualify for a federal Express Entry program, the Express Entry BC option links your provincial nomination to your federal profile. A nomination through Express Entry BC adds 600 points to your Comprehensive Ranking System score.

How BC Selects Candidates: The SIRS Score

BC ranks registrations using the Skills Immigration Registration System (SIRS). Your score reflects factors such as the skill level of your job, your hourly wage, the region where you will work, your education, your language ability and your directly related work experience. Higher wages and work outside Metro Vancouver can significantly improve a score.

In August 2026, occupation-targeted draws reportedly required minimum scores from about 68 to 102, while High Economic Impact draws required roughly 132 to 138 points or qualifying wage thresholds. Scores change from draw to draw, so treat these figures as a snapshot, not a target.

Step-by-Step: How to Apply Through BC PNP in 2026

Step 1: Secure a qualifying job offer

Obtain a full-time, indeterminate job offer from an eligible B.C. employer. Confirm the wage aligns with the occupation’s prevailing rate.

Step 2: Check Express Entry eligibility

If you meet the Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class or Federal Skilled Trades requirements, create an Express Entry profile so you can apply under Express Entry BC.

Step 3: Register in the BC PNP Online Portal

Create a registration with accurate details about your job, wage, education, language and experience. There is no registration fee.

Step 4: Wait for an invitation

BC invites the highest-scoring and highest-priority registrations. Registrations remain in the pool for a limited period, so keep yours updated.

Step 5: Submit a complete application

If invited, submit your full application with supporting documents within the deadline stated in your invitation.

Step 6: Apply for permanent residence

After nomination, Express Entry BC nominees receive 600 CRS points and typically receive an invitation in a following federal provincial nominee draw. Skills Immigration nominees outside Express Entry apply to IRCC separately.

Practical Tips for Tech Workers in 2026

  • Negotiate a wage that reflects market rates, because wage drives both your score and High Economic Impact eligibility.
  • Consider employers outside Metro Vancouver, which BC is actively prioritizing.
  • Raise your language scores, which help both your SIRS score and your federal CRS score.
  • Keep a federal Express Entry profile active, since category-based federal draws may offer another route.
  • Keep your work permit valid, because a nomination does not extend your temporary status.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the BC PNP Tech Pilot still running in 2026?

No. BC held its final draw for priority technology occupations on December 3, 2024. In 2026, tech workers apply through Skills Immigration: Skilled Worker, often with the Express Entry BC option, and compete for invitations under BC’s High Economic Impact or “Innovate” priority, which favours high-earning or high-scoring candidates.

Can software developers still get a BC PNP nomination?

Yes, but without a dedicated tech draw. Software developers with a full-time, indeterminate job offer from an eligible B.C. employer and at least two years of related experience can register under Skills Immigration. Strong wages, high language scores and jobs outside Metro Vancouver can improve their chances of an invitation.

How many BC PNP nominations are available in 2026?

British Columbia initially received 5,254 nominations for 2026. On August 18, 2026, it received 1,000 more, for a total of 6,254. Demand is much higher than supply, with BC reporting nearly 10,000 active registrations with job offers in May 2026.

Do I need a job offer for BC PNP in 2026?

For Skills Immigration streams, yes. BC requires a full-time, indeterminate job offer from an eligible B.C. employer, supported by an Employer Declaration Form. BC’s Entrepreneur Immigration streams do not require a job offer, but they have their own investment and business requirements.
