Mexico is one of Canada’s closest partners, linked by trade, tourism and decades of labour mobility. Mexican professionals can use trade agreement work permits, young Mexicans can work through International Experience Canada, and thousands of Mexican workers support Canadian agriculture each year. Many of these temporary routes can lead to permanent residence. But travel rules changed in 2024, and the right strategy depends on your profession and goals.

This guide explains Mexico to Canada immigration in 2026: travel requirements, work permit options, permanent residence pathways and practical tips.

Quick Summary

Mexican citizens generally need a visitor visa. They may apply for an Electronic Travel Authorization instead if they held a Canadian visa in the past 10 years or hold a valid US non-immigrant visa and are flying to Canada. Work options include CUSMA professional and intra-company transfer permits, International Experience Canada, LMIA-based work permits and agricultural programs. Permanent residence pathways include Express Entry and provincial nominee programs.

Visiting Canada From Mexico

Mexico is among the countries whose citizens can get an Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) instead of a visitor visa, but only if they have held a Canadian visitor visa in the past 10 years or currently hold a valid United States non-immigrant visa, and are flying to or through a Canadian airport. Other Mexican travellers need a visitor visa.

Work Permit Options for Mexicans

CUSMA professionals

The Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement (CUSMA) allows Mexican citizens in listed professional occupations, such as engineers, accountants, computer systems analysts and scientists, to receive LMIA-exempt work permits with a qualifying Canadian job offer and the required qualifications.

CUSMA intra-company transferees and investors

Mexican executives, managers and specialized knowledge employees of multinational companies may qualify for intra-company transfer permits. Traders and investors may also have options under CUSMA.

International Experience Canada

Mexico is an IEC partner country for young Mexicans aged 18 to 29. Available categories and quotas can change each season, so check the current IEC details before applying.

LMIA-based work permits and agriculture

Canadian employers can hire Mexican workers through LMIA-based permits. Many Mexican workers come through agricultural streams, including the Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program.

Permanent Residence Pathways

Express Entry

Mexican professionals can qualify through the Federal Skilled Worker Program or, after skilled work in Canada, the Canadian Experience Class. Strong English or French scores are key. Spanish speakers who learn French may benefit from French-language category draws.

Provincial Nominee Programs

Provinces recruit workers in agriculture, food processing, trades, health care and technology. Many streams require a job offer or experience in the province.

Agri-food and other pilots

Some agricultural and food processing workers may have specific permanent residence pathways, depending on current program availability.

  • Remember that flagpoling for most work permits ended in December 2024; apply online.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Mexicans need a visa to visit Canada in 2026?

Generally, yes. Mexican citizens can apply for an Electronic Travel Authorization instead only if they have held a Canadian visitor visa in the past 10 years or currently hold a valid US non-immigrant visa, and are flying to or through a Canadian airport. Everyone else needs a visitor visa.

Can Mexican professionals work in Canada under CUSMA?

Yes. Mexican citizens in listed professional occupations can receive LMIA-exempt work permits under CUSMA if they have a qualifying Canadian job offer and meet the education or credential requirements for their profession. Intra-company transferees and certain traders and investors may also qualify.

Is Mexico part of International Experience Canada?

Yes. Mexico is an IEC partner country for young Mexicans aged 18 to 29. Available categories and quotas can change each season, so check the current IEC information before creating a profile.

How can Mexican workers get permanent residence in Canada?

Common routes include the Canadian Experience Class after at least one year of skilled Canadian work, the Federal Skilled Worker Program, and provincial nominee programs that target occupations in agriculture, trades, health care and technology. Strong language scores improve your chances significantly.
