For multinational companies, moving the right person to Canada at the right time can decide whether a new office launches smoothly or a project stalls. The intra-company transfer (ICT) work permit lets eligible businesses bring executives, managers and specialized knowledge employees to Canada without a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). In 2024, IRCC tightened and restructured the category, and many older guides no longer reflect how it works.

This guide explains the ICT work permit in 2026 from an employer’s perspective: the three categories, who qualifies, how new Canadian offices are treated, maximum durations, spouse options and the steps to apply.

Quick Summary

The ICT work permit is LMIA-exempt under the International Mobility Program. IRCC uses three categories: transfers to establish a new enterprise in Canada (up to 1 year), executives and managers (up to 3 years initially, 7 years maximum) and specialized knowledge workers (up to 3 years initially, 5 years maximum). The foreign company must already be a multinational operating in more than one country, the employee needs at least one year of continuous full-time employment with the company in the past three years, and specialized knowledge workers must earn the prevailing wage.

Why Companies Use ICT Work Permits

  • No LMIA, which removes advertising requirements and the $1,000 LMIA fee.
  • Faster deployment of trusted, experienced employees.
  • Support for launching or expanding Canadian operations.
  • A path for key leaders who may later pursue permanent residence.

The Three ICT Categories

CategoryWho it coversDuration
Establishing a new enterpriseEmployees transferring to set up a new Canadian branch, subsidiary or affiliateUp to 1 year, non-renewable under this category
Executives and managersEmployees in executive or managerial roles (TEER 0 or 1)Up to 3 years initially; renewals in 2-year increments; 7-year maximum
Specialized knowledge workersEmployees with advanced proprietary knowledge plus advanced expertiseUp to 3 years initially; renewals in 2-year increments; 5-year maximum

Core Requirements for Every Category

  • A qualifying relationship between the foreign and Canadian companies, such as parent and subsidiary, branch or affiliate.
  • The foreign company must already operate as a multinational in more than one country; using Canada as its first foreign operation does not qualify.
  • The employee must currently work for the foreign company and have at least one year of continuous full-time employment with it in a similar role within the past three years.
  • The transfer must be temporary, with the role available to return to after the assignment.
  • The Canadian position must be at the executive, managerial or specialized knowledge level.

Specialized Knowledge: Raising the Bar

Specialized knowledge means advanced, proprietary knowledge of the company’s products, services, research, processes or management, combined with advanced expertise. General industry knowledge, even if valuable, is not enough. Employers should document training, unique responsibilities and why the knowledge cannot easily be found in Canada. Specialized knowledge workers must also be paid the prevailing wage for the occupation.

Opening a New Office in Canada

If you are transferring someone to open a Canadian office, you must show that you have secured physical premises, have a realistic plan to staff and grow the business, and can pay the employee. The initial permit under this category is limited to one year. After that, you need to show that the Canadian operation is doing business and meets the requirements of the executive and manager or specialized knowledge category for any further permit.

Trade Agreement ICTs

Some trade agreements, including CUSMA, CETA, CPTPP and the Canada-UK agreement, include their own intra-company transferee provisions. These can offer advantages for eligible nationals, so compare options before you apply.

Employer Steps

Step 1: Confirm eligibility and category

Review the corporate relationship, the employee’s employment history and the Canadian role.

Step 2: Pay the employer compliance fee

Pay the $230 employer compliance fee.

Step 3: Submit the offer of employment

Submit an offer of employment through the IRCC Employer Portal and share the offer number with the employee.

Step 4: The employee applies

The employee applies for the work permit with strong supporting documents, including organizational charts and evidence of the corporate relationship.

What About Spouses?

Spouses of transferees may qualify for spousal open work permits, but since January 21, 2025, eligibility generally depends on the transferee’s occupation, such as TEER 0 or 1 roles, and on the transferee’s permit having at least 16 months of validity remaining. Most executive and managerial transferees fall within TEER 0 or 1, but confirm the details before you plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who qualifies for an intra-company transfer to Canada?

Employees of a multinational company that operates in more than one country, transferring to a related Canadian entity as an executive, manager or specialized knowledge worker, or to establish a new Canadian enterprise. The employee generally needs at least one year of continuous full-time employment with the company in a similar role within the past three years.

How long can an ICT work permit last?

Executives and managers can receive up to 3 years initially, with renewals up to a 7-year maximum. Specialized knowledge workers can receive up to 3 years initially, up to a 5-year maximum. Transfers to establish a new Canadian enterprise are limited to one year under that category.

Does an ICT work permit need an LMIA?

No. Intra-company transfers are LMIA-exempt under the International Mobility Program. Employers still need to pay the $230 employer compliance fee and submit an offer of employment through the IRCC Employer Portal before the employee applies for the work permit.

Can a company use an ICT to open its first office outside its home country in Canada?

No. The foreign company must already operate as a multinational in more than one country. A business that would make Canada its first foreign operation does not qualify for the ICT work permit and should explore other options, such as other business or work permit pathways.
