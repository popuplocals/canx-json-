Your job title says “Manager.” Your duties say “cashier and stock supervision.” Which one decides your National Occupational Classification (NOC) code? For IRCC, the answer is your duties. Choosing a NOC code based on your title, your employer’s preference or a list of “in-demand jobs,” rather than what you actually did, is one of the most damaging mistakes an Express Entry applicant can make.

This guide explains how officers assess NOC codes under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR), why mismatches lead to refusals, how category-based draws raise the stakes and how to choose your NOC code correctly.

Quick Summary

Under paragraphs 75(2)(b) and (c), 87.1(2)(b) and (c) and the corresponding Federal Skilled Trades provisions of the IRPR, you must have performed the actions described in the NOC lead statement and a substantial number of the main duties, including all of the essential duties. Officers compare your reference letter with the official NOC description, not your job title. A mismatch can remove your qualifying experience, lower your CRS score, disqualify you from a category draw and, if the officer believes facts were misrepresented, lead to a five-year ban under section 40 of IRPA.

The Legal Test for NOC Codes

The IRPR uses the same core test for skilled work experience in each Express Entry program.

RequirementLegal source
Performed the actions in the NOC lead statementIRPR s. 75(2)(b) (FSW) and s. 87.1(2)(b) (CEC)
Performed a substantial number of the main duties, including all essential dutiesIRPR s. 75(2)(c) (FSW) and s. 87.1(2)(c) (CEC)
Occupation in TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3IRPR s. 75(2)(a) and s. 87.1(2)(a)
Category membership at the time of invitationIRPA s. 11.2 and Ministerial Instructions under s. 10.3

The legal question is not “What was my title?” but “Do my documented duties match this NOC’s lead statement and main duties?”

How Officers Assess Your NOC Code

Officers typically compare:

  • The NOC code you selected in your profile and application.
  • The duties described in your reference letters.
  • The official NOC 2021 description: lead statement, main duties and employment requirements.
  • Your other evidence, such as your resume, salary and employer type.

Red flags for officers include a senior title with junior duties, a salary well below what the occupation normally pays, duties copied from the NOC text and a NOC that does not fit the employer’s business.

Common NOC Mistakes

Choosing by title

A “Supervisor” may actually perform the duties of a frontline worker. A “Manager” in a small business may actually be a retail supervisor.

Choosing a NOC to fit a category draw

Some applicants select a NOC because it appears in a category-based draw, such as healthcare, STEM or trades, even though their duties fit a different code. This can lead to refusal under section 11.2 of IRPA if you did not actually meet the category’s requirements when invited.

Ignoring TEER levels

Your occupation’s Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities (TEER) level matters. Express Entry programs generally require TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 experience. If your real duties fit a TEER 4 or 5 occupation, that work does not qualify.

Multiple roles, one code

If your role changed over time, the duties for each period must match the NOC claimed for that period.

The Consequences of a NOC Mismatch

ConsequenceLegal basis
Experience not acceptedFails IRPR s. 75(2) or 87.1(2)
Refusal with no further assessment for FSWIRPR s. 75(3)
Loss of category eligibilityIRPA s. 11.2
CRS points recalculated below the draw cutoffIRPA s. 11.2
Possible misrepresentation findingIRPA s. 40(1)(a), with a five-year inadmissibility under s. 40(2)(a)

Not every NOC mistake is misrepresentation. An honest error in choosing a code is different from knowingly presenting false duties. However, officers assess whether information was material and could have induced an error, so careful, truthful documentation is your best protection.

How to Choose Your NOC Code Correctly

Step 1: List your real duties

Write down what you actually did each day, in your own words, before you look at any NOC description.

Step 2: Search the official NOC 2021 database

Search by keywords from your duties, not just your title.

Step 3: Compare the lead statement

Ask whether the lead statement describes your job overall.

Step 4: Compare the main duties

You should have performed a substantial number of the main duties, including all essential duties.

Step 5: Check the TEER level and employment requirements

Confirm the occupation qualifies for your program and that you meet any typical education or licensing expectations.

Step 6: Align your reference letter

Your letter should describe your real duties in the employer’s own words, showing clear alignment with the NOC.

If You Already Selected the Wrong NOC

If you are still in the pool and have not received an ITA, you can update your profile. If you have received an ITA based on a NOC that does not match your duties, get professional advice immediately. Depending on the circumstances, the right step may be to decline the ITA and correct your profile, rather than submitting an application you cannot support.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Express Entry be refused for the wrong NOC code?

Yes. Under sections 75 and 87.1 of the IRPR, your duties must match the NOC lead statement and a substantial number of its main duties, including all essential duties. If they do not, the officer may not accept your experience, and your application can be refused under section 11.2 of IRPA or subsection 75(3) of the IRPR.

Does IRCC look at my job title or my duties?

IRCC looks at your duties. Officers compare the duties described in your reference letters with the official NOC description. A job title alone does not decide your NOC code. Senior titles with junior duties, or duties copied from the NOC text, can raise concerns with officers.

Is choosing the wrong NOC code misrepresentation?

Not automatically. An honest mistake differs from knowingly presenting false information. However, section 40 of IRPA covers misrepresenting or withholding material facts that could induce an error, and officers assess the circumstances. Choosing your NOC carefully and describing your real duties truthfully is the best protection.

What should I do if I got an ITA with the wrong NOC code?

Get professional advice quickly. Depending on your situation, it may be safer to decline the ITA, correct your profile and wait for a new invitation rather than submit an application your documents cannot support. You generally have 60 days from your ITA to submit, so act promptly.
