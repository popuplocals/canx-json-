Applying for Canadian citizenship is an exciting milestone. It means you are ready to take the final step in your immigration journey and fully join the Canadian community. But what actually happens after you submit your application?

This guide explains the citizenship application process, expected timeline, and next steps so you know exactly what to expect and how to prepare.

Who Should Read This Guide?

This guide is for:

    • Permanent residents who are eligible to apply for Canadian citizenship.
    • Newcomers planning ahead for the citizenship process.
    • Applicants who have already submitted their application and want to understand the next steps.
    • Families helping a relative through the citizenship journey.

If you fall into any of these categories, this blog will help you navigate the process with confidence.

1. Acknowledgment of Receipt (AOR)

Once Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) receives your application, they will review it for completeness. If all required documents and fees are included, you will get an Acknowledgment of Receipt (AOR).

What you should know:

    • The AOR confirms that IRCC has officially started processing your application.
    • You will receive an application number, which you can use to check your status online.
    • If anything is missing, IRCC will return your application instead of issuing an AOR.

2. Application Processing

After the AOR, IRCC will begin detailed processing of your file. This includes:

    • Verifying eligibility (physical presence, language proficiency, income tax filings).
    • Background checks for security and criminality.
    • Confirming that you meet all requirements under the Citizenship Act.

During this stage, IRCC may contact you to request additional documents or clarification. Respond quickly to avoid delays.

3. Citizenship Test (if applicable)

If you are between 18 and 54 years old, you will be invited to take the citizenship test.

Test details:

    • The test covers Canadian history, geography, government, laws, and symbols.
    • It consists of 20 multiple-choice and true/false questions.
    • You must score at least 15 out of 20 to pass.
    • The test may be taken online, in person, or at a designated test centre.

If you do not pass the first time, IRCC will schedule a retest or an interview with a citizenship official.

4. Citizenship Interview (if required)

Some applicants may be asked to attend an interview with a citizenship officer.
This may happen if:

    • Your documents need further verification.
    • You have language test concerns.
    • You fail the citizenship test twice.

During the interview, the officer will review your eligibility and may test your knowledge of Canada orally.

5. Decision Made on Your Application

Once IRCC completes all checks and you meet all requirements, they will approve your application. If you are refused, you will receive a written explanation and instructions on your right to appeal.

6. Oath Ceremony Invitation

If approved, you will receive an invitation to attend the citizenship ceremony, where you will take the Oath of Citizenship.

At the ceremony:

    • You officially become a Canadian citizen.
    • You receive your citizenship certificate.
    • You can apply for a Canadian passport immediately after the ceremony.

Ceremonies can be in person or virtual.

7. Expected Processing Times

Processing times can vary depending on application volume and complexity.
As of 2025, the average citizenship processing time is approximately 12 months from the date IRCC receives your complete application.

Factors that can affect timelines:

    • Missing documents or incomplete forms.
    • Background check delays.
    • Additional verification requirements.

8. After You Become a Canadian Citizen

Once you take the oath and receive your certificate:

    • You can apply for a Canadian passport.
    • You can register to vote in federal, provincial, and municipal elections.
    • You gain all the rights and responsibilities of citizenship.

Tips to Avoid Delays

    • Ensure your application is complete and accurate before submission.
    • Keep your contact information up to date with IRCC.
    • Respond quickly to any document or information requests.
    • Study well in advance for the citizenship test.

Final Word

Applying for Canadian citizenship is a major step, but the process is straightforward if you know what to expect. By understanding the timeline and preparing for each stage, you can ensure a smoother journey from application to oath.
