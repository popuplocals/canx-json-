Hiring skilled foreign workers in Canada can give your business a competitive advantage, but it also comes with responsibilities. Canadian employers must follow specific rules when applying for a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) and managing work permits. Failure to comply with these rules can lead to fines, application refusals, inspections, or even bans on hiring foreign workers.

This practical guide helps employers understand the key compliance requirements for LMIA and work permits, common mistakes to avoid, and tips to ensure a smooth, legal hiring process.

What is an LMIA and Why Does Compliance Matter?

An LMIA is a document issued by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) that confirms an employer has tried to hire Canadians or permanent residents but could not find suitable candidates. It allows the employer to hire temporary foreign workers.

Compliance matters because:

    • It protects Canadian jobs while addressing labour shortages.
    • ESDC monitors employers to ensure they follow LMIA terms and immigration laws.
    • Non-compliance can result in penalties, withdrawal of LMIA approvals, or a ban on hiring foreign workers.
    • Both employers and foreign workers must strictly adhere to work permit conditions.

Employer Compliance Responsibilities

If you hire foreign workers through LMIA, you must:

    • Recruit Canadians and permanent residents first: Post the job on the Government of Canada’s Job Bank for at least 30 days and document your recruitment efforts.
    • Pay the correct wages: Offer wages equal to or above the provincial median wage for the occupation.
    • Keep records for six years: This includes job postings, recruitment documents, contracts, pay records, and correspondence.
    • Follow LMIA and work permit terms: This includes job duties, hours, wages, and workplace location.
    • Report changes immediately: Inform ESDC of any changes to the job, employer, or worker’s employment status.
    • Verify worker permits are valid and report any employment changes promptly.

Common LMIA and Work Permit Mistakes to Avoid

    • Incomplete Applications: Submitting LMIA applications without clear job descriptions, recruitment evidence, or mandatory documents can delay or deny approval.
    • Poor Recruitment Efforts: Not advertising properly or failing to consider Canadian candidates breaks program rules.
    • Underpaying Workers: Offering wages below the provincial median jeopardizes compliance and approvals.
    • Allowing Work Outside Permit Conditions: Permitting foreign workers to perform jobs or work hours not authorized can cause inspections and sanctions.
    • Inadequate Record-Keeping: Failing to retain required documents may lead to fines during inspections.
    • Missed Reporting Obligations: Not informing authorities of job changes or worker termination risks serious penalties.

How to Ensure Compliance

    • Prepare thoroughly: Make sure job ads meet official requirements and recruitment is well documented.
    • Pay accurate wages: Check provincial wage data before making job offers.
    • Inform and review with employees: Clearly explain work permit conditions to workers.
    • Maintain a compliance checklist: Regularly audit hiring and employment processes.
    • Consult immigration experts: Professional guidance can prevent costly mistakes and streamline applications.

Consequences of Non-Compliance

    • LMIA and work permit refusals or delays.
    • Monetary fines and penalties.
    • Inspections by ESDC and possible worksite audits.
    • Temporary Foreign Worker Program bans preventing future hiring.
    • Damage to business reputation among candidates and regulators.

Final Thoughts

Employers who hire foreign workers through LMIA must follow all rules carefully to protect their business and employees. Diligent recruitment, accurate wage offers, good record-keeping, and clear communication with workers are essential.
