Your work permit expires tomorrow. Your extension application went in last week. Can you still go to work on Monday? For most workers in Canada, the answer is yes, thanks to a rule called maintained status. But maintained status has strict limits, and a 2025 change made some common strategies risky. Misunderstanding it can cost you your job or your status.

This guide explains what maintained status means for workers in Canada in 2026, when it applies, what you can and cannot do, how travel and refusals affect it, and how to protect yourself.

Quick Summary

Maintained status lets you remain in Canada under the conditions of your current permit if you applied to extend your status before it expired and you stay in Canada. Workers who applied for a new work permit can usually keep working under the same conditions. Maintained status ends when IRCC decides, when you withdraw, or generally when you leave Canada. Since May 28, 2025, a subsequent application filed after your original status expires is returned unprocessed. After a refusal, you generally have 90 days to apply for restoration.

What Is Maintained Status?

Maintained status, sometimes called implied status, is a legal rule that lets temporary residents remain in Canada while Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) processes an application to extend their stay. It exists because IRCC cannot always decide extensions before current permits expire.

When Does Maintained Status Apply?

You generally have maintained status if:

  • You applied to extend or change your status before your current status expired.
  • You remain in Canada while the application is processed.
  • IRCC has not yet decided, and you have not withdrawn the application.

What Can You Do on Maintained Status?

Your situationCan you work?
You held a work permit and applied for a new work permit before it expiredYes, under the same conditions as your previous permit
You held a work permit and applied for a visitor recordNo, you must stop working when your permit expires
You held a study permit and applied for a PGWP before it expired, meeting the conditionsYes, you may be able to work full-time while waiting
You held an employer-specific permit and want to change employersGenerally no, not until a new permit for the new employer is approved

“Same conditions” matters. If your expired permit tied you to one employer, occupation or location, those limits continue on maintained status.

The May 2025 Change You Need to Know

On May 28, 2025, IRCC changed how it handles subsequent applications. If your original status expires and you submit another application after that date, IRCC returns the later application unprocessed, even if you were on maintained status from an earlier application. This ended the practice of filing a quick “placeholder” application and following it with a stronger one later. Today, the first application you file before expiry needs to be complete and correct.

When Does Maintained Status End?

Travelling While on Maintained Status

Leaving Canada is one of the riskiest choices you can make while on maintained status. When you return, a border officer decides whether to admit you and on what terms. You may be admitted as a visitor, which does not allow you to work, until IRCC approves your new permit. If IRCC refuses your application while you are abroad, you may not be able to return as a worker at all. Avoid travel unless it is essential, and get advice first.

Proving Maintained Status to Your Employer

Employers often ask for proof that you can keep working. Keep:

  • Your expired permit.
  • Your application submission confirmation, showing the date you applied before expiry.
  • Your fee payment receipt.

Employers can check IRCC’s guidance on hiring workers on maintained status, but they also need to confirm your conditions match the job.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is maintained status in Canada?

Maintained status lets you stay in Canada under the conditions of your current permit after it expires, as long as you applied to extend or change your status before the expiry date and remain in Canada. It lasts until IRCC decides your application or you withdraw it, and it generally ends if you leave Canada.

Can I work on maintained status?

Yes, if you held a work permit and applied for a new work permit before it expired. You must keep working under the same conditions as your previous permit. If you applied for a visitor record instead, you must stop working when your work permit expires. Eligible PGWP applicants may also be able to work.

Does leaving Canada end maintained status?

Generally, yes. If you leave Canada while your application is pending, a border officer decides whether to readmit you and on what terms, often as a visitor who cannot work until your new permit is approved. If IRCC refuses your application while you are abroad, returning as a worker may not be possible.

What happens to maintained status if my application is refused?

Maintained status ends on the date IRCC refuses your application. You must stop working immediately. You may be able to apply for restoration of status within 90 days, if you still meet the requirements for the status you want. Restoration requires an additional fee, and you cannot work while it is pending.
