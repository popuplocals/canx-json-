Spousal sponsorship is one of the most common and compassionate immigration pathways in Canada. Through this program, Canadian citizens and permanent residents can sponsor their spouse or partner for permanent residence. But the success of any spousal sponsorship application depends heavily on proving that the relationship is genuine and not entered into for immigration purposes.

In this blog, we’ll explain:

    • What IRCC considers a genuine relationship
    • Common types of evidence used to support your case
    • Red flags that may raise suspicion
    • Tips to strengthen your application and avoid refusal

Why Proving a Genuine Relationship Matters

Canada’s immigration authorities are committed to family reunification, but they also take immigration fraud seriously. A spousal relationship that appears to be primarily for the purpose of acquiring permanent residency may be refused, even if both parties are legally married or in a common-law relationship.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) assesses the authenticity of the relationship by reviewing your documents, personal history, and interviews, if necessary. You must demonstrate that your relationship is based on love, commitment, cohabitation, and shared responsibilities, not convenience or benefit.

What Types of Relationships Are Eligible?

You may sponsor your partner if your relationship falls into one of the following categories:

1. Spouse

Legally married under civil law and recognized by both countries.

2. Common-Law Partner

Lived together in a conjugal relationship for at least 12 consecutive months.

3. Conjugal Partner

Not legally married or living together due to immigration barriers, but in a committed relationship for at least 12 months.

Each of these requires different types of evidence, but all must prove the relationship is genuine.

Key Documents and Evidence to Prove a Genuine Relationship

IRCC looks at both objective evidence and subjective context when reviewing your file. A strong application includes a combination of the following:

1. Marriage Certificate or Cohabitation Documents

    • Marriage registration documents
    • Lease agreements or property titles with both names
    • Joint utility bills or shared insurance policies

2. Photographs

    • Pictures from the wedding, engagement, or shared events
    • Informal photos together in everyday settings
    • Photos with family members and friends

3. Communication Records

    • Call logs, text messages, emails, or social media chats
    • Letters or cards exchanged over time
    • Evidence of ongoing contact during long-distance periods

4. Financial and Legal Ties

    • Joint bank accounts or credit cards
    • Beneficiary designations in wills, insurance, or pension plans
    • Shared expenses or purchases (e.g., household goods, travel)

5. Travel and Visits

    • Travel itineraries showing visits to each other’s country
    • Passport stamps, flight tickets, or boarding passes
    • Photos and proof of activities during visits

6. Affidavits or Support Letters

    • Signed letters from family or friends confirming the relationship
    • Affidavits detailing the history of the relationship
    • Evidence of public recognition as a couple

7. Children or Dependents (if applicable)

    • Birth certificates of children born into the relationship
    • School or daycare records listing both parents
    • Custody agreements or support documents

Red Flags That May Raise Concern with IRCC

IRCC officers are trained to identify potential indicators of non-genuine relationships. Some common red flags include:

    • Significant age difference without cultural explanation
    • Short duration between meeting and marriage
    • Lack of shared language or communication barriers
    • Minimal contact or visits between partners
    • Contradictory details in submitted forms or interviews
    • Previously refused sponsorship applications or immigration history issues
    • Lack of integration with each other’s families

These do not automatically mean the relationship is fake, but they require strong supporting evidence and a clear explanation in your application.

Tips to Strengthen Your Spousal Sponsorship Application

    • Be honest and consistent: Never fabricate evidence or exaggerate your story.
    • Organize your documents: Label and organize photos, records, and correspondence in chronological order.
    • Write a detailed relationship timeline: Include how you met, how the relationship evolved, and key milestones.
    • Explain long-distance periods: If you have lived apart, describe how you maintained contact and plans to reunite.
    • Prepare for a possible interview: Be ready to answer personal questions about your partner and your shared life.
    • Address red flags directly: Do not ignore discrepancies or gaps. Provide a written explanation when needed.

What Happens If IRCC Is Not Convinced?

If IRCC has concerns about the relationship’s authenticity, they may:

    • Request additional documents
    • Schedule an interview with one or both partners
    • Issue a procedural fairness letter (PFL)
    • Refuse the application due to lack of evidence

You can respond to a PFL with new or clarifying evidence, but it is always best to build a strong case before submission to avoid delays or refusals.

Final Thoughts: A Genuine Relationship Needs Genuine Documentation

Successfully sponsoring your spouse or partner to Canada is about more than legal paperwork. It requires clear, consistent, and convincing proof that your relationship is based on love, trust, and a shared life—not immigration convenience.
