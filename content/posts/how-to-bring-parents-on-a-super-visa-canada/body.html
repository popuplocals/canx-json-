What Is the Super Visa?

The Super Visa Canada is a long-term, multiple-entry visitor visa designed specifically for parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens or permanent residents. Unlike a standard visitor visa (which allows only a six-month stay), the Super Visa permits:

    • Stays of up to 5 years at a time
    • Multiple re-entries over a 10-year validity period
    • Optional 2-year extensions from within Canada

While the Super Visa does not provide PR or citizenship, it offers a legally authorized way to keep your loved ones close for extended periods.

2025 Super Visa Eligibility Criteria

For Parents or Grandparents (the Applicant)

To qualify, the applicant must:

    • Be the biological or adoptive parent/grandparent of a Canadian citizen or PR
    • Apply from outside Canada
    • Be medically and criminally admissible to Canada
    • Prove intent to leave Canada after their authorized stay
    • Have valid private medical insurance (see below)

For the Host in Canada (the Child/Grandchild)

To support a Super Visa Canada application, the host must:

    • Be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or registered Indian
    • Be 18 years or older and living in Canada
    • Meet or exceed the Low Income Cut-Off (LICO) based on household size
    • Provide a signed invitation letter
    • Commit in writing to financially support the parent or grandparent throughout their stay

Updated 2025 Medical Insurance Requirements

As of January 28, 2025, IRCC has relaxed insurance rules, making coverage more accessible:

Insurance Must:

    • Be valid for at least 1 year from the applicant’s entry date
    • Offer minimum coverage of $100,000 CAD
    • Cover healthcare, hospitalization, and repatriation
    • Be paid in full, or purchased via deposit or installments if permitted
    • Be issued by a Canadian or approved international insurer (must appear on the OSFI-approved list)

Important: Proof of valid insurance must be presented at the Canadian border and maintained throughout the entire stay.

Required Documents for Super Visa Application

To successfully submit a Super Visa Canada application, prepare the following:

From the Applicant (Parent/Grandparent)

    • Passport and travel documents
    • Proof of relationship (e.g., birth certificate of child/grandchild)
    • Proof of private health insurance
    • Proof of insurance payment or installment plan
    • Completed medical exam results (as requested by IRCC)
    • Biometrics (if required)
    • Completed application forms

From the Host (Child/Grandchild in Canada)

    • Invitation letter detailing:
      • Your name and contact information
      • Your legal status in Canada
      • Family size and income details
      • Commitment to support the visitor
    • Proof of Canadian citizenship or PR (e.g., PR card, passport)
    • Proof of minimum income (NOA, pay slips, job letters)

How to Apply for a Super Visa (Step-by-Step)

Step 1: Prepare Documents
Gather all required forms, insurance, income proof, and medical exam (if applicable).

Step 2: Buy Health Insurance
Purchase an approved policy meeting IRCC requirements.

Step 3: Submit Application Online
Apply via the IRCC Portal from outside Canada. Make sure you create a secure account and complete all sections accurately.

Step 4: Provide Biometrics (if requested)
You will receive instructions on when and where to give your fingerprints and photo.

Step 5: Monitor Application Status
Processing times vary by country. Use the IRCC processing tool for updates.

Step 6: Travel to Canada
Once approved, ensure your parent or grandparent travels with:

    • Proof of insurance
    • Copy of the invitation letter
    • Valid passport and visa

Step 7: Optional: Apply for Extension (If Needed)
You can request a two-year extension from within Canada if their stay needs to be prolonged.

Benefits of the Super Visa in 2025

    • Stay up to 5 years per visit (compared to 6 months on regular visitor visa)
    • Multiple entries over 10 years
    • Year-round availability—no need to wait for a lottery

    • Strong legal basis for long-term family reunification
    • No employment or study restrictions for the host—they remain in full control
