Before March 2025, the comparison between LMIA and LMIA-exempt job offers in the Express Entry context was meaningful and frequently strategic. Both could generate 50 CRS bonus points under the right conditions, and the LMIA route could generate 200 points for senior management positions. Choosing between them was a real decision with real CRS score implications.

On March 25, 2025, IRCC eliminated all arranged employment CRS points. The comparison in terms of Express Entry bonus points became irrelevant overnight, because both pathways now produce the same number of bonus points: zero.

The comparison between LMIA and LMIA-exempt work permits remains highly relevant in 2026 — but the terms of the comparison have shifted entirely. The relevant questions are now about processing speed, cost efficiency, employer compliance obligations, and the quality and permanence of the employment relationship each pathway creates. These factors matter because the employment relationship is the foundation of the PNP nomination strategy, which is now the primary mechanism through which employer-backed workers pursue Canadian PR.

What Was Eliminated: The Old Comparison That No Longer Applies

For historical context and to correct any outdated guidance a reader may have received, here is what was eliminated:

  • LMIA-backed job offers in NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3: Previously generated 50 additional CRS points. Now generates zero.
  • LMIA-backed job offers in NOC TEER 0 Major Group 00 (senior management): Previously generated 200 additional CRS points. Now generates zero.
  • LMIA-exempt job offers (qualifying C10 intra-company transfers in NOC TEER 0 or 1): Previously generated 50 additional CRS points. Now generates zero.
  • LMIA-exempt job offers (other qualifying IMP categories): Previously generated 50 additional CRS points where applicable. Now generates zero.

Any advisor, website, or resource that currently states these bonus points are available is providing outdated information. The arrangement employment points system ceased to function on March 25, 2025, and as of May 2026, no reintroduction has been confirmed.

What Both Pathways Still Contribute to an Express Entry PR Strategy

With bonus points gone, both LMIA and LMIA-exempt work permits contribute to an Express Entry PR strategy in exactly the same ways: through the legal Canadian work experience they enable, and through the employment relationship they create that can support a PNP nomination.

Canadian Work Experience — Available from Both

Whether a candidate is working on an LMIA-based employer-specific work permit, an open work permit, a CUSMA work permit, or an ICT work permit, the Canadian work experience they accumulate in a skilled NOC occupation generates the same CRS points. One year of full-time skilled Canadian experience adds 40 CRS points (single applicant). Two years adds 53 points. Three years adds 64 points. These points build regardless of the work permit type.

CEC Eligibility — Available from Both

Canadian Experience Class eligibility in Express Entry is triggered by one year of full-time skilled Canadian work experience. This experience can be accumulated under any valid work permit that authorizes legal skilled employment in Canada. An LMIA work permit and an ICT LMIA-exempt work permit both create CEC-eligible experience, assuming the position is in NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3.

PNP Nomination Eligibility — Available from Both, Quality Matters

Provincial employer-driven PNP streams require a genuine employment relationship with an employer in the province and a permanent, full-time job offer. The work permit type is generally less important to the provincial stream than the genuineness and permanence of the employment relationship. Both LMIA and LMIA-exempt workers whose employers are committed to their permanent employment can typically access employer-driven PNP streams.

Where the LMIA sometimes demonstrates a stronger employment relationship signal is in the employer’s investment: obtaining a positive LMIA requires a $1,000 fee, weeks of advertising, and months of processing. An employer who has gone through this process for a specific worker has made a demonstrable commitment. Some provincial PNP officers may view this as relevant context when assessing the genuineness of the employment relationship, though it is not a formal eligibility criterion.

When LMIA Is the Right Choice in 2026

With CRS bonus points no longer available, the decision to pursue an LMIA rather than an LMIA-exempt pathway should be driven by genuine eligibility, not by an obsolete points calculation. An LMIA is the right choice when:

  • No LMIA-exempt pathway applies to the specific employer and worker situation.
  • The employer wants to hire a worker from any country in a position that does not fall under any trade agreement or corporate transfer exemption.
  • The employer is building a genuine long-term employment relationship and wants to use the LMIA as the foundation of a PNP nomination strategy.
  • The Global Talent Stream is available and the employer wants fast processing with the added benefit of the Labour Market Benefits Plan structure.

When LMIA-Exempt Is the Better Choice in 2026

An LMIA-exempt pathway is the better choice when it is genuinely available, not to generate CRS points that no longer exist, but to avoid the cost, time, and administrative burden of the LMIA process when an exemption accomplishes the same employment authorization outcome:

  • The worker is a US or Mexican citizen in a CUSMA-listed profession — the work permit can be issued at the border in a day with no ESDC involvement and no advertising requirement.
  • The employer is a multinational with a qualifying corporate relationship and the position is an executive, senior manager, or specialized knowledge worker — the C10 ICT pathway avoids the LMIA entirely.
  • The worker already holds an open work permit (PGWP, spousal OWP, bridging OWP) — no new work permit of any kind is needed for employment.
  • The position falls under another recognized IMP exemption category that applies to the specific employer-worker situation.

What About PGWP Holders: Does Any Employer Action Help?

Workers on Post-Graduation Work Permits often ask whether their employer can do anything to strengthen their Express Entry profile through a portal submission or job offer. Since the elimination of arranged employment points, the answer has changed significantly. Before March 2025, an employer could submit a qualifying job offer through the IRCC portal and generate 50 CRS points for a PGWP holder working in an eligible NOC position. Those points no longer exist.

In 2026, the most valuable thing a PGWP employer can do for a worker’s PR strategy is to support a PNP nomination through an employer-driven provincial stream once the worker meets the minimum experience requirement. This is more involved than a portal submission, but it delivers 600 CRS points rather than the 50 that no longer exist. Workers on PGWPs who are in genuine long-term employment relationships should discuss PNP nomination support with their employers as the primary PR strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an LMIA-exempt job offer help my Express Entry CRS score in 2026?

No. As of March 25, 2025, no job offer — LMIA-backed or LMIA-exempt — adds any CRS bonus points to an Express Entry profile. Both types of job offer now generate zero arranged employment CRS points. The comparison between LMIA and LMIA-exempt pathways for CRS point purposes is now moot. Both pathways contribute to an Express Entry PR strategy through Canadian work experience accumulation and the PNP nomination pathway, but neither generates direct bonus points.

What is the difference between LMIA and LMIA-exempt for Express Entry in 2026?

Since all arranged employment CRS points were eliminated on March 25, 2025, the Express Entry CRS comparison between LMIA and LMIA-exempt pathways no longer exists. Both produce zero bonus points. The relevant differences in 2026 are operational: LMIA requires ESDC assessment ($1,000 fee, 4+ weeks advertising, 8-20+ weeks processing) while LMIA-exempt pathways where applicable avoid ESDC entirely (only $230 IRCC compliance fee). Both pathways generate Canadian work experience and both support PNP nomination eligibility equally.

Can a PGWP employer submit a job offer to boost my Express Entry score?

In 2026, an employer’s job offer submission to the IRCC portal does not add any CRS points. Before March 25, 2025, a qualifying employer portal submission could add 50 CRS points for a PGWP holder in an eligible NOC position. Those points no longer exist. The most valuable action a PGWP employer can take for a worker’s PR strategy is to support a PNP nomination through an employer-driven provincial stream once the worker meets the minimum experience requirement. This delivers 600 CRS points rather than the 50 that were eliminated.

Does a CUSMA work permit still help with Canadian PR?

Yes, but not through CRS bonus points. A CUSMA work permit provides legal work authorization in Canada, which enables accumulation of Canadian work experience (up to 80 CRS points over time), CEC eligibility after one year of skilled Canadian work, and the foundation of a genuine employment relationship that can support a PNP nomination. These contributions are the same as for any other legal work authorization. The CUSMA pathway is valuable primarily for its speed and simplicity (often issued at the port of entry in a day for US citizens in eligible professions), not for any CRS advantage specific to the CUSMA category.

Will Express Entry job offer points come back?

IRCC’s 2026–27 Departmental Plan references an intention to reintroduce CRS points for qualifying job offers at some point in the future. However, as of May 2026, no implementation date, confirmed point values, or eligibility criteria have been announced. Immigration strategy should not be planned around the assumption that the points will return by any particular date. Workers and employers should build strategies based on the current reality of zero bonus points while monitoring IRCC announcements for any confirmed policy reintroduction.
