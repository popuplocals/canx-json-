Becoming a Canadian citizen is a major milestone for many immigrants. After years of working, studying, and building a life in Canada, citizenship offers the security of a Canadian passport, the right to vote, and permanent inclusion in Canadian society. Despite meeting the basic requirements, some applicants face a citizenship application refusedreceive refusals due to avoidable mistakes or overlooked details.

In this blog, we outline the top five reasons citizenship application refused cases happen and how you can avoid these pitfalls to improve your chances of success.

1. Not Meeting the Physical Presence Requirement

To apply for Canadian citizenship, you must be physically present in Canada for at least 1,095 days (3 years) within the 5 years before applying. This is one of the most common reasons for citizenship application refused decision.

What You Need to Know:

    • Only the time spent inside Canada with legal status counts toward physical presence
    • Time spent outside Canada, even for short trips or vacations, does not count
    • Days spent in Canada before becoming a permanent resident (as a temporary resident) can count as half-days, up to a maximum of 365 days

Common Mistakes:

    • Miscalculating days spent outside Canada
    • Forgetting to include travel history or using incorrect dates
    • Assuming time after applying counts toward the 1,095 days

Tip: Use the physical presence calculator on the IRCC website before applying to confirm your eligibility.

2. Unfiled or Incomplete Income Tax Returns

You must have filed at least 3 years of income taxes within the 5-year period before your application. Even if you had little or no income, filing a return is required— and missing this step is a leading cause of citizenship application refused outcomes.

Why It Matters:

    • IRCC cross-checks your tax information with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)
    • If your records are missing or incomplete, it may indicate you have not fully complied with your residency obligations

Common Mistakes:

    • Not filing taxes for years you were a resident
    • Submitting tax returns late or without proof
    • Failing to disclose foreign income if required

Tip: Before applying, make sure your tax history is complete and up to date.

3. Incorrect or Incomplete Application Forms

Submitting incomplete or inaccurate forms is a frequent reason for citizenship application refused delays or refusals.

What Can Go Wrong:

    • Leaving required sections blank
    • Providing inconsistent information about travel, employment, or addresses
    • Forgetting to sign and date the application
    • Using an outdated version of the form

IRCC expects applicants to carefully review their submissions. Even minor errors can lead to a returned application or outright refusal.

Tip: Always double-check the form version, follow the document checklist, and review every page before submitting.

4. Language Requirement Not Met

If you are between 18 and 54 years old, you must prove proficiency in either English or French by providing acceptable documentation.

Accepted Proof Includes:

    • Language test results (CELPIP, IELTS, or TEF)
    • Canadian secondary or post-secondary education certificates
    • CLB 4 or higher achieved through government-funded language programs

Why Refusals Happen:

    • Submitting documents not accepted by IRCC (e.g., employer letters, non-accredited certificates)
    • Failing to provide proof at all
    • Scores below the required benchmark

Tip: If you’re unsure whether your documents meet IRCC’s language requirements, consult a licensed immigration professional before applying.

5. Criminal History or Immigration Violations

A citizenship application refused decision can also result if you:

    • Are under a removal order
    • Are facing charges or convicted of an indictable offense
    • Have been involved in immigration fraud or misrepresentation
    • Are serving a sentence in Canada or abroad (including probation or parole)

Even minor issues, such as past misrepresentations on immigration forms, can affect your application if unresolved.

Tip: If you have a criminal or immigration history, seek legal advice before submitting your citizenship application.

Other Common Issues That Could Lead to Citizenship Application Refused

    • Failing the citizenship test or interview
    • Missing IRCC correspondence or failing to respond
    • Not attending your oath ceremony when scheduled
    • Applying too soon after receiving permanent residence

How to Avoid a Citizenship Refusal

    • Use IRCC’s physical presence calculator to confirm eligibility
    • File all required tax returns before applying
    • Prepare a complete, accurate, and well-documented application
    • Submit official, recognized proof of language proficiency
    • Disclose all previous immigration issues or legal matters honestly

Understanding the most common reasons for refusal can help you prepare a stronger application from the start.
