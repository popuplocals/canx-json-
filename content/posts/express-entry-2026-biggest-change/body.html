On November 3, 2026, Express Entry Canada 2026 enters a new era. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) will start awarding up to 70 extra Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points for Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation. It will also award up to 100 points for a qualifying job offer in one. For the first time, employers will not need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) for a job offer to count.

Immigration Minister Lena Metlege Diab announced the changes on October 16, 2026. Together, they rewrite how Canada ranks candidates for Canadian permanent residency. The new rules turn on your occupation’s wage level, not just your age, education, and language scores. Whether you are a skilled worker, an international graduate, or an employer, this guide explains what changes, who gains, and what to do in the next 18 days.

Quick Summary

From November 3, 2026, IRCC will award extra CRS points to candidates with at least 12 months of Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation (20, 40, or 70 points), or a qualifying job offer in one (50, 70, or 100 points). The two options do not stack; you receive whichever gives you more. An LMIA will no longer be required for these job offer points, or for job offers used to qualify under the Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programs. IRCC will recalculate every score in the pool automatically, but you must add job offer details to your profile before November 3. Invitation rounds will pause for up to two weeks during the recalculation.

What IRCC Announced on October 16, 2026

The announcement has two main parts.

First, IRCC is adding a new section to the CRS called the labour market integration factor. It rewards candidates whose Canadian work experience or job offer falls in a high-wage occupation, including engineers and some electricians.

Second, IRCC is removing the LMIA from the Express Entry job offer process. IRCC officers will now assess job offers directly, checking that each one is genuine and meets program requirements.

IRCC frames the changes as part of a wider effort to align Express Entry with Canada’s labour market. They build on two earlier steps: a dedicated Express Entry category for doctors with Canadian work experience (announced December 2025), and new and renewed category-based draws for health care, skilled trades, research, transportation, and education (announced February 2026).

Why This Is the Biggest Change to Express Entry Since It Launched

Since 2015, the CRS has rewarded the same core factors: age, education, language ability, and work experience. Job offer points always depended on an LMIA, an employer-side process that many businesses found slow and costly. In March 2025, IRCC removed job offer points entirely.

The November 2026 reform reverses that decision in a new form. Job offer points return, but only for high-wage occupations, and without an LMIA. For the first time, the CRS also assesses occupations by what the whole occupation earns across Canada. The type of work you do will now shape your score as much as how long you have done it.

What Counts as a High-Wage Occupation?

IRCC classifies a job as high-wage when the median wage of everyone in Canada working in that National Occupational Classification (NOC) code is at least 1.3 times the average median wage of the Canadian workforce.

Two points matter here:

  • Your personal salary does not decide your level. A highly paid worker in a lower-wage NOC does not qualify. A modestly paid worker in a high-wage NOC does.
  • IRCC uses three years of data. IRCC draws on Job Bank, the Labour Force Survey, and the Census of Population. Each NOC receives the highest level it reached in the last three years. The first list takes effect November 3, 2026, and uses data from 2023 to 2025.

The list sorts occupations into three levels:

  • Level 1: at least 2 times the average median wage (31 NOC codes)
  • Level 2: at least 1.5 times, but less than 2 times (89 NOC codes)
  • Level 3: at least 1.3 times, but less than 1.5 times (66 NOC codes)

That makes 186 occupations in total.

The New CRS Points: How the Numbers Work

Points for Canadian Work Experience in a High-Wage Occupation

You need at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation. IRCC then adds:

  • 70 points for 12 months or more in one or more Level 1 occupations
  • 40 points for 12 months or more combined in Level 1 or Level 2 occupations
  • 20 points for 12 months or more combined across any high-wage occupations

These points sit on top of your regular Canadian work experience points, which reach up to 80 for five years. A candidate without a spouse can earn up to 150 points in this factor. A candidate with an accompanying spouse or common-law partner can earn up to 140.

Eligible Canadian work experience generally must come from the last 10 years, in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation. Special rules apply to some physicians who provide publicly funded medical services.

Points for a Qualifying Job Offer in a High-Wage Occupation

  • 100 points for a Level 1 occupation
  • 70 points for a Level 2 occupation
  • 50 points for a Level 3 occupation

A qualifying job offer must generally be for full-time, continuous work in Canada for at least one year after you become a permanent resident. Skilled trades and physicians face specific additional requirements.

The No-Stacking Rule

You cannot combine the two options. If you qualify for both, IRCC awards whichever gives you more points.

A Simple Example

Take a single software engineer (NOC 21231, Level 2) with one year of Canadian work experience. Today, that year earns 40 CRS points. From November 3, the same candidate can earn an extra 40 points for high-wage experience. That’s 80 in total, a jump that can move a profile meaningfully up the pool.

The LMIA Change: What It Means

From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer will not need an LMIA to earn the new Express Entry points.

IRCC is also introducing a temporary public policy for candidates invited on or after November 3. It removes LMIA and LMIA-exemption requirements for job offers used to qualify under the Federal Skilled Worker Program and Federal Skilled Trades Program, as long as candidates meet the policy’s conditions.

IRCC officers will instead assess each job offer for genuineness, looking at whether:

  • the employer actively runs the business making the offer
  • the offer reflects the employer’s reasonable employment needs
  • the employer can reasonably meet the terms of the offer
  • the candidate can do the job and is likely to accept it
  • the employer follows federal and provincial employment and recruitment laws

If an employer revokes the offer, it no longer qualifies.

Best NOC Codes for Express Entry 2026: Who Gains Most

On IRCC’s list, these groups stand to benefit:

  • Technology: software engineers, developers, data scientists, cybersecurity specialists, and database analysts sit at Level 2. Computer and information systems managers sit at Level 1.
  • Engineering: civil, mechanical, electrical, chemical, and industrial engineers sit at Level 2. Mining and petroleum engineers sit at Level 1.
  • Health care: physicians, nurse practitioners, and veterinarians sit at Level 1. Registered nurses, pharmacists, physiotherapists, and dentists sit at Level 2.
  • Skilled trades: power system electricians, steamfitters, boilermakers, and elevator mechanics sit at Level 2. Industrial electricians, ironworkers, gas fitters, and heavy-duty equipment mechanics sit at Level 3.
  • Finance and business: financial managers sit at Level 1. Accountants, financial analysts, and HR professionals sit at Level 3.

Check your exact NOC code. Similar-sounding jobs can land in different places. For example, the list includes industrial electricians (72201) and power system electricians (72202), but not construction electricians (72200). Several common Express Entry occupations do not appear at all, including welders, plumbers, carpenters, cooks, and licensed practical nurses.

Who May Face Tougher Competition

Candidates outside high-wage occupations will keep their existing Canadian work experience points. However, as candidates in high-wage NOCs gain extra points, CRS cut-offs in general draws may rise. Candidates abroad with no Canadian experience or job offer could find it harder to compete on core factors alone.

These draws still depend on IRCC’s choices for each round. Category-based draws and Provincial Nominee Program streams remain options worth reviewing. The best path depends on your full profile, not one factor.

How to Improve Your CRS Score in 2026 Before November 3

  1. Confirm your NOC code. Match your actual duties to the NOC description, not just your job title.
  2. Check the high-wage list. Find your occupation’s level and calculate your possible new score.
  3. Count your months carefully. The new points need at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience.
  4. Update your job offer details before November 3. IRCC will recalculate work experience points automatically, but you must enter job offer information yourself.
  5. Gather supporting documents now. Strong employer letters, pay records, and offer letters will matter more now that IRCC officers will test genuineness directly.

Express Entry Draw Schedule 2026: The Pause

IRCC will recalculate every CRS score in the pool after November 3. That process may take up to two weeks, and IRCC will pause invitation rounds during that time. Plan for a short gap in draws in early to mid-November.

If IRCC updates the high-wage list after inviting you, it will keep the level that applied on the date of your Invitation to Apply (ITA).

What This Means for Canadian Employers

For employers, the reform removes the biggest hurdle in supporting a worker’s permanent residency through Express Entry. You no longer need an LMIA to give a skilled worker in a high-wage occupation a meaningful CRS boost.

The tradeoff is accountability. IRCC officers will examine your business, your hiring need, and your compliance with employment and recruitment laws. Employers should keep clean records and make offers only for real, ongoing roles. Nothing in the announcement changes LMIA rules for work permits; it applies to Express Entry job offers.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do the new Express Entry CRS points start?

The new points take effect on November 3, 2026. IRCC will automatically recalculate scores for candidates already in the pool, including points for eligible Canadian work experience. If you want job offer points, add the required job offer details to your profile before November 3. The recalculation may take up to two weeks, and IRCC will pause draws during that time.

Does my salary decide whether I qualify as high-wage?

No. IRCC looks at the median wage of everyone in Canada working in your NOC code, not your personal pay. A well-paid worker in a lower-wage occupation will not qualify, while a modestly paid worker in a listed occupation will. Confirm your NOC code carefully, because similar job titles can fall under different codes with different levels.

Do I still need an LMIA for a job offer under Express Entry in 2026?

From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer will not need an LMIA to earn the new CRS points. A temporary public policy also removes LMIA requirements for job offers used to meet Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades eligibility, for candidates invited on or after that date. IRCC officers will assess each offer for genuineness instead.

Can I get points for both Canadian work experience and a job offer?

No. The two options do not combine. If you qualify for both, IRCC gives you whichever provides more points. Under the labour market integration factor, a candidate without a spouse can receive up to 150 points, and a candidate with an accompanying spouse or partner can receive up to 140.

Will more CRS points guarantee me an ITA in Express Entry?

No. IRCC states that extra points do not guarantee an invitation. Each round sets its own requirements and cut-off score. Higher points improve your position in the pool, but your result depends on the draws IRCC holds and how your score compares to other candidates at that time.
