Canada has a building problem, and it is not a shortage of projects. It is a shortage of people to build them. For years, employers in construction and the skilled trades have struggled to find enough electricians, pipefitters, ironworkers, supervisors, and project managers, while many experienced tradespeople move toward retirement. Housing, energy, transit, and infrastructure all depend on these workers.

On November 3, 2026, Express Entry Canada 2026 starts working harder for them. Under new rules announced by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), 40 construction and trades occupations on the new high-wage list can earn up to 70 extra Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points for a qualifying job offer, or up to 40 extra points for Canadian work experience. IRCC even named electricians as an example when it announced the change. For skilled tradespeople who want Canadian permanent residency, this may be the strongest opportunity Express Entry has offered in years.

Quick Summary

From November 3, 2026, 40 construction, trades, and industrial occupations on IRCC’s high-wage list qualify for new CRS points: 70 or 50 points for a qualifying job offer, or 40 or 20 extra points for at least 12 months of Canadian work experience. Job offers no longer need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). The list covers managers, contractors and supervisors, hands-on trades, mechanics, inspectors, and energy and resource workers. Combined with existing category-based draws for skilled trades, these changes give tradespeople several strong paths to permanent residence.

Why Construction and Trades Matter So Much to Canada

Every new home, hospital, power line, and pipeline needs skilled hands. Governments across Canada have made housing and infrastructure top priorities, and that work cannot happen without a strong trades workforce. At the same time, an ageing workforce means many experienced tradespeople will leave the industry in the coming years, and not enough new workers are entering to replace them.

IRCC has already responded to this pressure. In February 2026, it announced new and renewed Express Entry categories to prioritize candidates in key sectors, including skilled trades. The November 3 changes now go further by rewarding trades workers directly in the CRS itself, so they can rank higher in the pool even outside trade-specific draws.

What the New CRS Points Mean for Tradespeople

Of the 186 occupations on IRCC’s high-wage list, we identified 40 that belong to construction, the skilled trades, and closely related industrial and resource work. Nineteen sit at Level 2 and 21 at Level 3. Here is what each level can earn:

  • Level 2 (at least 1.5 times the average median wage): 70 points for a qualifying job offer, or 40 extra points for 12 months of Canadian work experience
  • Level 3 (at least 1.3 times the average median wage): 50 points for a qualifying job offer, or 20 extra points for 12 months of Canadian work experience

Work experience points sit on top of the regular points you already earn for Canadian work experience. If you qualify for both a job offer and work experience points, IRCC awards whichever gives you more. The two do not stack.

To put that in perspective, a single candidate with one year of Canadian experience as a power system electrician would earn 40 points for that experience today, plus 40 high-wage points from November 3, for 80 in total. With a qualifying job offer instead, the same candidate could earn 70 job offer points.

Three Ways Tradespeople Can Benefit

1. Canadian Work Experience

If you already work in Canada in one of these occupations, 12 months of eligible experience can add 40 or 20 points automatically when IRCC recalculates scores after November 3. Eligible experience generally must be in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation and gained within the last 10 years.

2. A Qualifying Job Offer, Now Without an LMIA

From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation no longer needs an LMIA to earn CRS points. For Federal Skilled Trades Program candidates, IRCC’s job offer rules allow an offer from up to two employers, as long as it is for continuous, paid, full-time work for at least one year. IRCC has said specific requirements apply to skilled trades, so check its updated guidance before you rely on an offer.

3. The Federal Skilled Trades Program and Trade Category Draws

Most of the 40 occupations fall within NOC groups eligible for the Federal Skilled Trades Program, including Major Groups 72, 82, 83, 92, and 93, with some exceptions. Candidates in this program need either a valid job offer or a certificate of qualification in their trade issued by a Canadian province or territory. A temporary public policy also removes LMIA requirements for job offers used to meet Federal Skilled Trades eligibility, for candidates invited on or after November 3, 2026, if they meet its conditions.

The management, inspection, and instrument and avionics roles on this list fall outside the Federal Skilled Trades Program, but candidates in those occupations can apply through the Federal Skilled Worker Program or the Canadian Experience Class.

Certification: Plan Ahead

Many trades in Canada are regulated by provinces and territories. In some trades and provinces, certification is compulsory before you can work; in others, it is voluntary. The Red Seal Program sets an interprovincial standard for many trades and is a useful place to start. Contact the apprenticeship or trade certification authority in the province where you plan to work, and begin the process early. A clear certification plan also helps show an IRCC officer that you can perform the job you have been offered.

How to Improve Your CRS Score in 2026 as a Tradesperson

  1. Confirm your NOC code. Match your real duties to the NOC description. Supervisors and hands-on workers in the same trade often fall under different codes and levels.
  2. Document your experience well. Reference letters should describe your duties, hours, pay, and dates clearly.
  3. Look into certification early. It strengthens both your profile and your job prospects.
  4. Improve your language scores. Even small gains in English or French test results can add valuable points.
  5. Update your profile before November 3. IRCC recalculates work experience points automatically, but you must enter job offer details yourself.

What This Means for Employers in Construction

For contractors and builders who have struggled to fill skilled roles, the removal of the LMIA for Express Entry job offers is welcome news. You can now support a skilled worker’s permanent residency with a qualifying job offer, without the time and cost of an LMIA. IRCC officers will still assess every offer for genuineness, including whether your business is active, whether the role reflects a real need, and whether you comply with employment and recruitment laws. Clean records and realistic offers will make the process smoother.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which construction and trade jobs get extra CRS points in 2026?

From November 3, 2026, 40 construction, trades, and industrial occupations on IRCC’s high-wage list qualify. They include construction managers, trade contractors and supervisors, power system electricians, industrial electricians, steamfitters and pipefitters, boilermakers, ironworkers, gas fitters, crane operators, heavy-duty equipment mechanics, construction inspectors, and power engineers. Check your exact NOC code against IRCC’s official list.

How many CRS points can a tradesperson get under the new rules?

Level 2 trades can earn 70 points for a qualifying job offer or 40 extra points for 12 months of Canadian work experience. Level 3 trades can earn 50 points for a job offer or 20 extra points for experience. None of the construction and trade occupations sit at Level 1. Job offer and work experience points do not stack.

Do construction employers still need an LMIA to hire for Express Entry?

Not for Express Entry job offer points. From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer no longer needs an LMIA to earn CRS points, and a temporary public policy removes LMIA requirements for job offers used for Federal Skilled Trades eligibility. IRCC officers will assess each offer’s genuineness instead, so employers should keep clear business and payroll records.

Is a carpenter or plumber on the high-wage list?

No. Carpenters, plumbers, welders, and construction electricians are not on the current list. However, contractors and supervisors in carpentry trades (72013) are at Level 3. Workers in trades not on the list still earn regular CRS points and may qualify through the Federal Skilled Trades Program, trade category draws, or provincial programs.

Do I need a Canadian trade certificate for Express Entry?

For the Federal Skilled Trades Program, you need either a valid job offer or a certificate of qualification from a Canadian province or territory. Separately, many trades require provincial certification before you can legally work. Requirements differ by trade and province, so contact the relevant certification authority early in your planning.
