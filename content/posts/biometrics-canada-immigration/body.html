Fingerprints and a photo take about 15 minutes to give. Getting them wrong, late or at the wrong time can stall your application for weeks. Biometrics are a routine part of most Canadian immigration applications, yet they generate some of the most common questions we hear: Do I need them again? Does my child need them? What happens if I miss the deadline?

This guide answers every major question about biometrics for Canada immigration in 2026, using the rules Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) publishes, so you can book with confidence and keep your application moving.

Quick Summary

Most applicants for visitor visas, study and work permits, and permanent residence must give biometrics, meaning fingerprints and a photo. The fee is $85 per person, capped at $170 for families applying together. Temporary residence applicants give biometrics once every 10 years, but permanent residence applicants must give them with every PR application. Children under 14 and applicants over 79 are generally exempt, with no upper age exemption for asylum claimants.

What Are Biometrics?

Biometrics are your fingerprints and a digital photo. IRCC uses them to confirm your identity, check your background and help assess your application. Once collected, your biometrics also help border officers verify your identity when you arrive in Canada.

Who Needs to Give Biometrics?

You generally need to give biometrics if you apply for a visitor visa, study permit, work permit (excluding US nationals), permanent residence, an extension of your stay from inside Canada in many cases, or refugee or asylum status. IRCC’s official list of who needs to give biometrics sets out the details.

Who Is Exempt?

  • Canadian citizens and citizenship applicants, including passport applicants.
  • Existing permanent residents, with one important exception covered below.
  • Visa-exempt nationals coming to Canada only to visit, such as travellers using an Electronic Travel Authorization.
  • Children under 14 years of age.
  • Applicants over 79 years of age (there is no upper age exemption for asylum claimants).
  • Heads of state, heads of government and holders of diplomatic or official visas.
  • US visa holders transiting through Canada.

IRCC assesses your age on the date you submit your application. If you were 79 when you applied, you must give biometrics even if you turn 80 before your appointment.

How Long Are Biometrics Valid?

This is where many applicants get confused, because the rule differs by application type. According to IRCC’s guidance on when to give biometrics:

Application typeBiometrics rule
Visitor visa, study permit, work permitGive once every 10 years
Permanent residenceGive every time you apply for PR, even if you gave them before
PR card applicants who were under 14 at their original PR applicationMust give biometrics when applying for a PR card once they are 14 or older

IRCC also cannot issue a visa or permit valid beyond 10 years from the date you gave biometrics.

How to Give Your Biometrics: Step by Step

Step 1: Submit your application and pay the fee

After you apply and pay, IRCC issues a Biometric Instruction Letter (BIL). You cannot give biometrics without it.

Step 2: Book an appointment

Book at an official collection point. Outside Canada, this is usually a visa application centre (VAC). In the United States, you can use an Application Support Center. Inside Canada, select Service Canada locations collect biometrics for eligible applicants. Book early, because appointments fill up during busy seasons.

Step 3: Attend on time with the right documents

Bring your Biometric Instruction Letter and the valid passport or travel document you used in your application. Arrive early and follow the centre’s instructions for photos, such as removing glasses and head coverings that are not worn for religious or medical reasons.

Step 4: Meet the deadline

Your instruction letter sets a deadline to give biometrics, usually 30 days. If you cannot make it because of a genuine reason, such as centre closures or illness, contact IRCC through its official web form before the deadline and explain.

What If You Miss the Biometrics Deadline?

IRCC cannot continue processing without your biometrics. If you miss the deadline without explanation, IRCC may refuse your application. If you have a good reason, contact IRCC immediately, provide proof and ask for more time.

Tips for a Smooth Biometrics Appointment

  • Check your letter for the correct name and application number before attending.
  • Avoid henna, cuts or heavy creams on your fingertips before your appointment, as they can affect fingerprint quality.
  • Bring the same passport you listed in your application.
  • Keep your appointment receipt as proof you attended.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to give biometrics again for permanent residence?

Yes. IRCC requires permanent residence applicants to give biometrics every time they apply for permanent residence, even if they gave biometrics recently for a study permit, work permit or visitor visa. The 10-year reuse rule applies only to temporary residence applications such as visitor visas and permits.

How much does biometrics cost for Canada in 2026?

The biometrics fee is $85 per person. Families applying at the same time pay a maximum of $170, and groups of three or more performing artists with their staff pay a maximum of $255. Include the fee with your application payment to avoid delays in receiving your biometric instruction letter.

Do children need to give biometrics for Canada?

Children under 14 are exempt from giving biometrics. However, if a child was under 14 when IRCC received their permanent residence application and is now 14 or older, they must give biometrics when applying for a PR card. Asylum claimants follow different rules.

How long do I have to give biometrics after receiving the letter?

Your Biometric Instruction Letter sets the deadline, which is usually 30 days. If you cannot attend in time for a genuine reason, contact IRCC through its official web form before the deadline, explain the situation and include proof. Missing the deadline without explanation can lead to refusal.
