What Is LMIA and How Does It Affect Your Canadian Work Permit? [2025 Update]

If you’re planning to work in Canada or are an employer aiming to hire foreign talent, understanding the Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is critical. In 2025, the LMIA remains a foundational requirement under Canada’s Temporary Foreign Worker Program (TFWP), ensuring that the hiring of foreign nationals supports—not disrupts—the Canadian labour market. This guide explains everything you need to know about what is LMIA is, who needs one, how to apply, recent changes, and how it affects work permits and permanent residency pathways.

What Is LMIA (Labour Market Impact Assessment)?

A Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is an official document issued by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) that allows Canadian employers to hire foreign workers. It confirms that there is a genuine need for a foreign national to fill a job role and that no qualified Canadian citizen or permanent resident is available to do the work. When ESDC approves the application, a positive LMIA—also known as a confirmation letter—is issued. If the request is denied, it is referred to as a negative LMIA, and the employer cannot proceed with hiring a foreign worker for that position.

The LMIA process is a crucial safeguard to ensure that the hiring of foreign nationals does not adversely affect employment opportunities for Canadians. It helps maintain fair wages and working conditions, and it supports national efforts to close real labour gaps in sectors such as construction, hospitality, transportation, healthcare, and agriculture.

Why Is LMIA Required?

The main purpose of the LMIA requirement is to protect the Canadian labour market. Before hiring a foreign worker, an employer must prove to ESDC that they made genuine efforts to recruit Canadian citizens and permanent residents for the role. Employers must also demonstrate that the wage being offered meets or exceeds the prevailing median wage for that occupation in the region, and that working conditions align with Canadian labour standards. These requirements ensure that foreign workers are not hired as a cost-saving alternative to hiring locals, and that they are treated fairly once in Canada.

Who Needs an LMIA?

Most employer-specific work permits issued under the Temporary Foreign Worker Program require a positive LMIA. This includes jobs across various industries, from skilled trades and retail to caregiving and agriculture. However, not all work permits require an LMIA. There are several LMIA-exempt categories under Canada’s International Mobility Program (IMP), including open work permits for spouses of international students or skilled workers, post-graduation work permits (PGWP), intra-company transferees, and jobs covered under international agreements like CUSMA (formerly NAFTA) and CETA. It is important to assess your specific situation or job offer to determine whether the LMIA requirement applies.

Types of LMIA Applications

The LMIA program is divided into multiple streams based on wage levels, industry, and purpose. Employers must choose the right stream based on the role they are hiring for.

High-Wage LMIA applies when the job pays at or above the median wage for the province or territory. In this case, employers must submit a transition plan, which outlines how they will reduce their dependence on foreign labour over time. The transition plan might include efforts to train Canadian workers or assist foreign employees in obtaining permanent residency.

Low-Wage LMIA applies to positions that offer wages below the median threshold. Employers in this stream are subject to additional requirements, such as covering the cost of round-trip transportation for the foreign worker, providing affordable housing, offering private health insurance until the worker qualifies for public coverage, and registering the worker with the appropriate provincial workplace safety board. Additionally, employers hiring under the low-wage stream are often limited to a cap of 10% of their workforce being made up of low-wage foreign workers.

Global Talent Stream (GTS) is a fast-track LMIA stream for employers seeking to hire highly skilled workers in tech-related occupations or for roles that are considered essential and in-demand. Applications under this stream are typically processed within 10 business days, provided all documentation is accurate and the wage offered is competitive. Employers must also commit to a Labour Market Benefits Plan, which outlines how the hiring of a foreign worker will benefit Canada’s labour market in the long term.

Agricultural and Seasonal LMIA streams cater to primary agriculture employers and industries such as farming, meat processing, and fisheries. These employers must meet unique requirements related to seasonal work, housing standards, transportation, and safety. This stream also includes the Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP), which operates through bilateral agreements with participating countries.

How an LMIA Affects Your Canadian Work Permit

A positive LMIA is often the first step in the process of hiring a foreign worker or applying for a work permit in Canada. Once the employer has secured an approved LMIA from ESDC, the foreign worker can use this document to apply to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) for a closed work permit. The work permit will be tied specifically to the job and employer listed in the LMIA. This means the foreign worker cannot work for a different employer or in a different occupation unless a new LMIA and work permit are obtained.

The LMIA also confirms that the foreign worker will be paid fairly, provided a safe work environment, and granted access to benefits comparable to those received by Canadian workers in similar roles. In many industries, this system ensures a baseline of protection for both foreign workers and the broader Canadian workforce.

LMIA and Permanent Residency in 2025

As of 2025, a positive LMIA no longer provides additional points in the Express Entry Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS), which means that foreign workers cannot rely on LMIA-backed job offers to boost their CRS scores. However, LMIAs can still play a vital role in supporting permanent residency applications through other pathways.

For example, many Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs) consider LMIA-backed job offers as valid support for nominations. Some provinces specifically require job offers supported by an LMIA for certain in-demand occupations. Additionally, a valid job offer can demonstrate economic integration and intent to settle, which may be important factors in Humanitarian and Compassionate applications or other economic immigration programs. Moreover, an LMIA-supported work experience in Canada can help foreign workers qualify for the Canadian Experience Class after one year of skilled work under TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 categories.

How to Apply for an LMIA: Step-by-Step Guide

The LMIA application process involves multiple steps and documentation requirements. Employers must first determine whether the position requires an LMIA or qualifies for an exemption. If an LMIA is needed, the employer must then advertise the job position on at least three platforms, including the Job Bank, for a minimum of four consecutive weeks. The goal is to demonstrate that no Canadian or permanent resident applied who met the basic qualifications.

Once recruitment efforts are complete, the employer submits the LMIA application to ESDC along with supporting documents such as business registration, tax records, wage details, job descriptions, and recruitment logs. For high-wage roles, a transition plan is also mandatory. A processing fee of $1,000 per worker is required, and ESDC may follow up with phone interviews or request additional documentation before making a decision. Processing times vary depending on the stream and region, ranging from 10 business days under the Global Talent Stream to 6–12 weeks for standard applications.

Employer Responsibilities and Compliance

Employers who receive a positive LMIA and hire a foreign worker must adhere to strict compliance requirements. These include maintaining accurate payroll and employment records, adhering to the wage and working conditions promised in the offer, and ensuring the worker is treated fairly under Canadian labour laws. Employers may be subject to audits or inspections, and violations can result in fines, bans from hiring foreign workers in the future, or even criminal charges in cases of fraud or abuse.

For low-wage workers, employer responsibilities are even more extensive and include providing housing that meets municipal and provincial health standards, purchasing health insurance, and covering the costs of travel to and from the worker’s home country.

Recent Changes in 2025 LMIA Policy

In 2025, some key updates have affected the LMIA process. Most notably, the removal of CRS points for LMIA-supported job offers under Express Entry means that LMIA is now more directly linked to work authorization and employer needs rather than a points-based immigration boost. Additionally, ESDC has updated median wage thresholds across provinces, making it necessary for employers to stay current on wage requirements to avoid rejections. There is also increased scrutiny of employer compliance and more frequent site visits, particularly in sectors that are prone to abuse or non-compliance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Many LMIA applications fail due to avoidable errors. Common mistakes include submitting incomplete recruitment records, offering wages below the regional median, misclassifying the NOC code, or failing to align job duties with the listed occupation. Employers must also ensure that all documents are accurate and up to date, as discrepancies may lead to refusal. It is strongly recommended to consult a Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant (RCIC) or an experienced immigration professional to review applications before submission.

Conclusion

The Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is a critical part of Canada’s immigration and employment system. For employers, it is a tool that allows them to hire international talent when Canadian workers are not available. For foreign workers, it is often the first step in securing legal authorization to work in Canada. While the LMIA no longer adds points under Express Entry, it remains essential for obtaining closed work permits and can still support permanent residency applications through other immigration streams.

Understanding the rules, preparing proper documentation, and staying compliant with current wage and labour standards are key to a successful LMIA outcome.
