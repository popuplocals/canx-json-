India remains one of the largest source countries for Canadian permanent residents, and in 2026 Canada still plans to welcome 380,000 new permanent residents, with 239,800 of those spaces in economic programs. But the path looks different than it did a few years ago. Express Entry now favours candidates already in Canada and those in targeted categories, study permits are capped, and several provincial graduate streams have closed.

This guide gives Indian applicants a complete, realistic roadmap for Canada immigration from India in 2026: the main pathways, the documents you need, the rule changes that matter most and the mistakes that cost families time and money.

Quick Summary

Indian citizens need a visitor visa to travel to Canada. The main routes to permanent residence are Express Entry (Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class and Federal Skilled Trades), provincial nominee programs, family sponsorship and the study or work-to-PR pathway. Strong English scores, an Educational Credential Assessment, verified work experience and an Indian Police Clearance Certificate are essential. In 2026, category-based Express Entry draws and provincial nominations matter more than ever.

Canada's 2026 Immigration Picture

Canada’s 2026 to 2028 Immigration Levels Plan sets a target of 380,000 permanent residents in each of those years, including 239,800 economic immigrants in 2026. Temporary resident arrivals are lower, with targets of 230,000 new workers and 155,000 new students in 2026. For Indian applicants, that means competition remains strong, and careful planning matters.

Pathway 1: Express Entry

Express Entry manages three federal programs:

  • Federal Skilled Worker Program: for skilled workers abroad, with at least one year of continuous skilled work experience, CLB 7 language and enough points on the 67-point grid.
  • Canadian Experience Class: for people with at least one year of skilled Canadian work experience.
  • Federal Skilled Trades Program: for qualified tradespeople with a job offer or Canadian certificate of qualification.

Candidates receive a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score. In 2026, many Express Entry invitations went to candidates in categories such as French proficiency, healthcare and social services, and trades, as well as Canadian Experience Class candidates. Job offer points were removed in March 2025, so language scores and categories carry more weight.

Pathway 2: Provincial Nominee Programs

A provincial nomination through an Express Entry-linked stream adds 600 CRS points. Provinces target their own priority occupations. In 2026, Alberta, British Columbia and Saskatchewan prioritized health care, skilled trades and selected technology roles, while Ontario launched a new employer-driven stream. Many streams require a job offer or a connection to the province.

Pathway 3: Study in Canada, Then Work and Apply for PR

Studying in Canada can still lead to permanent residence, but it needs a plan. Most new students need a provincial attestation letter, although master’s and doctoral students at public institutions are exempt since January 1, 2026. After graduation, eligible students can get a Post-Graduation Work Permit, gain skilled Canadian experience and apply through the Canadian Experience Class or a provincial program.

Pathway 4: Work Permits

Some Indian professionals come to Canada through employer-supported work permits, such as LMIA-based permits or intra-company transfers for multinational employers. Canadian work experience can then support a permanent residence application.

Pathway 5: Family Sponsorship

Canadian citizens and permanent residents can sponsor spouses, common-law partners and dependent children. Parents and grandparents can visit for long periods on a super visa, since new Parents and Grandparents Program intake was paused on July 15, 2026.

Express Entry for Indian Applicants in 2026: What Works

  • Push English scores to CLB 9 or higher, which unlocks major transferability points.
  • Consider learning French; French draws carried some of the lowest cut-offs in 2026.
  • Claim every credential and include your spouse’s language and education where it helps.
  • Align your NOC code carefully with your actual duties.
  • Track category-based draws for healthcare, trades and other priority categories.

Common Mistakes Indian Applicants Make

  • Using unlicensed agents who promise guaranteed visas or job offers.
  • Paying for job offers or LMIAs, which is illegal and can lead to bans.
  • Submitting reference letters that do not match the claimed NOC duties.
  • Choosing study programs only for immigration, without checking PGWP eligibility.
  • Failing to declare previous refusals from any country.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best way to immigrate to Canada from India in 2026?

The best route depends on your profile. Express Entry suits skilled workers with strong English, education and experience. Provincial nominee programs suit candidates whose occupation or job offer matches a province’s priorities. Studying in Canada and then working can lead to PR, but requires careful program selection under the 2026 rules.

Do Indian citizens need a visa to visit Canada?

Yes. Indian citizens need a visitor visa (temporary resident visa) to travel to Canada, and must give biometrics as part of the application. Applicants should show a clear purpose of travel, ties to India, and enough funds for their stay. A visitor visa does not allow work or long-term study.

How do I get a Police Clearance Certificate from India for Canada?

Indian applicants generally apply for a Police Clearance Certificate through Passport Seva or, if living abroad, through an Indian embassy or consulate. Follow IRCC’s country-specific instructions, submit colour scans of the original and make sure the certificate meets IRCC’s timing rules for your current or past country of residence.

What CRS score do Indian applicants need in 2026?

There is no separate score for Indian applicants; everyone competes in the same pool. In 2026, Canadian Experience Class draws generally required scores in the high 510s to low 520s, while some category draws required much less. A provincial nomination adds 600 points and changes the picture entirely.
