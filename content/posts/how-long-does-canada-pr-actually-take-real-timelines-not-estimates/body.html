One of the most common questions people have when they start the PR process is simply: how long is this going to take? And the most honest answer is that it depends on the program you are applying through, how complete your application is, and what is happening in the immigration system at the time you apply.

Here are the real timelines, program by program, as realistically as they can be stated.

Processing Timelines by Program

Express Entry Programs: The Fastest Federal Route

Express Entry encompasses three programs: the Federal Skilled Worker Program, the Canadian Experience Class, and the Federal Skilled Trades Program. Once you receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA), IRCC has a target of processing complete applications within six months.

In practice, this target is met for most complete applications. However, requests for additional documents, medical follow-up, or background check delays can extend processing beyond six months. The six-month clock starts when IRCC receives your full application, not when you submit it, and not from the date of your ITA.

Getting to the ITA stage adds time before the six-month processing window begins. You need to first create your Express Entry profile, wait in the pool for a draw where your score is competitive, and receive the ITA. Depending on your CRS score and the programs being drawn, this waiting period can range from a few weeks (if your score is high) to indefinitely (if your score is below current draw cutoffs without a provincial nomination or other score boost).

Provincial Nominee Programs: The Two-Stage Reality

PNP applications involve two separate stages. In the first stage, you apply to the province for a nomination. This stage is processed by the provincial government, not IRCC, and timelines vary significantly by province and stream. Processing a provincial nomination can take anywhere from two to six months for some streams to over a year for others.

In the second stage, once you receive the provincial nomination, you then apply to IRCC for federal permanent residency. If you applied through the enhanced PNP stream linked to Express Entry, IRCC treats this as a priority application with a processing target of approximately six months. If you applied through the base PNP stream (separate from Express Entry), federal processing can take longer.

Total timeline for a PNP route from initial provincial application to receiving your PR: plan for 12 to 24 months in most cases, with significant variation by province and stream.

Spousal and Family Sponsorship: Depends on Who and How

Spousal sponsorship (sponsoring a spouse or common-law partner) is the fastest family class option. IRCC currently targets approximately 12 months for these applications. Applications submitted from within Canada, where the sponsored spouse is already present, are often processed somewhat faster than applications where the sponsored person is outside Canada.

Parent and Grandparent Program sponsorship is a very different timeline. The PGP has been historically slow, with processing taking two to five years or longer in some cases. IRCC has been working to reduce this backlog, but the PGP remains one of the slower programs in the system.

What Causes Delays Beyond the Stated Processing Times

There are several common reasons applications take longer than the standard processing times. A missing document or an incomplete form generates a procedural fairness letter or a request for information, which pauses processing until you respond. Responding slowly to these requests extends your timeline significantly.

1

Medical examination results that expire during processing require you to redo the exam. Immigration medical exams are valid for 12 months, so if your application is not finalized within that window and you submitted the exam early, you may need a new one.

2

Criminal background checks from certain countries take longer than others. If your police certificate is from a country with a slower processing system, factoring in extra time for this step is smart.

3

Applications with complex immigration histories, including previous refusals, periods of unauthorized status, or criminal inadmissibility concerns, are subject to more thorough review and will take longer regardless of the program.

How to Track Your Application

All IRCC applications can be tracked through your online IRCC account. The portal shows your application status, any action items you need to complete, and whether your file has moved to the decision stage. Checking the portal regularly is the most reliable way to stay informed about your application progress.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I leave Canada while my PR application is being processed?

Yes, you can travel while your PR application is being processed. However, if you are in Canada on implied status (your temporary permit expired but you applied to renew it before expiry), leaving Canada would end your implied status and you would need a valid travel document to re-enter. Checking your status situation before any international travel is important.

What happens if IRCC makes an error that delays my application?

If there is a documented processing error by IRCC, you can submit a webform to IRCC requesting a review. For applications that have significantly exceeded the posted processing time without a request for additional documents, a webform inquiry is also an appropriate step. A regulated consultant can help you determine when and how to follow up effectively.

How do I know when my PR has been approved?

IRCC will notify you through your online account and by email. If your application is approved, you will receive a Confirmation of Permanent Residence (COPR) and, if you are outside Canada, an immigrant visa. If you are already in Canada, you will be asked to confirm your arrival at a port of entry, where your PR status will be confirmed.
