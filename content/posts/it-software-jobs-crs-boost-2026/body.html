For the past two years, software developers, data scientists, and IT managers have watched Express Entry draws with growing frustration. Category-based draws went to health care, trades, French speakers, and other priority groups, while most technology occupations had no category of their own. Many highly skilled tech workers with strong profiles waited for invitations that never came.

On November 3, 2026, that changes. Under new rules from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), 11 IT and software occupations on the new high-wage list can earn up to 100 Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points for a qualifying job offer, or up to 70 extra points for Canadian work experience. The message is clear: Canada wants to attract and keep technology talent, and it is now rewarding tech workers directly in the CRS itself.

Quick Summary

From November 3, 2026, 11 IT and software occupations qualify for new CRS points under Express Entry Canada 2026. Computer and information systems managers sit at Level 1 (100 points for a job offer, or 70 extra points for 12 months of Canadian experience). Nine occupations, including software developers, software engineers, data scientists, and cybersecurity specialists, sit at Level 2 (70 or 40 points). Web developers sit at Level 3 (50 or 20 points). Job offers no longer need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). For tech workers left out of category-based draws, this could make a real difference.

Why Tech Workers Have Struggled in Express Entry

When IRCC launched category-based selection in 2023, it included a science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM) category that covered many IT occupations. In February 2025, IRCC updated that category and removed most IT occupations, keeping only cybersecurity specialists. When IRCC announced its 2026 categories in February 2026, the STEM category continued, but software developers, software engineers, data scientists, IT managers, and web developers were still not included.

That left most tech workers competing only in general draws, where they faced high cut-off scores. Many candidates with strong education, language results, and Canadian work experience in technology found it difficult to get an Invitation to Apply (ITA).

The November 3 changes do not create a new tech category. Instead, they do something potentially more powerful: they reward tech occupations in every candidate’s CRS score, so tech workers can rise in the pool for any draw where CRS rank matters.

What the New Points Mean in Practice

IRCC’s current CRS criteria give a single candidate 40 points for one year of Canadian work experience. From November 3, a single software developer (NOC 21232, Level 2) with one year of Canadian experience can earn 40 extra high-wage points on top of that, for 80 in total. A single IT manager (NOC 20012, Level 1) with one year of Canadian experience can earn 70 extra, for 110 in total.

For candidates with a qualifying job offer, the numbers are even stronger: 100 points for an IT manager, 70 for a software developer, and 50 for a web developer. Job offer and work experience points do not stack; IRCC awards whichever gives you more. The new factor caps at 150 points for candidates without a spouse and 140 for those with an accompanying spouse or common-law partner.

Why Canada Wants Tech Talent Now

IRCC has said the changes aim to attract and retain the skilled workers best positioned to succeed in the Canadian labour market, with more weight on factors linked to stronger economic outcomes. Technology occupations fit that goal closely. Most pay well above the national average, and the skills involved drive productivity across banking, health, retail, energy, government, and nearly every other sector.

Canada has also invested heavily in building its technology sector, including artificial intelligence research and a growing number of tech hubs in cities such as Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Waterloo, Calgary, and Ottawa. Those employers need skilled people, and these changes make it easier for Canada to keep the tech talent already working here and to attract more.

The US Context: Why Canada's Timing Matters

Canada is not making this change in isolation. In the United States, the path for foreign tech workers has become more uncertain.

  • The H-1B fee: A US presidential proclamation imposed a $100,000 payment on certain new H-1B petitions filed on or after September 21, 2025, mainly for workers outside the United States. Its implementation has since faced legal challenges, and its status has shifted during 2026.
  • The H-1B lottery: The United States introduced a wage-weighted selection process for H-1B registrations, which gives higher-paid applicants more chances in the lottery.
  • Green card backlogs: US employment-based green cards are subject to per-country limits. For applicants from high-demand countries such as India, waits in some categories can stretch for many years.

These rules change often and are subject to ongoing litigation, so check official US government sources for the latest position. But the overall picture is clear: many skilled tech workers who once looked only at the United States are now weighing their options. Canada’s new CRS points give them a strong reason to look north, with a defined path to permanent residence rather than an open-ended wait.

Three Paths for Tech Workers Under the New Rules

1. Already Working in Canada

If you have at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience in one of these occupations, IRCC will add your high-wage points automatically when it recalculates scores after November 3. Many tech workers on Post-Graduation Work Permits or employer-specific permits could see their scores rise immediately.

2. A Qualifying Job Offer, Without an LMIA

From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation no longer needs an LMIA to earn CRS points. For tech workers, this opens doors that were previously difficult, since many tech employers did not want to go through the LMIA process. IRCC officers will still assess every offer for genuineness, so the employer must be an active business with a real need for the role.

3. Applying From Outside Canada

Candidates abroad without Canadian experience can benefit through a qualifying job offer. Remote work for a foreign employer does not count as Canadian work experience, so focus on strengthening your core factors, such as language scores, and on building genuine connections with Canadian employers.

How to Improve Your CRS Score in 2026 as a Tech Worker

  1. Choose the right NOC code. Tech job titles vary widely. A “software engineer” in one company may do the work of a “software developer” in another. Match your duties to the NOC description, not the title. Note that the term “engineer” is regulated in Canadian provinces.
  2. Document your experience in detail. Reference letters should list your actual duties, technologies, hours, salary, and dates.
  3. Track your Canadian experience. You need at least 12 months of eligible experience in a high-wage occupation to earn the extra points.
  4. Maximize your language scores. Language ability remains one of the most valuable CRS factors.
  5. Update your profile before November 3. IRCC recalculates work experience points automatically, but you must enter job offer details yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which IT jobs get extra CRS points in Express Entry 2026?

From November 3, 2026, 11 IT and software occupations qualify: computer and information systems managers (20012), data scientists (21211), cybersecurity specialists (21220), business systems specialists (21221), information systems specialists (21222), database analysts (21223), computer systems developers (21230), software engineers (21231), software developers (21232), computer engineers (21311), and web developers (21234).

Is software developer a high-wage occupation for Express Entry?

Yes. Software developers and programmers (NOC 21232) sit at Level 2 of IRCC’s high-wage list. That means 70 CRS points for a qualifying job offer, or 40 extra points for at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience, added to your regular work experience points.

Is there an Express Entry category for IT workers in 2026?

Not for most IT occupations. The 2026 STEM category includes cybersecurity specialists (21220), but not software developers, software engineers, data scientists, IT managers, or web developers. However, the new high-wage points apply in the CRS itself, so they can help tech workers rank higher in the pool regardless of category.

Does remote work for a US company count as Canadian work experience?

Generally, Canadian work experience means work performed in Canada with proper authorization. Remote work for a foreign employer while living outside Canada does not count. If you work remotely from inside Canada, the rules can be complex, so get advice on your specific situation before claiming it.

Should I choose Canada over the US for my tech career?

That depends on your goals, family, and career plans. Canada now offers tech workers a clearer path to permanent residence through Express Entry, while US rules for H-1B visas and green cards remain uncertain.
