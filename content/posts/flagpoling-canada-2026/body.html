For years, thousands of temporary residents drove to the US border, turned around without entering the United States, and walked back into Canada with a new work permit in hand. Canada’s border agency processed more than 69,300 of these “flagpoling” requests in a single year. Then, on December 23, 2024, the government ended flagpoling for most work and study permits.

If you are wondering whether flagpoling is still worth it in 2026, especially as a future permanent resident, the short answer is: for most people, no. This guide explains what flagpoling was, what changed, who can still get permits at the border, how permanent residence applicants are affected and which options now work better.

Quick Summary

Since December 23, 2024, most temporary residents can no longer get work or study permits by flagpoling. Exceptions include US citizens and US lawful permanent residents, certain free trade agreement professionals and some of their spouses, international truck drivers in specific situations, and people with a pre-booked appointment with the Canada Border Services Agency. Most applicants should apply online and rely on maintained status while IRCC processes their application.

What Is Flagpoling?

Flagpoling describes leaving Canada, entering the United States or Saint Pierre and Miquelon without staying, and immediately returning to a Canadian port of entry to receive same-day immigration services from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA). People used it to avoid waiting months for an online decision, most often for post-graduation work permits, spousal open work permits and permit extensions.

What Changed on December 23, 2024?

According to the CBSA announcement, as of December 23, 2024, at 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time, foreign nationals with temporary resident status in Canada can no longer obtain work or study permits by flagpoling. The government said flagpoling diverted border resources from enforcement and added to cross-border wait times, and directed applicants to apply online with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

Who Can Still Get a Permit at the Border?

The CBSA announcement lists limited exceptions:

Even if you fit an exception, you must still meet every requirement for the permit and admissibility rules, and the officer makes the final decision.

What About Flagpoling to Become a Permanent Resident?

The December 2024 change targeted work and study permits. Most people who become permanent residents from inside Canada now confirm their status online through IRCC’s PR confirmation process, which avoids travel altogether. Some applicants may still complete landing at a port of entry, but service availability and hours differ by location. Check your confirmation instructions from IRCC and confirm with the CBSA before you travel, because leaving Canada always carries some risk.

The Hidden Risks of Leaving Canada

Flagpoling always required leaving Canada, even briefly, which carries risks people often overlook:

  • If you applied to extend your status from inside Canada, leaving can end your maintained status.
  • US authorities can deny entry or ask questions, depending on your nationality and documents.
  • A CBSA officer can refuse your permit or find issues with your admissibility.
  • Border wait times and limited service hours can make the trip longer than expected.

Smarter Alternatives in 2026

Apply online before your status expires

Submit your work or study permit application online through IRCC before your current status expires. Applying on time is the single most important step.

Rely on maintained status

If you apply to extend your status before it expires and stay in Canada, you can generally remain under the same conditions while IRCC decides. This is commonly called maintained status. If you leave Canada, maintained status usually ends, so plan travel carefully.

Consider a bridging open work permit

Some permanent residence applicants, including many Express Entry applicants whose work permits are expiring, may qualify for a bridging open work permit that lets them keep working while IRCC finishes their PR application.

Request urgent processing only when justified

IRCC considers urgent requests in limited circumstances. Prepare clear evidence if your situation qualifies.

So, Is Flagpoling Still Worth It in 2026?

For most temporary residents, flagpoling is no longer an option at all. For those covered by an exception, it can still save time, but it requires careful preparation and carries real risk. For future permanent residents, online processes and maintained status usually offer a safer, simpler path. The best strategy is to plan applications early so you never depend on a same-day border decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is flagpoling still allowed in Canada in 2026?

For most people, no. Since December 23, 2024, temporary residents can no longer obtain work or study permits by flagpoling. Limited exceptions apply to US citizens and US lawful permanent residents, certain free trade agreement professionals and some of their spouses, some international truck drivers and people with a pre-booked CBSA appointment.

Can I flagpole for a post-graduation work permit?

Generally, no. The December 2024 change ended flagpoling for post-graduation work permits for most applicants. Apply online before your study permit expires. If you apply on time and remain in Canada, you may be able to work under maintained status while IRCC processes your application, subject to the applicable conditions.

Can I still land as a permanent resident at the border?

The December 2024 change focused on work and study permits. Most applicants inside Canada now confirm permanent residence online, without travelling. Some may complete landing at a port of entry, but service availability varies by location. Follow IRCC’s confirmation instructions and check with the CBSA before you leave Canada.

What is maintained status in Canada?

Maintained status lets you remain in Canada under the conditions of your current permit if you applied to extend or change your status before it expired. You can generally keep working or studying under the same conditions while IRCC decides. Leaving Canada usually ends maintained status, so plan travel carefully.
