Quick Summary

STEM is an official 2026 Express Entry category, but no STEM draws were held by early October 2026. STEM professionals usually qualify through the Canadian Experience Class or Federal Skilled Worker Program and compete on their CRS scores, often supported by high language scores and Canadian experience. Provincial options include British Columbia’s high economic impact selection, Alberta’s Accelerated Tech Pathway and Ontario’s priority software developer occupation.

The STEM Category in 2026

IRCC confirmed STEM, including cybersecurity workers, as one of its 2026 priority categories. To qualify for a STEM draw, you would need at least 12 months of experience in an eligible STEM occupation within the past three years and eligibility for an Express Entry program. However, being an official category does not guarantee draws. In 2025 and through early October 2026, IRCC concentrated category invitations on French, healthcare, trades and new targeted categories.

How STEM Professionals Compete in Express Entry

Most STEM candidates rely on the Canadian Experience Class or the Federal Skilled Worker Program. Your Comprehensive Ranking System score decides your ranking. STEM professionals often have strong education and experience, so the biggest gains usually come from:

  • Language scores at CLB 9 or higher in all four abilities.
  • Canadian work experience, especially two years or more.
  • A second credential or advanced degree.
  • French at NCLC 7 or higher, which opens French draws.

Other Options for STEM Professionals

  • Intra-company transfers for employees of multinational technology and engineering firms.
  • Global Talent Stream LMIAs for eligible in-demand tech occupations, which are designed for faster processing.
  • Researchers with Canadian work experience category for university professors, lecturers and post-secondary research assistants.
  • Trade agreement work permits for engineers, computer systems analysts and other listed professionals from partner countries.

The Proposed High-Wage Factor

IRCC’s 2026 Express Entry reform proposals included new points for Canadian work experience or job offers in high-wage occupations. Many STEM roles pay above the median wage and could benefit if the change is implemented. Until IRCC announces it, current rules apply.

How to Stand Out as a STEM Candidate

  • Choose your NOC code carefully and match it to your duties.
  • Document achievements, projects and responsibilities in reference letters.
  • Gain Canadian experience through work permits or the Global Talent Stream.
  • Target provinces that value your specific skills.
  • Keep your Express Entry profile active and updated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there STEM Express Entry draws in 2026?

STEM is an official 2026 category, but IRCC had not held a STEM category draw by early October 2026. STEM professionals should not rely on a STEM draw alone. Most compete through the Canadian Experience Class or Federal Skilled Worker Program and explore provincial pathways that value technology and engineering skills.

What is the best way for tech workers to immigrate to Canada in 2026?

The best route depends on your profile. Many tech workers gain Canadian experience through work permits, then apply through the Canadian Experience Class. Provincial options include Alberta’s Accelerated Tech Pathway, British Columbia’s high economic impact selection and Ontario’s Workforce Priority Stream, which lists software developers as a priority.

Does the Global Talent Stream lead to permanent residence?

The Global Talent Stream is a work permit pathway, not a permanent residence program. However, the Canadian work experience you gain through it can help you qualify for the Canadian Experience Class or provincial programs, which can lead to permanent residence.

Do engineers need a licence to work in Canada?

Engineers who want to use the title “engineer” or practise regulated engineering work usually need a licence from a provincial engineering regulator. You can often immigrate before licensing, but you should start the process early, because it can take time and may require exams or Canadian experience.
