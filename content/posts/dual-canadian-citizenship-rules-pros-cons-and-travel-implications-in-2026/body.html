Canada is known worldwide for its welcoming approach to dual and multiple citizenships. Unlike many countries that restrict or prohibit dual nationality, Canada fully recognizes and supports it. This makes Canadian citizenship especially attractive for immigrants and their families. Here is everything you need to know in 2026.

What Is Dual Canadian Citizenship?

Dual citizenship means holding citizenship in Canada and at least one other country at the same time. Canada does not issue a special certificate for dual citizenship — it simply recognizes it. You do not need to apply for dual citizenship; it occurs automatically when you become a Canadian citizen while retaining your previous nationality (provided your home country also allows it).

Since 1977, Canada has permitted its citizens to acquire foreign citizenships without losing their Canadian status. Canadians can also naturalize in another country while keeping their Canadian citizenship.

Official Rules Under Canadian Law

  • Canada allows unlimited multiple citizenships.
  • You cannot lose Canadian citizenship unless you voluntarily renounce it through a formal process.
  • Children born to Canadian citizens abroad may acquire dual citizenship automatically.
  • There are no restrictions based on age, marital status, or time spent outside Canada.
  • You must follow the laws of all countries whose citizenship you hold.

Important Note: While Canada allows dual citizenship, not every country does. Some nations automatically revoke citizenship if you acquire another nationality. Always check with the embassy or consulate of your other country of citizenship before proceeding.

Major Pros of Dual Canadian Citizenship

  • Best of Both Worlds Enjoy rights, benefits, and opportunities in two (or more) countries — social services, education, healthcare, and work rights.
  • Powerful Passport Advantage The Canadian passport ranks among the world’s strongest, offering visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 181–182 destinations. You can choose the most convenient passport for each trip.
  • Global Mobility and Flexibility Easier international travel, business opportunities, and the ability to live, work, or retire in multiple countries.
  • Stronger Family Connections Maintain cultural and familial ties to your country of origin while building a future in Canada. Pass citizenship benefits to your children.
  • Enhanced Security Access to Canadian consular protection abroad and the right to return to Canada anytime.
  • Broader Career and Educational Opportunities Access jobs restricted to citizens in Canada and opportunities in your other country of citizenship.
  • Estate and Inheritance Planning Often simplifies property ownership, inheritance, and tax planning across borders.

Potential Cons and Challenges

  • Tax Reporting Obligations Some countries (notably the United States) tax citizens on worldwide income regardless of where they live. You may need to file taxes in multiple countries.
  • Military Service Requirements A few countries still have compulsory military service that could apply to dual citizens, especially males of certain ages.
  • Legal Complications Potential conflicts in laws regarding marriage, divorce, child custody, or inheritance between countries.
  • Entry and Exit Rules You must follow the passport and immigration rules of every country you hold citizenship in.
  • Renunciation Complexity If you ever wish to renounce Canadian citizenship, the process is formal, irreversible, and involves fees and potential tax implications.
  • Banking and Financial Hurdles Some international banks may view dual citizens as higher risk, complicating account opening or loans.

Travel Implications for Dual Canadian Citizens

This is one of the most important practical aspects:

  • Entering or Leaving Canada by Air: You must use a valid Canadian passport. You cannot board a flight to Canada using only your foreign passport.
  • Land or Sea Travel: A Canadian passport is strongly recommended, though rules may be slightly more flexible.
  • Entering Your Other Country: You must usually use the passport of that country when entering and exiting it.
  • Third Countries: You can generally choose which passport to use based on visa requirements or convenience.
  • Transit Through Canada: Dual citizens need a Canadian passport even for transit.

Best Practice: Always travel with both (or all) valid passports. Carry them in your carry-on luggage.
