Canada’s supply chains depend on truck drivers, and many drivers hope that a career on Canadian highways can lead to permanent residence. It can, but the route matters. In 2026, IRCC’s Express Entry transport category focuses on pilots, aircraft mechanics and inspectors, and automotive and truck mechanics. Transport truck drivers (NOC 73300) are not included. That means drivers need to look at other federal, provincial and employer-driven pathways.

Quick Summary

Transport truck drivers (NOC 73300) are a TEER 3 occupation. They are not part of the 2026 Express Entry transport category, which held one draw on August 7, 2026. Truck drivers can still qualify for the Canadian Experience Class with CLB 5 after a year of Canadian experience, and some provincial programs target drivers, including Ontario, which lists transport truck drivers as a priority occupation. Saskatchewan caps trucking at about 238 nominations in 2026.

Where Truck Drivers Fit in the System

Transport truck drivers fall under NOC 73300, a TEER 3 occupation. TEER 3 jobs qualify as skilled work for Express Entry programs. However, category-based draws only help when your occupation is on the list, and in 2026 the transport category covers only these occupations:

  • Air pilots, flight engineers and flying instructors (NOC 72600)
  • Aircraft mechanics and aircraft inspectors (NOC 72404)
  • Aircraft instrument, electrical and avionics mechanics, technicians and inspectors (NOC 22313)
  • Automotive service technicians, truck and bus mechanics and mechanical repairers (NOC 72410)

The only 2026 transport draw by early October was held on August 7, with 300 invitations at a minimum CRS of 470.

Pathway 1: Canadian Experience Class

After at least one year of full-time skilled work experience as a truck driver in Canada, you may qualify for the Canadian Experience Class. For TEER 3 jobs, the language minimum is CLB 5. Your Comprehensive Ranking System score then decides whether you receive an invitation, and recent CEC draws required scores in the high 510s to low 520s, so many drivers also need a provincial nomination.

Pathway 3: Community and Regional Programs

Programs such as the Rural Community Immigration Pilot and the Atlantic Immigration Program are employer-driven. If a designated employer in a participating community offers you a qualifying job, these programs may offer a route to permanent residence.

Pathway 4: LMIA-Based Work Permits

Many drivers first come to Canada on an employer-specific work permit supported by a Labour Market Impact Assessment. This builds the Canadian experience needed for CEC and provincial programs. Make sure your employer is genuine and compliant, and never pay for a job offer.

Practical Requirements for Drivers

  • Provincial commercial driver’s licence: you must hold the correct licence class for the province where you work.
  • Clean driving and criminal record: safety records matter to employers and immigration officers.
  • Language scores: CLB 5 for the Canadian Experience Class, with higher scores improving your CRS.
  • Logbooks and pay stubs: keep records proving full-time hours.

How to Improve Your Chances

  • Raise your language scores to CLB 7 or higher if possible.
  • Work for an employer in a province that values drivers.
  • Keep your work permit valid and plan provincial applications early.
  • Consider learning French for extra CRS points and French category draws.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are truck drivers eligible for the Express Entry transport category in 2026?

No. The 2026 transport category covers pilots, aircraft mechanics and inspectors, aircraft instrument and avionics technicians, and automotive service technicians and truck and bus mechanics. Transport truck drivers (NOC 73300) are not included, although they can still qualify for other Express Entry and provincial pathways.

Can truck drivers get PR through the Canadian Experience Class?

Yes. Truck drivers are a TEER 3 occupation, so one year of full-time Canadian work experience as a driver can qualify you for the Canadian Experience Class, with a minimum of CLB 5. You still need a CRS score high enough for an invitation, which is why many drivers also pursue provincial nominations.

Which provinces help truck drivers get PR in 2026?

Ontario lists transport truck drivers as a priority occupation under its new Ontario Workforce Priority Stream, which requires a job offer. Saskatchewan accepts trucking applications but caps the sector at about 238 nominations with scheduled intake windows. Other provinces and community programs may help drivers with qualifying job offers.

What language score do truck drivers need for PR?

For the Canadian Experience Class, truck drivers in TEER 3 need at least CLB 5 in all four abilities. Provincial programs set their own minimums. Higher language scores significantly improve your Comprehensive Ranking System score and your chances of receiving an invitation.
