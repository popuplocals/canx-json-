In recent years, Canada has taken deliberate steps to increase Francophone immigration outside Quebec, recognizing the importance of linguistic diversity and the vitality of French-speaking communities across the country. While Quebec independently manages its own immigration programs to protect and promote the French language, the federal government is committed to strengthening Francophone communities in other provinces and territories.

This blog explores why Canada is prioritizing Francophone immigration outside Quebec, how the programs work, and what this means for French-speaking applicants seeking permanent residence or work in Canada.

Why Canada Is Promoting Francophone Immigration Outside Quebec

French and English are Canada’s two official languages, and the federal government aims to support the development of minority Francophone communities in provinces and territories where French is not the dominant language. According to the Official Languages Act, Canada is committed to ensuring that French-speaking Canadians can live and thrive in communities across the country.

Key motivations behind this policy include:

    • Addressing population decline in rural and smaller Francophone communities
    • Supporting bilingualism and cultural diversity
    • Meeting labour market needs in under-served regions
    • Strengthening Canada’s national identity as a bilingual country

In 2019, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) committed to increasing the proportion of French-speaking immigrants outside Quebec to 4.4% by 2023. That target has since been surpassed, but the government has introduced new measures to sustain and grow Francophone immigration long term.

How Canada Supports Francophone Immigration Outside Quebec

Canada’s immigration system includes dedicated pathways and incentives to attract and retain French-speaking applicants. These are primarily targeted at skilled workers, international graduates, and temporary foreign workers who can contribute to Francophone communities across the country.

1. Express Entry – French-Language Proficiency Category

In 2023, IRCC introduced category-based Express Entry draws, including one specifically for candidates with strong French-language skills. Applicants with a CLB level of 7 or higher in French may be selected even with lower Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) scores than general draws.

Benefits:

    • Easier access to permanent residency
    • No requirement for English language scores
    • Applicable across all provinces outside Quebec

2. Francophone Mobility Program

The Francophone Mobility Program allows Canadian employers to hire French-speaking foreign workers without requiring a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA).

Eligibility Criteria:

    • Job must be located outside Quebec
    • Applicant’s habitual language of daily use must be French
    • Worker must qualify for an employer-specific work permit under the program

This program makes it easier for employers to fill labour gaps and promotes the growth of French-speaking communities across Canada.

3. Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs)

Several provinces offer targeted streams or bonus points for French-speaking applicants:

    • Ontario’s French-Speaking Skilled Worker Stream under the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP)
    • New Brunswick and Manitoba offer additional supports for bilingual and French-speaking applicants
    • Francophone preference or additional points under certain rural and community-focused PNPs

4. IRCC Settlement Services for Francophones

IRCC funds Francophone settlement services, including:

    • Language training in French
    • Employment support
    • Community integration programs
    • Referrals to Francophone schools and health services

These services help newcomers settle in Francophone minority communities and stay long term.

Recent Developments and Policy Updates

In 2024, IRCC announced a new Francophone Immigration Policy, which focuses on:

    • A long-term vision to support Francophone population growth
    • Ongoing category-based Express Entry draws targeting French speakers
    • Funding and support for Francophone organizations and employers
    • New promotional campaigns to attract French-speaking immigrants from Africa, Europe, and the Caribbean

Canada also increased its annual targets for Francophone immigration outside Quebec as part of the broader Immigration Levels Plan.

Who Should Consider Francophone Immigration Pathways?

The following individuals may benefit from Canada’s Francophone immigration priorities:

    • Skilled professionals with high French proficiency
    • Francophone international students in Canada
    • French-speaking workers from Africa, the Caribbean, Europe, or the Middle East
    • Individuals seeking to work or settle outside Quebec with language skills aligned to community needs

Why French-Speaking Immigrants Are Choosing Canada

    • Clear and growing demand for French-language skills
    • Competitive immigration advantages, including faster processing
    • Opportunities to live in safe, welcoming, and bilingual communities
    • Support from both government and community-based organizations

Final Thoughts: French Speakers Have a Unique Advantage in Canada

Canada’s commitment to supporting Francophone communities outside Quebec is not just a policy—it is a long-term national strategy. Whether you are a skilled worker, a student, or an international professional fluent in French, you may have a competitive advantage when applying through programs that prioritize linguistic diversity.
