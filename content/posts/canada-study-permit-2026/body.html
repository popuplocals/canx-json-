Canada expects to issue roughly 408,000 study permits in 2026, and it has reserved about 309,670 application spaces for students who need a provincial or territorial attestation letter. Those numbers tell you two things: Canada still welcomes international students, and it is managing the system far more tightly than it did a few years ago. A strong, well-documented application matters more than ever.

This guide explains what every international student needs to know about a Canada study permit in 2026: the cap and attestation letter rules, the new graduate student exemption, proof of funds, work rights, family options and how to apply.

Quick Summary

Most new study permit applicants need a letter of acceptance from a designated learning institution and a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL or TAL). Since January 1, 2026, master’s and doctoral students at public institutions are exempt from the PAL/TAL requirement and the cap. Students outside Quebec must show $22,895 for living costs plus first-year tuition and travel. Eligible students can work up to 24 hours per week off campus during academic sessions.

The 2026 Study Permit Cap and PAL/TAL Rules

Canada continues to cap the number of study permit applications it accepts. Most applicants must include a PAL or TAL from the province or territory where they will study. Provinces receive a share of the national cap and issue letters to their institutions.

You generally do not need a PAL or TAL if you are:

  • A master’s or doctoral student at a public institution (from January 1, 2026).
  • A primary or secondary school student.
  • An exchange student under an exchange agreement who does not pay tuition to the Canadian institution.
  • In certain priority groups, such as some government scholarship recipients.

Step 1: Choose a Designated Learning Institution and Program

Only designated learning institutions (DLIs) can host international students. If you plan to stay in Canada after graduation, check whether your program leads to a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP). Non-degree programs must also meet the PGWP field of study requirement for many students.

Step 2: Get Your Letter of Acceptance and PAL/TAL

Your institution issues your letter of acceptance and usually helps you obtain the PAL or TAL. Institutions verify letters of acceptance with IRCC, so make sure your details are correct.

Step 3: Prove Your Funds

Outside Quebec, a single applicant must show $22,895 for living expenses, plus first-year tuition and travel costs. Additional amounts apply for each family member. Acceptable proof includes bank statements, a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC), education loans, proof of paid tuition or housing, and sponsor letters with supporting documents. Quebec sets its own financial requirements.

Step 4: Prepare a Strong Study Plan

Explain why you chose your program and institution, how it fits your education and career history, and your plans after graduation. Officers look for logical progression and genuine intent to study.

Step 5: Apply Online and Give Biometrics

Submit your application online with all documents and fees. Give biometrics when instructed, and complete a medical exam if required.

Working While You Study

Eligible students can work off campus up to 24 hours per week during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks. On-campus work rules differ. You must be enrolled full-time and your study permit must allow work.

Bringing Your Family

Since January 21, 2025, spouses of students qualify for open work permits only if the student is enrolled in a master’s program of at least 16 months, a doctoral program or an eligible professional degree program. Since March 4, 2026, IRCC refuses these applications if the student is in their final academic term. Children can usually study in Canada while you study.

Rules to Remember After You Arrive

  • Stay enrolled and make reasonable progress in your studies.
  • Tell IRCC if you change institutions, and check whether you need a new study permit.
  • Keep your passport and study permit valid.
  • Respect work hour limits.

Common Study Permit Mistakes

  • Applying without a required PAL or TAL.
  • Weak proof of funds or unexplained deposits.
  • A generic study plan that does not connect to your background.
  • Choosing a program that does not lead to the outcome you want, such as a PGWP.
  • Working beyond permitted hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a PAL for a Canada study permit in 2026?

Most new study permit applicants need a provincial or territorial attestation letter. Since January 1, 2026, master’s and doctoral students at public institutions are exempt, as are primary and secondary students and certain exchange students and priority groups. Check with your institution and IRCC before applying.

How much money do I need for a Canada study permit in 2026?

Outside Quebec, a single applicant must show $22,895 for living expenses, plus first-year tuition and travel costs. Additional amounts apply for each accompanying family member. Quebec sets its own requirements. Use acceptable proof, such as bank statements, a GIC, education loans or proof of paid tuition.

How many hours can international students work in Canada?

Eligible students can work off campus up to 24 hours per week during regular academic sessions and full-time during scheduled breaks, such as summer or winter holidays. You must be a full-time student and your study permit must allow work. Breaking these rules can affect your status.

Can my spouse work in Canada while I study?

Only in certain cases. Since January 21, 2025, spouses qualify for open work permits only if the student is in a master’s program of at least 16 months, a doctoral program or an eligible professional program. Since March 4, 2026, applications are refused if the student is in their final academic term.
