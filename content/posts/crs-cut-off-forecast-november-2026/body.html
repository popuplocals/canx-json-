Every Express Entry candidate is asking the same question right now: once IRCC recalculates the pool and draws resume after November 3, 2026, what will the cut-off be? Nobody outside Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) can answer that with certainty, and anyone who gives you an exact number is guessing. What we can do is look at the data, apply the new rules, and work out which way scores are likely to move, and by roughly how much.

This forecast uses the latest publicly reported pool data, every draw held in 2026, and the official rules for the new high-wage points. Our conclusion: Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) cut-offs in most draw types are likely to rise, and in Canadian Experience Class (CEC) draws the rise could be significant. But the effect will be very uneven, and some candidates will actually be better placed than before.

Quick Summary

As of October 12, 2026, about 229,456 candidates were in the Express Entry pool, with roughly 94,000 scoring above 450. CEC draws in 2026 have closed between 507 and 523. From November 3, candidates with 12 months of Canadian experience in high-wage occupations gain 20, 40, or 70 points, and candidates with qualifying job offers gain 50, 70, or 100. Because CEC candidates are the group most likely to gain, we expect CEC cut-offs to rise, possibly into the 530 to 560 range in the first draws after the pause. Category draws will shift too. These are scenario estimates, not predictions, and IRCC’s draw sizes will shape the outcome.

How the New Points Change the Pool

From November 3, 2026, IRCC will add a new labour market integration factor to the CRS. Candidates can earn points through one of two routes:

  • Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation: 70, 40, or 20 additional points for at least 12 months, on top of regular Canadian experience points
  • A qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation: 100, 70, or 50 points, with no Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) required

The two do not stack. IRCC awards whichever is higher. Three groups will feel this differently:

  1. Candidates already in Canada in high-wage occupations gain automatically during the recalculation. This group overlaps heavily with CEC candidates.
  2. Candidates with qualifying job offers gain the most, but only if they update their profiles before November 3.
  3. Candidates abroad without Canadian experience or a job offer gain nothing directly, and may slip down the rankings as others move up.

The Missing Piece: Nobody Knows the Occupation Mix of the Pool

IRCC does not publish how many candidates in the pool work in each NOC code. That is the single biggest unknown in any forecast. We know 186 occupations qualify, including large groups such as software developers, registered nurses, accountants, engineers, and managers, which are common among Express Entry candidates. But we cannot say precisely what share of candidates will gain points, or how many will update their profiles with job offers.

For that reason, we model three scenarios rather than one number. Each assumes a share of candidates in the 451 to 600 range gain points, and an average gain across the 20, 40, and 70 point levels.

What We Expect for Other Draw Types

Category-Based Draws: A Shuffle Within Each Category

Inside a category, the question is not just whether scores rise, but who rises. In the healthcare and social services category, for example, registered nurses (Level 2) and nurse practitioners (Level 1) can gain points, while occupations that are not on the high-wage list cannot. Cut-offs in such categories may rise, and candidates in listed occupations will move ahead of those in unlisted ones. The same pattern is likely in the trades category, where many but not all trades appear on the list.

Physicians: Higher Numbers, Same Order

The physicians category requires Canadian work experience, and physicians sit at Level 1. Most candidates in this category should gain 70 points, so the cut-off is likely to rise by a similar amount, but the relative ranking of candidates may change very little.

Senior Managers: Likely Large Jumps

Several senior management occupations sit at Level 1 or Level 2. Because this category requires Canadian work experience, many candidates may gain 40 to 70 points. We expect cut-offs here to rise noticeably from the 389 to 429 range seen in 2026.

French-Language Proficiency: Moderate Rise

French draws include candidates from every occupation. Those in high-wage occupations with Canadian experience will rise, which could lift cut-offs from the 382 to 435 range, but French ability will remain the main advantage in these draws.

Provincial Nominee Program: Little Change in Practice

PNP candidates already receive 600 points for a nomination, so cut-offs in the 700s and 800s may tick up, but nominees will continue to receive invitations as before.

The Legal and Procedural Picture

Several legal and procedural rules will shape what happens after November 3:

  • The legal basis: The CRS is set through Ministerial Instructions under section 10.3 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act, which lets the Minister change the ranking system without amending the regulations. That is why these changes can take effect quickly.
  • The FSW and FST public policy: IRCC describes the removal of LMIA requirements for Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades eligibility as a temporary public policy for candidates invited on or after November 3, 2026. As a temporary measure, its conditions and duration may change.
  • Locked-in occupation levels: If IRCC updates the high-wage list after you receive an Invitation to Apply (ITA), it will keep the level that applied on the date you were invited.
  • Job offers must stay valid: IRCC has said a revoked job offer no longer qualifies. If losing job offer points drops your score below the cut-off of the round you were invited in, your application could be at risk.
  • Genuineness and misrepresentation: IRCC officers will assess every job offer for genuineness. A finding of misrepresentation carries a five-year ban on applying to come to Canada.
  • The profile system lag: IRCC’s online profile still asks candidates to select a positive LMIA when adding a job offer. IRCC has published placeholder values to enter until the system is updated. Follow its current instructions exactly.

Timing: When Will Draws Resume?

IRCC has said the recalculation may take up to two weeks, and invitation rounds will pause during that time. If the recalculation runs the full two weeks from November 3, the first draws could resume around mid-November 2026. IRCC has not announced a date, and it could resume earlier or later.

We also expect IRCC to watch the first post-change draws closely. It may adjust draw sizes or the mix of draw types once it sees the new score distribution. Any such decisions are IRCC’s to make, and we would not plan around guesses.

What Candidates Should Do Right Now

  1. Calculate your new score. Check your NOC code against the high-wage list, and work out your possible gain.
  2. Update your job offer before November 3. If you have a genuine job offer, add it to your profile now. IRCC will not do this for you.
  3. Check every detail in your profile. NOC codes, dates, and hours must be accurate, because they now carry more points.
  4. Keep improving your core factors. Language scores and French ability remain powerful, especially if you do not gain from the new points.
  5. Review provincial options. If your score is unlikely to compete in federal draws, a provincial nomination remains a strong path.

Frequently Asked Questions

What will the CRS cut-off be after November 3, 2026?

No one knows for certain. Based on 2026 draw data and the new high-wage points, we expect cut-offs to rise in most draw types. For Canadian Experience Class draws, which closed between 507 and 523 in 2026, our scenarios point to roughly 525 to 560 in the first draws after the pause. Treat this as an estimate, not a prediction.

When will Express Entry draws resume after November 3?

IRCC has said the recalculation may take up to two weeks, and invitation rounds will pause during that period. If it takes the full two weeks, draws could resume around mid-November 2026. IRCC has not announced a specific date, so watch its official rounds of invitations page.

Will my CRS score go down after November 3?

Based on IRCC’s announcement, the changes add points rather than remove them, so your own score should not fall. Check your recalculated score once IRCC updates the pool. However, if other candidates gain points and you do not, your ranking in the pool may drop. That is why cut-offs may rise even though no one loses points.

What happens if my job offer is withdrawn after I get an ITA?

IRCC has said a revoked job offer no longer qualifies. You would lose the job offer points. If your recalculated score falls below the cut-off of the round in which you were invited, your application could be refused. Tell IRCC about the change and get professional advice quickly.

Is it still worth staying in the pool if I do not get the new points?

Yes. Category-based draws, French-language draws, and provincial nominations still offer real opportunities. You can also improve your score through language tests, French, education, or additional work experience. A licensed consultant can help you find the most realistic route for your profile.
