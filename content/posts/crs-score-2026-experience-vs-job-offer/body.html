From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer can earn up to 100 Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points again. That headline sent many Express Entry candidates looking for employers. But if you already have at least one year of Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation, a job offer in the same wage tier will add exactly zero points to your CRS score 2026.

This is not a loophole or a glitch. It follows directly from how Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) built the new Labour Market Integration Factor. Below, we walk through every scenario so you can do the math before you change your strategy.

Quick Summary

IRCC’s new Labour Market Integration Factor scores you on either your Canadian work experience or a qualifying high-wage job offer, never both. You receive whichever score is higher. If your Canadian experience is in the same wage tier as your job offer, your experience always scores higher. A job offer only adds points when it sits in a higher wage tier than your experience, when your experience falls outside the high-wage list, or when you have no Canadian experience at all.

What Is the Labour Market Integration Factor?

The Labour Market Integration Factor is a new section of the CRS that takes effect November 3, 2026. It covers 186 high-wage occupations, grouped into three tiers based on how their median wage compares with the Canada-wide median: 1.3 times, 1.5 times, and 2 times.

The factor has two routes:

Route 1: Canadian work experience. You keep your existing work-length points for skilled Canadian experience (TEER 0 to 3). If you have at least 12 months of qualifying experience in a high-wage occupation, IRCC adds a bonus on top:

  • 1.3x tier: +20 points
  • 1.5x tier: +40 points
  • 2x tier: +70 points

Route 2: A qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation:

  • 1.3x tier: 50 points
  • 1.5x tier: 70 points
  • 2x tier: 100 points

Job offer points do not change based on whether you have a spouse. A job offer no longer needs a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) for these points, but an IRCC officer must be satisfied the offer is genuine.

How IRCC Calculates Your Score: The Higher of the Two, Not Both

This is the detail most people miss. IRCC adds your high-wage bonus to your regular Canadian work-length points, then compares that total with your job offer points. You receive the higher number. The maximum is 150 points for single applicants and 140 for applicants with an accompanying spouse or common-law partner.

So the real question is not “how many points is a job offer worth?” It is “how many points is a job offer worth compared with what I already have?”

How to Improve CRS Score 2026 Under the New Rules

Before you invest time in a job search purely for CRS points, run through these checks:

  1. Confirm your occupation. Find your National Occupational Classification (NOC) code on IRCC’s high-wage list and note its tier.
  2. Count your qualifying months. You need at least 12 months of full-time (or equivalent) experience in high-wage occupations to unlock the bonus. IRCC can combine experience across more than one eligible job.
  3. Compare tiers. A job offer only adds points if it sits in a higher tier than your experience, or if your experience is outside the list.
  4. Look beyond the Labour Market Integration Factor. Language scores, education, and a provincial nomination still move your score, and a job offer may still matter for program eligibility even when it adds no CRS points.
  5. Update your profile on time. IRCC recalculates experience points automatically, but you must enter job offer details in your profile yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get CRS points for both Canadian experience and a job offer in 2026?

No. Under the Labour Market Integration Factor, IRCC calculates both scores and awards only the higher one. Your high-wage experience bonus is added to your work-length points first, and that total is compared with your job offer points. If your experience total is higher, the job offer adds nothing to your CRS score, even if it is worth 100 points on paper.

Do I need an LMIA for Express Entry job offer points from November 3, 2026?

No. IRCC removed the LMIA requirement for qualifying job offers under the new factor. However, the offer must be in an eligible high-wage occupation, and an officer must be satisfied that it is genuine, that the employer runs a legitimate business, and that you have the qualifications to do the work. IRCC has published instructions on how to enter the offer in your profile.

Will my existing Express Entry profile be updated automatically?

Partly. IRCC recalculates Canadian work experience points, including the new high-wage bonus, for candidates already in the pool. Job offer points work differently: you must update your profile with the job offer details yourself. Check that your NOC codes, work history, and dates are accurate, because these determine whether you qualify for the high-wage bonus.

What if I have less than 12 months of high-wage Canadian experience?

You receive your regular work-length points but no high-wage bonus. Once you reach 12 months of qualifying experience, the bonus applies. If you are close to that mark, staying in your role a few more months may be worth more than a job offer. Compare your expected totals carefully before making a career move based only on CRS points.
