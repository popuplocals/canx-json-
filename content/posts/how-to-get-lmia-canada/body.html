Canada’s Temporary Foreign Worker Program looks very different in 2026 than it did a few years ago. Employers now face tighter caps on low-wage workers, longer advertising requirements, a refusal-to-process rule in high-unemployment cities and shorter work permit durations for low-wage roles. Employers who still treat a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) as a quick formality are the ones most likely to receive refusals.

This guide explains how to get an LMIA in Canada in 2026, step by step: choosing the right stream, meeting recruitment requirements, preparing your application, understanding caps and timelines, and staying compliant after approval.

Quick Summary

An LMIA is a decision from Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) that allows an employer to hire a temporary foreign worker. The processing fee is $1,000 per position. Whether a job falls in the high-wage or low-wage stream depends on whether the wage meets the provincial or territorial threshold, set at the median hourly wage plus 20%. Low-wage positions face a 10% cap (20% in some sectors), 8 weeks of advertising and a refusal-to-process rule in census metropolitan areas with unemployment of 6% or more.

What Is an LMIA?

An LMIA shows that hiring a foreign worker will have a positive or neutral effect on Canada’s labour market. A positive LMIA lets the worker apply to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) for an employer-specific work permit. Some LMIAs can also support permanent residence applications through provincial programs.

Thresholds update periodically, so confirm the current figure for your province on the day you apply. If your wage sits close to the line, a small difference can move you into a stricter stream.

Step 2: Confirm the Prevailing Wage

You must pay at least the prevailing wage, which is the higher of the median wage for the occupation and region on Job Bank or the wage you pay Canadian employees in the same job and location with similar experience. Only guaranteed wages count. Overtime, tips, bonuses and commissions do not.

Keep records of every advertisement, applicant and reason a candidate was not hired. ESDC can ask for these records during processing and inspections, and you must keep them for at least six years.

Step 4: Check Caps and Location Rules for Low-Wage Positions

  • The low-wage cap generally limits temporary foreign workers in low-wage positions to 10% of your workforce at a work location, or 20% in sectors such as construction, food manufacturing, hospitals and nursing facilities.
  • Some positions are exempt from the cap, including primary agriculture and short-duration roles of 120 days or less.
  • ESDC will not process low-wage applications for positions in census metropolitan areas with an unemployment rate of 6% or higher, subject to listed exceptions. The list of affected areas updates regularly.

Step 5: Prepare Employer Obligations

For low-wage positions, you must pay round-trip transportation, ensure suitable and affordable housing is available, and provide private health insurance until the worker is covered by the provincial plan. For high-wage positions, you must submit a transition plan describing how you will recruit, retain and train Canadians to reduce reliance on temporary workers.

Step 6: Submit Your Application and Pay the Fee

Apply through the LMIA Online Portal with your business documents, job details, recruitment proof and wage information. Pay the $1,000 processing fee per position, unless an exemption applies, such as for certain caregiver and primary agriculture positions.

Step 7: After a Positive LMIA

Send the worker a copy of the positive LMIA and the job offer. The worker applies to IRCC for a work permit. An LMIA is generally valid for 6 months for the worker to apply. Low-wage work permits are generally limited to one year.

Staying Compliant After Approval

ESDC and IRCC can inspect employers. You must provide the wages, duties and working conditions you promised, maintain a workplace free of abuse, and keep records. Non-compliance can lead to fines, a ban from the program and public listing.

Realistic Timelines

From the start of recruitment to the worker’s arrival, many employers should plan for several months, and often longer for low-wage roles because of the 8-week advertising requirement and processing times.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an LMIA cost in Canada?

The government processing fee is $1,000 per position requested. Some positions are exempt, such as certain primary agriculture and caregiver roles. Employers also face recruitment costs and, for low-wage positions, obligations such as transportation, housing support and private health insurance. Employers cannot recover the LMIA fee from workers.

How long does it take to get an LMIA in 2026?

Timelines vary by stream and location. Before applying, high-wage employers must advertise for at least 4 consecutive weeks and low-wage employers for at least 8. ESDC processing then adds more time. Many employers should plan for several months from the start of recruitment to the worker’s arrival.

What is the difference between high-wage and low-wage LMIAs?

The stream depends on whether the wage meets the provincial or territorial threshold, set at the median hourly wage plus 20%. High-wage applications require a transition plan. Low-wage applications face a workforce cap, longer advertising, refusal to process in high-unemployment cities, and employer obligations for transportation, housing and health insurance.

Can low-wage LMIAs be processed in every city?

No. ESDC does not process low-wage LMIA applications for positions in census metropolitan areas with an unemployment rate of 6% or higher, subject to certain exceptions. The list of affected areas changes regularly, so check ESDC’s current list before you begin recruitment.
