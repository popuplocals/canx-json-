Canada’s push to build more homes and infrastructure has put construction workers at the centre of its immigration priorities. In 2026, IRCC refocused the Express Entry trades category on construction and industrial trades, held a record 3,500-invitation trades draw on October 1, and the federal government committed to recruiting and training 80,000 to 100,000 new Red Seal trades workers by 2030. Provinces from British Columbia to Nova Scotia list construction as a priority.

This guide explains construction worker Canada immigration in 2026: federal and provincial pathways, certification, employer-driven routes and how to build a strong application.

Quick Summary

Construction trades such as carpenters, electricians, plumbers, bricklayers, roofers, concrete finishers, welders and HVAC mechanics are part of the 2026 Express Entry trades category, which held draws at CRS 477 and 476. The Federal Skilled Trades Program, provincial construction priorities, the Atlantic Immigration Program and community pilots offer more routes. Provincial certification is often required to work in regulated trades.

Construction Trades in the 2026 Express Entry Category

The 2026 trades category includes about 25 construction, installation and industrial occupations, such as carpenters, bricklayers, concrete finishers, roofers, painters and decorators, floor covering installers, electricians, plumbers, gas fitters, sheet metal workers, welders, HVAC mechanics and construction millwrights. Candidates need at least 12 months of work experience in an eligible trade within the past three years.

The Federal Skilled Trades Program

The Federal Skilled Trades Program requires two years of skilled trade experience within five years, CLB 5 in speaking and listening and CLB 4 in reading and writing, and either a valid job offer or a Canadian certificate of qualification.

Employer-Driven Routes

  • LMIA-based work permits: low-wage construction positions face a 20% cap rather than 10%, which gives construction employers more room to hire.
  • Atlantic Immigration Program: designated employers in Atlantic Canada can hire construction workers without an LMIA.
  • Rural Community Immigration Pilot: designated employers in 14 participating communities can support candidates.

Labourers and Helpers

Construction labourers and helpers are usually TEER 5 occupations and do not qualify for Express Entry or the trades category. Their main options are employer-driven: provincial streams that accept lower-skilled roles, such as Ontario’s TEER 4 and 5 pathway, community pilots and the Atlantic Immigration Program for TEER 4 roles.

Certification and Safety

Many construction trades require provincial certification, and most job sites require safety training. Certification also adds Express Entry skill transferability points when combined with language scores. IRCC has proposed future points for Red Seal certification.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can construction workers immigrate to Canada in 2026?

Yes. Many construction trades are part of the 2026 Express Entry trades category, which held draws at CRS 477 and 476. Construction workers can also use the Federal Skilled Trades Program, provincial construction priorities, the Atlantic Immigration Program, the Rural Community Immigration Pilot and LMIA-based work permits.

Which construction jobs are in the Express Entry trades category?

The 2026 trades category includes about 25 occupations, such as carpenters, bricklayers, concrete finishers, roofers, painters and decorators, electricians, plumbers, gas fitters, sheet metal workers, welders, HVAC mechanics and construction millwrights. You need 12 months of experience in an eligible trade within the past three years.

Can construction labourers get PR in Canada?

Construction labourers are usually TEER 5 and do not qualify for Express Entry. They may qualify through employer-driven programs that accept lower-skilled roles, such as Ontario’s TEER 4 and 5 pathway, certain provincial streams, community pilots, or the Atlantic Immigration Program for TEER 4 jobs.

Do construction workers need certification to work in Canada?

Many construction trades require provincial certification, especially regulated trades such as electrical and plumbing work. Requirements vary by province and trade. Certification also adds skill transferability points in Express Entry when combined with strong language scores, and IRCC has proposed future points for Red Seal certification.
