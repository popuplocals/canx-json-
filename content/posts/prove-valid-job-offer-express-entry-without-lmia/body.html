As of November 3, 2026, a job offer used in Express Entry no longer needs a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) introduced a temporary public policy that removes the LMIA requirement for Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) and Federal Skilled Trades (FST) applicants. On the same date, IRCC brought back Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points for job offers in high-wage occupations.

The LMIA is gone, but the review is not. When you apply for permanent residence, an officer must be satisfied that your job offer meets five conditions. This guide explains each condition, the evidence that supports it, and the issues that most often cause problems.

In Brief

The new public policy applies to FSW and FST applicants who received an Invitation to Apply (ITA) on or after November 3, 2026. Your job offer no longer needs an LMIA or a link to an employer named on your work permit. Instead, you must show that the employer operates a real business, needs the position, can meet the terms of the offer and has a clean compliance record, and that the job matches your experience or education. CRS points for a job offer are a separate question and apply only to high-wage occupations.

What Changed on November 3, 2026

Before this change, most job offers went through two reviews: an LMIA from Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC), and a review by IRCC. The new policy, issued under section 25.2 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA), replaces both with a single review by IRCC.

The policy applies if all of the following are true:

  • You applied for permanent residence under section 70 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR).
  • You applied in the Federal Skilled Worker Class (IRPR section 75) or the Federal Skilled Trades Class (IRPR section 87.2).
  • IRCC issued your ITA on or after November 3, 2026.

The policy has no fixed end date. It remains in effect until the Minister revokes it, which can happen at any time.

Program Eligibility and CRS Points Are Assessed Separately

A job offer can help you in two ways, and IRCC assesses each one on its own.

Program eligibility. For FSW applicants, a valid job offer earns 10 points for arranged employment on the 67-point selection grid, plus 5 adaptability points. For FST applicants, a valid job offer is one of two ways to qualify; the other is a certificate of qualification from a Canadian province or territory. The offer can be in any eligible occupation in TEER (Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities) category 0, 1, 2 or 3.

CRS points. The offer earns points only if the occupation appears on IRCC’s high-wage list:

Under the new Labour Market Integration factor, IRCC awards either your Canadian work experience points or your job offer points, whichever is higher. A job offer may therefore help you qualify for a program without increasing your CRS score. You can check your occupation on the IRCC high-wage occupations list.

The Five Conditions You Must Prove

The public policy sets out five conditions. The officer reviewing your permanent residence application must be satisfied that your job offer meets all of them. IRCC has not published a fixed document list for each condition, so the evidence below reflects what officers typically consider when they assess whether a job offer is genuine.

1. The Employer Is Actively Engaged in the Business

What it means: The employer must operate a real business connected to the job offer. A registered company with no activity does not meet this condition.

Evidence that helps:

  • Business registration or articles of incorporation, and a business licence where required
  • Canada Revenue Agency business number
  • Recent financial statements or corporate tax returns
  • Payroll records, such as a T4 Summary or payroll remittances
  • Proof of business premises, such as a lease
  • Evidence of current operations, such as client contracts or invoices

Example: A Calgary engineering firm with 60 employees, a leased office and filed tax returns meets this condition. A company incorporated four months ago with no employees, revenue or premises will find it difficult to do so.

2. The Offer Is Consistent With the Employer's Reasonable Employment Needs

What it means: The position must make sense for the employer’s business and size. Officers consider whether the employer needs the role or whether it exists mainly to support an immigration application.

Evidence that helps:

  • An organizational chart showing where the position fits
  • A letter from the employer explaining why the business needs the role
  • A detailed job description
  • Evidence of business activity that supports the role, such as new contracts or expansion
  • If you already hold the position, proof that you currently perform it

Example: A manufacturer with three production lines hiring an industrial electrician has a clear need. A small café offering a “Director of International Business Development” role does not.

3. The Offer Contains Terms the Employer Can Reasonably Fulfill

What it means: The employer must be able to pay the stated wage and provide the stated hours for the stated period. Officers compare the offer with the employer’s financial position.

Evidence that helps:

  • Financial statements or tax returns showing the business can support the salary
  • Payroll records showing the employer pays similar wages to current staff
  • A wage in line with the prevailing wage for the occupation and region, which you can check on Job Bank
  • A complete offer letter (see the next section)

Example: A business with $250,000 in annual revenue that offers two salaries of $120,000 each will need to explain how it can meet those terms.

4. The Job Matches Your Past Work Experience or Education

What it means: The job must be in an occupation where you have work experience or education. This works alongside an existing rule in IRPR subsection 82(2): you must be able to perform the job and be likely to accept it and carry it out.

Evidence that helps:

  • Reference letters from past employers showing your job title, duties, hours, wages and dates of employment
  • Degrees or diplomas, with an Educational Credential Assessment where required
  • Professional licence or registration, if the occupation is regulated in the province
  • Recent pay stubs, if you already work in the position

Example: A mechanical engineering graduate with five years of design experience, offered a mechanical engineer role, meets this condition. A retail supervisor with a business degree, offered a data scientist role, does not, because they have no experience or education in that field.

5. The Employer Has No Previous Non-Compliance

What it means: The employer must have no history of non-compliance with federal or provincial laws that regulate employment or recruitment in the province where you will work. This covers laws on hiring foreign nationals as well as general employment standards.

Evidence that helps:

  • A signed employer declaration confirming its compliance history
  • Confirmation that the employer does not appear on IRCC’s published list of non-compliant employers
  • Provincial employer registration, if the province requires it to hire foreign nationals

Example: An employer that received a provincial employment standards order for unpaid wages may not meet this condition, even if it has never hired a foreign worker. Ask the employer about its compliance history before you rely on the offer.

What Your Job Offer Letter Should Include

The offer letter is the central document. For FSW applicants, it must meet the definition of arranged employment in IRPR subsection 82(1). The letter should include:

  • The employer’s legal name, address and contact details, signed by an authorized person
  • The job title and NOC (National Occupational Classification) code
  • The main duties, consistent with the NOC description
  • The wage and benefits
  • Hours of work: full-time, at least 30 hours per week
  • Confirmation that the job is continuous and full-time, and lasts at least one year after IRCC issues the permanent resident visa
  • The work location
  • The start date, or confirmation of continued employment if you already work there

For FSW, the offer must come from a single employer. For FST, the offer can come from up to two employers, for continuous full-time work in your skilled trade totalling at least one year.

What the Policy Does Not Change

The policy removes the LMIA, the link to an employer named on your work permit and, for some work permit holders, the requirement to have worked one year for the employer. The specific provisions are listed in the policy: for FSW, subparagraphs 82(2)(a)(i) to (iii), 82(2)(b)(i) to (iii), 82(2)(c)(ii) and 82(2)(d)(iii), and paragraph 83(1)(e); for FST, the related clauses of paragraph 87.2(3)(d).

Other requirements remain:

  • The employer cannot be an embassy, high commission or consulate in Canada, or an employer excluded under the Regulations.
  • The job must be in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation for FSW, or in a skilled trade for FST.
  • You must still meet the language, education, work experience and admissibility requirements of your program.

Adding the Job Offer to Your Express Entry Profile

IRCC’s online profile has not yet been updated for the new rules. IRCC has asked candidates who hold a qualifying job offer without an LMIA to answer “Yes” to the LMIA question under “Work history” and enter these placeholder values: LMIA number 00000, issuance date 2026-01-01 and expiry date 2029-01-01. Enter these values only if your offer meets the conditions above, since an officer will review it when you apply. See the IRCC Express Entry changes page for current instructions.

Common Reasons a Job Offer May Not Be Accepted

  • The employer cannot show active operations or the ability to pay the wage
  • The position does not fit the size or nature of the business
  • The job is unrelated to the applicant’s experience or education
  • The offer letter leaves out key terms, such as hours, wage or duration
  • The employer has a history of employment or recruitment violations

A job offer that is not genuine can also lead to a finding of misrepresentation under section 40 of IRPA, which carries a five-year period of inadmissibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an LMIA for an Express Entry job offer?

Not if IRCC issued your ITA on or after November 3, 2026. A qualifying job offer can now support FSW or FST eligibility and earn CRS points without an LMIA. An officer must still be satisfied that the employer operates a real business, needs the role, can meet the terms and has no previous non-compliance, and that your background fits the job.

Does every job offer give CRS points?

No. Only job offers in occupations on IRCC’s high-wage list earn CRS points: 100, 70 or 50, depending on the level. An offer in another TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation can still help you qualify for the FSW or FST program, but it will not add CRS points. Check your NOC code on the official list before you update your profile.

Can I use a job offer from my current employer if I hold an open work permit?

Yes, if IRCC issued your ITA on or after November 3, 2026. Holders of open work permits, such as Post-Graduation Work Permits or spousal open work permits, previously needed an LMIA because no employer was named on their permit. The new policy removes that requirement. The offer must still meet the five conditions and all other program rules.

Does the policy apply if I received my ITA before November 3, 2026?

No. The policy applies only to FSW and FST applications that follow an ITA issued on or after November 3, 2026. If IRCC invited you before that date, the previous job offer rules apply to your application. A licensed consultant can help you confirm which rules govern your file.
