Landing in Canada for the first time? Congratulations! this marks the beginning of an exciting new chapter. Whether you’re arriving as a permanent resident, international student, temporary foreign worker, or family member, the first few days after landing are crucial for setting up your life in Canada.

This comprehensive checklist covers the key steps every newcomer should follow, from clearing immigration at the airport to getting a Social Insurance Number (SIN), health coverage, housing, and more.

Who Is This Guide For?

This checklist is designed to help:

    • New immigrants landing in Canada under Express Entry, PNP, or family sponsorship
    • International students starting their education
    • Temporary foreign workers arriving with valid work permits
    • Spouses and dependents accompanying a primary applicant
    • Visitors or parents arriving on Super Visas with long-term stay intentions

1. At the Airport: Immigration and Document Processing

Upon arrival at a Canadian airport, you’ll go through immigration and customs procedures. A Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) officer will verify your documents and may ask basic questions about your purpose of travel and where you plan to stay.

Documents to Carry:

    • Passport
    • Study or work permit approval letter (if applicable)
    • Confirmation of Permanent Residence (COPR)
    • Proof of funds
    • Letter of introduction (if issued by IRCC)
    • Canadian address

After clearance, you’ll receive:

    • A printed study or work permit (if applicable)
    • An entry stamp in your passport
    • Instructions for follow-up if further processing is required

Tip: Review all documents before leaving the airport to ensure your personal information and permit conditions are correct.

2. Apply for Your Social Insurance Number (SIN)

A SIN is required to legally work in Canada and access government programs.

How to Apply:

    • At the airport (available at some major airports like Toronto Pearson)
    • Online via the Service Canada website
    • In person at a local Service Canada Centre

What You Need:

    • Passport
    • Immigration document (e.g., study permit, work permit, or COPR)

3. Apply for Provincial Health Insurance

Each province manages its own public healthcare system. Apply for your health card shortly after arriving to ensure you’re covered.

Example Requirements by Province:

    • Ontario (OHIP): No waiting period for most permanent residents and work permit holders
    • British Columbia (MSP): Coverage begins after a two- to three-month waiting period
    • Alberta (AHCIP): Immediate coverage upon arrival for eligible individuals

If your province has a waiting period, consider buying private health insurance in the meantime.

4. Open a Canadian Bank Account

Opening a local bank account helps with managing finances, receiving payments, and building credit.

What You’ll Need:

    • Passport
    • Immigration document (study permit, work permit, or COPR)
    • Proof of address (e.g., hotel receipt or rental agreement)

Ask about accounts specifically designed for newcomers, which may include low or no monthly fees and options for establishing credit history.

5. Set Up a Mobile Phone Plan and Internet

Staying connected is important for job searches, navigation, communication, and accessing services.

You can choose from prepaid or monthly mobile plans, depending on your needs. Most plans require:

    • Identification
    • A compatible phone (either unlocked or purchased locally)

Many service providers also offer internet packages for home use, often bundled with mobile services.

6. Secure Short-Term or Long-Term Housing

If you haven’t arranged permanent accommodation before your arrival, it’s best to begin with short-term housing. These options offer flexibility and give you time to understand your surroundings:

    • Home-sharing rentals
    • Hotels or motels
    • Temporary student residences (if applicable)

For Long-Term Housing

Once you’re ready to settle, you can begin your long-term rental search. This typically involves renting an apartment, basement unit, or house. When exploring options, consider:

    • Proximity to work or school
    • Access to public transportation
    • Safety and affordability of the neighborhood
    • Lease terms (monthly, yearly, utilities included, etc.)

Check listings carefully and avoid sending deposits before viewing properties in person or verifying the legitimacy of the landlord.

7. Purchase Essential Supplies

You may need to buy basic household items such as:

    • Kitchenware and bedding
    • Toiletries and cleaning supplies
    • Seasonal clothing (especially warm gear for winter if arriving during cold months)

You can find these items at local grocery stores, department stores, or shopping centres throughout your city or town.

8. Access Free Newcomer Settlement Services

The Canadian government funds free settlement services to help immigrants and refugees adjust to life in Canada.

These services typically include:

    • Language classes in English or French
    • Job search assistance and resume workshops
    • Orientation sessions on housing, healthcare, and legal rights
    • Support with enrolling children in school

You can search for settlement agencies in your area using government tools or by contacting local community centers.

9. Enroll Children in School

If you are arriving with school-aged children, you’ll need to enroll them in the local public school system. Education is free up to grade 12.

Visit the school board office with:

    • Child’s passport
    • Immigration documents
    • Proof of address
    • Previous school records (if available)

Many schools offer English as a Second Language (ESL) support for new immigrants.

10. Begin Working, Studying, or Preparing for the Next Step

Depending on your situation:

    • Students should complete school registration, attend orientation, and obtain student ID cards
    • Workers should check in with their employer and confirm their schedule and onboarding procedures
    • Job seekers can begin preparing a resume in Canadian format and searching for suitable roles

Also, consider exploring your long-term immigration goals, such as applying for permanent residence through Express Entry or a Provincial Nominee Program once eligible.

Final Thoughts: Build a Strong Foundation

The first few weeks in Canada are a time of transition. Taking care of these critical tasks early on will help you feel more settled, confident, and prepared to thrive in your new environment.

Stay organized, ask questions, and make use of available support services. Most importantly, take the time to understand your rights, responsibilities, and options as a newcomer to Canada.
