India has been the top source country for Canadian permanent residents for multiple consecutive years, and Indian nationals represent one of the largest groups of temporary foreign workers in Canada at virtually every skill level. In 2026, this relationship continues to deepen. The Canada-India immigration corridor carries more volume, more variety, and more long-term institutional investment than any other bilateral immigration relationship in Canada’s history.

Indian workers navigate the full spectrum of Canada’s immigration system: as international students on study permits, post-graduates on PGWPs, temporary workers on LMIA and LMIA-exempt permits, and Express Entry candidates with some of the highest human capital scores in the pool. For Indian workers who enter through the LMIA pathway specifically, understanding the current policy environment — including the March 2025 elimination of arranged employment CRS bonus points — is essential for building a realistic and effective PR strategy.

The March 2025 Policy Change: What It Means for Indian LMIA Holders

Before March 2025, Indian workers on LMIA-based work permits in eligible NOC categories could claim 50 or 200 additional CRS points as arranged employment in their Express Entry profile. Many Indian Express Entry candidates built their PR strategies around this bonus. On March 25, 2025, IRCC eliminated these points entirely.

The elimination had a particularly noticeable impact on Indian candidates in the Express Entry pool because Indian nationals are among the most numerous users of the arranged employment pathway. Candidates who had been competitive at their threshold score using bonus points found themselves below that threshold after the change. IRCC acknowledged the disruption but maintained that system integrity required the elimination.

The correction for Indian LMIA holders in 2026 is to rebuild PR strategies around three pillars that remain fully intact: the Canadian work experience points earned through the LMIA work permit, the CEC eligibility created after one year of skilled Canadian work, and the PNP employer-driven nomination, which delivers 600 CRS points — more than the eliminated bonus ever provided.

Why Indian Workers Are Choosing Canada in 2026

Despite the policy change, Canada remains the most attractive immigration destination for Indian skilled workers. The Express Entry system’s human capital model rewards education, language proficiency, and work experience — attributes that Indian professionals possess in abundance from some of the world’s most competitive educational and professional environments.

The strong Indian diaspora communities in Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary, Brampton, and other cities create social and professional networks that ease transition. Canada’s multicultural framework, political stability, and pathway to citizenship within three to four years of obtaining permanent residence are significant draws. And for Indian technology professionals in particular, Canada’s geographic proximity to the United States creates professional opportunities and network effects that are not available in other immigration destinations.

Which Sectors Have the Most LMIA Demand for Indian Workers

Information Technology and Software Development

The technology sector remains the single largest employer of Indian workers in Canada through both LMIA and LMIA-exempt pathways. Indian software engineers, data scientists, cloud architects, and cybersecurity specialists are in sustained demand. For most technology positions, employers and workers should evaluate the Global Talent Stream before the standard LMIA — GTS Category B offers two-week processing for most software development and data science NOC codes, dramatically faster than standard LMIA timelines.

Healthcare

Canada’s documented healthcare staffing crisis creates strong demand for Indian registered nurses, pharmacists, physiotherapists, and allied health professionals. LMIA applications for healthcare positions from Indian applicants have grown significantly in recent years. The defining complexity remains provincial licensing: a positive LMIA and work permit do not authorize practice in a regulated health profession until the provincial regulatory body issues a licence. For Indian healthcare workers, the credential assessment process can take six months to over a year and must be initiated simultaneously with the LMIA application, not after.

Engineering

Canadian engineering firms in infrastructure, energy, construction, and mining actively recruit Indian engineers for civil, mechanical, electrical, and structural roles. Engineering is a regulated profession in Canada, and the provincial engineering regulator must be engaged for credential recognition before the worker can use the P.Eng. title or practice in contexts legally requiring licensure.

Finance and Accounting

Indian Chartered Accountants benefit from the Mutual Recognition Agreement between the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) and CPA Canada, which provides an abbreviated path to the CPA Canada designation. This recognition advantage makes Canadian accounting and finance sector employment particularly accessible for Indian CA holders.

Skilled Trades and Construction

Indian workers with credentials in electrical work, plumbing, welding, HVAC, and related trades are increasingly recruited by Canadian construction employers through the LMIA process. Provincial trades licensing and the Red Seal Program’s national standard apply to Indian tradespeople as to all foreign-trained trades workers.

The LMIA Process for Indian Applicants: Specific Considerations

Temporary Resident Visa Requirement

Indian nationals require a Temporary Resident Visa to enter Canada. The work permit application and the TRV are processed together when an Indian worker applies online from India, but the combined processing timeline includes both assessments. Employers planning specific start dates must account for TRV processing time in addition to LMIA processing and work permit processing. TRV processing for Indian nationals has been subject to significant backlogs at certain periods; building buffer time into the overall timeline is essential.

Biometric Requirements

Indian nationals must provide biometrics at a designated Visa Application Centre in India. If the applicant has not previously provided biometrics to IRCC, or if their previous biometrics have expired (valid for ten years), they must submit them after receiving IRCC instructions. This step adds additional time to the application process and must be planned for.

Educational Credential Assessment

For Express Entry profiles, an Educational Credential Assessment from a designated organization such as WES (World Education Services) is required. Indian workers who have not yet obtained an ECA should initiate this process early. ECAs are valid for five years and must remain valid throughout the PR application process. Candidates who obtained an ECA several years ago should confirm it remains valid.

English Language Requirements

English language proficiency is assessed through IELTS General Training or CELPIP for most Express Entry streams. Indian applicants who completed education in English typically perform well, but the test scores must be from approved tests taken within the required validity period (generally two years). For healthcare workers, provincial regulatory bodies often require higher language thresholds than the Express Entry minimum, so both sets of requirements must be confirmed.

Common Mistakes Indian LMIA Applicants Make in 2026

The most widespread error in 2026 is still expecting arranged employment CRS points that no longer exist. Workers who received advice before March 2025 about using LMIA job offers for Express Entry CRS bonuses are operating on outdated guidance. Their Express Entry strategy must be recalibrated around Canadian experience accumulation and PNP pathways.

The second most common mistake is allowing work permits to expire without timely renewal. Indian workers whose work permits expire lose their right to continue accumulating Canadian work experience, lose maintained status for work purposes, and create immigration status complications that affect every subsequent application. Renewing at least four months before expiry is the standard practice.

The third mistake is over-reliance on the employer for immigration planning. Employers who are unaware of the post-March 2025 policy environment may still assure workers that ‘the LMIA will help their immigration score’ without understanding that this is no longer accurate. Workers should engage their own RCIC from the time they receive their first Canadian work permit rather than relying on employer guidance for immigration strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Indian workers get an LMIA work permit in Canada?

Yes. Indian nationals can obtain LMIA-based work permits under the TFWP. The employer applies to ESDC for the LMIA, and once approved, the Indian worker applies for a combined work permit and Temporary Resident Visa. Biometric submission at a VAC in India is required. The total timeline from LMIA application to work permit issuance typically ranges from five to nine months for standard streams, though the Global Talent Stream can process eligible technology positions significantly faster.

How does an LMIA help Indian Express Entry applicants in 2026?

Since March 25, 2025, an LMIA no longer adds CRS bonus points to an Express Entry profile. The LMIA’s contribution to Indian Express Entry candidates in 2026 is through the Canadian work experience it enables (up to 80 CRS points over five or more years), the CEC eligibility created after one year of skilled Canadian work, and the PNP employer-driven nomination pathway, which delivers 600 CRS points upon provincial nomination. Workers should build their PR strategy around these three active pathways rather than the eliminated bonus points system.

What is the best PR pathway for Indian workers on LMIA permits in 2026?

The most reliable and powerful PR pathway for Indian workers on LMIA permits in 2026 is the PNP employer-driven nomination. After accumulating the required work experience in the province (typically three to twelve months), a worker whose employer is committed to permanent employment can apply through a provincial employer-driven PNP stream. A provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points, which guarantees an ITA in the next available Express Entry draw regardless of the candidate’s base score. In parallel, Canadian work experience accumulation and CEC eligibility strengthen the profile independently.

Do Indian workers need a TRV to enter Canada on an LMIA work permit?

Yes. Indian nationals require a Temporary Resident Visa to enter Canada regardless of their work permit status. When an Indian worker applies for a work permit from India, the TRV is assessed as part of the combined application process. The total processing timeline includes both the work permit and TRV assessments, and Indian applicants should budget for combined processing when planning start dates with their Canadian employer.

Does an ECA from WES expire for Express Entry purposes?

Educational Credential Assessments from WES and other designated organizations are valid for five years from the date of issue. Indian workers who obtained an ECA more than five years ago need a new assessment before their Express Entry profile can be created or updated to reflect the credential. Workers should confirm ECA validity at the time they create their profile and again before submitting a PR application, as an expired ECA at the PR application stage requires a fresh assessment that takes additional time.
