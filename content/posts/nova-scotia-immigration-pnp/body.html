If you searched for “Nova Scotia Demand: Express Entry” in 2026, you may have found conflicting information. That is because Nova Scotia rebuilt its nominee program this year. On February 18, 2026, the Nova Scotia Nominee Program (NSNP) consolidated its pathways into four streams, retiring several familiar names in the process.

This guide explains the new structure, how the current Nova Scotia: Express Entry stream works, who each stream serves, and how to position yourself for a Nova Scotia nomination in 2026.

Quick Summary

Since February 18, 2026, Nova Scotia has four nominee streams: Skilled Worker, Nova Scotia: Express Entry, Entrepreneur and Nova Scotia Graduate. The Nova Scotia: Express Entry stream combines the former Nova Scotia Experience: Express Entry and Labour Market Priorities streams and targets Express Entry candidates with Nova Scotia work experience. Nova Scotia said eligibility criteria stayed the same, and applications submitted before the change were not affected.

What Changed in February 2026?

Nova Scotia consolidated its program to make it simpler for applicants and employers. The province said eligibility criteria remain the same; the change reorganized existing pathways into four streams. Its application portal closed briefly from February 13 to 18, 2026, while the province modernized its systems.

The Four NSNP Streams in 2026

1. Skilled Worker

For candidates with a job offer from a Nova Scotia employer, including sub-criteria for construction workers and physicians. Priority sectors include health care, construction and manufacturing. This stream is not linked to Express Entry.

2. Nova Scotia: Express Entry

For candidates in the federal Express Entry pool with Nova Scotia work experience. A key requirement is one year of Nova Scotia work experience in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation. Nova Scotia can also select candidates whose profiles match its labour market priorities. A nomination adds 600 points to your Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score.

3. Entrepreneur

For experienced business owners and international graduates who want to start or buy and operate a business in Nova Scotia. Candidates must meet net worth, investment and business requirements.

4. Nova Scotia Graduate

For recent graduates of Nova Scotia institutions, including those in health care fields, with a full-time job offer related to their field of study.

How the Nova Scotia: Express Entry Stream Works

Step 1: Create a federal Express Entry profile

You must qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class or Federal Skilled Trades program and have an active profile.

Step 2: Build Nova Scotia work experience

The stream values candidates already contributing in the province, particularly those with one year of skilled Nova Scotia experience.

Step 3: Express interest and watch for selection

Depending on the pathway, Nova Scotia may invite candidates who express interest or select candidates directly from the Express Entry pool based on its labour market priorities.

Step 4: Apply to Nova Scotia

If selected, submit a complete application to the NSNP within the deadline.

Step 5: Accept the nomination and apply to IRCC

Accept your nomination in your Express Entry profile to receive 600 CRS points, then submit your permanent residence application after an invitation to apply.

Who Has the Best Chance in Nova Scotia?

Nova Scotia favours people who already have ties to the province and work in priority sectors. Your chances are strongest if you:

  • Work in Nova Scotia in a skilled occupation.
  • Have a job offer in health care, construction or manufacturing.
  • Graduated from a Nova Scotia post-secondary institution.
  • Have strong language scores.
  • Show a genuine intention to live in Nova Scotia long term.

Nova Scotia PNP vs the Atlantic Immigration Program

Nova Scotia also participates in the federal Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP), an employer-driven pathway for designated employers in the Atlantic provinces. If you have a job offer from a designated Nova Scotia employer, compare the AIP with the NSNP streams to see which fits your occupation, experience and timeline best.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the Nova Scotia PNP streams in 2026?

Since February 18, 2026, Nova Scotia has four streams: Skilled Worker, Nova Scotia: Express Entry, Entrepreneur and Nova Scotia Graduate. They consolidate several former streams and pilots, including Nova Scotia Experience: Express Entry, Labour Market Priorities, the Critical Construction Worker Pilot and International Graduates in Demand.

Is Nova Scotia Demand: Express Entry still open?

The former Nova Scotia Demand: Express Entry stream is not one of the four streams Nova Scotia operates in 2026. Express Entry candidates should look at the current Nova Scotia: Express Entry stream, which combines the former Nova Scotia Experience: Express Entry and Labour Market Priorities streams.

Do I need Nova Scotia work experience for the Nova Scotia: Express Entry stream?

A key requirement of the stream is one year of Nova Scotia work experience in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation. Nova Scotia can also select candidates whose profiles match its labour market priorities. Check the stream’s current criteria on Nova Scotia’s official website before applying.

Did the 2026 changes affect applications already submitted?

Nova Scotia said existing applications were not affected and that eligibility criteria remained the same. The changes applied to new submissions after February 18, 2026. If you had an application in progress, follow the instructions in your file and contact the NSNP if you are unsure.
