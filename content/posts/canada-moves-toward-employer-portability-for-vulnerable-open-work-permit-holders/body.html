Canada is gradually expanding employment flexibility for foreign workers who hold open work permits under vulnerable worker protections. Recent policy changes are making it easier for these individuals to change employers when they face risky or abusive situations. This blog explains what these changes mean, who benefits, and how affected workers can apply.

What Is an Open Work Permit for Vulnerable Workers?

Open work permits for vulnerable workers are a special measure by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) that allow individuals on employer-specific work permits to switch employers without having to wait for a new permit. This protection applies when the worker is experiencing abuse or at risk of abuse in their current workplace.

This permit ensures that foreign workers facing unsafe conditions can leave their jobs while remaining compliant with immigration rules and continuing their path to legal employment.

Expansion of Employer Mobility Under New Public Policy

In 2025, IRCC introduced measures that make it easier for temporary foreign workers to begin working for a new employer while waiting for a new LMIA-based work permit application to be processed.

This change is particularly significant for workers who hold employer-specific work permits. It allows them to avoid prolonged employment gaps that can lead to financial hardship and increased vulnerability.

How It Affects Vulnerable Workers

For vulnerable workers, the combination of open work permits and employer mobility policy offers a dual safety net:

    • An open work permit allows immediate departure from unsafe or abusive work situations.
    • The employer mobility policy allows timely transition to a new employer while awaiting approval for a new permit.

Together, these measures help vulnerable workers maintain income, avoid exploitation, and protect their rights in Canada.

Steps to Apply for an Open Work Permit as a Vulnerable Worker

If you are facing abuse and hold a valid employer-specific work permit, follow these steps:

  1. Gather evidence of abuse such as incident reports, written statements, witness affidavits, or medical records.
  2. Submit an online application for the open work permit for vulnerable workers and clearly explain your situation in a letter of explanation.
  3. Upload all supporting documents in one file under the “Client Information” section of your application.
  4. Apply from inside Canada as applications cannot be submitted at the port of entry.

IRCC handles such applications confidentially, and your employer will not be informed about your request.

Once approved, you will receive an open work permit, usually valid for 12 months, which allows you to work for almost any eligible employer in Canada.

If You Already Have Maintained Status or Are Changing Employers

Workers on maintained status or with valid permits may also benefit from the public policy that allows them to begin working for a new employer while their application is being processed. This reduces the downtime between jobs and ensures workers can remain financially stable.

Why This Policy Matters

Previously, the employer-specific nature of most work permits made it very difficult for foreign workers to leave abusive situations without risking their immigration status. This often left individuals trapped in unsafe working conditions.

The expanded protections and faster transition options now give workers greater control over their employment, improve workplace safety, and encourage fair treatment by employers.

Final Thoughts

Canada’s move toward employer portability for vulnerable open work permit holders is a major step forward in protecting foreign workers’ rights. With the combination of open work permits and flexible transition policies, affected workers now have more options to safeguard their well-being while maintaining legal employment.
