A Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is a key requirement for many Canadian employers seeking to hire foreign workers. Once an LMIA is approved, the foreign national can apply for a work permit. While LMIA approval is a positive step, it does not guarantee that Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) will issue a work permit.

In fact, many LMIA-based work permit applications are refused due to avoidable errors, missing information, or concerns about the applicant’s intent and eligibility. In this blog, we explain the Top Reasons LMIA Work Permit Refused cases happen and how to avoid common pitfalls.

What Is an LMIA-Based Work Permit?

An LMIA is a document issued by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) that confirms there is a need for a foreign worker to fill a job and that no Canadian citizen or permanent resident is available to do it.

Once an employer receives a positive LMIA, the foreign worker can apply for a closed work permit specific to the employer, job position, and location outlined in the LMIA.

Top Reasons IRCC Refuses LMIA-Based Work Permit Applications

1. Lack of Proof of Genuine Job Offer

Even with an approved LMIA, IRCC may refuse the application if the job offer appears questionable or inconsistent- making this one of the Top Reasons LMIA Work Permit Refused cases involve employer documentation issues.

Common issues include:

    • Inconsistencies between the job offer letter and the LMIA details
    • Missing or unsigned job contracts
    • Employer has a history of non-compliance or limited operational activity
    • Employer unable to prove capacity to pay the offered wages

How to Avoid It:
Ensure the job offer letter matches the LMIA exactly in terms of wages, duties, hours, and location. Include proof of business legitimacy such as business licenses, payroll records, and recent tax filings.

2. Concerns About the Applicant’s Intent

IRCC officers assess whether the applicant genuinely intends to work in Canada on a temporary basis and return to their home country after the authorized period. A key factor among the Top Reasons LMIA Work Permit Refused applications.

Red flags include:

    • Weak ties to the home country
    • History of previous visa refusals or immigration violations
    • Lack of relevant education or experience for the job
    • Inconsistent travel or employment history

How to Avoid It:
Submit a clear explanation letter outlining your temporary intent, your qualifications, your work experience, and your plan to return home after the work permit expires. Include ties to your home country such as family, property, or ongoing commitments.

3. Insufficient Proof of Qualifications or Work Experience

Even with a valid LMIA, the applicant must demonstrate that they are qualified for the job as per the National Occupation Classification (NOC) code used in the LMIA. Insufficient proof of qualifications is consistently among the Top Reasons LMIA Work Permit Refused by IRCC.

Common mistakes include:

    • Submitting vague or unauthenticated employment letters
    • Not meeting the required education or skill level for the NOC
    • Documents not translated or lacking proper certification

How to Avoid It:
Include detailed, verifiable employment letters that match the duties and skill requirements of the LMIA’s NOC. Submit educational certificates, training documents, and language test results if required. Ensure all foreign-language documents are translated and certified.

4. Inadequate or Missing Documentation

Work permit applications must be complete and properly formatted. Missing even one required document can result in a refusal — this is one of the most preventable Top Reasons LMIA Work Permit Refused cases occur.

Examples of missing items include:

    • Copy of the approved LMIA and job offer
    • Valid passport or travel document
    • Digital photo meeting IRCC specifications
    • Proof of relationship (if dependents are included)
    • Fee receipts or signed application forms

How to Avoid It:
Use the latest IRCC document checklist. Review every form for accuracy and completeness before submission. Double-check that uploads are clear, properly labeled, and in the required file format.

5. Employer Compliance Issues or Recent Violations

IRCC may refuse the application if the employer is found non-compliant — employer violations are consistently among the top reasons lmia work permit refused by IRCC:

    • Ineligible to hire foreign workers
    • On the IRCC’s or ESDC’s list of non-compliant employers
    • To have previously failed to meet work permit conditions

How to Avoid It:
Before applying, ensure the employer is in good standing with ESDC and not subject to ongoing investigations or bans. Employers should maintain clear records of previous LMIA hires and be prepared for audits.

6. Lack of Clarity in Application Purpose or Inconsistent Information

Sometimes, even small inconsistencies in the application can raise doubt for the reviewing officer.

Examples include:

    • Different job titles or duties in various documents
    • Gaps in employment history not explained
    • Conflicting information between the LMIA and personal statement

How to Avoid It:
Ensure all documents tell the same story. Include a concise explanation letter that outlines your background, how the job offer aligns with your skills, and why you are applying. Be honest and transparent.

What to Do When Top Reasons LMIA Work Permit Refused Cases Apply to You

If your application is refused, the first step is to request your GCMS notes to understand the detailed reasons. Understanding the Top Reasons LMIA Work Permit Refused applications helps you fix issues before reapplying. From there, you can decide to:

    • Reapply with corrected documents or stronger evidence
    • Apply under a different stream if eligible
    • File for reconsideration in rare cases

Do not submit the same application again without addressing the officer’s concerns. A repeat refusal can make future applications more difficult.

Final Thoughts: A Strong Work Permit Application Goes Beyond LMIA Approval

An LMIA approval is a positive first step, but it is not a guarantee that IRCC will issue a work permit. The application must still be supported by strong documentation, proof of qualifications, and a clear demonstration of temporary intent.
