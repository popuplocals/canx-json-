Express Entry gives you 60 days after an invitation to apply to submit a complete permanent residence application. For many applicants, the hardest document to secure within that window is not a degree assessment or a reference letter. It is a police certificate from a country they left years ago. Planning ahead turns this step from a scramble into a formality.

This guide explains who needs a police clearance for Canada immigration, the timing rules IRCC applies, how to request certificates from common countries, and what to do when a certificate is delayed.

Quick Summary

Permanent residence applicants generally need a police certificate from each country where they lived for six or more months in a row since age 18, apart from Canada. A certificate from your current country must be issued within six months before you submit. Certificates from other countries must be issued after you last lived there for six months or more. Submit colour scans of originals, with certified English or French translations where needed.

What Is a Police Certificate?

A police certificate shows whether you have a criminal record in a given country, and if you do, provides a copy of it. Countries use different names, including police clearance certificate, certificate of good conduct, criminal record check or judicial record extract. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) uses these certificates to assess admissibility. You can read IRCC’s general guidance on its police certificates page.

Who Needs a Police Certificate?

For permanent residence, you and each family member aged 18 or older generally need a police certificate from every country, other than Canada, where you lived for six or more consecutive months since turning 18. You do not need certificates for periods before age 18 or for time spent in Canada. Check your program’s instruction guide, because some programs set additional requirements.

The Timing Rules That Catch People Out

IRCC applies specific validity rules, explained on its page about when to get police certificates:

CountryTiming rule
Your current country of residenceCertificate must be issued within 6 months before you submit your application
Countries where you lived in the pastCertificate must be issued after the last time you lived there for 6 or more consecutive months
CanadaNo police certificate needed for time spent in Canada

IRCC may also ask for updated certificates during processing, especially if your application takes a long time.

Police Certificates and Express Entry

After you receive an invitation to apply, you have 60 days to submit a complete application, including police certificates. Because some countries take weeks or months to issue certificates, start requesting them as soon as you enter the Express Entry pool, or even earlier for countries known for long processing times.

If a certificate does not arrive in time despite your best efforts, upload proof that you requested it, such as a receipt or confirmation email, together with a letter explaining the delay. Submit the certificate as soon as you receive it.

How to Get Police Certificates From Common Countries

Each country has its own process, and IRCC publishes country-specific instructions you should always follow. Common examples include:

CountryTypical document and issuer
IndiaPolice Clearance Certificate (PCC) through Passport Seva or an Indian mission abroad
PhilippinesNational Bureau of Investigation (NBI) clearance
United StatesFBI Identity History Summary, plus state checks if IRCC instructions require them
United KingdomPolice certificate from ACRO Criminal Records Office

Requirements change, so confirm the current process on IRCC’s country-specific page and the issuing authority’s official website before you apply.

Format, Copies and Translations

  • Upload clear colour scans of your original certificates. IRCC may reject black-and-white photocopies or unofficial copies.
  • If a certificate is not in English or French, include the original and a translation from a certified translator.
  • Make sure names and dates of birth match your passport. If they differ, include documents explaining the difference.
  • Keep originals safe, because IRCC can ask to see them at any time.

What If You Have a Criminal Record?

A record does not automatically end your immigration plans, but you must disclose it honestly. Depending on the offence and how long ago it occurred, you may be deemed rehabilitated, or you may need to apply for criminal rehabilitation or another remedy. Never hide a record, because misrepresentation can lead to a five-year ban from applying.

Common Police Certificate Mistakes

  • Forgetting a country where you lived for six months or more, such as a past work assignment.
  • Submitting a certificate for your current country that is more than six months old.
  • Requesting the wrong type of certificate, for example a local check instead of a national one.
  • Waiting until after an invitation to apply to start the process.
  • Submitting uncertified translations.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who needs a police clearance certificate for Canada PR?

Generally, you and each family member aged 18 or older need a police certificate from every country, other than Canada, where you lived for six or more consecutive months since turning 18. Requirements can differ by program, so always check your program’s instruction guide and IRCC’s country-specific instructions.

How long is a police certificate valid for Canadian immigration?

A certificate from your current country of residence must be issued within six months before you submit your application. Certificates from countries where you lived in the past must be issued after the last time you lived there for six or more months. IRCC can request updated certificates during processing.

What if my police certificate does not arrive within 60 days of my ITA?

Upload proof that you requested the certificate, such as a receipt or confirmation, with a letter explaining the delay, and submit your application on time. Send the certificate as soon as it arrives. Starting requests when you enter the Express Entry pool greatly reduces this risk.

Do I need a police certificate from Canada?

No. IRCC does not require police certificates for time spent in Canada as part of a permanent residence application. However, you must still answer all background questions honestly, including questions about any charges or convictions in Canada or elsewhere.
