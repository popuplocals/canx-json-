Canada’s hospitals, clinics, and care homes run on skilled people, and across the country, they need more of them. Emergency departments, family practices, rehabilitation clinics, and diagnostic labs all depend on a steady supply of qualified health professionals. That is why health care has been one of Canada’s top immigration priorities for years.

On November 3, 2026, Express Entry Canada 2026 opens a bigger door. Under new rules from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), 22 healthcare occupations on the new high-wage list can earn up to 100 Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points for a qualifying job offer, or up to 70 extra points for Canadian work experience. For doctors, nurses, pharmacists, therapists, and technologists who want Canadian permanent residency, this is a real chance to move up the Express Entry pool.

Quick Summary

From November 3, 2026, 22 healthcare occupations qualify for new CRS points. Six sit at Level 1, including family physicians, specialists, and nurse practitioners (100 points for a job offer, or 70 extra points for 12 months of Canadian experience). Ten sit at Level 2, including registered nurses, pharmacists, physiotherapists, and dentists (70 or 40 points). Six sit at Level 3, including medical laboratory technologists and sonographers (50 or 20 points). These points work alongside the existing healthcare and social services draws, giving health professionals more than one way to stand out.

Why Healthcare Workers Benefit So Much

Health care already had a strong place in Express Entry. IRCC runs category-based draws for healthcare and social services occupations, and in December 2025 it announced a dedicated Express Entry category for doctors with Canadian work experience. In February 2026, IRCC confirmed healthcare as a continuing priority.

The November 3 changes add something new. Health professionals in listed occupations now earn extra points in the CRS itself. That helps in two ways: it raises their rank in healthcare category draws, and it makes them more competitive in every other draw where CRS rank matters. And because most healthcare occupations on the list sit at Level 1 or Level 2, the gains can be among the largest of any sector.

What the New Points Mean in Practice

IRCC’s current CRS criteria give a single candidate 40 points for one year of Canadian work experience. From November 3, here is how the new points could add up for a single candidate with one year of Canadian experience:

  • A family physician (NOC 31102, Level 1): 40 points for experience plus 70 high-wage points, for 110 in total
  • A registered nurse (NOC 31301, Level 2): 40 points for experience plus 40 high-wage points, for 80 in total
  • A medical laboratory technologist (NOC 32120, Level 3): 40 points for experience plus 20 high-wage points, for 60 in total

With a qualifying job offer instead, the same candidates could earn 100, 70, or 50 job offer points. Job offer and work experience points do not stack; IRCC awards whichever gives more. The new factor caps at 150 points for candidates without a spouse and 140 for those with an accompanying spouse or common-law partner.

Three Paths for Health Professionals

1. Already Working in Canada

If you have at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience in one of these occupations, IRCC will add your high-wage points automatically when it recalculates scores after November 3. Many internationally educated nurses and other health workers already in Canada could see their scores rise right away.

2. A Qualifying Job Offer, Without an LMIA

From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation no longer needs a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) to earn CRS points. Health authorities, hospitals, clinics, and pharmacies can now support a candidate’s permanent residence with a genuine job offer, without the LMIA process. IRCC officers will still assess each offer for genuineness. Specific job offer rules also apply to physicians providing publicly funded medical services.

3. Healthcare Category Draws

Healthcare and social services draws have continued through 2026, with cut-offs between 467 and 475. Candidates in listed occupations who gain the new points will rank higher within those draws. The physicians category, which requires Canadian work experience, continues as well.

Licensing: The Key Step for Health Professionals

Almost every healthcare occupation on this list is regulated in Canada. Before you can practise, you generally need to register with the regulatory body in the province or territory where you plan to work. For nurses, for example, that means the provincial nursing regulator; for pharmacists, the provincial pharmacy regulator.

Licensing matters for immigration too. For a job offer to count, an IRCC officer must be satisfied that you can do the work, and that you are likely to qualify for the licence your job requires. Starting the licensing process early strengthens both your job prospects and your application. Many provinces have also introduced faster routes for internationally educated health professionals, so check the current process with your regulator.

How to Improve Your CRS Score in 2026 as a Health Professional

  1. Confirm your NOC code. Match your duties to the right code. A nursing coordinator and a registered nurse, for example, fall under different codes.
  2. Start licensing early. Contact your provincial regulator and begin credential assessment as soon as possible.
  3. Document your experience carefully. Reference letters should describe your clinical duties, hours, and dates clearly.
  4. Boost your language scores. Strong English or French results add valuable points and are often required for licensing too.
  5. Update your profile before November 3. IRCC recalculates work experience points automatically, but you must enter job offer details yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which healthcare jobs get extra CRS points in Express Entry 2026?

From November 3, 2026, 22 healthcare occupations qualify, including family physicians, specialists, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, registered nurses, pharmacists, dentists, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, psychologists, dental hygienists, medical laboratory technologists, sonographers, respiratory therapists, paramedics, and dietitians. Check your exact NOC code against IRCC’s official list.

How many CRS points can a registered nurse get under the new rules?

Registered nurses (NOC 31301) sit at Level 2. That means 40 extra points for at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience, on top of regular experience points, or 70 points for a qualifying job offer. The two cannot be combined; IRCC awards whichever gives more.

Are licensed practical nurses on the high-wage list?

No. Licensed practical nurses are not on the current list, so they do not receive the new high-wage points. They keep their regular CRS points and may still qualify for healthcare category draws. Some LPNs choose to pursue registered nurse status, which is on the list at Level 2.

Do I need a Canadian licence to get the new points?

The points depend on your NOC code and Canadian work experience or job offer. However, most health occupations require provincial registration to practise, so Canadian work experience in these roles usually means you are already licensed. For job offers, an officer must be satisfied you are likely to qualify for the required licence.

Will healthcare category draw cut-offs go up after November 3?

They may. Healthcare draws in 2026 closed between 467 and 475. As candidates in listed occupations gain points, cut-offs could rise, and candidates in listed occupations will likely move ahead of those in unlisted ones. Watch the first draws after IRCC’s recalculation pause.
