If you’re visiting Canada and wish to Extend Your Stay in Canada longer than your authorized period, you must apply to extend your stay by obtaining a Visitor Record. Whether you are in Canada as a tourist, visiting family, or accompanying a spouse or child, a Visitor Record is the official document that allows you to legally remain in Canada beyond your initial entry period.

In this blog, we’ll explain what a Visitor Record is, who needs one, how to apply, when to apply, and what happens after submission.

What Is a Visitor Record?

A Visitor Record is an official document issued by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) that allows you to Extend Your Stay in Canada as a temporary resident. It is not a visa, and it does not allow re-entry if you leave the country. Instead, it confirms the date by which you must leave Canada or apply for further extension.

The Visitor Record:

    • Shows the new expiry date of your authorized stay
    • Is issued only within Canada
    • Must be applied for before your current status expires

Who Needs To Extend Your Stay in Canada With a Visitor Record?

You need a Visitor Record if you are:

    • A tourist or visitor who wants to Extend Your Stay in Canada beyond the standard 6-month period granted at entry
    • In Canada with implied status and want to maintain legal stay
    • A parent or grandparent on a Super Visa needing to extend their stay
    • An accompanying spouse or dependent child of a worker or student, but without their own work or study permit
    • Awaiting a decision on another application (e.g., PR, study permit) but your current stay is about to expire

Note: You cannot extend your stay indefinitely. IRCC will assess whether there are valid reasons for requesting an extension and whether your stay continues to meet the terms of your entry.

When to Apply to Extend Your Stay in Canada

You must apply before your current status expires, to Extend Your Stay in Canada which is either:

    • The date stamped in your passport at entry, or
    • 6 months from the date you entered if no stamp was given

Recommended timeline:

Apply at least 30 days before your current status expires to allow sufficient processing time.

How to Apply for a Visitor Record

Step 1: Gather Required Documents

    • A valid passport (must be valid for the entire requested stay)
    • Details of your travel history
    • A clear reason for wanting to  extend your stay in canada (e.g., family visit, waiting for decision, tourist activities)
    • Proof of sufficient funds to support yourself during the extended stay
    • A copy of your current immigration document or visitor stamp
    • Supporting documents (e.g., relationship to a Canadian resident, itinerary, explanation letter)

Step 2: Complete the Application

You can apply online via your IRCC account using the form IMM 5708 – Application to Change Conditions, Extend My Stay or Remain in Canada as a Visitor.

Be sure to:

    • Answer all questions completely and truthfully
    • Upload all required supporting documents
    • Provide a detailed explanation for the extension
    • Pay the correct fee (currently $100 CAD for most applications)

Step 3: Submit and Wait for a Decision

After submission, IRCC will:

    • Send you a confirmation of submission
    • Allow you to stay under implied status (if your application is submitted before expiry)
    • Notify you once a decision is made

What Is Implied Status?

If you apply for a Visitor Record before your current status expires, you are allowed to stay in Canada legally under the same conditions until a decision is made. This is called implied status.

For example:

    • If you entered as a visitor and applied for an extension before expiry, you can continue staying in Canada while waiting for a decision.
    • If your application is refused, you must leave Canada immediately or apply to restore your status if eligible.

Can You Travel During the Processing Period?

No. A Visitor Record does not allow re-entry to Canada. If you leave while your application is being processed, it is considered abandoned, and you must apply for a new visa or Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) and re-enter Canada.

Common Reasons Visitor Record Applications Are Refused

    • Late submission (after status has expired)
    • Insufficient proof of funds or ties to home country
    • Weak or unclear explanation for extending the stay
    • Past overstays or immigration violations
    • Lack of supporting documentation

What to Do If Your Visitor Record Is Refused

If your application is refused, you must:

    • Leave Canada immediately, or
    • Apply for restoration of status within 90 days of the refusal (if eligible)

Restoration allows you to regain legal status in Canada, but you cannot work or study during this period unless authorized.

Final Thoughts: Plan Ahead and Apply on Time

Extending your stay in Canada as a visitor is possible, but it requires timely action, strong documentation, and a clear reason. A Visitor Record is essential for maintaining legal status and avoiding future immigration complications.
