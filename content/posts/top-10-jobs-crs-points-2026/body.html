A single job offer can now be worth up to 100 Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points. From November 3, 2026, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) will award those points for a qualifying job offer in one of 186 high-wage occupations, and employers will no longer need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) to make it count.

That has sent thousands of Express Entry candidates searching for the same answer: which of these jobs can I realistically get? We reviewed all 186 occupations and picked 10 that combine strong points with fewer barriers to entry. But before you read the list, understand this clearly. Finding a job on this list is the easy part. Proving to an IRCC officer that the job offer is genuine will be the real test, and for many candidates, it will be hard.

Quick Summary

These 10 NOC codes stand out because they generally need no Canadian licence, and employers across many industries hire for them: 60010, 10011, 20012, 70010, 10022, 41402, 21232, 72014, 21234, and 60020. A qualifying job offer in these occupations can earn 50 to 100 CRS points, depending on the wage level. However, without an LMIA, IRCC officers will judge each offer’s genuineness directly. Candidates need real, matching experience, and employers need a real business need and the means to pay. Offers that fail that test can cost more than the points.

How We Chose These 10 Occupations

We assessed every occupation on IRCC’s high-wage list against four practical questions:

  1. Is the occupation free of mandatory licensing? Regulated professions such as engineering, nursing, law, teaching, and many trades require provincial licensing or certification. We left those out, because a candidate usually cannot start work without it.
  2. Do many types of employers hire for it? We favoured roles that businesses of many sizes and industries need, not just governments, hospitals, or a few large companies.
  3. Can the work be full-time and continuous? A qualifying job offer must generally be for full-time, continuous work for at least one year after permanent residence. Project-based and union-dispatched work makes that harder to show.
  4. What is the points value? Where all else was equal, we ranked higher-wage levels first.

This list reflects our professional analysis, not an IRCC ranking. Your own background, province, and employer will decide whether any of these roles fits you.

The CRS Points at Stake

  • Level 1 occupations: 100 points for a qualifying job offer, or 70 additional points for 12 months of Canadian work experience
  • Level 2 occupations: 70 points for a qualifying job offer, or 40 additional points for 12 months of Canadian work experience
  • Level 3 occupations: 50 points for a qualifying job offer, or 20 additional points for 12 months of Canadian work experience

If you qualify for both a job offer and Canadian work experience points, IRCC awards whichever option gives you more. The two do not stack.

1. Corporate Sales Managers (NOC 60010, Level 1)

Points: 100 for a job offer, or 70 for Canadian work experience.

Almost every company that sells a product or service needs someone to lead its sales team. Manufacturers, distributors, software firms, and service businesses all hire corporate sales managers, and the role generally needs no licence. That makes it one of the highest-value, most widely available codes on the list.

The challenge: This is a management role. An officer will expect you to have real experience managing sales staff, targets, and budgets, and will expect the employer to have an actual sales team for you to lead. A small business with no sales staff offering this title will struggle to pass review.

2. Human Resources Managers (NOC 10011, Level 1)

Points: 100 for a job offer, or 70 for Canadian work experience.

Any organization with a meaningful number of employees needs someone to manage hiring, policies, compensation, and employee relations. Voluntary professional designations exist, but the role is generally not licensed, and the skills transfer well across industries.

The challenge: The officer will ask whether the business is large enough to need a dedicated HR manager. A company with five employees will find that hard to justify. Candidates also need documented experience managing HR functions, not just supporting them. In Quebec, use of certain HR titles is regulated, so check local rules.

3. Computer and Information Systems Managers (NOC 20012, Level 1)

Points: 100 for a job offer, or 70 for Canadian work experience.

Technology now runs every type of business, and organizations need people to plan and manage their IT systems, teams, and projects. The role is unregulated, and demand spans finance, health, retail, government contractors, and tech companies.

The challenge: The officer will expect a real IT function: staff, systems, and a budget for you to manage. You will need proof of past leadership in technology, such as managing teams or major systems projects. A job title without a team behind it is a red flag.

4. Construction Managers (NOC 70010, Level 2)

Points: 70 for a job offer, or 40 for Canadian work experience.

Canada’s construction sector includes thousands of small and mid-sized general contractors, builders, and developers. Construction managers plan and oversee projects, budgets, and site teams. The role generally needs no personal licence, although voluntary credentials such as the Gold Seal certification exist, and some provinces license the construction business itself.

The challenge: Construction is cyclical and project-based. The officer will check whether the employer has steady, ongoing work to support a full-time role for at least one year after you become a permanent resident. Expect questions about the company’s active projects, contracts, and revenue, and about your own record of managing construction projects.

5. Advertising, Marketing and Public Relations Managers (NOC 10022, Level 2)

Points: 70 for a job offer, or 40 for Canadian work experience.

Businesses in nearly every industry need marketing leadership, from agencies and retailers to tech firms and professional services. The role is unregulated, and digital marketing skills travel well across borders.

The challenge: Officers know that “marketing manager” is a title some small businesses use loosely. The employer must show a real marketing function, a budget, and ideally staff or agencies to manage. Candidates should be ready to show past campaigns, results, and team leadership.

6. Business Development Officers and Market Researchers and Analysts (NOC 41402, Level 2)

Points: 70 for a job offer, or 40 for Canadian work experience.

This is one of the most flexible codes on the list. It covers people who research markets, identify growth opportunities, and develop business strategies. It is not a management role, so candidates do not need supervisory experience, and the work is unregulated.

The challenge: Because the code is broad, the officer will look closely at whether the duties in the offer actually match it. Vague duties or a role that looks like general sales or administration could fail. The employer should explain exactly what research and development work you will do, and why the business needs it.

7. Software Developers and Programmers (NOC 21232, Level 2)

Points: 70 for a job offer, or 40 for Canadian work experience.

Software developers write, test, and maintain code for applications and systems. Employers range from startups to banks, and the role is unregulated. Many Express Entry candidates already work in this field.

The challenge: Competition for these offers is intense, and remote-first hiring has made some employers cautious. The officer will check that the employer genuinely needs a developer, can pay a realistic wage, and that your skills match the job. Note the difference from software engineers and designers (21231): the title “engineer” is regulated in Canadian provinces, so make sure the code and title in your offer match your actual role.

8. Contractors and Supervisors, Other Construction Trades, Installers, Repairers and Servicers (NOC 72014, Level 3)

Points: 50 for a job offer, or 20 for Canadian work experience.

This code covers supervisors who lead crews of installers, repairers, and other construction tradespeople. Many small and mid-sized contractors hire for it, and the supervisory role itself does not always require personal trade certification.

The challenge: Employers will still expect hands-on trade experience, and some provinces may require certification depending on the trade you supervise. Check the rules in your province. Like construction managers, this role depends on the employer having steady, year-round work for a crew.

9. Web Developers and Programmers (NOC 21234, Level 3)

Points: 50 for a job offer, or 20 for Canadian work experience.

Web developers build and maintain websites and web applications. Agencies, e-commerce businesses, and companies of every size need them, and the role is unregulated. It is often more accessible than software development roles for candidates with strong portfolios.

The challenge: Many web development roles are freelance or contract-based, which does not meet the full-time, continuous requirement. The officer will expect a permanent, full-time position, an employer with enough work to support it, and duties that match web development rather than general IT support.

10. Retail and Wholesale Trade Managers (NOC 60020, Level 3)

Points: 50 for a job offer, or 20 for Canadian work experience.

Retail stores, wholesalers, and distributors across Canada need managers to run operations, staff, and inventory. This code has one of the widest employer bases on the list, from small independent stores to national chains, and it needs no licence.

The challenge: This code will likely draw some of the closest scrutiny. Officers are likely to look closely at small retail businesses that offer manager roles to foreign candidates. The employer must show that the store is large enough to need a manager, that the role involves real management duties, and that the business can pay the salary. A store owner who already manages the business alone will struggle to justify the role.

Why Proving a Job Offer Will Be the Hardest Part

It is tempting to read the November 3 changes as an easy route to 100 points. They are not. Under the old system, Employment and Social Development Canada tested a job offer through the LMIA process before it reached IRCC. That step is gone, and the full burden now sits with IRCC officers, who will decide whether each offer is genuine, often based on documents alone.

IRCC has said officers will consider whether:

  • the employer actively runs the business making the offer
  • the offer reflects the employer’s reasonable employment needs
  • the employer can reasonably meet the terms of the offer
  • the candidate is qualified, able to do the work, and likely to accept it
  • the employer complies with federal and provincial employment and recruitment laws

Every one of the 10 occupations above can fail on these questions. Management roles at small businesses raise the “reasonable need” question. Broad codes raise questions about whether duties match. Construction and web roles raise questions about continuous, full-time work. And for every role, a candidate whose experience does not match the job will face doubts about whether they can actually do it.

The consequences of getting it wrong are serious. A refused job offer can remove the points that earned your invitation. A finding of misrepresentation, for example over a fake or purchased job offer, can bar a candidate from applying to come to Canada for five years. Never pay for a job offer, and be wary of anyone who promises one.

How to Strengthen Your Chances

  1. Choose a role that matches your real experience. The best job offer is one that fits your work history so well that an officer has no reason to doubt it.
  2. Target employers with a real need. Growing businesses with genuine operations and existing teams make the strongest offers.
  3. Get the documents right. Ask the employer for business registration, financial records, an organizational chart, and a clear explanation of the role.
  4. Keep everything consistent. Your Express Entry profile, offer letter, and reference letters must tell the same story.
  5. Update your profile before November 3. IRCC will only award job offer points if you add the job offer details yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which jobs give the most CRS points in Express Entry 2026?

From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer in a Level 1 high-wage occupation earns 100 CRS points, the highest available. Level 1 includes 31 occupations, such as corporate sales managers, human resources managers, and computer and information systems managers. Level 2 offers earn 70 points, and Level 3 offers earn 50 points.

Do I need an LMIA for these job offers?

No. From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer no longer needs an LMIA to earn the new CRS points. Instead, IRCC officers will assess whether the offer is genuine. That shifts the burden of proof onto the employer’s documents and the candidate’s experience, so a strong, well-supported offer matters more than ever.

Is it easy to get a job offer in these occupations?

Not necessarily. These 10 occupations have fewer licensing barriers and a wider range of employers, but finding an offer is only the first step. IRCC officers will test whether the business needs the role, can pay for it, and whether you can do the job. Many offers, especially for management roles at small businesses, may face close scrutiny.

Can a small business offer me a manager job for CRS points?

IRCC allows job offers from any employer, including small businesses. However, the officer must be satisfied that the business genuinely needs a manager and can pay the salary. A small business with few or no staff offering a senior management title will likely face close questions. A non-management role that fits the business may be more credible.

What happens if IRCC decides my job offer is not genuine?

You would lose the job offer points, which could affect your eligibility if those points earned your invitation. If the officer finds misrepresentation, such as a fake or purchased offer, you could face a five-year ban on applying to come to Canada. Always make sure your job offer is real and fully documented.
