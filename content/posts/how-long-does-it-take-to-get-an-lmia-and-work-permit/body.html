One of the first questions employers and foreign workers ask when starting the process is simple: How long will everything take? Unfortunately, it is also one of the hardest questions to answer with certainty. Many people expect a straightforward timeline, but LMIA and work permit processing involves multiple stages, different government departments, recruitment requirements, and variables that can significantly affect overall timelines.

For workers, timing often becomes critical because work permits may be expiring, immigration status may be changing, or family and financial plans may depend on receiving approval. Employers also face pressure because labour shortages can affect operations, staffing schedules, revenue, and business growth. Understanding realistic timelines helps both employers and workers plan properly and avoid unnecessary surprises.

The LMIA and Work Permit Process Happens in Multiple Stages

One of the biggest misconceptions is that people assume there is a single processing timeline covering everything. In reality, obtaining an LMIA-based work permit usually involves several separate stages and each stage has its own timeline.

The process often begins with recruitment and advertising obligations. Once those requirements are completed, employers prepare and submit the LMIA application. After receiving an LMIA decision, the worker then proceeds with the work permit application itself. Immigration officers later assess the worker independently before issuing approval.

Recruitment Requirements Usually Add Significant Time

Many employers underestimate how much time recruitment requirements alone can add to the overall process. Before an LMIA application can even be submitted, employers are often required to conduct recruitment efforts designed to demonstrate that Canadian citizens and permanent residents were genuinely considered.

Advertising periods, candidate reviews, interviews, recruitment documentation, and record keeping all require time. Employers experiencing urgent labour shortages sometimes assume applications can be submitted immediately, only to discover that recruitment obligations must first be completed properly.

Recruitment Preparation Often Starts Before Advertising

Strong LMIA applications usually involve planning before advertisements even go live. Employers frequently need time to determine the correct occupation classification, establish wage structures, prepare job descriptions, and ensure advertising language aligns with program requirements.

Mistakes made during early recruitment planning sometimes create problems later. If advertisements contain incorrect wages, duties, or occupation details, employers occasionally need to restart portions of the process, creating additional delays that could have been avoided through stronger preparation.

LMIA Processing Times Can Vary Significantly

Once an application reaches Service Canada, processing timelines can vary considerably depending on the stream involved, application volume, and overall program demand.

Many people search online hoping to find one universal processing estimate, but timelines regularly change. Certain applications may move relatively quickly while others remain under review much longer. Government processing systems are affected by staffing levels, seasonal demands, labour market conditions, and policy changes.

Different LMIA Streams Can Have Different Timelines

Not every LMIA category follows the same process. High-wage applications, low-wage streams, agricultural categories, permanent residence support streams, and specialized programs may all experience different processing patterns.

Some streams receive priority treatment under specific circumstances while others involve additional reviews. Employers should avoid assuming another business’s timeline will automatically apply to their own application because timelines frequently differ depending on the category involved.

Employer Interviews Can Add Unexpected Delays

Many employers assume that once applications are submitted, the only remaining step is waiting for a final decision. However, Service Canada officers sometimes contact employers to clarify information or conduct interviews.

Requests for additional information, interview scheduling, document clarification, or supplementary evidence may extend processing times. While these requests do not automatically indicate problems, they often increase overall timelines because officers may pause assessments while awaiting responses.

Errors and Missing Information Frequently Cause Delays

One of the most avoidable reasons for delays involves application quality itself. Missing documents, inconsistent information, incorrect occupation classifications, wage concerns, or incomplete recruitment evidence frequently slow down processing.

Government officers may request clarification or additional evidence where information appears unclear. In some situations, employers discover issues only after submission, creating delays that could have been prevented through stronger preparation before filing.

Positive LMIA Approval Is Only the Beginning of Immigration Processing

Many employers and workers feel relieved after receiving a positive LMIA because they assume the difficult stage is complete. However, approval at this stage only concludes the labour market portion of the process.

After approval, the worker still needs to prepare and submit a work permit application through Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada. This creates a completely separate stage with its own requirements, documentation standards, and timelines.

Work Permit Processing Times Depend on Where the Applicant Applies

Work permit processing times vary significantly depending on where and how applications are submitted. Workers applying from inside Canada may experience different timelines than applicants applying overseas.

Processing also varies by country, visa office workload, document requirements, security checks, and application volumes. Two workers applying under similar circumstances from different countries may experience very different timelines.

Biometrics and Medical Requirements Can Extend Processing

Many workers underestimate how biometrics and medical examinations affect timelines. Depending on nationality, occupation, and previous residence history, applicants may need additional assessments before immigration decisions can be finalized.

Healthcare workers, childcare workers, agricultural workers, and individuals from certain countries frequently require immigration medical examinations. Delays commonly occur where appointments are unavailable, requirements are misunderstood, or examinations are completed late in the process.

Background and Security Reviews Can Affect Timelines

Immigration officers assess more than employment documents. Depending on circumstances, applications may involve background screening, travel history reviews, admissibility assessments, and security-related checks.

Some applications move through these stages quickly while others require more extensive review. Workers often become frustrated because these processes occur behind the scenes and timelines may be difficult to predict.

Temporary Public Policies Sometimes Affect Processing

Immigration processing does not remain static. Temporary public policies occasionally create changes affecting work permit processing, interim work authorization, or application procedures.

These policies may accelerate some applications or create special measures for certain groups of workers. Because temporary policies change regularly, employers and workers should avoid relying on outdated information or assumptions based on previous years.

Can Applications Be Processed Faster?

Many employers facing urgent labour shortages want to know whether applications can be expedited. In some situations, certain categories may qualify for priority treatment or faster processing measures depending on program requirements.

However, faster processing options remain limited and eligibility requirements vary significantly. Employers should avoid assuming urgency alone automatically results in accelerated review.

Why Planning Early Has Become More Important Than Ever

One of the biggest mistakes employers and workers make is waiting until the last moment before beginning the process. Businesses facing labour shortages sometimes begin planning only after staffing problems become severe. Workers frequently wait until permits approach expiry dates before considering next steps.

Current processing realities often require considerably more advance planning. Recruitment periods, LMIA assessments, work permit reviews, and additional requirements collectively create timelines that frequently extend much longer than expected.

So How Long Does the Entire Process Really Take?

The honest answer is that there is no universal timeline because every case is different. Recruitment obligations, LMIA processing, employer interviews, biometrics, medical examinations, work permit assessments, and background reviews all affect overall timing.

Some applications progress smoothly while others encounter additional review stages. Rather than focusing only on the fastest timelines available online, employers and workers should build realistic expectations and prepare for variables that may affect processing.

Final Thoughts

The LMIA and work permit process is not a single application with one predictable timeline. It is a multi-stage process involving recruitment, government review, labour market assessments, immigration processing, and ongoing documentation requirements.
