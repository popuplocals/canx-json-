For years, studying in Canada was seen as a reliable stepping stone to permanent residence. In 2026, that path still exists, but it has narrowed. Ontario closed its graduate streams, British Columbia ended its international graduate streams, Saskatchewan limited student eligibility to its own graduates, and PGWP eligibility now depends on language tests and, for some programs, your field of study. Students who plan their pathway from day one still succeed. Those who assume the old rules apply often run out of time.

This guide lays out a realistic, step-by-step study permit to PR pathway in Canada for 2026, from choosing a program to landing as a permanent resident.

Quick Summary

The most common study-to-PR pathway in 2026 is: complete a PGWP-eligible program, obtain a Post-Graduation Work Permit, gain at least one year of skilled Canadian work experience, and apply through the Canadian Experience Class or a provincial nominee program. Language scores, the right program choice and early skilled work experience matter more than ever, because several provincial graduate streams closed in 2026.

Step 1: Choose a Program With PR in Mind

Before you apply for a study permit, check that your program:

  • Is offered by a PGWP-eligible designated learning institution.
  • Meets the PGWP field of study requirement, if it applies to your program.
  • Is long enough to produce the PGWP length you need; master’s graduates can receive up to three years.
  • Is mostly in person, since programs with more than 50% distance learning generally do not qualify.
  • Leads to an occupation in demand in the province where you plan to live.

Step 2: Study Well and Protect Your Status

Stay enrolled full-time, respect work-hour limits and keep your study permit and passport valid. Gaps in enrolment or unauthorized work can affect your PGWP eligibility.

Step 3: Prepare for Your PGWP Before Graduation

Take your language test before you graduate. University graduates need CLB 7 and college graduates need CLB 5 in all four skills. Apply within 180 days of receiving confirmation that you completed your program, and apply before your study permit expires so you can work while IRCC processes your application.

Step 4: Get Skilled Canadian Work Experience Quickly

The Canadian Experience Class requires at least one year (1,560 hours) of skilled, paid work experience in Canada in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation within the past three years. Work you did while studying full-time does not count toward this requirement. Start your career job as early as possible after graduation.

Step 5: Build a Competitive Express Entry Profile

Your Comprehensive Ranking System score combines age, education, language and Canadian experience. A Canadian credential can add 15 or 30 additional points, and strong language scores can add far more. In 2026, Canadian Experience Class draws generally required scores in the high 510s to low 520s, while category-based draws for healthcare, trades and French speakers often required less.

Step 6: Explore Provincial Options

Provincial nominee programs still matter, but graduate pathways changed significantly in 2026:

ProvinceStudent-related changes in 2026
OntarioMaster’s and PhD Graduate streams revoked on May 30, 2026; new employer-driven stream launched June 26, 2026
British ColumbiaInternational Graduate and International Post-Graduate streams closed
SaskatchewanStudent eligibility limited to graduates of Saskatchewan institutions with related Saskatchewan work experience
Nova ScotiaNova Scotia Graduate stream for recent graduates, including health care fields, with a related job offer
AlbertaGraduate Entrepreneur Stream for Alberta graduates; worker streams generally require Alberta work and job offers

Studying in a province where you want to live and work gives you the best chance under many provincial programs.

Step 7: Apply for PR and Keep Working

Once you receive an invitation or nomination, submit a complete PR application. If your PGWP is expiring, you may qualify for a bridging open work permit to keep working while IRCC finishes your application.

Mistakes That Derail Study-to-PR Plans

  • Choosing a non-eligible program or institution for the PGWP.
  • Delaying the language test until after graduation.
  • Spending your PGWP in non-skilled jobs.
  • Ignoring provincial rule changes.
  • Letting your status lapse between permits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a study permit lead to PR in Canada?

Yes, but not directly. Most students complete a PGWP-eligible program, obtain a Post-Graduation Work Permit, gain at least one year of skilled Canadian work experience, and then apply through the Canadian Experience Class or a provincial nominee program. Planning from the start greatly improves your chances.

Does work experience during studies count for Canadian Experience Class?

No. Work you completed while you were a full-time student does not count toward the one year of skilled Canadian work experience required for the Canadian Experience Class. Experience gained after graduation, such as on a PGWP, in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation can count.

How long does it take to go from study permit to PR?

It depends on your program length and how quickly you gain skilled experience. A typical path includes your study program, PGWP processing, at least one year of skilled Canadian work, selection through Express Entry or a provincial program, and about 6 to 7 months of PR processing for Express Entry in late 2026.

Which provinces still have graduate streams in 2026?

Graduate options changed significantly in 2026. Ontario revoked its Master’s and PhD streams, British Columbia closed its international graduate streams, and Saskatchewan limited student eligibility to its own graduates. Nova Scotia offers a Nova Scotia Graduate stream, and Alberta has a Graduate Entrepreneur Stream. Check each province’s current rules.
