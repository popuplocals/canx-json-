For years, the arranged employment points system was one of the most strategically significant factors in Express Entry planning. A positive LMIA-backed job offer in an eligible NOC category added 50 or 200 CRS points to a candidate’s profile — in many cases, the difference between receiving an Invitation to Apply in the next draw and waiting another year. Entire immigration strategies were built around obtaining these points.

On March 25, 2025, IRCC removed those points entirely. The decision was driven by a documented, large-scale fraud problem: investigations had revealed that employers and intermediaries were selling fraudulent LMIA letters to Express Entry candidates for sums reportedly ranging from $20,000 to $75,000 per letter. The job offer points created a financial incentive for fraud that IRCC ultimately determined could not be managed through enforcement alone. Eliminating the points eliminated the incentive.

The immediate effect was visible in the Express Entry pool: thousands of profiles saw their CRS scores drop by 50 or 200 points overnight. Some candidates who had been competitive with the bonus became uncompetitive without it. IRCC acknowledged this disruption but maintained that increased system integrity was the correct trade-off.

In 2026, the landscape has stabilized. Candidates and employers who have accepted the new reality and adjusted their strategies are finding that viable PR pathways still exist for skilled workers with genuine employer relationships — they are just structured differently than before. This article explains the current state of play with precision, covers what the LMIA still contributes to a PR strategy, and sets the record straight on what is and is not currently available.

What Exactly Was Eliminated on March 25, 2025

The elimination was comprehensive and applied to both streams of arranged employment that previously existed in the Express Entry CRS:

  • LMIA-backed job offers: The 50-point bonus for most eligible NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, and 3 positions was eliminated.
  • Senior management LMIA bonus: The 200-point bonus for NOC TEER 0 Major Group 00 (senior management) positions was eliminated.
  • LMIA-exempt closed work permit job offers: The bonus for qualifying intra-company transfers (C10) and other LMIA-exempt employer-specific offers that previously generated 50 points was also eliminated.

The elimination applies to all candidates in the Express Entry pool as of March 25, 2025. Candidates who had already received an Invitation to Apply before that date, or who had already submitted a permanent residence application, were not affected. But everyone still in the pool on March 25, 2025 had their arranged employment points removed and their CRS scores recalculated.

Important Nuance: The 67-Point Federal Skilled Worker Grid

The LMIA elimination applies to the CRS additional points for arranged employment. It does NOT eliminate the arranged employment factor on the Federal Skilled Worker Program’s separate 67-point selection grid. The FSW 67-point grid awards up to 10 points for arranged employment, and that factor was not removed. However, the 67-point grid is only used to determine eligibility to enter the Express Entry pool under the FSW program, not to determine CRS ranking within the pool. Do not confuse these two separate systems.

Why IRCC Made This Decision: The Fraud Context

Understanding why the points were eliminated matters for understanding whether and how they might return. The fraud that drove the decision was not minor or marginal. ESDC and IRCC investigations revealed a market in which Canadian employers, sometimes operating as fronts with no genuine business purpose, were providing fraudulent positive LMIA letters to Express Entry candidates in exchange for large payments. The candidate would receive a genuine LMIA letter from a genuine ESDC assessment, based on a fraudulent application, and would use it to claim 50 or 200 additional CRS points.

The payments involved — reported to range from $20,000 to $75,000 per fraudulent LMIA — created a system in which the ability to pay for immigration advantage was effectively being sold. IRCC’s position was that this fundamentally compromised the integrity and fairness of the Express Entry system, which is supposed to select candidates based on their human capital attributes, not their ability to purchase a CRS advantage.

By eliminating the points entirely, IRCC removed the financial incentive for the fraud in a single regulatory action. No LMIA letter, genuine or fraudulent, now generates any CRS advantage. The fraud market for arranged employment points collapsed because the product being sold became worthless for its intended purpose overnight.

Could the Points Return? The Current 2026 Position

As of May 2026, the arranged employment CRS points have not been reintroduced. However, IRCC’s 2026–27 Departmental Plan has referenced an intention to reintroduce job offer points at some point in the future, potentially with redesigned eligibility criteria that would be more resistant to fraud. This is an intention, not a policy announcement. There is no implementation date, no confirmed point values, and no confirmed eligibility framework.

Candidates and employers should not plan their immigration strategies around the assumption that the points will return by any particular date. The announcement of an intention is not a commitment, and IRCC’s implementation timelines for policy changes have historically been difficult to predict. Any reintroduction of job offer points, if it occurs, is likely to include significantly tighter eligibility requirements, verification mechanisms, or structural safeguards designed to prevent the fraud that drove the original elimination.

What the LMIA Still Does for Express Entry Candidates in 2026

The elimination of arranged employment points did not eliminate the LMIA’s value in an immigration strategy. It significantly changed the nature of that value. In 2026, an LMIA contributes to a candidate’s PR pathway in three primary ways — none of which involve direct CRS bonus points.

Contribution 1: Legal Work Authorization Enabling Canadian Experience Accumulation

The LMIA enables a work permit. The work permit enables employment in Canada. Employment in Canada accumulates Canadian work experience. Canadian work experience is one of the most heavily weighted factors in the Express Entry CRS scoring system.

Under the core human capital factors, one year of full-time skilled Canadian work experience (in a NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation) is worth 40 CRS points for a single applicant and 35 for an applicant with a spouse. Three years of Canadian experience is worth 80 points (single) or 70 points (with spouse). These points are earned simply by working in Canada legally — and a positive LMIA is the mechanism that makes legal work possible for most foreign nationals without pre-existing Canadian connections.

This is not a trivial contribution. A candidate who arrives in Canada on an LMIA work permit and works in their NOC-classified position for two years has earned 80 additional CRS points from Canadian work experience alone — 60% more than they would have received from the now-eliminated 50-point arranged employment bonus. The work experience pathway is slower but, ultimately, generates more CRS value than the bonus ever did for most candidates.

Contribution 2: Canadian Experience Class (CEC) Eligibility

After accumulating one year of full-time skilled Canadian work experience within the three years preceding an Express Entry application, a candidate becomes eligible for the Canadian Experience Class — the most regularly drawn and most competitive stream for candidates with Canadian work history. CEC draws have historically occurred at lower CRS thresholds than general Federal Skilled Worker draws, and category-based draws targeting specific occupations have selected CEC-eligible candidates at thresholds below the general pool minimum.

The LMIA that enables the work permit that enables the year of Canadian experience is therefore the indirect foundation of CEC eligibility. Without the LMIA, there is no work permit. Without the work permit, there is no year of Canadian experience. Without the year of Canadian experience, there is no CEC eligibility. The causal chain from LMIA to CEC eligibility remains intact even though the CRS bonus at the end of the chain has been removed.

Contribution 3: Provincial Nominee Program Employer-Driven Nominations

The most powerful contribution an LMIA-backed employer relationship can make to a PR strategy in 2026 is not through Express Entry CRS points at all — it is through the PNP nomination. A provincial nominee nomination adds 600 CRS points to an Express Entry profile, which is functionally equivalent to a guarantee of an ITA in the next available federal draw regardless of the candidate’s base CRS score.

An employer who has obtained a positive LMIA, hired a foreign worker, and built a genuine employment relationship with that worker over six to twelve months is exactly the kind of employer who can support a provincial nominee nomination through employer-driven PNP streams. These streams exist specifically to allow employers to permanently secure workers they have already invested in training and integrating. The LMIA is the foundation on which this employer relationship is built, and the PNP nomination is the culmination of that relationship’s immigration value.

In 2026, the strategic model for an LMIA-to-PR pathway looks like this: LMIA, work permit, Canadian work experience accumulation, employer commitment to permanent hiring, PNP employer-driven nomination, 600 CRS points, ITA, PR application. This is longer and requires more active employer engagement than the old bonus-points model, but it is structurally sound and produces permanent residence reliably for workers in genuine employer relationships.

How the CRS Landscape Has Shifted Since March 2025

The elimination of arranged employment points did not push CRS cutoff scores down by 50 points across the board, as some predicted. The relationship between removed bonus points and draw cutoffs is more complex. Category-based draws, which now dominate Express Entry draw activity, select for specific occupations and skill profiles rather than simply ranking all candidates by CRS score. These draws have produced selection cutoffs that vary widely by category, with healthcare draws selecting at scores in the low 400s and technology draws in the mid-400s, while general draws have remained more competitive.

The practical effect of the elimination for the average LMIA work permit holder in Canada is that their Express Entry profile is now competing based on their genuine human capital: their language scores, their educational credentials, their age, and their Canadian work experience. Workers who have invested in strong language scores and who are accumulating Canadian experience are well-positioned in this environment. Workers who had been counting on a job offer bonus to compensate for weaker human capital attributes now need to address those attributes directly.

What Employers Should Understand

For Canadian employers who obtained LMIAs with the intention of supporting workers’ Express Entry CRS scores, the March 2025 change altered the immediate immigration benefit their workers receive from the job offer. However, the employment relationship itself, the Canadian work experience accumulation, and the potential for a PNP employer-driven nomination all remain as valuable as ever.

Employers who understand this and who are genuinely committed to retaining their foreign workers long-term are now in a better position to use the LMIA as part of a comprehensive retention and PR support strategy: support the worker’s PNP application through an employer-driven stream, continue the employment relationship through the PNP process, and consider the LMIA and the subsequent permanent hiring commitment as a unified immigration investment rather than two separate steps.

Employers who were using the LMIA primarily to generate CRS points for workers they had no genuine intention of employing long-term have found that the elimination removed their mechanism. This is precisely the outcome IRCC intended.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do LMIA job offers still give CRS points in Express Entry in 2026?

No. As of March 25, 2025, IRCC eliminated all arranged employment CRS points from the Express Entry system. Job offers backed by an LMIA or an LMIA-exempt work permit no longer add any points to a candidate’s CRS score. Before this change, qualifying job offers added either 50 or 200 additional CRS points. Those points are now eliminated. IRCC’s 2026–27 Departmental Plan has mentioned an intention to reintroduce job offer points in the future, but no implementation date, confirmed values, or eligibility criteria have been announced as of May 2026.

Why did IRCC eliminate arranged employment CRS points?

IRCC eliminated arranged employment points to combat widespread fraud in the LMIA system. Investigations revealed that employers and intermediaries were selling fraudulent LMIA-backed job offer letters to Express Entry candidates for reported amounts of $20,000 to $75,000 per letter. The job offer points created a financial incentive for this fraud by making LMIA letters commercially valuable as immigration tools independent of genuine employment. By eliminating the points, IRCC removed the financial incentive for the fraud market. The change was first announced in December 2024 and took effect March 25, 2025.

Does an LMIA still help with permanent residence if there are no CRS points?

Yes, significantly. An LMIA enables a work permit, which enables legal employment in Canada, which accumulates Canadian work experience — worth up to 80 CRS points for three years of skilled Canadian work. That experience also creates eligibility for the Canadian Experience Class in Express Entry. Most importantly, the employment relationship created through the LMIA is the foundation for a Provincial Nominee Program employer-driven nomination, which adds 600 CRS points — far more than the eliminated 50 or 200 point bonus ever provided. The LMIA’s value for PR has shifted from immediate CRS bonus to longer-term pathway foundation.

My Express Entry score dropped after March 2025 because of this change. What should I do?

If your CRS score dropped because arranged employment points were removed, your strategy should focus on two areas. First, strengthen your human capital factors: retake language tests to improve IELTS or CELPIP scores, ensure your educational credential assessment is current and correctly claimed, and continue accumulating Canadian work experience. Second, explore Provincial Nominee Program options with your employer: if your employer is genuinely committed to retaining you and your province of work has an employer-driven PNP stream, a provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points — twelve times the 50-point bonus you lost. Consulting an RCIC is strongly recommended for a current strategy assessment.

Were LMIA-exempt job offers also affected by the March 2025 elimination?

Yes. The March 25, 2025 elimination applied to all arranged employment CRS points, including those from LMIA-exempt closed employer-specific work permits such as intra-company transfers (C10). Before the change, qualifying LMIA-exempt job offers in NOC TEER 0 or 1 could generate 50 additional CRS points. Those points were eliminated along with the LMIA-backed points. As of May 2026, no job offer type — LMIA or LMIA-exempt — generates additional CRS points in Express Entry.
