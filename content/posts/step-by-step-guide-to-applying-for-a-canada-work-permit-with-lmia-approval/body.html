What Is Canada LMIA work permit and Why Does It Matter?

A Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) is a document issued by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) that allows Canadian employers to hire foreign workers. It is essentially a test that proves:

    • The employer genuinely tried to hire Canadians or permanent residents first.
    • The job is real, full-time, and follows labour market standards.
    • The foreign worker is necessary for the role.

If the Canada LMIA Work Permit is Positive, the employer can offer the job to a foreign national. The foreign national can then use the LMIA to apply for a work permit through IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada).

Step-by-Step Process to Apply for a Canada LMIA work permit
Approval

Step 1: Find a Genuine Job Offer from a Canadian Employer

Start by securing a valid job offer. The offer must be:

    • Full-time and non-seasonal (except for agricultural or seasonal roles)
    • Aligned with a TEER code in the NOC 2021 system
    • From an employer willing to go through the LMIA process

Where to search:

    • Job Bank Canada
    • Indeed.ca
    • Industry-specific platforms
    • Regulated immigration consultants or trusted recruitment firms

Tip: Always verify the legitimacy of the employer. Scams are common.

Step 2: Employer Applies for LMIA

The employer must apply to ESDC and submit:

    • A detailed job description, wage rate, and work conditions
    • Proof of recruitment efforts (ads, job postings, interview notes)
    • A transition plan (for high-wage roles)
    • $1,000 CAD application fee per worker
    • Business legitimacy documents (incorporation certificate, tax returns, payroll records)

Step 3: Receive Positive LMIA and Job Offer Letter

Once approved, the employer receives a positive LMIA (valid for 6 months) and must issue a formal employment offer letter including:

    • Job title and TEER code
    • Wage and benefits
    • Start date and duration
    • Work location
    • Employer contact information

Both documents are mandatory for your Canada LMIA Work permit application.

Step 4: Confirm You Meet IRCC Eligibility for Work Permit

You must demonstrate to IRCC that you:

    • Have a genuine job offer supported by LMIA
    • Are qualified for the position (education, training, work experience)
    • Have no criminal record (police certificate required for some countries)
    • Are in good health (medical exam required for some occupations or countries)
    • Will leave Canada at the end of your work permit
    • Can financially support yourself and dependents

Step 5: Prepare Your Application Documents

Common required documents include:

    • Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity)
    • Positive LMIA approval letter
    • Signed job offer letter
    • Educational credentials and experience letters
    • Resume or CV
    • Police clearance certificate (if applicable)
    • Medical exam proof (if applicable)
    • Biometrics receipt
    • Completed application forms (e.g., IMM 1295)

Tip: Keep a PDF copy of every document for your records.

Step 6: Apply for Your Canada LMIA Work Permit

Submit your work permit application:

    • Online via IRCC portal (recommended)
    • Or at a Visa Application Centre (VAC) in your home country

Fees to pay:

    • Work Permit: $155 CAD
    • Biometrics: $85 CAD

Step 7: Monitor the Processing and Respond Promptly

Processing time depends on the applicant’s country and LMIA stream. Average timelines:

    • Global Talent Stream: 1 to 2 weeks
    • Regular LMIA streams: 2 to 3 months or more

Use IRCC’s online account to:

    • Track your application
    • Upload additional documents if requested
    • Check messages from IRCC

Step 8: Travel to Canada and Get Your Work Permit at the Border

If approved, you will receive a Port of Entry (POE) Letter of Introduction.

When arriving in Canada:

    • Present your POE Letter, passport, LMIA, and job offer
    • Answer questions truthfully to the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) officer
    • If everything is in order, the officer will issue your work permit on the spot

Common Reasons for Work Permit Refusal (and How to Avoid Them)

Understanding why Canada LMIA Work Permit are refused can help you avoid costly mistakes.

  1. Job Offer Is Not Genuine
    • IRCC doubts the legitimacy of the offer (for example, job does not exist or the employer is not real)
    • The employer is not registered or lacks business legitimacy

Solution: Work only with verified Canadian employers. Provide detailed company documents if requested.

  1. Weak Family Ties or Immigration Intentions
    • IRCC is concerned you will not leave Canada after your permit expires
    • Your family is entirely in Canada, or you lack proof of ties to your home country

Solution: Include proof of property, family, or employment in your home country; a signed declaration of intent to return; and evidence of financial and social ties.

  1. Insufficient Work Experience or Skills
    • You do not meet the qualifications required for the TEER level of the job

Solution: Provide clear work experience letters, reference letters, or relevant certifications aligned with the job duties.

  1. Incomplete or Incorrect Application
    • Missing documents, unsigned forms, poor-quality scans, or wrong answers

Solution: Follow IRCC’s document checklist precisely. Review your application multiple times before submitting.

  1. Failure to Respond to IRCC Requests
    • IRCC asks for clarification or additional information and you do not respond in time

Solution: Check your IRCC account frequently and respond immediately to any requests.

  1. Medical or Criminal Inadmissibility
    • You fail the medical exam or have a past criminal record

Solution: Be honest in your application. If you have past issues, provide upfront disclosure and legal documents where needed.

Can I Bring My Family on a Work Permit?

Yes. You can include your immediate family:

    • Spouse or common-law partner: May apply for an open Canada LMIA Work Permit
    • Dependent children: Can apply for study permits or visitor records

Make sure to apply for their permits at the same time or after yours is approved.

Can I Apply for Permanent Residency Later?

Yes, and this is often the long-term goal for many foreign workers. Once you gain Canadian work experience, you may qualify for PR through:

    • Express Entry (CEC stream)
    • Provincial Nominee Program (PNP)
    • Agri-Food Immigration Pilot, Atlantic Immigration Program, or RNIP
