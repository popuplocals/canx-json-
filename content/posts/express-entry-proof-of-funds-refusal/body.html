Proof of funds sounds simple: show that you have enough money to settle in Canada. In practice, it is one of the most common sources of Express Entry problems. Sudden deposits, borrowed money, gifts without documentation, funds held in non-standard institutions and bank letters missing required details can all lead an officer to conclude that your funds are not truly available to you.

This guide explains the legal requirement for settlement funds, who must show them, what counts and what does not and how to prepare a proof of funds package that holds up.

Quick Summary

Under subparagraph 76(1)(b)(i) of the IRPR, Federal Skilled Worker applicants must hold transferable and available funds, unencumbered by debts or other obligations, equal to half the minimum necessary income for their family size. Federal Skilled Trades applicants face a similar requirement under section 87.2. Canadian Experience Class applicants, and applicants with a valid job offer who are authorized to work in Canada, are exempt. Borrowed money and property equity do not count. Funds must be available when you apply and when your visa is issued.

Requirements for Bank Letters

According to IRCC, official letters from financial institutions must be printed on the institution’s letterhead and include:

  • The institution’s contact information.
  • Your name.
  • Outstanding debts, such as credit card debts and loans.
  • Account numbers, the date each account was opened, the current balance and the average balance for the past six months.

Common Proof of Funds Problems

Sudden large deposits

A large deposit shortly before applying can suggest borrowed money. Explain the source with documents, such as a property sale deed, a gift deed with the donor’s financial evidence or investment redemption records.

Undocumented gifts

Gifts from family can generally be used if they are genuine and the money is truly yours. Provide a gift deed, proof of the donor’s relationship and capacity to give and proof of transfer.

Borrowed funds

Loans do not count, and funds encumbered by debts or obligations do not meet section 76. Presenting borrowed money as your own can lead to a misrepresentation finding.

Funds in non-standard institutions

Officers may question funds held in microfinance or unfamiliar institutions. Applicants on public forums report refusals and reconsideration requests involving microfinance funds. Holding funds in a well-established bank with clear statements reduces risk.

Missing six-month history

Letters showing only current balances, without six-month averages and account opening dates, may be considered insufficient.

Funds that drop before visa issuance

Funds must remain available when your visa is issued, not just when you apply.

How to Prepare a Strong Proof of Funds Package

  • Keep settlement funds in a recognized bank for at least six months, where possible.
  • Request official letters with every element IRCC lists.
  • Document the source of any large deposits.
  • Use gift deeds and donor evidence for gifted funds.
  • Show any debts, as required, and make sure remaining funds still meet the threshold.
  • Recheck IRCC’s current amounts before submitting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who needs proof of funds for Express Entry?

Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades applicants generally need proof of funds, unless they have a valid job offer and are authorized to work in Canada. Canadian Experience Class applicants are exempt. The legal requirement for Federal Skilled Workers is in subparagraph 76(1)(b)(i) of the IRPR.

Can I use gifted money for Express Entry proof of funds?

Generally, yes, if the gift is genuine and the money is truly yours. Provide a gift deed, evidence of the donor’s relationship and financial capacity and proof of the transfer into your account. Borrowed money presented as a gift can lead to refusal and a possible misrepresentation finding.

Can borrowed money count as proof of funds?

No. IRCC does not accept borrowed money, and section 76 of the IRPR requires funds that are unencumbered by debts or other obligations. Presenting a loan as your own savings can lead to a misrepresentation finding under section 40 of IRPA and a five-year inadmissibility.

What must an Express Entry bank letter include?

According to IRCC, official bank letters must be on the institution’s letterhead and include its contact information, your name, outstanding debts such as credit cards and loans, account numbers, account opening dates, current balances and the average balance for the past six months.
