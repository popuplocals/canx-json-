Under Canada’s Express Entry Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS), you can receive 15 additional CRS points if you or your accompanying spouse/common-law partner have a brother or sister living in Canada who is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.

While the rule sounds simple, siblings points in express entry are one of the most commonly misclaimed CRS factors. Many applications are refused or reassessed because applicants misunderstand who qualifies as a sibling or fail to provide proper documentation.

This guide explains exactly how to claim the 15 CRS Siblings Points in Express Entry correctly, what documents IRCC requires, and how to avoid costly mistakes.

Who Can Claim 15 CRS Siblings Points in Express Entry?

You can answer YES to the sibling question in Express Entry only if all conditions below are met.

The sibling may belong to:

  • You, or
  • Your accompanying spouse or common-law partner

Your sibling must:

  • Be 18 years of age or older
  • Be a Canadian citizen or Permanent Resident
  • Be currently living in Canada
  • Be related to you by blood, adoption, or as a step-sibling
  • Share at least one parent with you or your spouse

If even one condition is not met, the correct answer is NO, and you must not claim the 15 CRS Siblings Points in Express Entry

Who Is Considered a “Sibling” for CRS Purposes?

IRCC recognizes only the following as siblings:

  • Biological brother or sister
  • Adopted brother or sister
  • Step-brother or step-sister (through parents’ marriage)

Critical rule:
The relationship must involve at least one shared parent.

Relatives That Do NOT Qualify for CRS Sibling Points

The following relatives cannot be used to claim the 15 CRS points:

  • Cousins
  • Brother-in-law or sister-in-law
  • Parents or grandparents
  • Children or grandchildren
  • Aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews

These relatives may still be relevant under the Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) Adaptability factor, but they do not provide CRS Siblings Points in Express Entry.

How to Answer the Sibling Question Correctly

  • Answer YES only if:
    • All requirements are met and
    • You can provide documentary proof if requested
  • Answer NO if:
    • The sibling does not live in Canada
    • The sibling is not a PR or citizen
    • There is no shared parent
    • Documents cannot reasonably be obtained

Claiming sibling points without proof is risky and often leads to CRS point removal or refusal.

Documents Required to Claim Sibling Points (3 Mandatory Categories)

Claiming sibling points is a three-part proof requirement:

  1. Status (PR or citizenship)
  2. Residence (living in Canada)
  3. Relationship (shared parent)

All three must be satisfied.

Proof of PR or Citizenship of the Sibling

This confirms that your sibling is legally a Canadian PR or citizen.

Acceptable documents (any one):

  • PR card (front and back)
  • Confirmation of Permanent Residence (IMM 5292 / IMM 5688)
  • Record of Landing (IMM 1000)
  • Canadian Citizenship Certificate
  • Canadian passport (biographical page)

The document must clearly show:

  • Sibling’s full name
  • PR or citizenship status

If the PR card is expired

An expired PR card can still be used as proof of status. To strengthen the file, include:

  • Proof of PR card renewal application, and/or
  • A sworn affidavit from the sibling confirming PR status and renewal plans

Proof That the Sibling Is Living in Canada

IRCC awards sibling points only if the sibling is physically residing in Canada at the time of e-APR submission.

Status alone is not enough.

Best practice

  • Upload at least two documents
  • Use documents from different sources
  • Prefer documents issued within 3 months

Acceptable documents include:

Government ID

  • Canadian driver’s licence
  • Provincial ID

Housing

  • Utility bills
  • Lease agreement or mortgage statement

Financial

  • Bank or credit card statements with a Canadian address

Tax

  • CRA Notice of Assessment
  • T4

Employment

  • Employment letter
  • Recent pay stubs

If your sibling is living outside Canada, even temporarily, you cannot claim sibling points.

Proof of Sibling Relationship (Shared Parent)

This is used to prove that you and your sibling share at least one parent.

Acceptable documents:

  • Birth certificates of both Siblings Points inEexpress Entry listing the same parent(s)
  • School records or passports listing parents’ names
  • Legal adoption documents
  • Marriage certificate of parents (for step-siblings)

IRCC must be able to clearly trace a parent–child link.

Common Scenarios & How to Handle Them

No birth certificate available

You may submit:

  • School certificates
  • Passports showing parents’ names
  • Notarized affidavits

Always include a Letter of Explanation (LoE).

Parents’ names spelled differently

Provide:

  • Affidavit explaining the variation
  • Marriage certificate or name-change document

Minor spelling differences are usually accepted when explained properly.

No documentary proof at all (rare cases)

  • Submit a detailed affidavit
  • Explain why documents are unavailable
  • State willingness to undergo DNA testing if IRCC requests it

 Do not submit DNA results unless IRCC specifically asks.

What Happens If You Claimed Siblings Points in Express Entry but Can’t Prove Them?

At the e-APR stage, IRCC reassesses all CRS points.

If proof is missing, IRCC may:

  • Remove the 15 CRS Siblings Points in Express Entry
  • Recalculate your score

If your revised score falls below the draw cut-off, your application may be refused.

This is a technical reassessment, not a penalty — but the result is the same.

What NOT to Do

  • Do not upload unrelated or altered documents
  • Do not stay silent about missing proof
  • Do not assume IRCC will “understand”

These actions can raise misrepresentation concerns.

Correct & Safe Approach If Documents Are Unavailable

If Siblings Points in Express Entry documents cannot be obtained:

  • Disclose the issue clearly in a Letter of Explanation
  • Explain why documents are unavailable
  • Ask IRCC to reassess your application without sibling points

Transparency protects you. Silence does not.

Strategic Question Before Submitting Your e-APR

Ask yourself:

Would my CRS score still meet the draw cut-off if the 15 sibling points were removed?

  • YES → Proceed with disclosure
  • NO → Decline the ITA and resubmit without sibling points

Final Takeaway

Claiming 15 CRS points for a Canadian sibling can significantly improve your Express Entry score — but only if claimed correctly.

Remember:

  • Relationship alone is not enough
  • Residence in Canada is mandatory
  • Documentation must support status, residence, and shared parentage

Mistakes in sibling points are one of the top causes of Express Entry refusals.
