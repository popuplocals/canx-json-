Saskatchewan has 4,761 initial nomination spaces for 2026, and it has decided exactly how it wants to use them. At least half go to priority sectors such as health care, agriculture, skilled trades, energy, mining, manufacturing and technology. Hospitality, trucking and retail now face firm caps and scheduled intake windows. For many applicants, the Saskatchewan PNP in 2026 works very differently from the program they heard about a year ago.

This guide explains how the Saskatchewan Immigrant Nominee Program (SINP) works in 2026, which streams are available, how the sector system affects your chances, what changed for international students, and how to apply.

Quick Summary

Saskatchewan has 4,761 initial nominations in 2026. At least 50% go to priority sectors with year-round intake. Capped sectors, including accommodation and food services, trucking and retail, receive up to 25% and accept applications only in scheduled windows. Main pathways include International Skilled Worker (Employment Offer, Occupation In-Demand and Saskatchewan Express Entry), Saskatchewan Experience and business streams. Points-based categories require at least 60 out of 110 points.

Within the capped group, Saskatchewan set specific limits: about 714 nominations for accommodation and food services, 238 for trucking and 238 for retail. Saskatchewan has warned that changing or misrepresenting an industry code to avoid a cap is a program integrity violation.

The Main SINP Streams in 2026

International Skilled Worker: Employment Offer

For skilled workers with a job offer from a Saskatchewan employer that holds an approved Job Approval Letter. This category uses the SINP points grid.

International Skilled Worker: Occupation In-Demand

For skilled workers without a Saskatchewan job offer who have experience in an eligible in-demand occupation. Candidates submit an Expression of Interest (EOI) and wait for an invitation.

International Skilled Worker: Saskatchewan Express Entry

For candidates in the federal Express Entry pool with experience in an eligible occupation. Candidates submit an EOI to Saskatchewan. A nomination adds 600 points to your Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score.

Saskatchewan Experience

For people already living and working in Saskatchewan, including categories for existing work permit holders, health professionals and other specific groups. Requirements vary by category.

Entrepreneur and Farm categories

Business pathways for entrepreneurs and experienced farmers who plan to establish or buy and actively manage a business or farm in Saskatchewan. These categories have their own net worth, investment and business plan requirements.

The SINP Points Grid: 60 Out of 110

Several categories use Saskatchewan’s points assessment grid. You need at least 60 out of 110 points. Human capital factors, such as education, skilled work experience, language ability and age, account for up to 70 points. Connection to Saskatchewan, such as a job offer, close family in the province, or past work or study in Saskatchewan, accounts for up to 30 points. A master’s degree or PhD earns 23 points for education, and a bachelor’s degree earns 20.

Meeting 60 points makes you eligible, but it does not guarantee an invitation. Saskatchewan invites the candidates who best match its priorities in each draw.

Important Changes for International Students

Saskatchewan tightened its rules for graduates in 2026. Student-based eligibility now generally applies only to graduates of Saskatchewan designated learning institutions who lived in the province during their studies and gained work experience related to their field of study in Saskatchewan. Graduates who studied outside Saskatchewan no longer qualify under the student pathway.

Step-by-Step: How to Apply to the SINP

Step 1: Identify your category and sector

Determine which SINP category fits your situation and whether your occupation falls into a priority, capped or other sector.

Step 2: Check your points

For points-based categories, calculate your score on the SINP grid and confirm you reach at least 60.

Step 3: Submit an EOI or employer-supported application

Some categories require an EOI and an invitation. Others depend on an employer’s Job Approval Letter and your application.

Step 4: Apply within your deadline

If invited, submit your full application and pay the $350 application fee.

Step 5: Apply to IRCC for permanent residence

After nomination, apply for permanent residence through Express Entry or a separate IRCC application, depending on your stream.

Tips for Saskatchewan Applicants in 2026

  • Check whether your occupation is in a priority sector, because those applicants enjoy year-round intake.
  • If you work in a capped sector, track intake windows and your work permit expiry date closely.
  • Strengthen your language scores to improve your points.
  • Make sure your employer’s industry classification is accurate.
  • Keep your Express Entry profile active if you qualify.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many nominations does Saskatchewan have in 2026?

Saskatchewan received 4,761 initial nominations for 2026. At least half are reserved for priority sectors such as health care, agriculture, skilled trades, energy, mining, manufacturing and technology. Capped sectors, including hospitality, trucking and retail, can receive up to 25%, and other sectors up to 25%.

What is the minimum score for SINP?

Points-based SINP categories require at least 60 out of 110 points on Saskatchewan’s assessment grid. Human capital factors provide up to 70 points and connection to Saskatchewan up to 30 points. Reaching 60 makes you eligible, but Saskatchewan invites candidates based on its priorities, so a higher score helps.

Can I apply to Saskatchewan PNP without a job offer?

Yes, in some categories. The Occupation In-Demand and Saskatchewan Express Entry categories do not require a Saskatchewan job offer, although you need experience in an eligible occupation and must receive an invitation. Other categories, such as Employment Offer, require a job offer from an employer with an approved Job Approval Letter.

Can international graduates from other provinces apply to SINP?

Under the 2026 changes, student-based eligibility generally applies only to graduates of Saskatchewan designated learning institutions who lived in Saskatchewan during their studies and gained related work experience in the province. Graduates who studied elsewhere should check whether they qualify under another SINP category.
