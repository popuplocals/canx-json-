Of all the requirements in an LMIA application, the recruitment requirement generates the most misunderstanding, the most errors, and the most refusals. Part of the reason is that the formal rules, read on their own, sound simple: advertise for four weeks, document the outcomes, explain why Canadians were not hired. What the formal rules do not convey is that ESDC officers are assessing not just whether the employer followed the technical steps, but whether those steps were conducted with genuine intent to find a Canadian worker.

The distinction matters enormously in practice. An employer who ran a real, open, genuine recruitment campaign and truly could not find a qualified Canadian has a strong LMIA application even if some technical aspects of the advertising were imperfect. An employer who ran technically compliant advertising with no genuine intention of hiring any Canadian who applied has a fragile LMIA application even if every box was checked. Officers know the difference, because they see both types of files every week.

This article explains what genuine recruitment looks like in 2026, from the mandatory platforms and duration requirements to the content standards for job postings to the documentation practices that distinguish authentic efforts from performative ones. It also covers the officer assessment lens so that employers can build recruitment campaigns that satisfy both the letter and the spirit of ESDC’s requirements.

The Legal Basis for the Recruitment Requirement

The requirement for employers to demonstrate genuine recruitment efforts before hiring a foreign worker is grounded in the TFWP’s core policy mandate: Canadian citizens and permanent residents must have priority access to available jobs in Canada. The LMIA exists specifically to enforce this priority by requiring employers to prove, with documented evidence, that they could not find a qualified Canadian or PR for the position before turning to a foreign worker.

ESDC’s operational guidance on recruitment requirements draws on the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations and on published employer guidelines, which are updated periodically. Employers should check the current ESDC guidance on recruitment requirements at Canada.ca at the time of their application, because specific platform requirements and advertising content standards have been updated in recent years and may continue to evolve.

Mandatory Advertising Platform 1: The Government of Canada’s Job Bank

Job Bank (jobbank.gc.ca) is the Government of Canada’s free national job board and the only platform ESDC designates as mandatory for every LMIA application in standard TFWP streams. Posting on Job Bank is not optional, regardless of how active or appropriate other platforms may be for the specific occupation.

To post on Job Bank, employers must create a free account at jobbank.gc.ca/employer. The job posting process is relatively straightforward, and Job Bank postings are indexed by occupation and location, making them searchable by Canadian job seekers across the country. One practical advantage of Job Bank postings is that ESDC officers have direct access to verify that a posting existed and when it was live, reducing potential credibility concerns that can arise with external platform postings.

What Must Be in the Job Bank Posting

The Job Bank posting must contain the same core information as all other postings in the recruitment campaign: job title, location, wage or wage range, required qualifications, duties, and application instructions. Postings that list the wage as ‘to be negotiated’ or ‘competitive’ without providing any numerical range have been questioned by ESDC officers as inadequate, because they may discourage qualified Canadian applicants who cannot determine whether the wage is appropriate for their experience level.

The posting should remain active for the entirety of the minimum four-week advertising period. Taking the posting down before the minimum period is complete is an error that will appear in the documentation, because officers look at both the posting start date and end date when reviewing Job Bank evidence.

Additional Platform Requirements: Minimum Two Other Platforms

Beyond Job Bank, ESDC requires advertising on at least two additional platforms. The choice of these platforms is not arbitrary. Officers expect the additional platforms to be appropriate to the occupation, the industry, and the target population of qualified Canadian workers. Choosing platforms that are unlikely to reach qualified candidates, whether because they serve a different demographic or because they are not used by professionals in the relevant field, signals either carelessness or deliberate misdirection.

Choosing the Right Platforms for Your Occupation

General Professional and Skilled Worker Positions

For positions that attract a broad range of skilled workers, such as administrative roles, management positions, and general professional positions, widely used job boards such as Indeed Canada, LinkedIn, Monster Canada, or Workopolis are appropriate additional platforms. These platforms are well-known, actively used by Canadian job seekers, and easily documented for ESDC submission.

Technology and IT Positions

For technology positions, tech-specific platforms are more appropriate than general job boards and present a stronger application. Platforms such as Stack Overflow Jobs, GitHub Jobs (where active), Dice, and LinkedIn with tech-specific targeting are relevant. For Global Talent Stream applications, ESDC’s own in-demand occupation list creates a context where the sophistication of the recruitment effort is more closely scrutinized.

Healthcare and Regulated Professional Positions

Healthcare positions should be advertised through sector-specific channels. Provincial nursing or physician association job boards, hospital network job sites, and professional association publications are appropriate. For physician positions, advertising through the Canadian Medical Association or the provincial medical college’s employment resources demonstrates sector-appropriate recruitment.

Skilled Trades Positions

Trades positions should include advertising through the relevant trades associations or union halls if applicable, provincial apprenticeship boards, and trades-specific job platforms. For positions in provinces where trades are regulated, advertising through the provincial apprenticeship authority’s job posting service is both appropriate and demonstrates awareness of the regulated nature of the occupation.

Agricultural Positions

For agricultural positions under the standard Agricultural stream (not SAWP), advertising should include sector-specific channels such as provincial agricultural associations, rural employment centers, and agriculture-focused job boards. These are more appropriate than general urban-centric job boards for positions on farms or in food processing facilities.

How Many Additional Platforms Is Enough?

ESDC requires a minimum of two additional platforms beyond Job Bank. However, more advertising is generally stronger. For positions in sectors with documented labour shortages, or for applications where the employer anticipates that ESDC may scrutinize the recruitment effort closely, advertising on three or four additional platforms provides a more compelling body of evidence. The incremental cost of additional job board postings is modest compared to the cost of a refusal and reapplication.

Duration Requirements: Four Consecutive Weeks Minimum

The minimum recruitment period is four consecutive weeks. All postings must run for this entire period without gaps. If a posting expires before the four-week minimum is complete and is not renewed immediately, the gap counts against the employer’s documented recruitment period.

The four consecutive weeks must be completed before the LMIA application is submitted. An application submitted while recruitment is still in progress, or submitted based on a recruitment period that ended many months ago, does not satisfy this requirement. ESDC wants to see a recruitment effort that reflects current labour market conditions, not historical ones.

The Recency Requirement: How Old Is Too Old?

ESDC has not published a precise maximum age for recruitment evidence, but the general practice in the field is that recruitment efforts completed within the three to four months immediately preceding the LMIA submission are generally considered current, while evidence from six months or more before submission may be questioned as stale. The underlying policy rationale is that labour market conditions can change over time, and evidence that was accurate several months ago may not accurately represent today’s market.

For employers who conducted recruitment, found no suitable Canadian applicants, and are now submitting an LMIA several months later for operational or administrative reasons, maintaining some form of ongoing recruitment activity during the intervening period, and documenting it, strengthens the position considerably.

Content Requirements: What Must Be in Every Job Posting

ESDC specifies minimum content requirements for job postings. An ad that does not meet these requirements does not satisfy the advertising requirement even if it ran on the correct platforms for the correct duration.

Required Content Elements

  • Job title: Must be the actual title for the position, consistent with the NOC code description. Employers should not invent creative titles that obscure the nature of the role.
  • Job duties: A meaningful description of the primary responsibilities of the position. Not a one-line summary, but enough detail that a qualified candidate could assess whether they meet the requirements.
  • Wage or wage range: A numerical wage figure or range is expected. ‘Competitive salary’ or ‘to be discussed’ without any numerical context has been treated by officers as an insufficient disclosure that may have discouraged qualified applicants.
  • Location of work: The specific city and province where the work will be performed. For remote positions, clarity on whether the role is genuinely fully remote or requires periodic presence at a specific location.
  • Required qualifications: Education, experience, certifications, or other requirements that are genuinely necessary for the position. Requirements that are unrealistically specific or that serve primarily to narrow the pool to a single pre-selected candidate will be questioned.
  • Contact information: How to apply, including email address, website, or mailing address as appropriate for the platform.

Language Requirements in Postings

If a job posting requires fluency in a language other than English or French, ESDC will question whether this is a genuine bona fide occupational requirement or an unjustified restriction. Language requirements must be directly related to the nature of the work. A customer service position serving a French-speaking market may legitimately require French fluency. The same reasoning does not apply to administrative positions where no client interaction occurs.

Requirements for fluency in foreign languages, such as Mandarin, Tagalog, or Hindi, are particularly scrutinized by ESDC officers because they can effectively exclude most Canadian applicants while appearing to be facially neutral requirements. If the position genuinely requires a foreign language skill, the employer must be prepared to explain and document why that skill is essential to the performance of the job duties.

What Constitutes a Genuine Recruitment Effort: The Officer’s Assessment Lens

The written rules tell an employer what they must do. Understanding how officers assess whether those steps were done genuinely tells an employer what they must do well.

Reviewing Each Application Individually

Officers expect employers to review every application from a qualified Canadian or PR individually and apply the stated job requirements consistently. If the job posting requires five years of experience, an applicant with four years who is otherwise well-qualified should receive meaningful consideration or a clear explanation of why the experience gap was disqualifying given the specific nature of the role. If the job posting requires a specific certification and an applicant has an equivalent certification from a different body, the employer should address whether that equivalent was considered and why it was or was not found sufficient.

The review of Canadian applications must be genuine. Officers are alert to situations where the documented review appears to have been completed very quickly, where all decisions were made on the same day without realistic time for individual assessment, or where the rejection reasons are so uniform that they appear copied and pasted across applicants.

Proportionality of Rejection Reasons to Position Requirements

Rejection reasons must be proportionate to the requirements stated in the job posting. If the posting required a Bachelor’s degree and an applicant holds a diploma, rejecting them solely for lacking the degree is reasonable. If the posting required experience with a specific software platform and an applicant has extensive experience with a directly comparable platform, rejecting them solely because they do not have experience with the specific platform named in the posting will invite questions about whether the requirement was genuine or constructed to exclude Canadian applicants.

The ‘Designed to Fail’ Pattern

One of the clearest warning signs officers look for is what might be called the designed-to-fail recruitment. The pattern usually looks something like this: the job posting runs on generic platforms unlikely to reach the target professional population, the posting contains requirements so specific that they describe only the pre-selected foreign worker’s exact profile, or the wage offered is at the very bottom of the acceptable range in a market where competitive wages are significantly higher. Canadian applicants who do apply are rejected rapidly with vague reasons, and the employer submits the LMIA application citing inability to find qualified Canadians.

Officers recognize this pattern because it appears with some regularity. Applications that fit this profile face significantly greater scrutiny, and in many cases receive a negative determination based on a finding that the recruitment effort was not genuine. The consequences of a finding of misrepresentation in an LMIA application extend beyond the immediate refusal and can include employer bans and referral to ESDC’s enforcement division.

Documenting Recruitment: The Record-Keeping Obligations

Documentation of the recruitment effort is not just a submission requirement. It is a compliance requirement that persists after the LMIA is approved. ESDC inspectors conducting employer audits will ask to see recruitment records from previous LMIA applications, and employers must be able to produce them.

Maintain complete records of all recruitment activity including all job postings across all platforms with dates, all applications received organized chronologically, all correspondence with applicants including invitation to interview and rejection communications, and all interview records and assessment notes. These records should be retained for at least six years from the date of the LMIA issuance.

Digital Documentation Best Practices

For online job postings, take screenshots of the posting on each platform at the time it is live, capturing the full posting content, the platform name in the URL bar, and the posting date where it is visible on the page. Save these screenshots as dated PDF files with the platform name in the filename. Back up the files in a format that will remain accessible for the six-year retention period.

For recruitment logs, use a structured format with consistent fields for each applicant entry. Spreadsheet-based logs that can be printed and exported are practical for both submission and retention. Email correspondence with applicants should be archived in a designated folder that is backed up regularly.

Occupation-Specific Recruitment Considerations

When Standard Platforms Are Not Sufficient

For highly specialized occupations, particularly those in science, technology, or specialized healthcare, advertising only on general job boards is unlikely to reach the relevant candidate pool and may be viewed by ESDC officers as a deliberate avoidance of effective recruitment. Employers should think carefully about where qualified Canadian candidates in their target occupation actually look for jobs and ensure their advertising appears in those channels.

Using Recruitment Agencies as Part of Genuine Effort

Engaging a professional recruitment agency to assist with the search for qualified Canadians can be part of a documented genuine recruitment effort. The key is that the engagement must be genuine: the agency must have been given a legitimate brief, must have conducted a real search, and must be able to provide documentation of their efforts. Using a recruitment agency engagement as a paper formality without actual search activity does not constitute genuine recruitment.

Recent Changes to Advertising Requirements in 2026

ESDC has continued to refine its recruitment requirement guidance in response to patterns of non-genuine recruitment that have emerged over time. The emphasis on sector-appropriate advertising platforms has increased, and officers have been given additional guidance on identifying performative recruitment patterns. Employers who were accustomed to a more lenient standard for recruitment evidence in earlier years may find that their previous approach no longer satisfies current officer expectations.

There is also increased scrutiny on the connection between the wage offered in advertising and the wage being paid to comparable Canadian employees. If the recruitment advertising offered a wage significantly below what the employer pays its Canadian staff in similar roles, officers will question both the legitimacy of the wage offer and whether the advertised position was genuinely designed to attract qualified Canadian applicants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where must I advertise before applying for an LMIA?

You must advertise on the Government of Canada’s Job Bank (jobbank.gc.ca) plus at least two additional platforms appropriate to the occupation and industry. For most standard positions, acceptable additional platforms include major job boards such as Indeed Canada, LinkedIn, and Monster Canada. For specialized occupations, sector-specific platforms such as professional association job boards, trades association boards, or healthcare-specific sites are expected in addition to or instead of general boards. ESDC expects the additional platforms to be genuinely likely to reach qualified Canadian candidates in the target profession.

How long do I need to advertise a job for an LMIA?

The minimum advertising period is four consecutive weeks. All postings must remain active for this full period. The advertising must be recent, typically completed within three to four months before the LMIA application is submitted. Older advertising evidence may be questioned by ESDC officers as not reflecting current labour market conditions. Simply meeting the four-week minimum is the floor; stronger applications include longer recruitment periods and more platforms.

Can online job boards like Indeed count for LMIA advertising?

Yes, general online job boards such as Indeed Canada, LinkedIn, Monster Canada, and Workopolis are accepted as additional platforms for most standard TFWP positions. However, for specialized or professional positions, ESDC expects these general boards to be supplemented with or replaced by sector-specific platforms more likely to reach qualified candidates in the relevant field. The appropriateness of the advertising platforms to the occupation is an element of the officer’s assessment of whether the recruitment effort was genuine.

Can I specify language requirements other than English or French in my job posting?

Requirements for languages other than English or French in job postings will be scrutinized by ESDC officers as potential unjustified restrictions that may have narrowed the candidate pool inappropriately. Such requirements are only accepted where the employer can demonstrate that the language skill is a genuine bona fide occupational requirement directly tied to the performance of specific job duties, such as serving a client base that communicates in a specific language. Employers should be prepared to defend any non-English, non-French language requirement with clear, documented evidence of operational necessity.

What happens if my recruitment effort did not attract any qualified Canadian applicants?

If the documented recruitment effort was genuine and no qualified Canadian or PR applied for the position or was suitable for hire, this outcome supports the LMIA application. Document every application received and every outcome clearly, including applications from individuals who were clearly unqualified. If no applications were received at all, document the advertising effort thoroughly and consider whether this reflects a genuine market shortage or whether the advertising was insufficient in reach or attractiveness. ESDC officers will consider both possibilities when reviewing an application where no Canadian applicants were found.
