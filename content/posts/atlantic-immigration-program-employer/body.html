For employers in New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador, the Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP) offers something rare in Canadian immigration: a way to hire international talent and support them to permanent residence without a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). In exchange, the program asks employers to do more than hire. They must become designated, support settlement and help newcomers stay.

This guide explains the AIP from the employer’s side: how designation works, what a valid job offer looks like, how endorsement and settlement planning fit together, what compliance obligations you take on, and how to build a hiring plan that delivers retention and return on investment.

Quick Summary

The AIP is an employer-driven permanent residence program for the four Atlantic provinces. Employers must be designated by their province before hiring through the program. Designated employers offer full-time, non-seasonal jobs, connect candidates with a settlement service provider for a settlement plan, and request provincial endorsement. The program does not require an LMIA. Each province manages designation, endorsement and its own allocations and priorities, which can change during the year.

Why Employers Use the AIP

  • No LMIA requirement for AIP job offers, which saves time and recruitment advertising costs.
  • A direct route to permanent residence for your employee, which strongly supports retention.
  • Access to candidates already in Canada, including international graduates of Atlantic institutions, and candidates abroad.
  • Settlement support built into the process, which helps families integrate and stay in the region.

Step 1: Become a Designated Employer

Before you can make an AIP job offer, your province must designate your business. Designation is free. Each province sets its own process, but employers generally must:

  • Operate a legitimate business in good standing in the Atlantic province.
  • Demonstrate a genuine need to fill the position.
  • Complete required intercultural competency training.
  • Commit to supporting the newcomer’s settlement and integration, including working with settlement service providers.
  • Comply with provincial employment standards, workplace safety and immigration rules.

Provinces can refuse, suspend or revoke designation if an employer fails to meet program obligations.

Step 2: Make a Qualifying Job Offer

AIP job offers must be full-time and non-seasonal, generally at least 30 hours per week. The offer’s duration depends on the occupation’s skill level:

Occupation levelJob offer requirement
TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3Full-time, non-seasonal, lasting at least one year from the date of permanent residence
TEER 4Full-time, non-seasonal and indeterminate (permanent)

You must pay at least the prevailing wage for the occupation in your region, and the job must match the candidate’s experience where the program requires it.

Step 3: Confirm the Candidate Meets Requirements

The candidate must meet federal AIP criteria, which IRCC sets. Key requirements include:

  • At least one year (1,560 hours) of work experience in the past five years, in an occupation related to the job offer, unless the candidate qualifies as an eligible international graduate of an Atlantic institution.
  • Language ability of CLB/NCLC 5 for TEER 0 to 3 jobs, or CLB/NCLC 4 for TEER 4 jobs.
  • Education that meets the program’s level for the occupation, with an Educational Credential Assessment for foreign credentials.
  • Proof of settlement funds, unless the candidate is already living and working in Canada with a valid work permit.

Step 4: Arrange the Settlement Plan

Each candidate and accompanying family member works with a designated settlement service provider organization to complete a needs assessment and receive a settlement plan. The plan covers practical needs such as housing, language training, employment support and community connections. Encourage your new hire to start this step early, because it is required before endorsement.

Step 5: Request Provincial Endorsement

Once you have a valid job offer and the candidate has a settlement plan, you apply to your province for endorsement. If the province endorses the job offer, it issues an endorsement certificate. Provincial endorsement timelines vary.

Step 6: The Candidate Applies for PR, and Possibly a Work Permit

With the endorsement certificate, the candidate applies to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) for permanent residence. If you need the candidate to start working sooner, the candidate may be eligible for an employer-specific work permit supported by the province while IRCC processes the PR application.

Compliance: Your Ongoing Obligations

Designation brings responsibility. Employers should keep accurate records, meet every commitment in the job offer and support settlement. In July 2026, IRCC updated its guidance for situations where a designated employer changes ownership and loses designation. In those cases, the applicant must notify IRCC promptly and, if the province continues its support, provide updated endorsement documents within the required timeframe. Plan ahead for ownership changes, restructuring or relocations so your employees are not caught off guard.

Provincial Differences in 2026

Each Atlantic province manages designation, endorsement volumes and priorities independently, and allocations can be limited. Some provinces focus on specific sectors such as health care, construction and manufacturing, while others prioritize candidates already working in the province. Before you recruit, confirm your province’s current AIP intake, priorities and any limits.

Building an AIP Hiring Strategy That Delivers ROI

  • Map your hard-to-fill roles and check TEER levels and job offer rules for each.
  • Budget time for designation, settlement planning and endorsement before the candidate’s start date.
  • Prioritize candidates who match your community’s lifestyle and want to stay long term.
  • Partner early with local settlement agencies, housing resources and language programs.
  • Keep a compliance checklist for every AIP hire.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does an employer get designated for the Atlantic Immigration Program?

Employers apply to their provincial government for designation, which is free. Requirements vary by province but usually include operating a business in good standing, showing a genuine need for the position, completing intercultural competency training and committing to support the newcomer’s settlement. Provinces can suspend or revoke designation for non-compliance.

Do AIP employers need an LMIA?

No. Job offers made through the Atlantic Immigration Program do not require a Labour Market Impact Assessment. Instead, the employer must be designated by the province, make a qualifying full-time, non-seasonal job offer and obtain provincial endorsement after the candidate receives a settlement plan.

What job offers qualify for the Atlantic Immigration Program?

Job offers must be full-time and non-seasonal. For TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupations, the job must last at least one year from the date the candidate becomes a permanent resident. For TEER 4 occupations, the job must be indeterminate, meaning permanent. Wages must meet the prevailing rate for the occupation and region.

Can an AIP candidate start working before getting permanent residence?

Often, yes. With a provincial endorsement, the candidate may be eligible for an employer-specific work permit supported by the province while IRCC processes the permanent residence application. The candidate remains responsible for keeping their work authorization valid, especially if the employer’s situation changes.
