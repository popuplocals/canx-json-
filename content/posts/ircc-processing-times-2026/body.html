Quick Summary

IRCC publishes estimated processing times for every major application type on its official tool. In late 2026, Express Entry permanent residence applications took roughly 6 to 7 months, Express Entry-linked provincial nominee applications about 7 months, base provincial nominee applications about 13 months, and family sponsorship anywhere from 18 to 28 months or more. These are estimates, not guarantees. A complete, accurate application remains the single biggest factor you control.

How IRCC Calculates Processing Times

Many applicants assume the posted number is a promise. It is not. IRCC states clearly that its estimates are neither a maximum nor a guarantee. The department uses two different methods depending on the application type.

Forward-looking estimates

For many programs, IRCC projects how long a new application will take based on the number of files already waiting, the number of new applications it expects, and the staff capacity assigned to that program. IRCC updates these estimates monthly for permanent residence and weekly for temporary residence. Forward-looking estimates also account for the time needed to collect biometrics.

Historical (backward-looking) estimates

For other programs, IRCC looks back and measures how long it took to finish 80% of applications over a recent period: the past six months for permanent residence, or the past 8 to 16 weeks for temporary residence. In plain language, 1 in 5 applicants waited longer than the posted figure.

When the clock starts

Processing time runs from the day IRCC receives your complete application until an officer makes a final decision. For paper applications, it starts when your complete application arrives at the IRCC processing office. For online applications, it starts on the day you submit. Time spent on an incomplete application that IRCC returns does not count, because you must start again.

Why Your Application May Take Longer Than the Average

Every application is unique, and several factors push individual files beyond the posted estimate:

  • Incomplete forms, missing signatures, or missing documents that trigger a request for more information.
  • Additional security or background checks, which can add months and rarely come with an explanation.
  • Medical exam results that require further testing or specialist reports.
  • Inconsistencies between your forms and your documents, such as different dates of employment.
  • Requests for interviews, police certificates from additional countries, or updated documents.
  • Changes in your circumstances, such as marriage, a new child or a new address, that IRCC must assess.
  • Program caps or high application volumes in a particular stream or visa office.

What You Can Do to Keep Your File Moving

You cannot control IRCC’s workload, but you can control the quality of what you submit. In our experience, the files that move smoothly share a few habits:

  • Submit a complete application the first time, using IRCC’s document checklist for your specific program.
  • Make every date, job title and address consistent across forms, reference letters and supporting documents.
  • Respond to every IRCC request quickly, ideally well before the deadline.
  • Keep your contact details and address current in your IRCC account.
  • Give biometrics and complete medical exams as soon as you receive instructions.
  • Keep copies of everything you send, with submission confirmations and courier tracking numbers.

What to Do If Your Application Is Past the Processing Time

First, check your status online and confirm the estimate for your exact application type. If your file has passed the posted processing time, you can contact IRCC through its official web form or call the IRCC Client Support Centre at 1-888-242-2100 (Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. local time, within Canada). You can also request your Global Case Management System (GCMS) notes to see exactly where your file stands.

For serious, prolonged delays far beyond the normal processing time, some applicants contact their Member of Parliament’s office or, after legal advice, apply to the Federal Court for an order called mandamus that compels IRCC to decide. These steps make sense only after you have exhausted simpler options.

Fees Changed in 2026, Too

Processing times are not the only moving target. On April 30, 2026, IRCC increased several permanent residence fees. The economic class processing fee for a principal applicant rose from $950 to $990, and the right of permanent residence fee rose from $575 to $600. Always check the current fee list before you pay, because underpaying can delay or stop your application.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is IRCC taking to process applications in 2026?

It depends on the application. In September 2026, IRCC estimated about 6 months for Canadian Experience Class applications, about 7 months for Federal Skilled Worker and Express Entry-linked provincial nominee applications, about 13 months for base provincial nominee applications, and 18 to 26 months for spousal sponsorship. Check IRCC’s official tool for the current figure.

Are IRCC processing times guaranteed?

No. IRCC states that its processing times are estimates, not a maximum or a guarantee. Many figures reflect how long it took to finish 80% of applications, which means some applicants wait longer. Security checks, medical follow-ups, missing documents and high volumes can all extend an individual file.

When does IRCC processing time start?

Processing time starts on the day IRCC receives your complete application. For online applications, that is usually the submission date. For paper applications, it is the date the application reaches the processing centre. If IRCC returns your application as incomplete, the clock resets when you submit a new, complete one.

What should I do if my application is past the IRCC processing time?

Check your application status online first, then contact IRCC through its web form or the Client Support Centre once your file exceeds the posted time. Requesting your GCMS notes can reveal what is happening inside your file. For long, unexplained delays, a regulated consultant or lawyer can assess further options.

How often does IRCC update processing times?

IRCC updates forward-looking estimates monthly for permanent residence applications and weekly for temporary residence applications. Historical estimates also refresh regularly. Because figures change, check the official tool on the day you plan major decisions such as travel, job changes or lease renewals.
