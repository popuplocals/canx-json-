Before March 2025, foreign workers on LMIA work permits had two routes through which that job offer could directly support their Express Entry PR pathway: the arranged employment CRS bonus points, and the PNP nomination. The first route no longer exists. As of March 25, 2025, IRCC eliminated all CRS bonus points for arranged employment, removing the direct Express Entry scoring advantage that LMIA job offers previously provided.

What this means in 2026 is that the PNP employer-driven nomination is not merely one strategy among several — it is now the primary mechanism through which an LMIA employer relationship translates into a competitive advantage in the PR system. And it is a powerful one. A PNP nomination delivers 600 CRS points, which is far more than the eliminated 50-point arranged employment bonus ever provided. In practical terms, 600 additional CRS points means that virtually any candidate who receives a provincial nomination will receive an ITA in the very next Express Entry draw following the nomination, regardless of their base score.

This shift has not reduced the value of an LMIA work permit for immigration purposes. If anything, it has increased the strategic premium on the genuine employment relationship that the LMIA represents — because that relationship is now the pathway, not the paperwork. An employer who values a foreign worker enough to obtain an LMIA, sponsor a work permit, and then support a PNP nomination is creating a PR outcome that the old CRS points model could not reliably guarantee for workers with lower base scores.

How PNPs Work in the Context of Express Entry

The Provincial Nominee Program and the federal Express Entry system interact through the enhanced PNP mechanism. When a province nominates a candidate through a stream aligned with Express Entry, the nomination adds 600 CRS points to the candidate’s profile. The candidate then receives an ITA in the next available Express Entry draw and applies for federal permanent residence through Express Entry.

This 600-point addition is the largest single CRS boost available to any Express Entry candidate. No other factor in the CRS scoring system — not a higher language test score, not additional Canadian work experience, not an educational credential upgrade — comes close to producing this magnitude of advantage. The PNP nomination is the single most powerful PR tool available to workers with genuine LMIA employer relationships in 2026.

Which PNP Streams Accept LMIA-Backed Job Offers

Most major provincial PNP programs have employer-driven streams that accept LMIA-backed permanent job offers as a core eligibility element. These streams allow employers with genuinely permanent job offers to nominate foreign workers directly for provincial nomination, bypassing the competitive Express Entry CRS threshold.

Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP)

Ontario’s Foreign Worker Stream within the OINP targets candidates who are already working in Ontario on a valid work permit with a permanent, full-time job offer from an Ontario employer. For candidates on LMIA work permits in Ontario, this stream is often the most direct PNP pathway. The position must be in an eligible NOC category and the candidate must meet minimum language and work experience requirements.

British Columbia Provincial Nominee Program (BC PNP)

BC’s Skills Immigration streams provide pathways for workers already employed in BC who hold a full-time, permanent job offer from a BC employer. The BC PNP Skilled Worker stream and related streams accept LMIA-backed job offers where they exist. BC also operates a Skills Immigration Ranking system that scores applicants using provincial criteria, with draws occurring at score thresholds that vary by occupational category.

Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP)

Alberta has maintained active employer-driven PNP nomination streams that allow Alberta employers to directly nominate foreign workers when a genuine, full-time, permanent job offer meets the stream’s requirements. Alberta’s significant labour shortages in energy, construction, healthcare, and technology have driven particularly active employer-driven draw activity. The AAIP also includes rural employer streams for operations outside Calgary and Edmonton.

Saskatchewan Immigrant Nominee Program (SINP)

Saskatchewan’s Employer Job Offer stream allows Saskatchewan employers to nominate workers with valid, full-time, permanent job offers in eligible occupations. The worker must have relevant experience in the offered occupation and must intend to settle permanently in Saskatchewan. Saskatchewan has historically maintained accessible PNP pathways for workers in trades, agriculture, and skilled occupations.

Manitoba Provincial Nominee Program (MPNP)

Manitoba’s Skilled Worker in Manitoba stream targets workers already employed in Manitoba by an employer offering permanent, ongoing employment. Manitoba has specific pathways for workers in childcare, trucking, manufacturing, and other provincial priority sectors.

Atlantic Immigration Program (AIP)

The Atlantic provinces operate the federal-provincial Atlantic Immigration Program, which allows designated employers in Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island, and Newfoundland and Labrador to hire and nominate foreign workers for permanent residence. The AIP bypasses Express Entry CRS competition entirely, making it particularly valuable in the post-arranged-employment-points environment.

What Workers Must Do to Leverage Their LMIA for PNP

Build Genuine Work Experience in the Province

Most employer-driven PNP streams require a minimum period of work experience in the province with the nominating employer, typically ranging from three months to one year. Workers should be aware of these thresholds and plan their PNP application timing accordingly. The experience must be in the occupation described in the LMIA and must be in the province where the nominating employer operates.

Maintain a Valid Work Permit Throughout the PNP Process

PNP applications can take several months to process. During this period, the worker must maintain valid work permit status. Workers whose permits are approaching expiry while a PNP application is pending should apply for renewal well in advance. If the PNP nomination is received while a renewal is pending with IRCC, the 600 CRS points are added and the ITA process proceeds, but a gap in valid status during the PNP process can create complications.

Ensure the Job Offer Meets Provincial Stream Requirements

Provincial PNP streams have specific requirements for the job offer that may differ from ESDC’s LMIA requirements. Common provincial requirements include: the position must be permanent rather than fixed-term, the position must be full-time, the wage must meet the provincial stream’s minimum threshold, and the position must be in a NOC category eligible for the specific stream. An LMIA job offer that does not meet the provincial stream’s requirements cannot support a PNP application even if it was valid for the LMIA itself.

Strengthen the Express Entry Profile in Parallel

While pursuing a PNP nomination, workers should maintain and improve their Express Entry profiles independently. Improve language test scores where possible, ensure the educational credential assessment is current and accurately claimed, and confirm the profile reflects all accumulated Canadian work experience. A strong human capital profile, combined with the 600-point PNP nomination, creates the most robust possible PR application. Without the arranged employment points, human capital factors carry more relative weight than they did before March 2025.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an LMIA help me get nominated by a province in 2026?

Yes. An LMIA-backed work permit is the foundation of one of the most reliable PNP pathways available in 2026. Most provincial employer-driven PNP streams require a genuine, full-time, permanent job offer from an employer in the province — and an employer who has already obtained an LMIA and hired the worker is exactly the profile these streams are designed for. The 600 CRS points from a provincial nomination are far more valuable than the arranged employment bonus that was eliminated in March 2025. Workers on LMIA permits with committed employers should prioritize the PNP route.

Does an LMIA job offer add CRS points anymore?

No. IRCC eliminated all arranged employment CRS points on March 25, 2025. Before this change, qualifying LMIA-backed job offers added 50 or 200 CRS points. Those points are gone. However, the LMIA still contributes to PR through Canadian work experience accumulation (up to 80 CRS points over three years), CEC eligibility after one year, and PNP employer-driven nominations (600 CRS points). The PNP nomination remains the most powerful boost available in the current system.

How many Express Entry points does a PNP nomination give?

A provincial nominee nomination adds 600 CRS points. This is the largest single CRS boost available in the Express Entry system. In practice, 600 points means that virtually any candidate who receives a provincial nomination will receive an Invitation to Apply in the very next available Express Entry draw, regardless of their base CRS score. This is why the PNP employer-driven nomination has become the primary PR strategy for LMIA work permit holders since the arranged employment bonus was eliminated.

How long must I work in a province before applying to its PNP?

Most provincial employer-driven PNP streams require a minimum of three to six months of work experience in the province with the nominating employer, though some streams require up to one year. Requirements vary by province and stream and are updated periodically. Workers should research the specific minimum experience requirements for the provincial stream they are targeting before beginning the application process. Always verify current requirements at the province’s official immigration website.

Can I apply to multiple PNPs at the same time?

You can express interest in multiple provincial PNP streams simultaneously, but you can only hold one active PNP nomination at a time. Once a province nominates you, you are expected to genuinely intend to settle and work in that province. Misrepresenting your intention to settle in a province while planning to move elsewhere immediately is treated seriously by IRCC. Workers should apply to PNP streams in provinces where they genuinely intend to build their lives.
