Most LMIA refusals do not happen because the job is unsuitable. They happen because the application is incomplete, the recruitment evidence is weak, or the business documents raise questions. Understanding the application process in detail, from the documents ESDC expects to the questions officers ask, is the best way to protect your $1,000 fee and your hiring timeline.

This walkthrough explains the LMIA application process for Canadian employers in 2026: what to prepare, how to submit, how to respond during processing, common refusal reasons and what happens after a decision.

Quick Summary

Prepare business legitimacy documents, a detailed job offer, proof of recruitment and wage information before applying. Submit through the LMIA Online Portal and pay $1,000 per position unless exempt. ESDC may contact you with questions; respond quickly and accurately. After a positive LMIA, give the worker the decision letter and job offer so they can apply for a work permit, usually within 6 months.

Phase 1: Before You Apply

Confirm your stream

Check whether your wage meets the high-wage threshold for your province or territory (median hourly wage plus 20%). If not, the position falls in the low-wage stream, with stricter rules.

Confirm location rules and caps

For low-wage positions, check that your work location is not in a census metropolitan area where ESDC refuses to process low-wage applications because unemployment is 6% or higher, and that you are within the 10% (or 20% for certain sectors) low-wage cap.

Run your recruitment

Advertise on Job Bank and through additional methods. High-wage positions require at least 4 consecutive weeks of advertising; low-wage positions require at least 8 consecutive weeks, within the 3 months before you apply. Keep at least one recruitment activity running until you receive a decision.

Phase 3: Submitting Through the LMIA Online Portal

Create or access your LMIA Online Portal account, complete the application, upload documents and pay the fee. Make sure every detail is consistent: job title, NOC code, wage, hours and location must match across the application, advertisements and job offer.

Phase 4: During Processing

An ESDC officer reviews your application and may contact you by phone or email to clarify details, such as recruitment results, wages or the genuineness of the job. Respond promptly and consistently. Avoid improvising answers; check your records before replying.

Phase 5: The Decision

Positive LMIA

You receive a confirmation letter and annex. Send a copy to the worker with the signed job offer. The worker then applies to IRCC for a work permit. An LMIA is generally valid for 6 months for the worker to apply.

Negative LMIA

ESDC explains the reasons. You can address them and submit a new application, which requires a new fee and often new recruitment.

Common Reasons for LMIA Refusals

  • Insufficient or non-compliant recruitment, such as ads that ran too briefly or lacked required details.
  • Wages below the prevailing wage.
  • Unclear business legitimacy or inability to pay wages.
  • Job duties inconsistent with the NOC code.
  • Rejecting qualified Canadian applicants without valid reasons.
  • Missing low-wage obligations or exceeding the cap.
  • Inconsistent information across documents.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents are needed for an LMIA application?

Employers typically need business legitimacy documents, a detailed job offer with NOC code, wages and duties, proof of recruitment, a summary of Canadian applicants, wage information, a transition plan for high-wage positions, details of low-wage obligations where applicable, and proof of the $1,000 fee payment unless exempt.

Can ESDC contact me during LMIA processing?

Yes. ESDC officers often contact employers by phone or email to clarify recruitment, wages, job duties or business details. Respond promptly, consistently and accurately, using your records. Inconsistent answers can raise doubts about the genuineness of the job offer and lead to a refusal.

What happens after a positive LMIA?

You receive a confirmation letter and annex. Send a copy to the worker with the signed job offer, and the worker applies to IRCC for a work permit. The LMIA is generally valid for 6 months for the worker to apply, so coordinate timing carefully.

Why are LMIA applications refused?

Common reasons include insufficient recruitment, wages below the prevailing wage, unclear business legitimacy, job duties that do not match the NOC code, rejecting qualified Canadian applicants without valid reasons, exceeding low-wage caps and inconsistent information. Careful preparation addresses most of these risks.
