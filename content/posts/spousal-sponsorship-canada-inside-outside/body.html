Quick Summary

Outland sponsorship is for applicants living outside Canada or who want flexibility to travel, and it comes with a right of appeal to the Immigration Appeal Division if refused. Inland sponsorship is for couples living together in Canada, and the sponsored spouse can usually get an open work permit while waiting, under a public policy extended through December 31, 2026. In September 2026, IRCC estimated about 18 months for outland and about 26 months for inland applications.

Inland Sponsorship: Pros and Cons

Advantages

  • The sponsored spouse can usually apply for an open work permit while waiting.
  • The couple can live together in Canada throughout processing.

Disadvantages

  • Longer estimated processing time in 2026.
  • The applicant must keep valid temporary status in Canada.
  • Travel outside Canada carries risk, and the applicant must live with the sponsor.
  • No appeal to the Immigration Appeal Division if refused.

Outland Sponsorship: Pros and Cons

Advantages

  • Shorter estimated processing time in 2026.
  • The applicant can travel freely, subject to visa rules.
  • The sponsor can usually appeal a refusal to the Immigration Appeal Division.

Disadvantages

  • The couple may live apart during processing.
  • Applicants abroad cannot work in Canada unless they have separate authorization.

Can You Apply Outland While Living in Canada?

Yes. Some couples living in Canada choose the outland route for its appeal rights or faster estimated processing. The applicant still needs valid status to remain in Canada, and work authorization depends on their own permit situation. This choice should be made carefully, ideally with professional advice.

Sponsor Requirements

  • Be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or registered Indian, at least 18 years old.
  • Sign an undertaking to support your spouse for 3 years after they become a permanent resident.
  • Not be barred, for example by an unpaid immigration loan, certain criminal convictions, or having been sponsored as a spouse yourself within the past 5 years.
  • Permanent resident sponsors must live in Canada; citizen sponsors abroad must show they will live in Canada when the spouse lands.

For spouses with no dependent children who have their own children, there is no minimum income requirement.

Proving a Genuine Relationship

Whichever route you choose, you must prove your relationship is genuine and was not entered into mainly for immigration. Strong evidence includes:

  • Marriage certificate or proof of common-law cohabitation.
  • Photos together over time, with family and friends.
  • Communication history.
  • Joint finances, leases, bills or insurance.
  • Proof of visits and travel together.
  • Evidence of family involvement and wedding ceremonies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is inland or outland spousal sponsorship faster in 2026?

In September 2026, IRCC estimated about 18 months for outland sponsorship and about 26 months for inland sponsorship. Processing times change regularly and individual cases vary, so check IRCC’s official processing times tool before choosing. Speed is only one factor; appeal rights, work permits and travel also matter.

Can my spouse work in Canada during inland sponsorship?

Usually, yes. Spouses and partners sponsored from inside Canada who live with their sponsor can generally apply for an open work permit while the sponsorship is processed, under a public policy extended through December 31, 2026. They must also hold valid status, maintained status or be eligible for restoration.

Can I travel during inland spousal sponsorship?

Travel is risky during inland sponsorship. The applicant must live with the sponsor in Canada and maintain status, and re-entry is not guaranteed. If you expect to travel frequently, outland sponsorship may suit you better. Get advice before making travel plans during processing.

What happens if a spousal sponsorship is refused?

For outland applications, the sponsor can usually appeal the refusal to the Immigration Appeal Division. For inland applications, there is no appeal to the Immigration Appeal Division; the main option is judicial review at the Federal Court. This difference is a key reason some couples choose the outland route.
