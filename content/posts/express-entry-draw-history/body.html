By the end of September 2026, IRCC had held roughly 58 Express Entry rounds in a single year and issued more than 130,000 Invitations to Apply (ITAs), a record pace according to industry trackers. Yet a Canadian Experience Class (CEC) candidate needed around 518 points, while a physician needed fewer than 200. That gap tells the real story of Express Entry draw history: the system no longer runs one race. It runs many.

This guide traces Express Entry from its 2015 launch to the targeted, category-driven system of 2026. You will learn how draw types evolved, what drives CRS cut-offs up or down, and how to read the trends to plan your own path to Canadian permanent residency.

Quick Summary

Express Entry launched on January 1, 2015, with mostly “all-program” draws. Since 2023, IRCC has shifted toward program-specific draws (CEC, PNP) and category-based draws (French, healthcare, trades and more). In 2026, CEC cut-offs mostly sat between 510 and 525, PNP cut-offs between 697 and 805, and category cut-offs ranged from 169 (physicians) to the high 470s (healthcare, trades). Draw size strongly influences cut-offs: bigger draws usually mean lower scores.

How Express Entry Draws Work

Express Entry manages applications for three federal programs: the Federal Skilled Worker Program, the Canadian Experience Class and the Federal Skilled Trades Program, plus Express Entry-aligned streams of the Provincial Nominee Program. Candidates create a profile and receive a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score. In each round, IRCC invites the highest-ranked eligible candidates up to a set number of invitations.

Each round has a type. General rounds invite from all programs. Program-specific rounds target one program, such as CEC or PNP. Category-based rounds, introduced in 2023, invite candidates who meet a category such as French proficiency or healthcare experience. IRCC publishes the date, number of invitations, minimum score and tie-breaking timestamp for every round.

Phase 1 (2015 to 2019): The Early Years

In its first year, Express Entry produced high cut-offs because job offer points, worth up to 600 at the time, pushed many candidates above 700. IRCC reduced job offer points in November 2016 and introduced additional points for French and Canadian study, which rebalanced the pool. Through 2017 to 2019, most draws were all-program rounds with cut-offs usually in the 430 to 470 range (approximately; verify historical figures against IRCC’s archive).

This era established the pattern many candidates still associate with Express Entry: regular, roughly biweekly draws open to all programs. That pattern no longer describes the system, which is why older advice can mislead you.

Phase 2 (2020 to 2022): The Pandemic Disruption

COVID-19 reshaped Express Entry. IRCC shifted to CEC-only and PNP-only draws because candidates inside Canada could land without travel. On February 13, 2021, IRCC invited 27,332 CEC candidates with a cut-off of just 75, the lowest score in Express Entry history. Meanwhile, Federal Skilled Worker candidates waited, as IRCC paused all-program draws from late 2020 until July 2022.

When all-program draws resumed in July 2022, cut-offs started above 550 and gradually eased. This period showed candidates a critical lesson: IRCC can change draw priorities quickly, and policy, not just your score, decides who gets invited.

Phase 3 (2023 to 2025): The Rise of Category-Based Selection

In 2023, IRCC introduced category-based selection, allowing it to invite candidates with specific skills or attributes regardless of whether they topped the overall pool. The original categories covered French proficiency, healthcare, STEM, trades, transport, and agriculture and agri-food. Education joined in 2025, and a physicians category was announced late that year.

On March 25, 2025, IRCC removed job offer points from the CRS entirely, which lowered many scores and reshuffled the pool. Through 2025, French draws dominated category selection, with roughly 48,000 French ITAs for the year according to industry analysis.

Phase 4 (2026): Targeted, Frequent and Highly Segmented

In February 2026, IRCC confirmed ten categories for the year: French, healthcare and social services, STEM, trades, education, transport, physicians with Canadian experience, senior managers with Canadian experience, researchers with Canadian experience, and skilled military recruits. Agriculture and agri-food was dropped. IRCC also raised the minimum work experience for renewed categories from six months to one year within the past three years.

What the Trends Tell Us

Three patterns stand out in recent Express Entry draw history. First, draw size drives cut-offs. When IRCC doubled CEC rounds to 2,000 invitations in September 2026, cut-offs eased from a peak in the low 520s to 518. Second, in-Canada candidates with Canadian experience continue to receive priority, with CEC draws accounting for an estimated 42% of 2026 invitations.

Third, specialization pays. Candidates in narrow categories, such as physicians or senior managers, face much lower cut-offs because fewer people qualify. A candidate with a 400 score has no realistic chance in a CEC draw but may succeed in a French or senior manager round.

Understanding the Express Entry Draw Schedule in 2026

IRCC does not publish a fixed Express Entry draw schedule. In practice, 2026 draws often arrived in clusters: a PNP round followed within a day or two by a CEC round and sometimes a category round. September 2026 alone saw PNP, CEC, physicians, healthcare and senior manager rounds. Watching for these clusters helps you anticipate activity, but never assume a draw will occur on a specific date.

How to Use Draw History to Plan Your Strategy

Draw history should shape your plan, not your anxiety. Compare your score against the draw types you actually qualify for, not just the headline CEC number. If you have French, healthcare or trades experience, track those categories closely. If you sit well below every relevant cut-off, focus on a provincial nomination or on raising language scores.

  • Identify every draw type you qualify for, including categories.
  • Track cut-offs for those draw types over the last six to twelve months.
  • Set a realistic target score with a margin of 10 to 20 points above recent cut-offs.
  • Build a backup plan through a PNP stream.

Policy Watch

IRCC consulted in 2026 on merging FSW, CEC and FST into a single federal high-skilled program and on recalibrating the CRS. It also consulted from August to September 2026 on 2027 categories and signalled it may narrow them. Past trends may not predict future draws once these changes take effect.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the lowest CRS score ever in Express Entry?

The lowest general cut-off came on February 13, 2021, when IRCC invited 27,332 Canadian Experience Class candidates with a minimum score of 75. Among category-based draws, a physicians round on May 2, 2026 reportedly closed at 169. These were exceptional events tied to specific policy goals, so they do not indicate typical cut-offs.

Is there an official Express Entry draw schedule for 2026?

No. IRCC does not publish advance dates for Express Entry draws. In 2026, draws frequently arrived in clusters, with PNP, CEC and category rounds held within a few days of each other. Monitor IRCC’s rounds of invitations page and keep your profile updated so you are ready whenever a relevant draw happens.

Why are CEC cut-offs so much higher than category cut-offs?

CEC draws invite from a large pool of candidates with strong Canadian experience and high language scores, so competition pushes cut-offs above 510. Category draws invite only candidates who meet specific criteria, such as French ability or physician licensing. Smaller eligible pools usually produce lower cut-offs, which is why categories matter so much in 2026.

Will CRS cut-offs go down in 2026 or 2027?

Nobody can predict cut-offs with certainty. They depend on draw size, pool composition, immigration levels targets and policy changes. IRCC has proposed CRS reforms that could reshape scores, possibly starting late 2026 or in 2027. Plan with a safety margin above recent cut-offs and maintain a backup strategy, such as a provincial nomination.
