Your permanent resident card expires, but your permanent resident status does not. That single fact surprises thousands of newcomers every year, and it changes how you should approach PR card renewal in Canada. Renewing is usually simple and costs just $50, yet small mistakes, such as applying too early or ignoring the residency obligation, cause delays, returned applications and, in serious cases, questions about your status.

This guide walks you through every step of a PR card renewal in 2026: when to apply, which forms and documents you need, how long it takes, how to request urgent processing, and what to do if your card expires while you are outside Canada.

Quick Summary

Apply to renew your PR card when it has 9 months or less before it expires. The fee is $50 per person, and you can apply online through the Permanent Residence Portal. You need form IMM 5444, a recent photo, a copy of your current card, identity documents and proof that you meet the residency obligation of 730 days in Canada within the past five years. In late September 2026, IRCC estimated about 38 days for renewals.

PR Card vs PR Status: Know the Difference

Your PR card is a travel and identity document that proves your status. Your PR status is the legal right to live in Canada. According to IRCC’s guidance on PR status, you do not lose your PR status when your card expires. You lose status only through specific events, such as an official determination after an inquiry, voluntary renunciation, a removal order taking effect, or becoming a Canadian citizen.

Even so, you need a valid PR card or a Permanent Resident Travel Document (PRTD) to board a flight or other commercial transport to Canada. That is why timely renewal matters if you travel.

When Should You Renew Your PR Card?

IRCC asks you to apply when your card will expire within 9 months. If your card remains valid for more than 9 months (270 days), do not apply for renewal, because IRCC may return your application. Exceptions include a lost, stolen, damaged or destroyed card, a legal name change, or a change to your sex or gender identifier.

Step-by-Step: How to Renew Your PR Card

Step 1: Confirm you meet the residency obligation

You must have been physically present in Canada for at least 730 days in the five years before your application. The days do not need to be consecutive. Some days outside Canada can count, for example when you accompany a spouse, common-law partner or parent who is a Canadian citizen, or when a Canadian business or the public service employs you full-time abroad. Count your days carefully before you apply.

Step 2: Prepare your forms

The main form is the Application for a Permanent Resident Card (IMM 5444). The document checklist (IMM 5644) shows what to include. If someone represents you, add the Use of a Representative form (IMM 5476).

Step 3: Gather your documents

  • A copy of your current PR card (front and back).
  • A copy of your valid passport or other primary identity document.
  • Proof of your residency in Canada, such as employment records, tax assessments, lease agreements or school records.
  • A photo that meets IRCC specifications, taken within the last 12 months.
  • Your fee receipt.

Step 4: Pay the $50 fee and apply online

IRCC encourages online applications through the Permanent Residence Portal, which runs 24/7. Online applicants upload one digital photo. Paper applicants submit two identical printed photos.

Step 5: Give biometrics if asked

Most PR card applicants do not need to give biometrics. However, if you were under 14 when IRCC received your original permanent residence application and you are now 14 or older, you must give biometrics with your PR card application.

Step 6: Pick up or receive your new card

IRCC mails most new cards to your Canadian address. In some cases, IRCC asks you to pick up your card in person. Keep your address updated throughout processing, because Canada Post will not forward PR cards.

How Long Does PR Card Renewal Take in 2026?

In late September 2026, IRCC estimated about 38 days for PR card renewals and about 35 days for new cards. Check the official IRCC processing times tool for the current estimate, because times change often. Applications that raise residency questions can take considerably longer.

Urgent PR Card Processing

If you must travel urgently, you can request urgent processing. Qualifying reasons, set out in IRCC’s PR card application guide, include your own serious illness, the serious illness or death of a family member, a job-related trip, or another crisis. You must include proof, such as travel tickets and a doctor’s letter or employer letter. Urgent processing is not guaranteed.

What If Your PR Card Expires While You Are Outside Canada?

You cannot renew your PR card from outside Canada. Instead, apply for a Permanent Resident Travel Document (PRTD) through IRCC before you travel home. The PRTD application also includes a residency assessment. If you do not meet the 730-day obligation, explain any humanitarian reasons in your application.

Travellers sometimes re-enter Canada at a land border in a private vehicle with an expired card, but airlines usually will not board you without a valid PR card or PRTD. Do not rely on this route without advice.

Lost, Stolen or Damaged PR Card

If you lose your card or someone steals it, apply for a replacement using the same PR card application. Include a police report or incident number if you have one. If your card is damaged, follow IRCC’s instructions to destroy it and provide proof. Report a stolen card to police promptly to reduce the risk of identity fraud.

Common PR Card Renewal Mistakes

  • Applying when the card still has more than 9 months of validity.
  • Submitting a photo that does not meet IRCC specifications.
  • Miscounting days in Canada, or not keeping travel records.
  • Letting your mailing address change without updating IRCC.
  • Booking international travel before your new card arrives.

Frequently Asked Questions

When can I renew my PR card in Canada?

Apply when your PR card will expire within 9 months. If it remains valid for more than 9 months, IRCC asks you not to apply, unless your card is lost, stolen, damaged or destroyed, or you have changed your legal name or gender identifier. Applying too early can lead to a returned application.

How much does PR card renewal cost in 2026?

The PR card application fee is $50 per person. You pay online when you submit through the Permanent Residence Portal, or include your receipt with a paper application. Check IRCC’s fee list before paying, because fees can change and an incorrect payment can delay processing.

Do I lose my PR status if my PR card expires?

No. An expired PR card does not end your permanent resident status. You keep PR status until an official decision ends it, you renounce it, a removal order takes effect, or you become a citizen. However, you need a valid PR card or a PR travel document to board flights to Canada.

How long does PR card renewal take?

In late September 2026, IRCC estimated about 38 days for PR card renewals. Applications with residency questions, missing documents or photo problems can take longer. Check the official processing times tool before you book travel, and request urgent processing only if you meet IRCC’s criteria.

Can I renew my PR card from outside Canada?

No. PR card renewal applications must be made by people in Canada. If your card expires while you are abroad, apply for a Permanent Resident Travel Document so you can return. IRCC will review whether you meet the residency obligation as part of that application.
