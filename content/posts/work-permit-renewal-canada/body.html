A work permit renewal looks simple on paper: fill in the form, pay the fee, submit before expiry. In practice, small mistakes, such as applying for the wrong permit type, submitting a “placeholder” application or travelling at the wrong time, cause thousands of workers to lose their status every year. Since May 2025, the rules around maintained status have become even less forgiving.

This guide explains how work permit renewal in Canada works in 2026, the mistakes that cause the most damage, and a practical checklist to keep your status and your job safe.

Quick Summary

Apply to extend your work permit online before your current permit expires. If you apply on time and stay in Canada, you can usually keep working under the same conditions while IRCC decides, which is known as maintained status. Since May 28, 2025, a second application submitted after your original status expires is returned unprocessed, even if you were on maintained status. If your status expires, you generally have 90 days to apply for restoration.

Step 1: Confirm Which Permit You Need

A “renewal” usually means a new application. Your options depend on your situation:

  • Employer-specific permit: usually requires a new positive Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) or a valid LMIA exemption, plus your employer’s offer of employment where required.
  • Open work permit: such as a bridging open work permit or an eligible spousal open work permit.
  • Changing employers: you need a new permit before starting work for a new employer, unless your permit is open or a specific policy applies.

Step 2: Apply Before Your Permit Expires

IRCC recommends applying well before expiry. Applying early gives you time to fix problems and avoids the stress of last-minute uploads. If your passport expires soon, renew it first, because your work permit cannot extend beyond your passport’s validity.

The Most Costly Work Permit Renewal Mistakes

Mistake 1: Waiting until the last day

Technical problems, missing documents or an expired LMIA can leave you without a valid application on the day your permit expires.

Mistake 2: Submitting a "placeholder" application

Some workers submit an incomplete application just to get maintained status, planning to file the real one later. Since May 28, 2025, if your original status expires and you submit a subsequent application after that date, IRCC returns it unprocessed, even if you were on maintained status. If the placeholder is refused or returned, you may be left without status.

Mistake 3: Applying for the wrong type of status

Maintained status protects work only if you applied to extend your stay as a worker. If you applied for a visitor record instead, you must stop working when your work permit expires.

Mistake 4: Leaving Canada during processing

If you leave Canada while your application is pending, your maintained status generally ends. You may be able to re-enter as a visitor, but you may not be able to work until IRCC approves your new permit.

Mistake 5: Changing employers too soon

On an employer-specific permit, you generally cannot start working for a new employer until you receive a permit for that employer, unless a specific policy allows it.

Mistake 6: Ignoring a refusal

If IRCC refuses your application, maintained status ends on the date of the refusal. Stop working immediately and seek advice on restoration within 90 days.

What If Your Status Already Expired?

If your work permit expired and you did not apply in time, you may be able to apply for restoration of status within 90 days of losing it, provided you still meet the requirements for the permit you seek. You must pay a restoration fee in addition to the work permit fee, and you cannot work until IRCC restores your status and issues a new permit.

Work Permit Renewal Checklist

  • Confirm the permit type you need and your eligibility.
  • Check your passport validity.
  • Secure an LMIA or offer of employment early, if needed.
  • Submit a complete application online before expiry.
  • Keep proof of your submission date.
  • Stay in Canada and work only under your current conditions.
  • Monitor your IRCC account and respond quickly to requests.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I renew my work permit in Canada?

Apply as early as you can once you meet the requirements, and always before your current permit expires. Applying early leaves time to fix problems, obtain a new LMIA or offer of employment, and renew your passport if needed. Applying before expiry also lets you keep working under maintained status in most cases.

Can I keep working after my work permit expires?

Yes, if you applied to extend your work permit before it expired, you stay in Canada, and your application is still in process. This is maintained status. You must continue under the same conditions, such as the same employer for an employer-specific permit, until IRCC decides.

What happens if my work permit renewal is refused?

Your maintained status ends on the date of the refusal, and you must stop working. You may be able to apply for restoration of status within 90 days if you still meet the requirements. A regulated consultant can review the refusal reasons and help you decide whether to restore, reapply or explore other options.

Can I submit a second work permit application while the first is processing?

You can, but timing matters. Since May 28, 2025, if your original status expires and you submit a subsequent application after that date, IRCC returns it unprocessed, even if you were on maintained status. Submit a complete, correct application before your status expires rather than relying on a second application.
