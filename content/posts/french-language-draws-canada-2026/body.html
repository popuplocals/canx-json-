If there is one skill that transformed Express Entry chances in 2026, it is French. French-language proficiency draws issued 30,500 invitations in the first half of the year alone, and on August 19, 2026, IRCC invited 5,000 French-speaking candidates with scores as low as 382. Compare that with Canadian Experience Class draws, which required scores around 518 to 521 in September. For many candidates, French is the difference between waiting years and receiving an invitation.

This guide explains how French language draws work in 2026, who qualifies, how many points French adds, and how to prepare for French tests effectively.

Quick Summary

French-language proficiency draws invite Express Entry candidates who score NCLC 7 or higher in all four French abilities on TEF Canada or TCF Canada. In 2026, French draws issued large rounds, often 4,000 to 5,000 invitations, with cut-offs ranging from 382 to 420. French at NCLC 7 also adds 25 or 50 additional CRS points, plus second-language points. Canada aims to increase French-speaking immigration outside Quebec.

How French Category Draws Work

French-language proficiency is one of IRCC’s priority categories for category-based selection. To be eligible, you must be in the Express Entry pool, qualify for an Express Entry program and score at least NCLC 7 in all four French abilities: reading, writing, listening and speaking.

Which French Tests Count?

IRCC accepts TEF Canada and TCF Canada for Express Entry. Results must cover all four abilities and be less than two years old. Check IRCC’s equivalency charts to convert test scores to NCLC levels.

Why Canada Prioritizes French

Canada wants to strengthen French-speaking communities outside Quebec and has set a goal of increasing French-speaking permanent residents admitted outside Quebec, aiming for 12% by 2029. Other francophone-focused options include francophone mobility work permits for jobs outside Quebec and the Francophone Community Immigration Pilot.

How to Prepare for NCLC 7

  • Assess your current level with a practice test or a qualified teacher.
  • Build a study plan focused on all four abilities, especially speaking and writing.
  • Use structured courses, conversation practice and timed mock exams.
  • Book your test once mock scores consistently reach NCLC 7 or higher.
  • Keep improving English too, since CLB 5+ in English unlocks the full 50 points.

Who Benefits Most From French?

  • Candidates with scores between about 380 and 480 who cannot reach CEC cut-offs.
  • Candidates from French-speaking countries or with French education.
  • Candidates aged over 30 who need to offset declining age points.
  • Speakers of Romance languages such as Spanish, Portuguese or Italian, who may find French easier to learn.

Frequently Asked Questions

What French level do I need for Express Entry French draws?

You need at least NCLC 7 in all four French abilities: reading, writing, listening and speaking, on TEF Canada or TCF Canada. You must also be in the Express Entry pool and qualify for an Express Entry program, such as the Federal Skilled Worker Program or Canadian Experience Class.

What were the CRS cut-offs for French draws in 2026?

Reported 2026 French draw cut-offs ranged from 382 to 420, with large rounds of 4,000 to 8,500 invitations. The lowest was 382 on August 19, 2026. These results were far below the Canadian Experience Class cut-offs of around 518 to 521 in September 2026.

How many CRS points does French give?

NCLC 7 or higher in all four French abilities adds 25 additional points, or 50 points if you also score CLB 5 or higher in all four English abilities. French as a second official language can add up to 24 more points for a single applicant.

Is it worth learning French for Canadian immigration?

For many candidates, yes. French can add up to 50 additional CRS points and qualifies you for French category draws, which had some of the lowest cut-offs in 2026. Reaching NCLC 7 takes significant effort, so assess your starting level and timeline before committing.
