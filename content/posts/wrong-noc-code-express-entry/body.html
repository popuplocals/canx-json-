A National Occupational Classification (NOC) code is only five digits long, but in an Express Entry application it carries enormous weight. It decides whether your work experience qualifies, which Express Entry program you can use, how many Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points your experience earns, which category-based draws you can enter and how an officer reads every employment document you submit. When the NOC code you declared does not match the duties in your experience letter, or the duties in your letter point to a different NOC than the one you declared, the officer may conclude that you have not proven the work experience the law requires.

In our practice, and in reports from other immigration professionals, NOC mismatches are among the most common reasons Express Entry applications for permanent residence are refused, alongside problems with the experience letter itself. The two problems are closely linked. A strong letter cannot rescue the wrong NOC, and the right NOC cannot be proven with a weak letter.

This guide focuses on one subject only: the wrong NOC code. It explains the legal test under the Immigration and Refugee Protection Regulations (IRPR), how NOC 2021 is structured, how officers assess NOC claims, the most common causes of mismatches, occupations that applicants frequently confuse, the legal consequences and a step-by-step method to choose, document and defend the right code.

Quick Summary

Under paragraphs 75(2)(b) and (c) and 87.1(2)(b) and (c) of the IRPR, you must have performed the actions described in the lead statement of your NOC and a substantial number of its main duties, including all of the essential duties. Officers compare three things: the NOC you declared, the duties in your experience letters and the official NOC 2021 description. If they do not align, your experience may not be accepted, you may lose category eligibility and your application can be refused under section 11.2 of IRPA. A knowingly false NOC claim can also lead to a five-year misrepresentation finding under section 40. Choose your NOC from your real duties, never from your job title or a list of in-demand jobs.

Part 1: The Legal Framework

The duties test in the IRPR

The IRPR sets the same core occupational test for skilled work experience in the main Express Entry programs.

ProvisionWhat it requires
IRPR s. 75(2)(a)FSW: at least one year of continuous full-time work, or equivalent part-time work, in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupation within the last 10 years
IRPR s. 75(2)(b)FSW: you performed the actions described in the lead statement for the occupation as set out in the NOC
IRPR s. 75(2)(c)FSW: you performed a substantial number of the main duties of the occupation, including all of the essential duties
IRPR s. 75(3)FSW: if you do not meet these requirements, the application is refused and no further assessment is required
IRPR s. 87.1(2)(a) to (c)CEC: the same lead statement and main duties test, applied to at least one year of skilled work in Canada within the last three years
IRPR s. 87.2FST: two years of experience in an eligible skilled trade, assessed against the NOC description of that trade

The Express Entry rules in IRPA

ProvisionRelevance to your NOC
IRPA s. 10.3Ministerial Instructions set ranking and category-based selection, which depend on your occupation
IRPA s. 11.2(1)No permanent resident visa if, at invitation or when the application is received, you did not meet the criteria or hold the qualifications on which you were ranked
IRPA s. 16(1)You must answer truthfully and produce the evidence the officer reasonably requires
IRPA s. 40(1)(a) and 40(2)(a)Misrepresenting material facts, including duties or occupation, can lead to a five-year inadmissibility

What the legal test really asks

The test does not ask what your job was called. It asks what you did. Specifically, it asks whether your documented duties:

  • Reflect the actions in the lead statement of the NOC you claimed.
  • Include a substantial number of that NOC’s main duties.
  • Include all of the essential duties that define the occupation.

If your duties fail this test for the NOC you declared, the officer may find that you do not have the qualifying experience, even if you worked hard, full-time and for many years.

How decisions are reviewed

If an officer refuses your application and you seek judicial review in the Federal Court, the Court generally reviews the decision on a standard of reasonableness, as set out by the Supreme Court of Canada in Canada (Minister of Citizenship and Immigration) v. Vavilov, 2019 SCC 65. In practice, this means the Court asks whether the officer’s decision was justified, transparent and intelligible in light of the evidence, not whether the Court would have chosen a different NOC. Getting the NOC right at the application stage is far more effective than trying to fix it later.

Part 2: How NOC 2021 Works

IRCC has used NOC 2021 for Express Entry since November 16, 2022. Understanding its structure helps you avoid many mistakes.

The five-digit code

According to Statistics Canada, each digit in a NOC 2021 code has a specific meaning.

DigitMeaning
First digitBroad occupational category, such as business, health or trades
Second digitTEER category: Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities
Third digitSub-major group
Fourth digitMinor group
Fifth digitUnit group, the most detailed level

For example, in 62020, the second digit “2” indicates TEER 2.

TEER categories

TEERGeneral description (Statistics Canada)
TEER 0Management occupations
TEER 1Usually requires a university degree
TEER 2Usually requires a post-secondary program of two to three years, an apprenticeship of two to five years, or involves supervisory or significant safety responsibilities
TEER 3Usually requires a post-secondary program of less than two years, an apprenticeship of less than two years, or more than six months of on-the-job training
TEER 4Usually requires completion of secondary school or several weeks of on-the-job training
TEER 5Usually requires a short work demonstration and no formal education

Express Entry programs generally require experience in TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 occupations. Experience in TEER 4 or 5 does not qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker class or the Canadian Experience Class.

The parts of a NOC unit group description

Every NOC unit group has several components, and each matters for your application.

ComponentWhat it tells youHow to use it
Lead statementThe general description and boundaries of the occupationYour job overall must match this description
Example titlesJob titles commonly used in the labour marketHelpful for searching, but titles alone do not decide your NOC
InclusionsBorderline titles that belong in this groupCheck whether your role is specifically included
ExclusionsRelated occupations classified elsewhereCheck whether your role is specifically excluded and belongs in another code
Main dutiesThe most significant duties of the occupationYou must have performed a substantial number, including essential duties
Employment requirementsTypical education, training, experience and licensingConsider whether your background is consistent
Additional informationProgression and mobility between occupationsHelps understand related roles

The exclusions section is one of the most overlooked tools. If your job title appears in another unit group’s exclusions, or if the description of your role points elsewhere, that is a strong signal to reconsider your code.

Part 3: How Officers Assess Your NOC

Officers typically compare three sources and look for alignment.

SourceWhat the officer checks
Your Express Entry profile and applicationThe NOC you declared for each period of experience
Your experience letters and supporting documentsThe duties, title, hours, salary and employer you documented
The official NOC 2021 descriptionThe lead statement, main duties, exclusions and employment requirements

Officers may also consider your resume, your education, your salary level, the nature of the employer’s business and your previous immigration applications, including work permit and Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) records.

The two-way mismatch

NOC problems usually take one of two forms, and applicants often have both.

Mismatch typeWhat happensExample
Declared NOC does not match the letterYou claim a NOC, but your letter describes duties that do not fit its lead statement and main dutiesYou declare a retail manager code, but your letter describes serving customers and operating the cash register
Letter points to a different NOCYour letter describes duties that fit another NOC better than the one you declaredYou declare a software engineer code, but your letter describes troubleshooting user computers, which fits a user support technician code

In the second case, the officer may conclude that your real occupation is the other NOC. If that NOC is in a lower TEER category, or not in the category you were invited under, the consequences can be serious.

Red flags officers commonly notice

  • A senior title with junior duties.
  • Duties copied word for word from the NOC text.
  • A salary well below what the occupation normally pays.
  • An employer whose business does not need the claimed occupation.
  • A NOC that changed between your profile, work permit, LMIA and permanent residence application without explanation.
  • A NOC that matches a category draw but not your career history.
  • Management claims in a very small business without staff to manage.

Part 4: The Root Causes of Wrong NOC Codes

1. Choosing by job title

Job titles vary widely between employers and countries. A “manager” in one company may be a supervisor in another and a frontline worker in a third. Titles are a starting point for searching, never the basis for a decision.

2. Chasing category-based draws

Category draws create a strong temptation to choose a NOC that appears on a category list. If your duties do not truly match, you risk refusal under section 11.2 of IRPA, because you did not meet the category requirements when invited.

3. TEER inflation

Applicants sometimes select a higher TEER occupation to qualify or to earn more points, such as a manager code instead of a supervisor code or a supervisor code instead of a worker code. Officers are alert to this pattern.

4. Copying the NOC into the letter

Copying NOC duties into the letter may make the code appear to match, but it often signals that the letter does not describe the real job, and it can claim duties you never performed.

5. Different job title conventions abroad

In many countries, titles such as “executive,” “officer” or “engineer” are used broadly. An “IT executive” or “sales officer” may correspond to very different NOC codes depending on the duties.

6. Combining several roles into one NOC

If you were promoted or changed roles, each period must be assessed against the NOC for that role. One code for all your years of experience is often wrong.

7. Inconsistent NOCs across applications

Your work permit, LMIA, provincial nomination and Express Entry profile may each list a NOC. If they differ, officers may ask why. Sometimes the difference is legitimate, such as a promotion or a change in duties, but it must be explainable and supported.

8. Relying on an agent or a template

Unlicensed agents sometimes assign NOC codes to fit programs rather than the applicant’s real work. Under section 40 of IRPA, you can be responsible for indirect misrepresentation even if someone else chose the code.

9. Ignoring exclusions

Many applicants read only the lead statement and example titles. The exclusions often identify the correct code for roles that look similar.

10. Using outdated NOC 2016 codes

NOC 2016 used four-digit codes and skill levels. Express Entry now uses five-digit NOC 2021 codes and TEER categories. Older guides, letters and templates may refer to codes that no longer apply.

Honest error or misrepresentation?

Not every wrong NOC is misrepresentation. Choosing a code in good faith, based on an honest understanding of your work, is different from knowingly presenting false duties to fit a code. However, officers assess whether the information was material and could induce an error, and the Federal Court has treated the exception for innocent mistakes narrowly. The safest protection is a NOC that you chose carefully and duties that are true.

Part 7: A Step-by-Step Method to Choose the Right NOC

Step 1: Describe your job without looking at any NOC

Write your real duties for each role, in your own words. Include who you reported to, who reported to you, the decisions you made, the tools and systems you used and how you spent most of your time.

Step 2: Identify your main purpose

In one sentence, describe the main purpose of your job. This sentence will be compared with lead statements.

Step 3: Search NOC 2021 using duties, not titles

Use keywords from your duties and main purpose. Shortlist two to four candidate unit groups.

Step 4: Compare lead statements

For each candidate, ask whether the lead statement describes your job overall. Eliminate any that describe a more senior, more technical or different role.

Step 5: Read the exclusions

Check whether your role appears in the exclusions of any candidate and where the exclusions point you instead.

Step 6: Build a duty map

List the main duties of your leading candidate and mark each one you performed.

NOC main dutyPerformed?Evidence in my letter
Main duty 1YesSpecific duty described in employer’s words
Main duty 2YesSpecific duty described in employer’s words
Main duty 3NoNot part of my role
Main duty 4YesSpecific duty described in employer’s words
Main duty 5PartlyHonest description of what I did

Step 7: Confirm the essential duties

Identify the duties that define the occupation. A supervisor code requires real supervision. A manager code requires real planning, directing and controlling. A professional code usually involves the professional work described. If you did not perform the defining duties, the NOC is not yours.

Step 8: Check the TEER and employment requirements

Confirm that the TEER qualifies for your program and that your education, training and any licensing are consistent with the occupation.

Step 9: Test consistency

Check your proposed NOC against your salary, your employer’s business, your resume and every previous IRCC application. Prepare an explanation for any legitimate differences.

Step 10: Decide, document and keep your reasoning

Record why you chose the NOC and keep your duty map. If an officer later raises a concern, your reasoning and evidence will help you respond.

Part 8: Aligning Your Letter and Your NOC, the Right Way

The relationship between your letter and your NOC must run in one direction: your real duties decide your NOC, and your letter describes those real duties. The wrong approach is to choose a NOC first and then rewrite the letter to fit it.

Wrong approachRight approach
Pick a NOC from a category list, then ask the employer to copy its dutiesWrite your real duties, then find the NOC that matches them
Change the letter to remove duties that point to a lower TEERAccept the NOC your real duties support and find the right program or strategy
Use one NOC for all roles over many yearsAssess each role and period separately
Copy NOC wording to guarantee a matchAlign your duties with the NOC in the employer’s own words, with specific details

What a well-aligned letter looks like

A well-aligned letter makes the officer’s job easy. The overall description of your role reflects the lead statement. The listed duties cover a substantial number of the main duties, including the essential ones, with specific details about systems, volumes, teams and responsibilities. The title, salary and employer’s business are consistent with the occupation.

Part 9: Special Situations

Promotions and changing duties

Assess each role separately. If you moved from a TEER 3 role to a TEER 2 supervisory role, only the periods in qualifying occupations count, and each must match its own NOC.

Hybrid roles

Many jobs combine duties from several occupations. Choose the NOC that reflects the main purpose of your role and the majority of your time. Do not claim two NOCs for the same period.

Small businesses

In small businesses, people often do a bit of everything. Officers may look closely at management claims where the business has few or no other staff. A manager code generally requires real managerial functions, such as planning, directing and controlling the business or a department, usually through staff.

Work abroad with unusual titles

Explain your role through duties, not titles. If your employer uses titles that do not translate well, the duties must carry the explanation.

LMIA and work permit occupations

If you work in Canada on an LMIA-based work permit, the LMIA and permit identify a NOC and job duties. If you claim a different NOC for that work in Express Entry, officers may ask why. Your actual duties must also be consistent with your work permit conditions, because paragraph 87.1(3) of the IRPR excludes unauthorized work from Canadian Experience Class experience.

Provincial nominations

A provincial nomination is often tied to a specific occupation. Make sure your Express Entry profile and application are consistent with the occupation in your nomination, or explain any legitimate difference.

Federal Skilled Trades

For the Federal Skilled Trades class under section 87.2 of the IRPR, your occupation must be an eligible skilled trade, and your experience is assessed against the NOC description of that trade. Trade titles often overlap. For example, a worker who installs, maintains and repairs electrical systems must show duties that match the specific electrical trade claimed, and any provincial or territorial certification should be consistent with that trade. Because Federal Skilled Trades applicants also need a qualifying job offer or a certificate of qualification, make sure the occupation in that offer or certificate matches the NOC you declare.

Writing a letter of explanation for NOC differences

Sometimes a difference between documents is legitimate. Your title may not match common Canadian titles, your LMIA may list a different NOC from the one your duties later evolved into or your previous work permit application may have used a code that was later corrected. In these cases, a short letter of explanation can prevent an officer from drawing the wrong conclusion. An effective letter:

  • Identifies each document and the NOC or title it shows.
  • Explains clearly and factually why they differ, such as a promotion, a change in duties or a correction.
  • Refers to the evidence that supports the NOC you are now claiming, such as an updated job description or a supervisor letter.
  • Maps your duties to the lead statement and main duties of the NOC you are claiming.
  • Avoids exaggeration and remains consistent with every other document in your application.

Never use a letter of explanation to cover a NOC you cannot support. If your duties do not match, the right step is to change your NOC, not to explain away the mismatch.

Category-based draws

For most occupation-based categories, IRCC requires at least 12 months of full-time work, or an equal amount of part-time work, in a single eligible occupation within the past three years. The experience does not need to be continuous. Your NOC must appear on the category list and must be supported by your duties.

Part 10: Illustrative NOC Case Studies

The following scenarios are illustrative composites based on common situations. Names and details are fictional and do not describe specific clients. They show how the legal test works in practice.

Case study 1: The "manager" who supervised

Ravi worked for four years as “Restaurant Manager” at a single-location quick-service restaurant with nine staff. He declared NOC 60030, Restaurant and food service managers. His letter listed six duties: preparing weekly staff schedules, training new employees, handling customer complaints, counting cash at closing, ordering supplies from an approved list and helping on the line during busy periods. The owner set the budget, hired staff, decided prices and handled suppliers.

Assessment: Ravi’s duties reflect supervising and co-ordinating food service workers, not planning, directing and controlling the restaurant’s operations. His duties align more closely with NOC 62020, Food service supervisors. Because 62020 is TEER 2, his experience could still qualify, but his declared NOC was not supported. Had he submitted his application under 60030, he risked a finding that he did not have the experience claimed.

Lesson: Choose the NOC your duties support. A TEER 2 supervisor code that matches is far stronger than a TEER 0 manager code that does not.

Case study 2: The developer who provided support

Mei worked as an “IT Engineer” for a mid-sized company and declared NOC 21232, Software developers and programmers, hoping to qualify for a STEM category draw. Her letter described installing operating systems, setting up user accounts, resolving hardware and software problems reported by employees and maintaining the help desk ticket system. She occasionally wrote small scripts to automate tasks.

Assessment: The main purpose of Mei’s role was first-line technical support for computer users, which aligns with NOC 22221, User support technicians. Occasional scripting does not make her a software developer. If invited in a category draw based on 21232, her application could be refused under section 11.2 of IRPA because her duties did not support that NOC.

Lesson: Never choose a NOC to fit a category. Choose the NOC that fits your work, then check which categories, if any, include it.

Case study 3: The promotion that changed everything

Daniel worked as a retail salesperson for two years, then was promoted to department supervisor for 18 months. He declared NOC 62010, Retail sales supervisors, for all three and a half years.

Assessment: Only the 18 months as a supervisor can be assessed under 62010. The first two years align with NOC 64100, Retail salespersons and visual merchandisers, which is TEER 4 and does not qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker class or the Canadian Experience Class. Daniel still had more than one year of qualifying experience, but his profile overstated it, which could reduce his CRS points and raise credibility concerns.

Lesson: Assess each role separately, with its own dates, duties and NOC, and claim only the qualifying periods.

Responding to a procedural fairness letter about your NOC

If an officer raises a NOC concern, your response should:

  • Address the officer’s specific concern directly.
  • Map your duties against the lead statement and main duties of your NOC.
  • Provide additional evidence, such as job descriptions, performance reviews, organizational charts, work samples and supervisor letters.
  • Explain any differences between titles and duties.
  • Remain factual, respectful and consistent with your earlier documents.

Judicial review deadlines

Under paragraph 72(2)(b) of IRPA, an application for leave for judicial review must be filed within 15 days for matters arising in Canada and 60 days for matters arising outside Canada. Seek advice promptly if you are considering this route.

Part 14: The NOC Pre-Submission Checklist

  • I chose my NOC based on my real duties, not my title or a category list.
  • My job overall matches the NOC lead statement.
  • My letter shows a substantial number of the main duties, including the essential duties.
  • I checked the exclusions and confirmed my role is not classified elsewhere.
  • My NOC is TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 for FSW or CEC, or an eligible trade for FST.
  • Each role and period has its own NOC where duties changed.
  • My letter describes real duties in the employer’s own words, not copied NOC text.
  • My salary, employer and resume are consistent with the NOC.
  • My NOC is consistent with my work permit, LMIA and nomination, or I can explain the difference.
  • If I was invited in a category draw, my NOC is on the list and I met the experience requirement on the invitation date.
  • I kept my duty map and reasoning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Express Entry be refused because of the wrong NOC code?

Yes. Under sections 75 and 87.1 of the IRPR, your duties must match the lead statement and a substantial number of the main duties of your NOC, including the essential duties. If they do not, your experience may not be accepted and your application can be refused under section 11.2 of IRPA or subsection 75(3) of the IRPR.

Does IRCC decide my NOC by job title or by duties?

By duties. Officers compare the duties in your experience letters with the official NOC 2021 description. Your job title is only a starting point. A senior title with junior duties, or duties that fit a different occupation, can lead an officer to conclude that your real occupation is a different NOC.

What happens if my duties match a lower TEER occupation?

If your duties fit a TEER 4 or 5 occupation, that experience generally does not qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker class or the Canadian Experience Class. If they fit a different TEER 0 to 3 occupation, your experience may still qualify, but your CRS points, category eligibility and credibility can be affected.

Can I change my NOC code after receiving an ITA?

Your application must be consistent with the qualifications on which you were ranked. If you discover after an ITA that your declared NOC does not match your duties, get advice quickly. Depending on your situation, it may be safer to decline the ITA, correct your profile and wait for a new invitation.

Is choosing the wrong NOC code misrepresentation?

Not automatically. An honest, good-faith choice differs from knowingly presenting false duties. However, section 40 of IRPA covers misrepresenting or withholding material facts that could induce an error, and the exception for innocent mistakes is narrow. Choosing carefully and documenting your real duties truthfully is the best protection.
