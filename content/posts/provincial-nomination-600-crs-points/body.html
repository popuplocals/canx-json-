On September 28, 2026, IRCC invited 733 provincial nominees with a minimum CRS score of 725. That number looks impossible until you remember one thing: every one of those candidates carried a 600-point nomination. Strip that away and many would have sat between 125 and 300 points. A provincial nomination remains the single most powerful way to raise your CRS score 2026.

This guide explains how the 600-point boost works, the difference between enhanced and base nominations, how provinces select candidates, and how to decide whether a PNP belongs in your strategy.

Quick Summary

An Express Entry-aligned (“enhanced”) provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points once you accept it in your profile, which virtually ensures an ITA in a following PNP draw. Base nominations, issued outside Express Entry, do not add CRS points and follow a separate application process. Each province sets its own streams, priorities and selection methods. In 2026, PNP draw cut-offs ranged from 697 to 805, reflecting the 600-point boost.

What Is the Provincial Nominee Program?

The Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) lets provinces and territories nominate people who meet their local economic needs. Quebec runs its own separate selection system and does not participate. Nunavut also does not run a PNP. Each participating province or territory designs its own streams for skilled workers, graduates, tradespeople, entrepreneurs and in-demand occupations.

A nomination does not grant permanent residence on its own. IRCC still makes the final decision, including medical, security and admissibility checks.

How the 600 Points Work

Once a province nominates you through an enhanced stream, you accept the nomination in your Express Entry profile. IRCC then adds 600 points to your score. Because the maximum score without a nomination is 600, the boost lifts nominees above all non-nominated candidates. IRCC holds PNP-specific rounds roughly every two weeks in 2026, so nominees typically receive an ITA in the next PNP round.

Two Ways Provinces Select Candidates

Route 1: Notification of Interest Through Express Entry

Some provinces search the federal Express Entry pool and send a Notification of Interest (NOI) directly to candidates whose profiles match their needs. You then apply to the province within its deadline. To make yourself visible, select the provinces that interest you in your Express Entry profile, or choose “all provinces and territories.”

Route 2: Apply to the Province Directly

Other streams require you to register an expression of interest or apply to the province first, often with a job offer, a provincial connection or experience in a priority occupation. Once the province approves you, it nominates you, and you create or update your Express Entry profile to accept the enhanced nomination.

Who Has the Best Chance of a Nomination in 2026?

Provinces favour candidates who fill local labour shortages and are likely to stay. In 2026, many provinces prioritized healthcare, skilled trades, technology, early childhood education and French-speaking candidates. A connection to the province, such as previous study, work, a job offer or family, often strengthens your position.

  • Experience in an occupation on a province’s priority list.
  • A job offer from an employer in the province.
  • Graduation from a post-secondary institution in the province.
  • Current work experience in the province.
  • French language ability, particularly for francophone-focused streams.

PNP vs Express Entry Canada: Which Path Is Faster?

For candidates with high scores, waiting for a CEC or category draw may be faster than applying for a nomination, because provincial processing adds months. For candidates well below federal cut-offs, a nomination is often the only realistic route. The decision depends on your score, occupation and provincial connections, not on a universal rule.

How Long Does Express Entry Take in 2026 With a Nomination?

Timelines vary widely. Provincial processing can take weeks or many months depending on the stream. After a nomination, you typically wait for the next PNP draw, then have 60 days to submit a complete permanent residence application. IRCC’s Express Entry service standard targets most complete applications within about six months, but check IRCC’s current processing times page, since actual times change.

Common PNP Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying to a stream you do not clearly meet, which can waste fees and delay plans.
  • Missing the deadline after an NOI or ITA.
  • Claiming intent to live in a province you do not plan to settle in.
  • Letting your Express Entry profile or language results expire during provincial processing.
  • Paying anyone who promises a “guaranteed” nomination.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many CRS points does a provincial nomination give?

An enhanced, Express Entry-aligned provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points once you accept it in your Express Entry profile. Base nominations issued outside Express Entry do not add CRS points; instead, you apply for permanent residence through a separate process. As of early October 2026, the 600-point value remained in effect.

Does a provincial nomination guarantee permanent residence?

No. A nomination makes an invitation highly likely and strongly supports your application, but IRCC makes the final decision. You must still meet federal eligibility, medical, security and admissibility requirements, and your documents must support every claim. Misrepresentation or ineligibility can still lead to refusal.

Can I get a provincial nomination without a job offer?

Yes, in some streams. Several provinces select candidates directly from the Express Entry pool based on occupation, experience or language ability, without requiring a job offer. Other streams do require a job offer from an employer in the province. Each province publishes its own criteria, which change frequently.

Is PNP or Express Entry faster in 2026?

It depends on your profile. If your score already exceeds recent CEC or category cut-offs, a direct Express Entry invitation may come faster than adding provincial processing time. If your score sits well below federal cut-offs, a nomination often offers the most realistic route. Compare current cut-offs and provincial processing times before choosing.
