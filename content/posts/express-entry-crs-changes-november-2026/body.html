As of today, November 3, 2026, Express Entry Canada 2026 has a new rulebook. On October 15, 2026, Immigration Minister Lena Metlege Diab signed revised Ministerial Instructions for the Express Entry system. These Instructions change how Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) calculates your Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score, bring back job offer points, and add new points for work in high-wage occupations. If you plan to file a PR application Canada through Express Entry, these rules now decide where you rank in the pool.

The headline changes are easy to state. The rest of the Instructions matter just as much, because small rules about work experience, test expiry dates, and job offer genuineness decide whether you get your points. This guide walks through every section of the new Instructions in plain language, so you understand your CRS score 2026 and what to do next.

Quick Summary

From November 3, 2026, the CRS has a new core factor called “labour market integration,” worth up to 150 points for single applicants and 140 points for applicants with an accompanying spouse. You receive the higher of two scores: Canadian work experience points (now with bonus points for high-wage occupations) or points for a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation.

Job offers no longer need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) to earn CRS points. The maximum possible CRS score rises from 1,200 to 1,270. IRCC publishes a list of high-wage occupations, and your occupation’s level on that list at the time of your Invitation to Apply (ITA) sets your points.

What Are the Ministerial Instructions, and Why Do They Matter?

Ministerial Instructions are the legal rules the Minister issues under section 10.3 of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA) to run Express Entry. They decide who can enter the pool, how IRCC scores you, how long your profile stays active, and how long you have to respond to an ITA. In short, they are the legal foundation of every Express Entry draw 2026.

IRCC issues new versions whenever policy shifts. The October 15, 2026 version carries repeals and amendments dated November 3, 2026, which is why that date matters. From today forward, IRCC ranks the pool and issues invitations under these rules. If you received your ITA before November 3, ask your representative how IRCC treats your file, because transition rules can vary.

Which Programs Does Express Entry Cover in 2026?

Express Entry manages applications for four groups:

  1. Federal Skilled Worker (FSW) class
  2. Canadian Experience Class (CEC)
  3. Federal Skilled Trades (FST) class
  4. Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) candidates nominated through an Express Entry stream

The Instructions list eleven provinces and territories that run Express Entry linked nomination streams: Ontario, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Manitoba, British Columbia, Prince Edward Island, Saskatchewan, Alberta, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon, and the Northwest Territories. Quebec and Nunavut do not appear on this list. Quebec runs its own selection system.

How the Express Entry System and Pool Work

Creating your profile

You submit an “expression of interest,” better known as your Express Entry profile, through IRCC’s online system. If a physical or mental disability prevents you from using the online system, IRCC must offer another way to submit, including a paper form. IRCC records the date and time it receives your profile, and that timestamp matters later as a tie-breaker.

Test results and credential assessments have expiry dates

When you create your profile, your language test results must be less than two years old, and your Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) must be less than five years old. These limits keep applying while you sit in the pool. If your language test turns two years old or your ECA turns five years old, IRCC treats you as no longer meeting the related requirements, and you lose eligibility until you update. The CRS also stops awarding the related points. This rule catches many candidates off guard, so check your expiry dates today.

How long your profile stays in the pool

Your profile stays in the pool until the earliest of these dates:

  1. The day your profile shows you no longer qualify
  2. One year after IRCC received your profile
  3. If you receive an ITA, the day you submit your PR application, or the day your 60-day window ends if you did not decline

You can update your profile at any time before you receive an ITA, and IRCC recalculates your CRS score each time. That means it is not too late to add a new job offer, a new test score, or new work experience.

Eligibility: Who Can Receive an ITA Express Entry Invitation?

To receive an ITA, your profile must show you meet the requirements of FSW, CEC, or FST, as if your profile were a full PR application. IRCC makes this decision based only on what you enter in your profile, and the Instructions state that an officer must not substitute their own evaluation at this stage. In practice, this places the responsibility on you. If you overstate your experience or choose the wrong NOC code, the problem surfaces later, at the PR application stage, where it can lead to refusal or a misrepresentation finding.

You can only apply under one class

If you qualify for more than one federal program, the Instructions direct which one you use. If you qualify for CEC, you apply under CEC. In any other case, you apply under FSW. This rule does not apply when IRCC invites you through a category-based draw grouping, such as a healthcare or trades category. IRCC makes this decision based on the information in your profile.

Your ITA is valid for 60 days

Your ITA window starts the day after IRCC issues it and ends on the 60th day after that. You must submit your complete PR application within that window. Gather documents before you receive an invitation, not after.

What Information IRCC Can Share With Provinces

The Instructions allow IRCC to share specific profile information with the eleven participating provinces and territories so they can find and nominate candidates. The list includes your age, profile dates, primary occupation, NOC codes, years of skilled work experience (Canadian and foreign), skilled trades experience, the province where you gained your work experience, your education, whether and where you studied in Canada, your Designated Learning Institution (DLI) number, your language results, family size, whether you have relatives in Canada, whether you meet the minimum income threshold, your CRS points by category, and whether you hold a qualifying job offer and whether that offer is in the province.

For candidates, this matters because provinces now see more detail about where you live, studied, and worked. A province can search the pool for candidates with ties to it. Keep this information accurate and complete in your profile.

The CRS still has four categories, but the totals have changed.

The core category rose by 70 points because of the new labour market integration factor. Under the core factors, IRCC now scores four things: age, education, official language proficiency, and labour market integration.

If two candidates have the same CRS score at a draw cut-off, IRCC breaks the tie using the date and time each profile was submitted. Earlier profiles win.

The Big Change: Labour Market Integration

This is the heart of the new Instructions. The labour market integration factor gives you the higher of two scores:

  1. Your Canadian work experience points (duration points plus any high-wage bonus), or
  2. Your qualifying job offer points

You do not receive both. IRCC takes whichever is greater, up to 150 points (single) or 140 points (with spouse).

What counts as a "high-wage occupation"?

The Instructions define a high-wage occupation as one where the median wage is at least 1.3 times the median wage of the entire Canadian workforce. IRCC places each high-wage occupation in one of three levels:

High-wage levelOccupation median wage compared with national median
2 times levelAt least 2 times
1.5 times levelAt least 1.5 times, but under 2 times
1.3 times levelAt least 1.3 times, but under 1.5 times

IRCC prepares and publishes this list on its website and builds it from Job Bank, Labour Force Survey, and Census of Population data for the three years before publication. IRCC can amend the list from time to time.

Two points deserve emphasis. First, the occupation sets your level, not your personal salary. A high salary in an occupation that is not on the list earns no high-wage points. Second, IRCC uses the occupation’s level as it appears on the list at the time it issues your invitation. If IRCC updates the list while you wait in the pool, your points can change.

The rules your Canadian work experience must meet

Not every hour of Canadian work counts. Under the new Instructions, your experience must meet all of these rules:

  1. You gained it in Canada within the 10 years before IRCC assigns the points.
  2. It is full-time (at least 30 hours a week) or the part-time equivalent.
  3. It is in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation, with one or more employers, paid by wages or commission.
  4. You held temporary resident status during the work and during any full-time study or training.
  5. You performed the lead statement and a substantial number of the main duties, including all essential duties, of the NOC code you claim.
  6. You listed the five-digit NOC code for each occupation in your profile.

These periods do not count:

  • Work while you were a full-time student
  • Self-employment (for the TEER-based route)
  • Unauthorized work

Overtime or two jobs at once still count as a single period of full-time experience for duration points. However, for the high-wage bonus, IRCC evaluates simultaneous jobs as separate periods to decide which level applies.

A special rule for physicians

The Instructions create a separate route for physicians in NOC unit groups 31100, 31101, and 31102 who provide publicly funded medical services. Their Canadian experience counts under its own paragraph, and the self-employment exclusion applies only to the TEER-based route. This reflects how many doctors in Canada bill provincial health plans rather than work as salaried employees. If you are a physician, confirm how IRCC treats your billing arrangement before you rely on these points.

Option B: Qualifying job offer points

Job offer points are back, but only for high-wage occupations:

Qualifying job offer inCRS points
An occupation at the 2 times level100
An occupation at the 1.5 times level70
An occupation at the 1.3 times level50
An occupation not on the high-wage list0

An offer outside the high-wage list earns no CRS points. Remember that you receive the higher of your job offer points and your work experience points, not both.

What makes a job offer "qualifying"?

The Instructions replace the old “offer of arranged employment” with a new “qualifying offer of employment.” To qualify, the offer must be full-time (at least 30 hours a week), in Canada, and continuous for at least one year from the day IRCC issues your PR visa. It must also fit one of these types:

  1. A physician offer in NOC 31100, 31101, or 31102 to provide publicly funded medical services
  2. A TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 offer from a single employer, paid by wages or commission
  3. A skilled trades offer from up to two employers, paid by wages or commission

Embassies, high commissions, consulates, and certain employers named in the Regulations cannot make a qualifying offer.

The new definition does not mention an LMIA. IRCC has confirmed that from November 3, 2026, you do not need an LMIA for a job offer to count for program eligibility or CRS points. For employers who planned their LMIA Canada 2026 strategy around Express Entry support, this is a major shift.

IRCC will test whether the offer is genuine

Removing the LMIA does not remove scrutiny. An officer must confirm the offer is genuine, considering whether:

  1. The employer actively runs the business the offer relates to
  2. The offer fits the employer’s reasonable employment needs
  3. The employer can reasonably fulfill the offer’s terms
  4. You are qualified for, able to perform, and likely to accept and carry out the job, based on your past experience or education related to the NOC lead statement and main duties
  5. The employer has complied with federal or provincial employment and recruitment laws in the province where you will work

The officer can request information from you or the employer. If the employer revokes the offer, it stops being a qualifying offer, and you lose the points. Employers should expect IRCC to ask for business records, organization charts, and proof of the role’s need.

How to Improve CRS Score 2026 Under the New Rules

  1. Check your NOC code against IRCC’s high-wage list. Match your real duties to the NOC lead statement and main duties, not your job title. Choosing a code because it sits on the list, when your duties fit a different code, creates serious misrepresentation risk.
  2. Update your profile now. Add any qualifying job offer details and recent work experience. IRCC recalculates your score after each update.
  3. Track your expiry dates. Retake language tests before they turn two years old and renew ECAs before five years.
  4. Push your language scores. Moving from CLB 8 to CLB 9 can unlock major skill transferability points.
  5. Consider French. NCLC 7 in all skills adds up to 50 points.
  6. Look at the Provincial Nominee Program. A nomination still adds 600 points. The PNP vs Express Entry Canada question is no longer either-or, since many candidates use both.
  7. Employers: review offers before you issue them. Make sure each offer is full-time, lasts at least one year from PR visa issuance, and comes with records that show genuine need.

What This Means for Employers

For Canadian employers, the removal of the LMIA requirement for Express Entry job offers lowers cost and saves time. However, IRCC moves the burden to the genuineness test. Employers should expect requests for evidence that the business is active, the role is needed, and the business has complied with employment laws. An employer with past compliance problems may see offers rejected. Keep in mind that this change concerns Express Entry points and eligibility. Work permit Canada rules, including when you need an LMIA to hire a temporary foreign worker, are separate.

How Long Does Express Entry Take in 2026 After These Changes?

The changes do not alter the 60-day window to submit your application after an ITA. IRCC reported that it would pause invitation rounds for up to two weeks starting November 3 while its systems recalculate scores across the pool. Expect the Express Entry draw schedule 2026 to resume after that, with cut-off scores that may shift as high-wage candidates move up. Check IRCC’s processing times tool for current figures, because these change often.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an LMIA to get Express Entry job offer points in 2026?

No. From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer does not need an LMIA to earn CRS points or to meet program eligibility. However, the offer must be in a high-wage occupation on IRCC’s list to earn points, and an officer must confirm it is genuine. Offers outside the list earn no points, even if they are otherwise valid.

Can I get both job offer points and high-wage work experience points?

No. The labour market integration factor gives you the higher of the two, not the total. The maximum is 150 points for single applicants and 140 for applicants with an accompanying spouse. If your Canadian experience already scores higher than your offer, the offer adds nothing to your CRS score.

What are the best NOC codes for Express Entry 2026?

Occupations at the 2 times level on IRCC’s high-wage list earn the most points: 70 for one year of Canadian experience or 100 for a qualifying offer. Still, the best NOC code is the one that truly matches your duties. Claiming a code you did not perform can lead to refusal or a misrepresentation finding, which carries a five-year bar.

Does my salary decide my high-wage level?

No. IRCC uses the national median wage of the occupation, not your personal salary. IRCC places each occupation on its published list based on Job Bank, Labour Force Survey, and Census data. Your points depend on your occupation’s level on that list at the time IRCC issues your invitation.

What does this mean for Express Entry for Indian applicants 2026?

The rules apply equally to all nationalities. Applicants abroad, including from India, can benefit mainly through qualifying high-wage job offers, which no longer need an LMIA. Strong English scores, foreign experience, and provincial nominations remain the main ways to build a competitive score for candidates without Canadian experience.
