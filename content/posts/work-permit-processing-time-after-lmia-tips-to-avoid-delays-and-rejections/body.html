Applying for a work permit in Canada based on a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) can be a complex and time-sensitive process. Understanding the expected processing times and knowing how to avoid common pitfalls are essential for both employers and foreign workers to ensure smooth hiring and immigration.

In this blog, we explain what affects the work permit processing time after LMIA, provide current timelines as of 2025, and share practical tips to help you avoid delays and rejections.

Understanding LMIA and Work Permit Processing Times in 2025

The LMIA is a key document that Canadian employers must obtain to demonstrate there is a need to hire a foreign worker because no suitable Canadian citizen or permanent resident is available for the job.

Once you have a positive LMIA, the foreign worker applies for a work permit based on that LMIA. The process involves two major steps with separate timelines:

    • LMIA Processing Time: This varies based on the stream (e.g., Global Talent, High-Wage, Seasonal Agricultural). As of mid-2025, LMIA processing can range from as fast as 7 business days for the Global Talent Stream to up to 3–4 months for streams like the High-Wage or Permanent Resident stream.

    • Work Permit Processing Time after LMIA Approval: After receiving the positive LMIA, IRCC processes the work permit application. Average processing times for employer-specific work permits usually range from 8 to 21 weeks, depending on the applicant’s country of residence and the processing centre.

Factors That Affect Work Permit Processing Time After LMIA

Several elements influence how long your work permit application takes:

    • Completeness of Application: Missing documents or incomplete forms lead to processing delays.
    • Biometric Submission: Biometrics must be provided quickly when requested; delays in this step extend overall times.
    • Type of Work Permit and Stream: Specialized permits or those under certain streams (e.g., Global Talent Stream) have faster processing.
    • Volume of Applications: Seasonal variations and application backlogs can cause fluctuations.
    • Regional Variations: Applications processed inside Canada versus outside may vary in timing, and processing centres in different countries have different workloads.
    • Security and Background Checks: Extended checks can add time in some cases.

Tips to Avoid Delays and Rejections in Work Permit Applications After LMIA

1. Submit a Complete and Accurate Application

Double-check that all required forms, supporting documents, and fees are included. An incomplete application is often the primary cause of delays and refusals.

2. Apply Early and Plan Ahead

LMIA and work permit processing can take months. Start the LMIA process early and file the work permit application immediately after receiving a positive LMIA.

3. Use the Correct Work Permit Stream

Choosing the appropriate LMIA stream and work permit category based on the job and candidate profile can reduce unnecessary delays.

4. Respond Promptly to IRCC Requests

If IRCC asks for additional information or documents, respond quickly to avoid application hold-ups.

5. Ensure Biometrics Are Provided on Time

When biometrics are requested, provide them promptly at a designated Service Canada centre or visa application centre.

6. Maintain Legal Status When Applying from Inside Canada

If the applicant is in Canada, ensure their status is valid and apply for extensions before expiry to benefit from implied status.

7. Keep Track of Processing Times

Regularly check IRCC and Service Canada websites for current LMIA and work permit processing times to adjust timelines accordingly.

8. Work with Experienced Immigration Professionals

A licensed consultant or lawyer can help identify errors, ensure compliant applications, and advocate for expedited processing if eligible.

Common Reasons Why Work Permit Applications Get Delayed or Refused After LMIA

    • Submission of incomplete or inconsistent documents
    • Missing biometrics or delays in biometric submission
    • Applicant failing to meet eligibility criteria, such as valid status, qualifications, or inadmissibility due to security issues
    • Errors in the LMIA job offer or misunderstanding of the job terms
    • Late application submission beyond the validity of the LMIA or permit expiration

Final Thoughts

Work permit processing after LMIA approval requires careful coordination, attention to detail, and timely action. While some factors like application volumes may be beyond your control, adhering to best practices can minimize delays and improve your chances of approval.

By understanding current processing times, preparing complete applications, and seeking professional guidance, both employers and foreign workers can navigate Canada’s immigration system more smoothly in 2025.
