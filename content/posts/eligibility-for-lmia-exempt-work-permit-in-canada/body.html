Canada offers a unique opportunity for citizens of certain countries to work without needing a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). If you’re from a country that has a Free Trade Agreement (FTA) with Canada, you may qualify for an LMIA exempt work permit—making your path to working in Canada faster and easier. This guide explains who is eligible, how the process works, and what you need to know to take advantage of these programs.

What Is an LMIA Exempt Work Permit?

Under normal circumstances, Canadian employers must obtain an LMIA to prove that no Canadian or permanent resident is available to do the job before hiring a foreign worker. This process can be lengthy and complex.

However, through the International Mobility Program (IMP), certain foreign nationals can bypass the LMIA requirement—provided they meet specific criteria, such as being citizens of countries with FTAs with Canada.

Why Does Canada Offer LMIA Exempt Work Permit?

Canada’s free trade agreements are designed to:

    • Facilitate economic growth
    • Attract skilled talent
    • Enhance global business and mobility

These agreements allow professionals, business people, and youth from partner countries to access Canadian job opportunities more easily.

Which Countries Qualify for LMIA Exempt Work Permits?

You may be eligible if you’re a citizen of a country with an FTA with Canada. Key agreements include:

    • CUSMA (formerly NAFTA): United States, Mexico
    • CETA: European Union member states (e.g., France, Germany, Spain)
    • Canada-UK Trade Continuity Agreement: United Kingdom
    • CPTPP: Australia, Japan, Singapore, Chile, Peru, Vietnam, Malaysia, Mexico, New Zealand, Brunei
    • Pacific Alliance: Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru
    • Other FTAs: South Korea, Panama, and more

Additionally, International Experience Canada (IEC) provides LMIA-exempt permits for youth from over 30 countries—including Australia, Germany, Ireland, Japan, and others—under the Working Holiday, Young Professionals, and International Co-op streams.

Types of LMIA Exempt Work Permits

Depending on your country and the applicable FTA, you may qualify under one of the following categories:

    • Professionals and Technicians: For individuals in approved skilled occupations
    • Intra-Company Transferees: Employees moving within the same company to a Canadian branch
    • Investors and Business Visitors: For those conducting business or investing in Canada
    • Youth Mobility Programs: Through IEC for young professionals, students, and travelers

Each FTA outlines specific roles and qualifications, so it’s important to review the agreement that applies to your situation.

Key Benefits of LMIA Exempt Work Permits

    • Faster Processing Times: No need to wait for an LMIA approval
    • Less Bureaucracy: Simpler paperwork for both employer and worker
    • No Job Advertising Required: Saves employers time and resources
    • Pathway to Permanent Residency: These permits may lead to PR through Express Entry or PNP.

How to Apply for an LMIA Exempt Work Permit

1. Check Your Eligibility

Ensure your country has an FTA with Canada and your occupation is covered.

2. Get a Job Offer

Obtain a valid job offer from a Canadian employer. It should include salary, job duties, and duration.

3. Employer Submits Offer

The employer must use the IRCC Employer Portal to submit the offer and pay a compliance fee ($230 CAD).

4. Prepare Required Documents

    • Passport (proof of citizenship)
    • Job offer letter
    • Resume and qualifications
    • Documents specific to your FTA category

5. Apply for Your Work Permit

Submit your application online or through a visa office outside Canada. Attach all documents and pay the applicable fees.

6. Await a Decision

Processing times are typically faster than LMIA-based permits, but they vary by country and category.

Key Tips for a Successful Application

    • Double-check eligibility: Review the FTA terms specific to your country and occupation
    • Submit complete documentation: Incomplete files are a common reason for delays or refusals
    • Employer compliance is crucial: Employers must follow all IRCC rules or risk penalties
    • Seek professional guidance: Consider consulting an immigration professional for complex cases

Conclusion

If you are a citizen of a country with a Free Trade Agreement with Canada, you may qualify for an LMIA exempt work permit, which offers a faster, simpler way to gain Canadian work experience. Always verify the specific rules for your country and occupation and ensure your application is complete and accurate.
