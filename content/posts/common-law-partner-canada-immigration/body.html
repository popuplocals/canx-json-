For immigration purposes, Canada treats common-law partners much like married spouses. You can sponsor a common-law partner, include one in your permanent residence application or support their work permit. But there is one requirement you cannot skip: you must prove you have lived together in a marriage-like relationship for at least 12 continuous months. Many applications fail not because the relationship is not genuine, but because the evidence is thin.

This guide explains how common-law partnerships work in Canadian immigration, what officers look for, which documents prove cohabitation best, how to handle time apart and the mistakes that lead to refusals.

Quick Summary

A common-law partner is someone you have lived with in a conjugal, marriage-like relationship for at least 12 continuous months. Short absences for work, travel or family reasons are generally acceptable if you continued to share a home. The strongest evidence shows a shared address over time, such as joint leases, bills, bank accounts and government documents, supported by a statutory declaration of common-law union and photos and correspondence.

What Counts as a Common-Law Relationship?

To qualify, you and your partner must have:

  • Lived together continuously for at least 12 months in a marriage-like relationship.
  • Shared a home, not just stayed together occasionally.
  • A relationship that is genuine and not entered into mainly for immigration purposes.

Same-sex and opposite-sex couples are treated equally. If you cannot live together because of serious legal or immigration barriers, the conjugal partner category may apply instead.

The 12-Month Rule and Short Absences

The 12 months must be continuous, but brief periods apart are generally acceptable. For example, a work trip, a family visit or a short business assignment does not usually break cohabitation, as long as you kept your shared home and your relationship continued. Explain any significant time apart in a letter with supporting evidence.

The Statutory Declaration of Common-Law Union

Most applicants submit IRCC’s Statutory Declaration of Common-Law Union, signed by both partners and commissioned by an authorized official. It confirms the date you began living together. It is important, but it is not enough on its own. Officers expect documents that support the date and the shared address.

Building a Strong Evidence Package

  • Cover the full 12 months, not just the most recent weeks.
  • Use documents from different sources, such as banks, landlords and governments.
  • Make sure addresses match exactly across documents.
  • Include a timeline letter explaining how your relationship developed and when you moved in together.
  • Explain gaps, moves or time apart honestly.

Common Mistakes That Lead to Refusals

  • Relying only on photos and a statutory declaration.
  • Documents showing different addresses during the claimed cohabitation period.
  • Claiming common-law status before reaching 12 months together.
  • Inconsistent move-in dates across forms.
  • Failing to explain long periods apart.

Common-Law Partners in Different Applications

Sponsorship

Canadian citizens and permanent residents can sponsor common-law partners through the family class, inside or outside Canada.

Economic applications

Common-law partners can be included as accompanying family members in Express Entry and other economic programs. Their language, education and Canadian work experience may add points to your profile.

Work permits

Common-law partners may qualify for spousal open work permits in certain situations, subject to the rules that narrowed eligibility in January 2025.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do you need to live together to be common-law in Canada?

You must live together continuously in a conjugal, marriage-like relationship for at least 12 months. Short absences for work, travel or family reasons are generally acceptable if you maintained your shared home and relationship. Explain any significant time apart with supporting evidence.

What documents prove common-law cohabitation?

Strong evidence includes joint leases or mortgages, joint bank accounts, shared bills, government documents showing the same address, insurance naming each other, and a Statutory Declaration of Common-Law Union. Support these with photos, travel records, correspondence and letters from people who know you as a couple.

Is a statutory declaration enough to prove common-law status?

No. The Statutory Declaration of Common-Law Union is important, but officers expect supporting documents that confirm you shared a home for at least 12 continuous months. Combine it with leases, bills, bank records and government documents showing the same address throughout the period.

Can I sponsor my common-law partner if we lived apart for a few weeks?

Usually, yes. Brief periods apart, such as work trips or family visits, do not generally break the 12-month cohabitation requirement if you kept your shared home and continued the relationship. Explain the time apart in your application and provide supporting evidence, such as travel bookings.
