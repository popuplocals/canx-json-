For international graduates in Canada, the first job after graduation has always mattered. From November 3, 2026, it matters more than ever. Under new Express Entry rules, 12 months of Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation can add up to 70 Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points on top of the points you already earn for that experience.

That means two graduates with the same degree, the same language scores, and the same year of work in Canada could now end up with very different CRS scores, based only on the occupation they chose. If you hold a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP), or will soon, this guide explains what the changes mean for your path to Canadian permanent residency and how to plan your next move.

Quick Summary

From November 3, 2026, Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) will award 70, 40, or 20 extra CRS points for at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience in one of 186 high-wage occupations. These points add to your regular Canadian work experience points. For international graduates, the occupation of your first skilled job in Canada now directly affects your score. Graduates already in the Express Entry pool will have their scores recalculated automatically, and invitation rounds will pause for up to two weeks while IRCC does this.

Why International Graduates Feel This Change Most

Most international graduates enter Express Entry through the Canadian Experience Class (CEC), which requires at least 12 months of skilled work experience in Canada within the last three years. That work experience is exactly what the new points reward.

Until now, a year of skilled Canadian work earned the same CRS points whether you worked as a software developer or in any other eligible skilled job. From November 3, IRCC will also look at which occupation that year was in. If the occupation appears on IRCC’s high-wage list, your score can rise significantly. If it does not, your points stay as they are, but other candidates may move ahead of you.

How the New Points Work for Canadian Work Experience

You need at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation. IRCC then adds:

  • 70 points for 12 months or more in one or more Level 1 occupations (at least 2 times the average median wage)
  • 40 points for 12 months or more combined in Level 1 or Level 2 occupations (at least 1.5 times the average median wage)
  • 20 points for 12 months or more combined across any high-wage occupations (at least 1.3 times the average median wage)

IRCC can count combined months across occupations at the same level or higher. For example, six months in one Level 2 job and six months in another Level 2 job can together qualify for 40 points.

Eligible Canadian work experience generally must be in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation and gained within the last 10 years. Work done while you were a full-time student does not count toward CEC eligibility, so plan on building most of your qualifying experience after graduation.

What This Looks Like in Real Numbers

IRCC’s current CRS criteria award a single candidate 40 points for one year of Canadian work experience, 53 for two years, and up to 80 for five years. Here is how the new points could change that for a single graduate with one year of Canadian experience:

  • A data scientist (NOC 21211, Level 2): 40 points for experience plus 40 high-wage points, for 80 in total
  • An accountant (NOC 11100, Level 3): 40 points for experience plus 20 high-wage points, for 60 in total
  • A graduate in an eligible skilled occupation not on the list: 40 points for experience, with no additional points

These examples show only the work experience part of the score. Your full CRS score also depends on age, education, language ability, and other factors. Candidates with an accompanying spouse or common-law partner earn slightly fewer points for Canadian work experience, and the new factor caps at 140 points for them instead of 150.

Choosing Your First Job: Best NOC Codes for Express Entry 2026

Many international graduates study in fields that lead directly to high-wage occupations. On IRCC’s list, these graduate-friendly roles stand out:

  • Technology: software developers and programmers (21232), data scientists (21211), cybersecurity specialists (21220), and database analysts (21223) at Level 2; web developers and programmers (21234) at Level 3
  • Business and finance: business development officers and market researchers and analysts (41402) at Level 2; financial auditors and accountants (11100), financial and investment analysts (11101), and human resources professionals (11200) at Level 3
  • Engineering technology: electrical and electronics engineering technologists and technicians (22310) and construction inspectors (22233) at Level 3
  • Health care: registered nurses (31301) at Level 2; medical laboratory technologists (32120) and medical sonographers (32122) at Level 3

Some of these occupations require provincial licensing or registration, including nursing and many health professions. Others, such as accounting and engineering roles, may need a professional designation for certain titles or duties. Check the rules in your province before you plan your career path around a specific code.

Keep in mind that your NOC code depends on your actual duties, not your job title. A “business analyst” title, for example, could fall under different codes depending on the work. Make sure your employer reference letter describes your duties clearly and accurately.

Already in Canada? Plan Your 12 Months Carefully

The new points add urgency to career planning for graduates on a PGWP, because your permit has a fixed end date. A few practical steps can help:

  1. Check the high-wage list before accepting a job. If you have two similar offers, the occupation could make a difference of 20 to 70 points.
  2. Track your months precisely. The points need at least 12 months of eligible experience. Keep pay stubs, contracts, and tax records.
  3. Work full-time where possible. IRCC counts full-time work, or an equal amount of part-time work. Part-time work takes longer to add up to 12 months.
  4. Watch your permit expiry date. Make sure you can reach 12 months of eligible experience, and receive an invitation, before your PGWP runs out.
  5. Avoid self-employment for your qualifying year. Self-employed work does not count toward CEC eligibility.

What About Job Offers?

From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation can earn 50, 70, or 100 CRS points, and it no longer needs a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). However, you cannot combine job offer points with high-wage work experience points. IRCC awards whichever gives you more.

For some graduates, a strong job offer could be worth more than their high-wage experience points. But IRCC officers will assess every job offer for genuineness, including whether the employer genuinely needs the role and whether you are qualified for it. A job offer must generally be for full-time, continuous work for at least one year after you become a permanent resident. If you already have one, add the details to your Express Entry profile before November 3.

If Your Occupation Is Not on the List

Not every graduate will work in a high-wage occupation, and that does not end your Express Entry journey. You still earn regular points for Canadian work experience. You can also improve your score through higher language test results, French language ability, or additional education.

Category-based draws and the Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) remain important options. Many provinces run streams for international graduates who studied in that province. The right route depends on your full profile, not one factor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do international graduates get the new high-wage CRS points?

Yes, if you have at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation. From November 3, 2026, IRCC will add 70, 40, or 20 points depending on the occupation’s wage level. These points add to your regular Canadian work experience points. Your degree alone does not earn high-wage points; the work experience does.

Does work experience during my studies count for the new points?

Work done while you were a full-time student does not count toward Canadian Experience Class eligibility. For that reason, most international graduates should plan to build their qualifying 12 months after graduation, usually on a PGWP. Check IRCC’s current rules for your exact situation before you count any months.

How long does Express Entry take in 2026 for PGWP holders?

IRCC aims to process most Express Entry applications within about six months after you submit a complete application, though times vary. Before that, you need a profile, a qualifying score, and an invitation. Plan around your PGWP expiry date, and note that invitation rounds will pause for up to two weeks after November 3 while IRCC recalculates scores.

What if my job is not on the high-wage list?

You will keep your regular CRS points for Canadian work experience, but you will not receive the new high-wage points. You can still improve your score through language results, French ability, or education. Category-based draws and provincial programs for international graduates are also worth reviewing with a licensed consultant.

Should I change jobs to get high-wage points?

Only if the move makes sense for your career and your timeline. Changing occupations restarts the clock for that occupation, and you need 12 months combined in qualifying roles. A job change close to your PGWP expiry can create risk. Calculate your options carefully, ideally with professional advice, before making a move.
