Hiring foreign workers can be an excellent solution for Canadian employers facing persistent skills shortages. It opens doors to global talent, helps fill critical roles, and strengthens a company’s competitiveness. At the same time, the process comes with legal requirements that must be followed carefully to avoid penalties, delays, or rejected applications.

If your business is planning to hire international talent in 2025, understanding the key legal considerations will ensure compliance and help you secure the right candidates quickly and efficiently.

1. Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) Compliance

In most cases, employers must obtain a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA) before hiring a foreign worker. This document, issued by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC), confirms that hiring a foreign worker will not negatively affect the Canadian labour market.

Key points to know:

    • Ensure job advertisements meet federal requirements (length, platforms, and content)
    • Offer wages and working conditions that meet or exceed the prevailing standards in your industry and region
    • Keep detailed recruitment records to demonstrate genuine efforts to hire Canadians or permanent residents first

Failing to follow LMIA requirements can result in application refusal or penalties.

2. Understanding LMIA-Exempt Categories

Not all foreign worker hires require an LMIA. Some fall under LMIA-exempt categories through the International Mobility Program (IMP), such as:

    • Intra-company transferees
    • Workers under international agreements like CUSMA
    • Certain post-graduation work permit holders

Employers must still submit an offer of employment through the Employer Portal and pay the compliance fee, even for LMIA-exempt hires.

3. Work Permit Rules and Validity

Once the LMIA is approved (or if the worker is LMIA-exempt), the candidate must apply for a work permit through Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC). Employers should:

    • Ensure the work permit type matches the job offer and LMIA details
    • Verify the permit’s start and end dates to avoid employing someone without valid status
    • Track expiration dates and start extension processes well in advance

4. Adhering to Employment Standards

Foreign workers are entitled to the same rights as Canadian employees. This includes:

    • Minimum wage compliance for the province or territory
    • Overtime pay and hours of work regulations
    • Paid vacation, holidays, and workplace safety protections

Employers should familiarize themselves with provincial employment standards to avoid violations.

5. Meeting Employer Compliance Obligations

ESDC conducts inspections to ensure employers are following program requirements. You may be asked to provide:

    • Proof of wages paid to foreign workers
    • Records of working conditions
    • Evidence that the job duties match the LMIA or work permit details

Non-compliance can lead to fines, bans from hiring foreign workers, and public disclosure of violations.

6. Providing Safe and Adequate Housing for Certain Roles

In sectors such as agriculture, employers are often required to provide housing for foreign workers. The housing must:

    • Meet local health and safety standards
    • Pass required inspections before occupancy
    • Be maintained throughout the employment period

Failing to meet housing standards can jeopardize your ability to participate in the Temporary Foreign Worker Program.

7. Respecting Immigration and Human Rights Laws

Employers must ensure that hiring practices do not discriminate against candidates based on race, gender, religion, or other protected grounds under the Canadian Human Rights Act. It is also essential to:

    • Avoid retaining a worker without valid work authorization
    • Provide fair treatment and equal opportunities to both domestic and foreign workers

8. Preparing for Renewals and Permanent Residency Pathways

Many foreign workers hope to transition to permanent residency. Employers who support this process often gain more loyal and committed employees. Familiarity with programs like the Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) or the Express Entry system can help employers retain talent long-term.

Conclusion

Hiring foreign workers can solve critical labour shortages, but it requires careful planning, strict adherence to legal requirements, and a commitment to fair treatment. From LMIA compliance to employment standards and immigration laws, each step must be handled with accuracy to avoid costly mistakes.
