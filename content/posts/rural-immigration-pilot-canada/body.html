The Rural and Northern Immigration Pilot (RNIP) helped thousands of newcomers settle in smaller Canadian communities between 2019 and 2024. It closed to new applications on August 31, 2024. In its place, Canada launched the Rural Community Immigration Pilot (RCIP) in January 2025, with 14 participating communities across six provinces. If you are searching for a rural immigration pilot in Canada in 2026, RCIP is the program you need to understand.

This guide explains how RCIP works, which communities participate, who qualifies, how to apply, and how to decide whether rural life in Canada is the right fit for you and your family.

Quick Summary

RNIP closed to new applications on August 31, 2024, and was replaced by the Rural Community Immigration Pilot (RCIP), launched on January 30, 2025. RCIP covers 14 communities in Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, British Columbia and Nova Scotia. Candidates need a full-time, non-seasonal job offer from a designated employer, a community recommendation, qualifying work experience, language scores of CLB 4 to 6 depending on the job, at least secondary education, settlement funds and a genuine intention to live in the community.

What Is the Rural Community Immigration Pilot?

RCIP is an employer-driven, community-based pathway to permanent residence. Local economic development organizations in each participating community designate employers, set priority sectors and recommend candidates to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC). The goal is to fill local labour shortages and help newcomers settle for the long term.

How to Apply Through RCIP: Step by Step

Step 1: Choose a community

Research the 14 communities, their priority sectors and their cost of living. Choose one where your skills match local needs and where you genuinely want to live.

Step 2: Find a designated employer

Each community publishes a list of designated employers. Your job offer must come from one of them and match a priority occupation where required.

Step 3: Apply for a community recommendation

Submit your application to the community’s economic development organization. If it recommends you, you receive a certificate of recommendation.

Step 4: Apply to IRCC for permanent residence

Submit your permanent residence application with the recommendation, job offer and supporting documents.

Step 5: Consider a work permit

With a recommendation and job offer, you may be eligible for a work permit so you can start working while IRCC processes your PR application.

Is a Rural Immigration Pilot Right for You?

RCIP offers a real advantage: you compete for a recommendation within one community rather than against the entire Express Entry pool. That can make it accessible to candidates whose CRS scores are below federal cut-offs, including workers in TEER 4 and 5 occupations. But it suits people who want to build a life in a smaller community, not those who plan to move to a big city after landing.

  • Good fit: you value community, lower housing costs and shorter commutes, and your skills match local needs.
  • Good fit: your employer is already designated and your job is in a priority sector.
  • Poor fit: you plan to move to Toronto or Vancouver soon after landing.
  • Poor fit: your occupation has little demand in any participating community.

What About Francophone Communities?

Alongside RCIP, Canada launched the Francophone Community Immigration Pilot to support French-speaking newcomers in selected francophone minority communities outside Quebec. If you speak French, explore that pilot as well.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Rural and Northern Immigration Pilot still open in 2026?

No. The Rural and Northern Immigration Pilot closed to new applications on August 31, 2024. Canada replaced it with the Rural Community Immigration Pilot (RCIP), launched on January 30, 2025, which covers 14 communities in Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta, British Columbia and Nova Scotia.

Which communities participate in the Rural Community Immigration Pilot?

The 14 communities are North Bay, Sudbury, Timmins, Sault Ste. Marie and Thunder Bay in Ontario; Steinbach, Altona/Rhineland and Brandon in Manitoba; West Kootenay, North Okanagan Shuswap and Peace Liard in British Columbia; Moose Jaw in Saskatchewan; Claresholm in Alberta; and Pictou County in Nova Scotia.

Do I need a job offer for RCIP?

Yes. You need a full-time, non-seasonal, permanent job offer from an employer designated by the participating community, and you need a certificate of recommendation from that community’s economic development organization. Without both, you cannot apply for permanent residence through RCIP.

What language level do I need for RCIP?

Language requirements depend on your job offer’s TEER category: CLB 6 for TEER 0 and 1, CLB 5 for TEER 2 and 3, and CLB 4 for TEER 4 and 5. You must take an approved language test, and your results must be valid when you apply.
