Becoming a permanent resident through spousal sponsorship is a major milestone. For many sponsored spouses, the next goal is Canadian citizenship: a Canadian passport, the right to vote and the security of never needing to renew status again. Being sponsored by a Canadian spouse does not create a special shortcut to citizenship, but the general rules can work in your favour, especially if you lived in Canada before becoming a permanent resident.

This guide explains the citizenship requirements for sponsored spouses in 2026, how to count your days, what time before permanent residence can count, tax and language rules, the citizenship test, fees and common mistakes.

Quick Summary

Sponsored spouses follow the same citizenship rules as other permanent residents. You must have been physically present in Canada for at least 1,095 days in the five years before you apply, filed taxes for at least three of those years if required, met language requirements and passed the citizenship test if you are 18 to 54, and not be prohibited. Days spent in Canada as a temporary resident or protected person before becoming a permanent resident can count as half days, up to 365 days.

Do Sponsored Spouses Get Faster Citizenship?

No. Marriage to a Canadian citizen does not shorten the citizenship requirements. Since 2017, there has also been no conditional permanent residence for sponsored spouses, so your PR status is not tied to staying in the relationship for a set period. You qualify for citizenship on the same basis as any other permanent resident.

Requirement 1: Physical Presence

You must have been physically present in Canada for at least 1,095 days (three years) during the five years immediately before the date you apply. The days do not need to be consecutive.

Counting time before permanent residence

If you lived in Canada as a temporary resident, such as a visitor, student or worker, or as a protected person, within the five-year period before you became a permanent resident, each of those days can count as half a day, up to a maximum of 365 days of credit. This can help many inland-sponsored spouses who lived in Canada during processing.

Use the official calculator

IRCC provides an online physical presence calculator. Keep travel records, passport stamps and entry and exit information to support your count.

Requirement 2: Income Taxes

You must have filed income taxes for at least three years within the five-year period, if you were required to file under the Income Tax Act. Keep your notices of assessment.

Requirement 3: Language

If you are 18 to 54 years old, you must show adequate knowledge of English or French, generally at Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) 4 or higher in speaking and listening. Acceptable proof includes approved test results and certain education or language program records.

Requirement 4: Citizenship Test

Applicants aged 18 to 54 must pass a test on Canada’s history, values, institutions and symbols, including rights and responsibilities of citizens. The official study guide covers the material.

Requirement 5: No Prohibitions

Certain criminal charges, convictions, being under a removal order or misrepresentation can prohibit you from becoming a citizen or delay your application.

What About Your Sponsorship Undertaking?

Your sponsor’s undertaking to support you, generally three years for a spouse or partner, continues even after you become a citizen. Citizenship does not cancel the undertaking. If you separate, the undertaking still applies to the sponsor for its full term.

Fees in 2026

Citizenship applicants pay a processing fee plus the right of citizenship fee for adults. The right of citizenship fee increased to $123 on March 31, 2026. Check IRCC’s fee page for the current total before applying.

Common Mistakes Sponsored Spouses Make

  • Applying a few days short of 1,095 days after miscounting trips.
  • Forgetting short trips abroad, including day trips to the United States.
  • Assuming marriage to a Canadian shortens the requirements.
  • Not keeping proof of language ability.
  • Leaving an expired PR card unrenewed when planning travel.

Frequently Asked Questions

How soon can a sponsored spouse apply for Canadian citizenship?

A sponsored spouse follows the same rules as other permanent residents. You need at least 1,095 days of physical presence in Canada in the five years before applying. Days in Canada as a temporary resident or protected person before PR can count as half days, up to 365 days of credit.

Does marrying a Canadian make citizenship faster?

No. Marriage to a Canadian citizen does not shorten citizenship requirements. Sponsored spouses must meet the same physical presence, tax filing, language and knowledge requirements as other permanent residents. However, time spent in Canada before becoming a permanent resident may count partially toward physical presence.

Does my time in Canada before PR count toward citizenship?

Yes, partially. Each day you spent in Canada as a temporary resident or protected person within the five years before applying, and before you became a permanent resident, counts as half a day, up to a maximum of 365 days of credit toward the 1,095-day requirement.

Does the sponsorship undertaking end when I become a citizen?

No. The sponsor’s undertaking, generally three years for a spouse or common-law partner, continues for its full term even after the sponsored person becomes a citizen. It also continues if the couple separates. The sponsor remains responsible for repaying certain social assistance received during that period.
