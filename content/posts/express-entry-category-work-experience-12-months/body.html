Category-based draws have become one of the fastest routes to an Invitation to Apply (ITA) in Express Entry. Healthcare, STEM, trades, education, transport and French-language draws often invite candidates with lower Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) scores than general or Canadian Experience Class draws. But an invitation from a category draw comes with a legal condition: you must actually have met that category’s requirements when you were invited. If you did not, your application can be refused, even after IRCC invited you.

This guide explains the current work experience rule for occupation-based categories, the legal basis for category refusals and how to confirm your eligibility before you accept an ITA.

Quick Summary

According to IRCC’s category-based selection page, most occupation-based categories require at least 12 months of full-time work experience, or an equal amount of part-time experience, in a single eligible occupation within the past three years. The experience does not need to be continuous and can generally be gained in Canada or abroad, except in categories that require Canadian experience. Under section 11.2 of IRPA, an officer may not issue a permanent resident visa if you did not meet the category requirements at the time of your invitation or when IRCC received your application.

The Legal Basis for Category-Based Selection

Category-based selection rests on two parts of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (IRPA):

ProvisionRole in category draws
IRPA s. 10.3Allows the Minister to give instructions on how candidates are ranked and invited, including categories based on economic goals
Ministerial InstructionsSet out each category, its eligible occupations and its requirements
IRPA s. 11.2(1)Prohibits issuing a PR visa if, at the time of invitation or when the application was received, you did not meet the criteria or category requirements
IRPA s. 11.2(2)Provides limited exceptions, such as an age-related change after the invitation

This means a category ITA is conditional. The officer will look back and ask: on the day you were invited, did you actually meet the category’s requirements?

According to reporting by Moving2Canada, IRCC moved most categories from six months to 12 months of experience in February 2026. Always confirm the requirement that applied to your specific draw, because requirements can change between draws.

The 2026 Categories

IRCC’s category-based selection page lists the following categories:

  • French-language proficiency.
  • Healthcare and social services occupations.
  • Science, technology, engineering and math (STEM) occupations.
  • Trade occupations.
  • Education occupations.
  • Transport occupations.
  • Physicians with Canadian work experience.
  • Senior managers with Canadian work experience.
  • Researchers with Canadian work experience.
  • Skilled military recruits.

Why Category Applicants Get Refused

Not enough experience in a single occupation

Six months as a nurse aide and six months as a personal support worker does not equal 12 months in a single eligible occupation. Each occupation counts separately.

Experience outside the three-year window

Experience must fall within the past three years. Older experience may count for your program but not for the category.

The occupation is not on the category list

Your NOC must match one of the occupations listed for that category. Close is not enough.

NOC mismatch

If your duties do not match the eligible NOC under the lead statement and main duties test, the officer may find that you were never eligible for the category.

Experience that does not meet the program requirements

You must still meet your underlying program, such as the Canadian Experience Class under section 87.1 of the IRPR or the Federal Skilled Worker class under section 75. Category eligibility does not replace these requirements.

Status and employment changes

If the occupation or experience you relied on is not supported by your documents when IRCC receives your application, section 11.2 can apply at that stage as well.

How to Check Your Eligibility Before Accepting an ITA

  • Confirm which category invited you and the requirements in force for that draw.
  • Count your months of experience in the single eligible occupation, within the past three years, using reference letters.
  • Confirm your NOC code matches your real duties.
  • Confirm you also meet the requirements of your underlying Express Entry program.
  • Keep evidence for every claim, including hours per week, dates and duties.

If anything does not add up, get advice before you submit. Declining an ITA you cannot support is usually far better than submitting an application that will be refused.

A Note on Upcoming Changes

IRCC’s Forward Regulatory Plan for 2026 to 2028 proposes replacing the current Express Entry programs with a single new class, according to CIC News. As of the date of this article, those changes are proposals, not law. Current applicants are assessed under the rules in force, but you should watch for updates.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much work experience do I need for an Express Entry category draw?

According to IRCC, most occupation-based categories require at least 12 months of full-time work, or an equal amount of part-time work, in a single eligible occupation within the past three years. The experience does not need to be continuous. Physicians, senior managers and researchers must have the experience in Canada.

Can IRCC refuse me after a category-based ITA?

Yes. Under section 11.2 of IRPA, an officer may not issue a permanent resident visa if, at the time of the invitation or when IRCC received your application, you did not meet the category requirements. An ITA is conditional on your documents proving that you were eligible.

Can I combine experience from two occupations for a category?

Generally, no. IRCC requires the 12 months of experience to be in a single occupation listed for that category. Experience in two different eligible occupations is counted separately, so six months in each would not meet the requirement for most categories.

Does category experience have to be continuous?

No. According to IRCC’s category-based selection page, the 12 months of experience does not need to be continuous, but it must fall within the past three years and be in a single eligible occupation. Your underlying Express Entry program may have its own separate continuity rules.
