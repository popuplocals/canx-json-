What Is a Post Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)?

A PGWP is an open work permit that allows international students who graduate from eligible Canadian schools to work for any employer in canada. Depending on the length of your program, you may receive a PGWP valid for up to three years.

Why it matters:

    • It provides Canadian work experience—a key factor for permanent residence.
    • It allows flexibility in employment—no job offer or LMIA required.
    • It’s non-renewable, so choosing the right school and program the first time is critical.

How to Choose a Canadian School That Qualifies for a PGWP: A Smart Guide for International Students

Step 1: Choose a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)

A Designated Learning Institution (DLI) is a school approved by the Canadian government to host international students.

Why it’s important: Only graduates of DLIs can apply for a PGWP.

How to check:

    • Visit the official IRCC DLI list.
    • Filter by province, then find your school and confirm its DLI number.
    • Double-check whether the school and program specifically state PGWP eligibility.

Pro Tip: Being a DLI alone doesn’t guarantee PGWP eligibility—not all programs at DLIs qualify.

Step 2: Confirm Your Program Is PGWP-Eligible

Even within DLIs, only certain programs are PGWP-eligible.

Program length

    • Must be at least 8 months long (or 900 hours in Quebec).
    • Must be completed through full-time study, except for the final semester.

Program type

    • Degree programs (bachelor’s, master’s, PhD): generally PGWP-eligible.
    • Non-degree programs (diplomas, certificates): eligibility depends on field of study and recent IRCC updates.

Step 3: Check the Field of Study Requirements

Recent Changes as of July 4, 2025

Canada’s PGWP eligibility list for non-degree programs is now linked to fields of study that correspond with jobs in long-term labour shortages. These lists can change based on shifting economic needs.

Here’s what you need to know:

    • On June 25, 2025, IRCC removed 178 fields of study from the eligible list and added 119 new ones.
    • On July 4, 2025, IRCC made further adjustments, adding back some of the fields that were removed on June 25.
    • The restored fields of study will remain PGWP-eligible until the next list update in early 2026.

What if you applied during the transition?

If you submitted your study permit application between June 25 and July 4, 2025, for a program that was initially removed but then re-added on July 4—you will still meet the PGWP field of study requirement when you graduate.

How to check your field:

    1. Ask your school for the program’s CIP code (Classification of Instructional Programs).
    2. Cross-check that CIP code with the latest PGWP-eligible list published by IRCC.

Reminder: Degree programs are still PGWP-eligible regardless of field of study. These changes mostly affect non-degree programs (e.g., diplomas, certificates).

Step 4: Meet Language and Other PGWP Conditions

Canada now requires language proof for most students seeking PGWP eligibility.

Language Requirements:

    • CLB/NCLC 7 for degree programs
    • CLB/NCLC 5 for eligible non-degree programs

Other Key Conditions:

    • Maintain full-time status during studies
    • Apply for your Post Graduation Work Permit within 180 days of receiving your final transcript
    • You must have had a valid study permit at some point during those 180 days

Summary Checklist: Picking the Right School and Program

    • Confirm the school is a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)
    • Make sure the program is full-time and at least 8 months long
    • Verify the program’s CIP code is PGWP-eligible
    • Meet language proficiency requirements
    • Maintain full-time student status
    • Stay informed via the IRCC website and consult advisors regularly

Important Considerations

    • You can only get a PGWP once—choose your program wisely.
    • Fully online programs are not eligible.
    • Always keep documentation: transcripts, program completion letters, and permits.
    • Post Graduation Work Permit-eligible fields of study are subject to change—updates are based on labour market needs.
