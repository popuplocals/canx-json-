Few steps in the Canadian immigration process create more quiet anxiety than the immigration medical exam. Applicants worry about old injuries, chronic conditions, a family member’s health or simply not knowing what will happen in the room. The reality is far less dramatic. For most applicants, the medical exam for Canada immigration is a routine appointment that takes a few hours and leads to no further questions.

This guide explains who needs a medical exam in 2026, when to book it, what the panel physician checks, how long results stay valid, and what happens if a health issue comes up, including how Canada’s “excessive demand” rules work.

Quick Summary

All permanent residence applicants and their family members, even those not coming to Canada, need an immigration medical exam. Express Entry applicants complete an upfront exam after receiving an invitation to apply. Most other PR applicants wait for IRCC’s instructions and must complete the exam within 30 days. Only IRCC-approved panel physicians can perform the exam, and results stay valid for 12 months.

Who Needs an Immigration Medical Exam?

According to IRCC’s medical exam requirements for permanent residents, all permanent residence applicants and their family members must complete a medical exam, even family members who are not coming to Canada with you. This rule matters, because an unexamined family member can prevent you from sponsoring them later.

Some temporary residence applicants also need a medical exam, depending on their country of residence, how long they plan to stay and the type of work they will do, such as healthcare or childcare.

When Should You Get Your Medical Exam?

Express Entry applicants

If you receive an invitation to apply through Express Entry, book an upfront medical exam with a panel physician before you submit your application. Include the proof of exam the physician gives you.

Other permanent residence applicants

For most other PR programs, IRCC sends medical instructions after you submit your complete application. You must complete the exam within 30 days of receiving those instructions.

Exemption for some applicants already in Canada

IRCC has a temporary public policy, extended until October 5, 2029, that exempts certain applicants in Canada from a new exam if they completed a recent immigration medical exam with low or no risk findings. Read the policy conditions carefully before relying on it.

Choosing a Panel Physician

Only physicians on IRCC’s approved panel can perform immigration medical exams. Your family doctor cannot do it, even if they know your history well. Use IRCC’s “find a panel physician” tool, linked from its medical exam pages, to locate an approved clinic. Panel physicians set their own fees, so ask about the cost when booking.

What to Bring to Your Appointment

  • A valid passport or other government-issued photo identification.
  • Your medical instruction form, if IRCC provided one.
  • Your glasses or contact lenses.
  • A list of current medications.
  • Medical records or specialist reports for any past or ongoing condition.
  • Your vaccination records, if available.

Bringing complete medical records for any known condition is one of the best ways to avoid a later request for more information.

What Happens During the Exam?

The panel physician reviews your medical history, records your height, weight, blood pressure and vision, and conducts a physical examination. Depending on your age and history, you may also complete:

TestWho usually needs it
Chest X-rayApplicants aged 11 and older
Blood and urine testsApplicants aged 15 and older
Additional tests or specialist referralsApplicants with specific findings or medical history

The physician sends results directly to IRCC, usually electronically. You do not normally receive the results yourself.

What Are Officers Looking For?

An immigration medical exam assesses whether a health condition is likely to endanger public health or public safety, or might cause excessive demand on Canada’s health or social services. It does not judge whether you are “healthy enough” in a general sense. Many people with controlled chronic conditions immigrate to Canada every year.

Understanding Excessive Demand

Excessive demand applies when the expected cost of your health or social services would exceed a threshold IRCC sets annually, or when your needs would add to waiting lists in a way that affects Canadians’ access to care. Importantly, the excessive demand rule does not apply to everyone. Sponsored spouses, common-law partners and dependent children of Canadian citizens and permanent residents, as well as refugees and protected persons and their dependants, are exempt from the excessive demand assessment.

If an officer believes you may fall under excessive demand, IRCC sends a procedural fairness letter before deciding. You then have a chance to respond with medical evidence and, where relevant, a plan for how you will manage costs.

How Long Are Medical Results Valid?

Medical exam results are valid for 12 months. If your permanent residence is not finalized within that period, IRCC may ask you to complete a new exam. Your confirmation of permanent residence usually cannot extend beyond your medical validity, so delays can matter.

Common Medical Exam Mistakes

  • Visiting a regular doctor instead of an IRCC panel physician.
  • Failing to disclose past conditions, which can raise credibility concerns later.
  • Leaving a non-accompanying family member unexamined.
  • Missing the 30-day deadline after IRCC sends instructions.
  • Booking an upfront exam too early, so results expire during processing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who needs a medical exam for Canada PR?

All permanent residence applicants and their family members need an immigration medical exam, including family members who are not coming to Canada. Express Entry applicants complete an upfront exam after an invitation to apply, while most other PR applicants wait for IRCC’s instructions and complete the exam within 30 days.

How long is a Canadian immigration medical exam valid?

Immigration medical exam results are valid for 12 months. If your application is not finalized within that time, IRCC may request a new exam. Because your confirmation of permanent residence usually cannot extend beyond your medical validity, time your exam carefully and respond promptly to IRCC requests.

Can my family doctor do the immigration medical exam?

No. Only IRCC-approved panel physicians can perform immigration medical exams for Canada. Use IRCC’s online tool to find a panel physician near you. You can still bring records and specialist reports from your family doctor to help the panel physician assess any existing condition.

Will a health condition stop me from immigrating to Canada?

Not necessarily. Many people with controlled conditions immigrate successfully. IRCC assesses risks to public health and safety and, for some applicants, whether a condition might cause excessive demand on health or social services. Sponsored spouses, dependent children and refugees are exempt from excessive demand, and IRCC must give you a chance to respond before refusing.
