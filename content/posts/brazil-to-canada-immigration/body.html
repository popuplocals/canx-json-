Brazilians are among the most active newcomers in Canada’s study, work and skilled immigration programs, drawn by career opportunities, safety and quality of life. Many start as students, then build careers and apply for permanent residence. In 2026, that study-to-PR path still works, but it requires smarter planning, while Express Entry rewards Brazilians who invest in strong English and, increasingly, French.

This guide explains Brazil to Canada immigration in 2026: travel rules, study and work options, permanent residence pathways, key documents and practical tips.

Quick Summary

Brazilian citizens generally need a visitor visa, but those who held a Canadian visa in the past 10 years or hold a valid US non-immigrant visa may qualify for an Electronic Travel Authorization when flying to Canada. Popular pathways include studying in Canada, employer-supported work permits, Express Entry and provincial nominee programs. Strong English or French scores, an Educational Credential Assessment and well-documented work experience are essential.

Visiting Canada From Brazil

Brazil is one of the countries whose citizens can apply for an Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) instead of a visitor visa if they have held a Canadian visitor visa in the past 10 years or currently hold a valid United States non-immigrant visa, and are flying to or through a Canadian airport. Other Brazilian travellers need a visitor visa.

Pathway 1: Study in Canada

Many Brazilians come to Canada as students. In 2026, most new students need a provincial attestation letter, although master’s and doctoral students at public institutions are exempt since January 1, 2026. Choose programs that lead to a Post-Graduation Work Permit and occupations in demand. Spouses of students qualify for open work permits only in certain programs, such as master’s programs of at least 16 months, doctoral programs and eligible professional degrees.

Pathway 2: Work in Canada

Brazilian professionals may qualify for employer-supported work permits through an LMIA or an LMIA exemption, such as intra-company transfers for multinational employers. Canadian work experience supports permanent residence applications later.

Pathway 3: Express Entry

Brazilian professionals in IT, engineering, finance, health care and other skilled fields can qualify through the Federal Skilled Worker Program or, after working in Canada, the Canadian Experience Class. Language scores have the biggest impact on your Comprehensive Ranking System score.

The French advantage

Some Brazilians find French easier to learn because of similarities with Portuguese. French at NCLC 7 or higher in all four abilities adds 25 to 50 CRS points and opens French-language category draws, which carried some of the lowest cut-offs in 2026. Francophone mobility work permits can also help French-speaking workers find jobs outside Quebec.

Pathway 4: Provincial Nominee Programs

Provinces recruit Brazilian workers in technology, health care, trades and other sectors. Many streams require a job offer or a connection to the province.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Brazilians need a visa to visit Canada?

Usually, yes. However, Brazilian citizens who have held a Canadian visitor visa in the past 10 years or currently hold a valid US non-immigrant visa may apply for an Electronic Travel Authorization instead when flying to or through a Canadian airport. Everyone else needs a visitor visa.

What is the best way to immigrate to Canada from Brazil?

It depends on your profile. Skilled professionals with strong English or French may qualify through Express Entry. Many Brazilians start with studies or employer-supported work permits, gain Canadian experience and then apply through the Canadian Experience Class or provincial programs.

Can learning French help Brazilians immigrate to Canada?

Yes. French at NCLC 7 or higher in all four abilities adds 25 to 50 CRS points and opens French-language category draws, which carried some of the lowest cut-offs in 2026. French skills may also qualify workers for francophone mobility work permits outside Quebec.

Can my spouse work in Canada while I study?

Only in certain cases. Since January 21, 2025, spouses qualify for open work permits only if the student is in a master’s program of at least 16 months, a doctoral program or an eligible professional program. Since March 4, 2026, applications are refused if the student is in their final term.
