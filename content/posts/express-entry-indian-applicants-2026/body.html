Express Entry has long been a popular route to Canada for Indian professionals, especially in technology, engineering, and finance. On November 3, 2026, the rules that rank them will change. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) will begin awarding extra Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points for Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer in one of 186 high-wage occupations, and job offers will no longer need a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA).

For Indian applicants, the impact depends heavily on one question: are you applying from India, or are you already working in Canada? This guide explains what changes for each group, which occupations popular with Indian professionals benefit, and the risks every applicant should know about.

Quick Summary

From November 3, 2026, Express Entry candidates can earn extra CRS points for 12 months of Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation (20, 40, or 70 points), or a qualifying job offer in one (50, 70, or 100 points). Indian applicants already working in Canada in occupations such as software development or IT management could see meaningful score increases. Applicants in India without Canadian experience can only benefit through a qualifying job offer, which IRCC officers will now assess closely for genuineness. Foreign work experience points do not change.

What Changes on November 3, 2026

IRCC is adding a new section to the CRS called the labour market integration factor. It rewards two things:

  • Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation: 70, 40, or 20 additional points for at least 12 months, depending on the occupation’s wage level
  • A qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation: 100, 70, or 50 points, with no LMIA required

Candidates cannot combine the two. IRCC awards whichever gives more points. The maximum under the new factor is 150 points for a candidate without a spouse and 140 for a candidate with an accompanying spouse or common-law partner.

IRCC will recalculate every score in the pool automatically. Invitation rounds will pause for up to two weeks while it does this.

Indian Applicants Already in Canada: The Biggest Winners

Many Indian professionals in Canada work in occupations that appear on IRCC’s high-wage list. If you have at least 12 months of eligible Canadian work experience in one of these roles, the new points could lift your score significantly:

  • Software developers and programmers (21232): Level 2, 40 additional points
  • Software engineers and designers (21231): Level 2, 40 additional points
  • Data scientists (21211) and cybersecurity specialists (21220): Level 2, 40 additional points
  • Computer and information systems managers (20012): Level 1, 70 additional points
  • Financial auditors and accountants (11100) and financial analysts (11101): Level 3, 20 additional points
  • Registered nurses (31301): Level 2, 40 additional points

For example, a single software developer with one year of Canadian experience currently earns 40 points for that experience. From November 3, the same candidate can earn an additional 40, for 80 in total. Check that your NOC code matches your actual duties, because IRCC assesses the code, not the job title.

Indian Applicants Applying From India: What Changes for You

If you have no Canadian work experience, the new work experience points do not apply to you. Your score continues to rely on core factors: age, education, language ability, foreign work experience, and the skill transferability combinations that link them.

Your main route to the new points is a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation. A Level 1 job offer can add 100 points, which can transform a profile. But this is also where applicants need the most caution, because IRCC officers will now decide whether each job offer is genuine without an LMIA behind it.

As more candidates with Canadian experience gain points, CRS cut-offs in general draws may rise. That could make it harder for candidates from abroad to compete on core factors alone. Category-based draws, French language ability, and Provincial Nominee Program options become more important to review.

Job Offers From India: Proceed With Care

The removal of the LMIA is good news for genuine employers and candidates. It also creates a risk. Without an LMIA, some people may try to sell fake or paper job offers to applicants abroad, promising quick CRS points.

IRCC has said officers will assess whether the employer actively runs the business, whether the offer reflects a reasonable need, whether the employer can meet the terms, whether the candidate can do the job and is likely to accept it, and whether the employer complies with employment and recruitment laws. A job offer that fails these tests will not earn points, and a finding of misrepresentation can bar a candidate from applying to come to Canada for five years.

Protect yourself with a few simple rules:

  • Never pay an employer or agent for a job offer.
  • Be wary of anyone who guarantees CRS points, an invitation, or permanent residence.
  • Verify that the employer is a real, operating Canadian business.
  • Make sure the job matches your real education and experience.
  • Work only with a licensed Canadian immigration consultant or lawyer. You can check a consultant’s licence on the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants public register.

How to Improve Your CRS Score in 2026 as an Indian Applicant

  1. Maximize your language scores. Language ability is one of the most valuable CRS factors. Strong results in an approved English test, and any French ability, can add significant points.
  2. Get your Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) right. Indian degrees need an ECA from an IRCC-designated organization to earn education points. Make sure your credentials are assessed at the correct level.
  3. Document your foreign work experience carefully. Reference letters should describe duties that match your NOC code, with dates, hours, and pay.
  4. Consider Canadian experience. For candidates who can build it, 12 months in a high-wage occupation now adds both regular and high-wage points.
  5. Review provincial options. Several provinces run tech and occupation-specific streams that can lead to a provincial nomination.

  • Before November 3, 2026: Candidates with a job offer must add the details to their Express Entry profile.
  • November 3, 2026: New points and LMIA changes take effect.
  • Up to two weeks after November 3: IRCC recalculates scores and pauses invitation rounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do the new CRS points help Indian applicants applying from India?

Only through a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation, unless you already have Canadian work experience. The new work experience points require at least 12 months of eligible experience in Canada. Foreign work experience points do not change. Applicants abroad should focus on language scores, education, and provincial options alongside any genuine job offer.

Do Indian applicants need an LMIA for an Express Entry job offer in 2026?

No. From November 3, 2026, a qualifying job offer no longer needs an LMIA to earn the new CRS points. However, IRCC officers will assess every offer for genuineness, so employers must be real, operating businesses with a genuine need for the role. Never pay for a job offer, as fake offers can lead to a five-year ban.

Is software development a high-wage occupation for Express Entry?

Yes. Software developers and programmers (NOC 21232) and software engineers and designers (NOC 21231) both sit at Level 2 of IRCC’s high-wage list. That means 40 additional points for 12 months of Canadian work experience, or 70 points for a qualifying job offer. Web developers and programmers (NOC 21234) sit at Level 3.

How long does Express Entry take in 2026 for Indian applicants?

IRCC aims to process most Express Entry applications within about six months after it receives a complete application, though times vary. The wait before an invitation depends on your CRS score and the draws IRCC holds. Indian applicants should also allow time for language tests, the ECA, and police certificates.

Will CRS cut-offs rise after November 3, 2026?

They may, but no one can predict exact numbers. Candidates with high-wage Canadian experience or job offers will gain points, which could push cut-offs higher in general draws. Category-based draws set their own cut-offs. Watch the first draws after the recalculation pause before making major decisions.
