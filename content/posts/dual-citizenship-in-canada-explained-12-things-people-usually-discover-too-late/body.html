One of the most common questions people ask after becoming a permanent resident is:

“If I become Canadian, do I lose my original citizenship?”

It sounds like a simple question.

But it creates a lot of confusion.

People hear different things from friends, family members, WhatsApp groups, social media, and online forums. One person says Canada allows dual citizenship. Another says you automatically lose your original nationality. Someone else says it depends on where you were born.

Eventually people end up more confused than when they started.

At Can X Global Solutions, we often speak with individuals who delayed applying for Canadian citizenship because they believed becoming Canadian meant giving something up.

The reality is more nuanced.

If you are planning to apply for Canadian citizenship or are simply exploring your options, here are some things worth understanding before making assumptions.

1. Canada Generally Allows Dual Citizenship

Let’s start with the good news.

Canada generally allows individuals to hold Canadian citizenship while keeping citizenship from another country.

This means becoming Canadian does not automatically mean surrendering your original nationality.

However, many people stop reading after hearing that.

That creates problems.

Because Canada only controls Canadian rules.

Your home country controls its own.

2. Your Home Country May See Things Differently

This is where many people get surprised.

Some countries fully allow dual citizenship.

Some allow it only under certain situations.

Some require paperwork.

Others restrict it completely.

Many people focus entirely on Canadian rules and never check what happens on the other side.

That mistake usually gets discovered late.

3. Having Two Passports Does Not Always Mean You Have Dual Citizenship

People often assume:

“I have two passports, so I obviously have dual citizenship.”

Not necessarily.

Passports and legal citizenship status do not always tell the same story.

Some people hold travel documents or overseas nationality status that creates confusion.

It is important to understand exactly what legal status you hold rather than assuming.

4. Dual Citizenship Is Not Just About Travel

When people think about citizenship, they usually think about passports.

But citizenship can affect much more.

Depending on the country involved, questions can arise regarding:

  • property ownership
  • inheritance rights
  • taxation rules
  • government obligations
  • voting rights

Many people discover these details only years later.

5. Some Countries Want You to Use Their Passport

This catches many travelers off guard.

Some countries require their own citizens to enter and leave using that country’s passport.

People often discover this while standing at an airport counter.

If you eventually hold more than one citizenship, understanding travel rules becomes important.

6. Your Children May Benefit Too

Parents often focus entirely on themselves and overlook something important.

Children can sometimes receive citizenship rights through:

  • parents
  • place of birth
  • ancestry
  • timing of birth

Many families later discover their children already had rights they never knew existed.

7. Becoming Canadian Does Not Solve Every International Issue

Some people believe Canadian citizenship automatically protects them everywhere.

Life is not always that simple.

If another country also recognizes you as its citizen, legal situations can become more complicated.

Understanding how countries view dual nationality matters more than many people realize.

8. Marriage Does Not Automatically Create Citizenship

This misunderstanding appears constantly.

People still believe marrying a Canadian immediately creates citizenship rights.

Marriage can create immigration pathways.

Marriage itself does not create automatic citizenship.

There are still processes, applications, and legal requirements involved.

9. Citizenship Can Affect Future Generations

Many people applying today focus only on their own situation.

But citizenship can extend beyond one person.

Questions eventually come up:

Will children qualify?

Can citizenship pass down?

Will future rules affect my family?

Thinking long-term matters.

10. Small Details Matter More Than People Think

Many citizenship issues involve details people overlook:

  • previous immigration history
  • travel patterns
  • family history
  • country of birth
  • documentation differences

Two people with similar backgrounds can end up with completely different outcomes.

11. Internet Advice Is Often Wrong

Immigration myths spread fast.

Especially online.

You have probably heard things like:

“You automatically lose your old citizenship.”

“Marriage gives you citizenship.”

“Children automatically inherit everything.”

Citizenship rules are highly fact-specific.

What worked for one person may not apply to another.

12. Most People Ask Questions Too Late

This may be the biggest lesson of all.

Many people start asking questions after:

  • submitting applications
  • booking travel
  • discovering document problems
  • receiving unexpected news

Earlier planning usually creates smoother outcomes.
