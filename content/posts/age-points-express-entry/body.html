A single applicant aged 29 earns 110 CRS points for age. On their 30th birthday, that drops to 105. By 35, it falls to 77, and by 40, to 50. Age is the one Express Entry factor you cannot improve, which is exactly why it demands a strategy. Many candidates over 30 still receive invitations every month; they simply compensate in other areas.

This guide shows you the full age points table for your CRS score 2026, explains when IRCC locks your age, and outlines practical ways to offset the decline.

Quick Summary

Express Entry awards maximum age points between 20 and 29: 110 for single applicants and 100 with an accompanying spouse. Points start declining at 30 and reach zero at 45. IRCC calculates age points based on your age when you receive an ITA, and IRCC has stated that proposed 2026 reforms would not change age-related CRS points. Candidates over 30 can offset the decline through language, French, Canadian experience and provincial nomination.

When Does IRCC Lock Your Age?

Your CRS score updates as your age changes while you remain in the pool, so a birthday can lower your score overnight. Once IRCC issues you an ITA, your age points are based on your age on the date of the invitation. If you have a birthday after receiving your ITA but before submitting your application, IRCC does not reduce your points for that birthday (verify current IRCC instructions before publishing).

Age in the FSW 67-Point Grid

Federal Skilled Worker candidates also face an age factor on the 67-point eligibility grid. Applicants aged 18 to 35 earn the maximum 12 points, then lose one point per year until reaching zero at 47. Unlike CRS age points, this grid determines eligibility, not ranking, so older FSW candidates must make up points through education, language and experience to reach 67.

Does Age Matter for Every Draw Type?

Age counts in your CRS score for every Express Entry draw. However, its practical impact varies. Category-based draws with lower cut-offs, such as French proficiency, senior managers or physicians, leave more room for older candidates. The senior managers round on September 16, 2026, for example, closed at 389, a level many experienced candidates over 35 can reach.

Strategies to Offset Age Point Decline

1. Push Language Scores to CLB 9 or Higher

Language offers the biggest controllable gain. Reaching CLB 9 in all four abilities can add roughly 80 points through core and transferability factors for a candidate with strong education and foreign experience, more than offsetting the age difference between 30 and 37.

2. Learn French

NCLC 7 or higher in all four French abilities adds 25 to 50 additional points and opens French category draws, which saw cut-offs between 393 and 419 in the first half of 2026.

3. Build Canadian Work Experience

Each additional year of Canadian experience adds core points and can unlock transferability tiers. For in-Canada workers, the jump from one to two years often adds 30 to 50 points in total.

4. Pursue a Provincial Nomination

A provincial nomination adds 600 points, which makes age far less decisive. Some provincial streams do apply their own age criteria, so check each stream carefully.

5. Compare Principal Applicant Options

If you have a spouse or partner, calculate scores with each person as principal applicant. A younger spouse with strong language results may produce a higher overall score.

6. Leverage Senior Experience Categories

Experienced professionals may qualify for categories such as senior managers or researchers with Canadian work experience, where cut-offs in 2026 have sat well below CEC levels.

Timing Your Profile Around Your Birthday

If your birthday falls soon and you already meet eligibility requirements, submit your profile now. You secure a tie-breaking timestamp and remain eligible for any draw held before your birthday. Continue improving other factors inside the same profile afterwards.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age do you get the most points in Express Entry?

Candidates aged 20 to 29 earn the maximum age points: 110 for single applicants and 100 for applicants with an accompanying spouse or common-law partner. Points begin declining at 30 and reach zero at 45. Age points form part of the core human capital factors in the Comprehensive Ranking System.

Can I get Canadian PR through Express Entry after 40?

Yes, although age points fall sharply after 40, reaching zero at 45. Older candidates can still succeed through strong language scores, French ability, Canadian work experience, category-based draws such as senior managers, or a provincial nomination worth 600 points. Each profile differs, so assess your full score rather than age alone.

Do I lose age points if I have a birthday after my ITA?

Generally, no. IRCC bases your age points on your age on the date you receive your Invitation to Apply. A birthday between your ITA and your application submission should not reduce those points. Before your ITA, however, your score updates automatically as your age changes, so birthdays can lower your ranking.

Will IRCC change age points in 2026?

IRCC told stakeholders at an April 2026 webinar that age-related CRS points would not change under its proposed Express Entry reforms, according to CIC News reporting. Other CRS factors, such as sibling, spouse and French bonus points, may change. Monitor Canada.ca for official Ministerial Instructions before relying on any proposed change.
