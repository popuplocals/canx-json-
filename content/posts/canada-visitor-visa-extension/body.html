Visitors are usually allowed to stay in Canada for up to six months, but life does not always fit into a fixed window. A grandchild arrives early, a family member falls ill, or a visit simply needs more time. The good news is that you can ask to extend your stay from inside Canada. The bad news is that many people misunderstand how it works: you do not “extend your visa,” you apply for a visitor record, and timing is everything.

This guide explains how a Canada visitor visa extension works in 2026, when and how to apply, what to include, what happens while you wait and what to do if IRCC refuses.

Quick Summary

To stay longer in Canada as a visitor, apply online for a visitor record before your current status expires. The processing fee is $100. If you apply on time and stay in Canada, you can usually remain under maintained status while IRCC decides. Your visitor visa (the counterfoil in your passport) controls entry, not how long you can stay. If your status expires, you generally have 90 days to apply for restoration.

Visitor Visa vs Visitor Record vs Status

DocumentWhat it does
Visitor visa (temporary resident visa)Allows you to travel to and enter Canada; it does not set how long you can stay
Stamp or date set at entrySets your authorized stay, usually up to 6 months
Visitor recordExtends or changes your authorized stay; issued from inside Canada
Super visaAllows parents and grandparents to stay up to 5 years per entry

A visitor visa that remains valid for years does not mean you can stay for years. Your authorized stay is what matters.

When Should You Apply?

Apply before your authorized stay ends. IRCC recommends applying at least 30 days before your status expires. If your passport is close to expiry, renew it first, because IRCC cannot extend your stay beyond your passport validity.

How to Apply for a Visitor Record: Step by Step

Step 1: Confirm your current status end date

Check the date stamped in your passport or your entry record. If no date was written, you are generally authorized for six months from the date you entered.

Step 2: Prepare a clear explanation

Explain why you need more time, how long you plan to stay and how you will support yourself. Be specific and honest.

Step 3: Gather supporting documents

  • Passport and proof of current status.
  • Proof of funds for the extended stay.
  • A letter from your host in Canada, with their status documents, if you are staying with family.
  • Proof of ties to your home country, such as employment leave, property or family responsibilities.
  • A return ticket or travel plans.
  • Medical letters, if your extension relates to health.

Step 4: Apply online and pay the $100 fee

Submit your application through your IRCC account and keep your submission confirmation.

Step 5: Wait inside Canada

If you applied on time, you can usually stay in Canada under maintained status until IRCC decides.

Maintained Status for Visitors

Maintained status lets you remain in Canada under the same conditions while IRCC processes your visitor record application. Visitors on maintained status cannot work or study beyond what visitors are allowed to do. If you leave Canada, maintained status generally ends. Since May 28, 2025, if your original status expires and you submit another application after that date, IRCC returns it unprocessed, so submit a complete, correct application the first time.

What If IRCC Refuses Your Extension?

If IRCC refuses your visitor record, your maintained status ends on the date of the refusal. You must leave Canada or, if eligible, apply for restoration of status within 90 days of losing status. Restoration involves an additional fee and requires you to show you still meet the requirements.

Can Visitors Work or Study?

Visitors cannot work in Canada without authorization. Visitors can generally take short courses of six months or less. If your plans change to work or study, you need the appropriate permit, and some applications have specific rules about applying from inside Canada.

Common Visitor Extension Mistakes

  • Waiting until after your status expires.
  • Confusing visa validity with authorized stay.
  • Weak explanations or insufficient proof of funds.
  • Leaving Canada while the application is pending.
  • Submitting a second application after status expires.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I extend my visitor visa in Canada?

You apply online for a visitor record before your current authorized stay ends. The visitor record, if approved, sets a new date by which you must leave Canada. The processing fee is $100. Include a clear explanation, proof of funds and proof of ties to your home country.

When should I apply for a visitor extension in Canada?

Apply before your status expires, ideally at least 30 days before. If you apply on time and remain in Canada, you can usually stay under maintained status while IRCC processes your application. Applying after your status expires requires restoration, which has stricter rules and extra fees.

Can I stay in Canada while my visitor extension is processing?

Yes, if you applied before your status expired and you remain in Canada. This is called maintained status, and you keep the same conditions as before. If you leave Canada, maintained status generally ends. If IRCC refuses your application, maintained status ends on the date of refusal.

What happens if my visitor record application is refused?

Your maintained status ends on the date of the refusal. You must leave Canada or, if eligible, apply for restoration of status within 90 days of losing status. Restoration requires an additional fee and proof that you still meet the requirements for the status you want.
