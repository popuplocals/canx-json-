A CRS score of 480 failed to win an invitation in any Canadian Experience Class draw of September 2026. The same 480 cleared the trades draw on October 1 and the healthcare draw on September 4. So what counts as a good CRS score in 2026? The honest answer is: it depends on which draws you qualify for.

This guide breaks down what a “good” CRS score 2026 looks like for each draw type, using real cut-offs from IRCC rounds. You will also learn how to judge your own number and what to do if you fall short.

Quick Summary

There is no single “good” CRS score in 2026. For CEC draws, competitive scores sat around 515 to 525. For healthcare and trades categories, around 475 to 477. For French-language draws, roughly 393 to 419 in the first half of 2026. For senior managers, 389 in September. With a provincial nomination, almost any eligible score becomes competitive, because the nomination adds 600 points.

Why "Good" Depends on Your Draw Type

IRCC now runs several types of rounds: Canadian Experience Class (CEC), Provincial Nominee Program (PNP), and category-based rounds for groups like French speakers, healthcare workers and tradespeople. Each round invites from a different slice of the pool. A candidate who qualifies only for general or Federal Skilled Worker selection faces a very different target from a nurse with French skills.

Note that IRCC has held very few draws open to all programs in recent years. That means Federal Skilled Worker candidates without Canadian experience, French or a category usually need a PNP nomination or a very high score.

What a Good Score Looks Like by Profile

In-Canada Workers and Graduates (CEC)

For CEC candidates, scores in the mid-500s offered the strongest position in 2026. A typical high scorer is under 30, holds a bachelor’s or master’s degree, scores CLB 9 or higher, and has two or more years of Canadian experience. If you sit between 480 and 515, focus on language retests and a second year of Canadian work.

Overseas Skilled Workers (FSW)

Federal Skilled Worker candidates without Canadian experience face the steepest climb. Without French, a category or a nomination, even 500 may not secure an invitation. For this group, “good” often means “PNP-ready”: a profile strong enough to attract a provincial Notification of Interest.

French Speakers

French ability changes the equation entirely. A candidate at 410 with NCLC 7 or higher in all four French abilities likely held a competitive position for French draws in the first half of 2026, while the same score had no chance in a CEC round.

Healthcare and Trades Workers

Healthcare and trades categories required scores in the mid to high 470s in 2026. IRCC also now requires at least 12 months of qualifying experience in the last three years for renewed categories, up from six months.

How to Judge Your Own Score

Start by listing every draw type you qualify for. Then compare your score with the last three to six cut-offs for each. If you are within 10 points, small improvements and the tie-breaking rule may matter. If you are 30 or more points below, a structural change, such as French or a nomination, usually works better than waiting.

  • Under 400: focus on eligibility for French, a category, or a PNP stream.
  • 400 to 470: target French draws, senior manager or other categories, and PNPs.
  • 470 to 515: track healthcare and trades categories; push language and Canadian experience.
  • 515 and above: competitive for most CEC draws; keep your profile current and accurate.

Raising a "Not Quite Good Enough" Score

The fastest gains usually come from language retests, claiming missed credentials, and correcting profile errors. Longer-term moves include learning French, adding Canadian work experience and pursuing a provincial nomination. PNP vs Express Entry: When a Nomination Beats a Higher Score

A provincial nomination adds 600 points, which is why PNP round cut-offs sit far above other draws. If your score falls well below CEC levels but your occupation matches a province’s priorities, a nomination may offer a faster route than chasing another 40 points. Provinces each set their own criteria, so research streams carefully.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good CRS score for Express Entry in 2026?

It depends on the draw type. In 2026, CEC draws generally required about 515 to 525, healthcare and trades categories about 475 to 477, French draws about 393 to 419, and senior managers 389 in September. Identify every draw you qualify for and compare your score against those specific cut-offs, not just the headline number.

Is a CRS score of 450 good in 2026?

A 450 score usually falls short of CEC, healthcare and trades cut-offs seen in 2026. It can still be competitive for French-language draws or certain narrow categories, and it may attract provincial interest in some streams. With 450, focus on French, category eligibility or a provincial nomination rather than waiting for general cut-offs to drop.

Is 500 a good CRS score?

A 500 score placed you above 2026 healthcare, trades, French and senior manager cut-offs, but below most CEC cut-offs. If you qualify for one of those categories, 500 is strong. If you qualify only for CEC or FSW selection, consider raising your language scores or pursuing a provincial nomination to improve your chances.

How can I tell if my CRS score is competitive?

Compare your score against the last three to six cut-offs for every draw type you qualify for, using IRCC’s rounds of invitations page. A margin of 10 to 20 points above recent cut-offs gives a reasonable cushion. Remember that cut-offs fluctuate, so no score guarantees an invitation.
