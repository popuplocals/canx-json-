On March 25, 2025, IRCC implemented one of the most significant changes to the Express Entry system since the program launched in 2015. The arranged employment points — 50 or 200 additional CRS points for candidates with qualifying job offers — were eliminated entirely. The decision was driven by documented, large-scale fraud in which fraudulent LMIA letters were being sold to Express Entry candidates for sums reported to reach $75,000 per letter. By removing the financial incentive, IRCC effectively collapsed the fraud market overnight.

This change fundamentally altered the direct relationship between an LMIA and a candidate’s Express Entry CRS score. A candidate who holds an LMIA-backed job offer in 2026 receives no additional CRS points from that offer. Their profile is competitive based on their human capital: language scores, education, age, and Canadian work experience.

But the LMIA’s value for permanent residence has not disappeared. It has restructured. The LMIA enables a work permit. The work permit enables Canadian work experience. The Canadian work experience earns CRS points, qualifies the candidate for the Canadian Experience Class, and supports a PNP application. The PNP nomination — not available without the employment relationship the LMIA created — delivers 600 CRS points, more than any arranged employment bonus ever could for most candidates. The LMIA is the starting point of a pathway that leads to permanent residence. The pathway is just longer than the old shortcut was.

Pathway 1: Canadian Work Experience Accumulation

The most direct and reliable way an LMIA work permit contributes to a PR application in 2026 is through the Canadian work experience the permit enables. Under the CRS, Canadian work experience in a skilled NOC category generates substantial points:

Canadian Work Experience (NOC TEER 0-3)CRS Points — Single Applicant
Less than 1 year (or TEER 4/5)0 points
1 year of skilled experience40 points
2 years of skilled experience53 points
3 years of skilled experience64 points
4 years of skilled experience72 points
5 or more years of skilled experience80 points

For a worker entering Canada on an LMIA work permit in a TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 position, every year of employment in Canada is building toward these CRS points. Three years of Canadian skilled experience generates 64 points — more than the eliminated 50-point arranged employment bonus provided, and earned through genuine work rather than paperwork. Five or more years generates 80 points.

These experience points accumulate in addition to all other human capital factors (education, language, age). A candidate with strong language scores, a post-secondary credential, and three years of Canadian work experience has a substantially stronger Express Entry profile in 2026 than one who had relied on the 50-point bonus without other competitive factors.

Canadian Experience Class Eligibility: The One-Year Milestone

After accumulating one year of full-time skilled Canadian work experience within the three years preceding an Express Entry application, a candidate becomes eligible for the Canadian Experience Class. CEC draws have consistently occurred at lower CRS thresholds than general Federal Skilled Worker draws, and category-based draws targeting specific occupations have selected CEC-eligible candidates at particularly competitive thresholds.

For workers in healthcare, technology, trades, and other priority occupations, category-based CEC draws have been conducted with cutoffs as low as the mid-400s, compared to general draw cutoffs that often sit above 480 or higher. The CEC eligibility milestone, triggered by the first year of Canadian work on an LMIA work permit, is one of the most valuable immigration milestones a foreign worker can reach.

Pathway 2: PNP Employer-Driven Nomination — The Primary Strategy

The PNP employer-driven nomination has become the primary PR pathway for LMIA work permit holders in the post-March 2025 environment. A provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points, which is functionally a guarantee of an ITA in the next available federal Express Entry draw regardless of the candidate’s base CRS score.

To access this pathway, a worker typically needs: a genuine employment relationship with an employer who is committed to retaining them permanently, a minimum period of work experience in the province (usually three to twelve months), a position in an occupation eligible for the provincial stream, and a job offer that meets the provincial stream’s specific requirements including being permanent and full-time.

An employer who has obtained a positive LMIA, integrated a foreign worker into their operation, and is genuinely committed to retaining that person long-term is exactly the profile that employer-driven PNP streams are designed to serve. The LMIA created the employment relationship. The PNP converts that employment relationship into a provincial nomination that delivers 600 CRS points.

This pathway is longer than the old arranged employment bonus route — it requires genuine employer commitment to permanent hiring rather than just a signed job offer letter. But for candidates in genuine employment relationships, it produces more reliable and more powerful PR outcomes than the bonus points ever could.

Does an LMIA Automatically Lead to PR?

No. This was not true before March 2025 and it remains not true. An LMIA does not create any PR obligation, any PR guarantee, or any automatic step toward permanent residence. A worker can hold an LMIA-based work permit in Canada for several years and never receive an ITA if their Express Entry CRS score remains below competitive draw thresholds and they have not pursued a PNP nomination or other pathway.

The LMIA creates the precondition for work authorization. Work authorization creates the precondition for Canadian experience. Canadian experience creates the precondition for CEC eligibility and PNP eligibility. PNP eligibility, pursued actively with a committed employer, creates the conditions for a nomination. The nomination produces the ITA. At every stage, active pursuit and strategic planning are required. The LMIA starts the chain; it does not complete it.

What Happens to the Job Offer If the Employer Withdraws

If an employer withdraws a job offer at any stage of the PR process, the immigration consequences depend on what stage has been reached. There are no arranged employment points to lose — those no longer exist. The impact is to the underlying employment relationship and the PNP pathway.

Impact on Canadian Work Experience

If employment ends, the accumulation of Canadian work experience points stops. Points earned for experience already accumulated are retained in the profile. If the candidate subsequently finds a new employer and a new work permit, experience continues accumulating. The experience points are not lost; they simply stop growing while the candidate is not employed in Canada.

Impact on PNP Application

If an employer withdraws a job offer while a PNP application based on that employer relationship is in progress, the PNP application is affected. Provincial stream eligibility typically requires an active employment relationship and a valid job offer at the time of application. A withdrawn offer undermines the application’s foundation. Workers who lose employment while a PNP application is pending should contact their immigration consultant immediately to assess their options.

Practical Action Steps for Workers Using an LMIA for PR in 2026

  • Arrive in Canada and establish legal status — confirm your work permit is valid and consistent with the LMIA position.
  • Create your Express Entry profile immediately if you have not already — do not wait for employment to begin accumulating points.
  • Research the PNP streams in your province and confirm the minimum experience requirements for employer-driven streams.
  • After accumulating the minimum experience threshold, discuss PNP nomination support with your employer directly.
  • Maintain strong language test scores — with bonus points eliminated, language is a larger share of your competitive CRS score.
  • Apply for work permit renewal well before expiry — at least four months in advance — so your Canadian experience accumulation is continuous.
  • If a category-based Express Entry draw targets your occupation at a threshold below your current score, submit an application promptly.
  • Do not rely exclusively on employer verbal assurances about PR support — pursue your own independent strategy in parallel.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does an LMIA automatically lead to PR in Canada?

No. An LMIA enables a work permit and supports a PR strategy, but it does not create any automatic PR pathway. Since March 25, 2025, an LMIA no longer adds CRS bonus points to an Express Entry profile. The LMIA contributes to PR through the Canadian work experience it enables (up to 80 CRS points), the CEC eligibility it creates after one year of skilled work, and the PNP employer-driven nomination pathway it supports. Each of these pathways requires active pursuit by the worker; none happens automatically.

How do LMIA job offers affect Express Entry in 2026?

As of March 25, 2025, LMIA job offers no longer add any CRS points to an Express Entry profile. Before this date, qualifying offers added 50 or 200 points. In 2026, the LMIA’s Express Entry contribution is indirect: it enables a work permit, which enables Canadian work experience accumulation (up to 80 CRS points over five or more years), which also creates CEC eligibility after one year. The most powerful current contribution of an LMIA employer relationship is through the PNP nomination pathway (600 CRS points).

Is Canadian work experience from an LMIA work permit eligible for CEC?

Yes, provided the work is in a NOC TEER 0, 1, 2, or 3 occupation. Full-time skilled Canadian work experience accumulated while working on an LMIA work permit qualifies for the Canadian Experience Class after one year. Workers in TEER 4 or 5 positions do not accumulate CEC-eligible experience, which is a significant limitation for workers in these categories when planning their PR strategy.

Can I get PR if my LMIA is for a TEER 4 or 5 position?

Workers in TEER 4 or 5 positions cannot use that work experience for the CEC or for CRS work experience points through Express Entry. Other pathways may still apply, including provincial nominee programs that accept workers in lower-skilled positions where genuine employer commitment exists, the Atlantic Immigration Program, and the Rural and Northern Immigration Pilot in designated communities. An RCIC can assess whether any of these alternative pathways apply to a specific TEER 4 or 5 worker’s situation.

My employer told me their LMIA will help my Express Entry score. Is that correct?

Not anymore. Before March 25, 2025, a qualifying LMIA-backed job offer added 50 or 200 points to a candidate’s CRS score. IRCC eliminated these arranged employment points entirely on that date. In 2026, an LMIA job offer adds zero CRS bonus points to an Express Entry profile. If your employer or any advisor has told you otherwise recently, that advice reflects outdated information. An LMIA still supports your PR strategy through Canadian work experience accumulation and the PNP nomination pathway, but it no longer directly boosts your CRS score.
