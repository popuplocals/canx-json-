Employers who approach the LMIA process for the first time consistently underestimate how long it takes. They budget for the fee, they prepare the documents, and then they wait. And wait. In many cases, they have already promised a foreign worker a start date, made business commitments that depend on that person being in place, or both. When the LMIA takes longer than expected, the consequences ripple outward across the business, the worker, and sometimes the worker’s existing immigration status.

Understanding LMIA processing timelines in 2026 requires more than knowing a number. It requires understanding why the number varies, what factors push processing longer, how different streams compare, and what concrete steps an employer can take to minimize delay. That is what this article provides.

How ESDC Measures Processing Time

ESDC defines processing time as the number of business days from when a complete application is received to when a final decision is issued. This definition contains an important qualifier: complete. An application that is missing documents, contains errors, or requires an RFI is not ‘complete’ in the ESDC sense until those issues are resolved. This means the clock effectively pauses during any back-and-forth between the employer and ESDC.

ESDC publishes processing time targets on its website, but unlike IRCC, which publishes real-time processing time estimates updated weekly, ESDC’s LMIA processing time information tends to be less granular and less frequently updated. Employers should treat published processing time ranges as general guidance rather than precise commitments.

Processing Times by TFWP Stream in 2026

High-Wage Stream

High-Wage LMIA applications in 2026 are taking approximately 8 to 16 weeks for straightforward applications in sectors where ESDC has established labour market baselines. However, applications in sectors with complex labour market conditions, applications where the transition plan requires closer review, or applications that receive RFIs can take 16 to 24 weeks or longer.

From a practical standpoint, employers in the High-Wage stream should plan for a minimum of four months from the date of application submission to having a positive LMIA in hand, assuming the application is complete and well-prepared. Planning for five to six months provides a more reliable operational buffer.

Low-Wage Stream

Low-Wage stream applications generally process in a similar timeframe to High-Wage applications, approximately 8 to 16 weeks for complete files. However, Low-Wage applications are subject to additional scrutiny around accommodation requirements, workforce caps, and sector-specific restrictions, all of which can generate RFIs and extend processing.

In sectors that have experienced elevated TFWP scrutiny, including food service, retail, and accommodation, Low-Wage applications may face additional review stages that extend processing beyond the standard range. Employers in these sectors should factor this into their planning and, where possible, consider whether they can restructure wages to access the High-Wage stream, which sometimes carries less friction despite the additional Transition Plan requirement.

Global Talent Stream

The Global Talent Stream is designed to process LMIA applications within two weeks of receiving a complete application. In practice, GTS applications for Category B occupations (defined in-demand tech roles) that are complete and straightforward often meet or come close to this target. Category A applications, which require endorsement from a recognized referral partner, can take slightly longer due to the referral process itself.

The two-week target is real, but it is contingent on the application being genuinely complete at the time of submission. GTS applications that trigger RFIs, which can happen when the employer’s business legitimacy requires verification, when the wage does not clearly align with the GTS wage requirements, or when the position’s duties do not clearly match the claimed NOC code, can take considerably longer. The lesson for GTS applicants is that speed at submission saves far more time than scrambling after an RFI.

Primary Agriculture Stream

Agricultural LMIA applications under the Seasonal Agricultural Worker Program (SAWP) operate on a seasonal cycle. ESDC processes these applications in advance of the planting and harvest seasons, and the processing framework is structured around bilateral agreements with Mexico and the Caribbean countries. SAWP applications submitted early in the season, ideally in January or February for spring arrivals, tend to process more smoothly than late submissions.

The Agricultural Stream (which covers non-SAWP agricultural positions and allows workers from any country) processes on a more standard timeline, typically eight to twelve weeks, but agricultural employers should note that occupational licensing, certification, or credential recognition requirements for certain specialized agricultural positions can create additional delays outside the LMIA process itself.

Caregiver Positions

LMIA applications for caregiver positions, when they are required (which depends on whether the position falls under the TFWP traditional caregiver stream or one of the pilot programs), generally process within eight to sixteen weeks. However, this area of immigration policy has been in significant flux, and employers or families seeking to hire caregivers should confirm the current applicable pathway before investing in an LMIA application that may not be the required or most efficient route.

What Causes LMIA Applications to Take Longer

Incomplete Applications

This is the single most controllable cause of processing delay, and also the most common. An application that is missing a required document, contains a form with fields left blank, or includes supporting documents that do not match the information in the form will either result in an RFI or, in some cases, be refused outright. Every day spent collecting and submitting additional documents after submission is a day added to the processing clock.

Requests for Information (RFIs)

When an ESDC officer reviewing an application has questions or concerns that cannot be resolved from the materials submitted, they issue an RFI. RFIs pause the effective processing clock until the employer responds. Employers who receive an RFI and take two or three weeks to respond have added that time to their total processing timeline. Employers who respond within a few days with complete, well-organized responses keep the process moving.

The most common triggers for RFIs include: questions about the business legitimacy (particularly for newer businesses or businesses with unusual financial records), questions about specific elements of the recruitment effort (such as why a particular qualified Canadian was not hired), questions about the wage level relative to ESDC’s prevailing wage data, and questions about the Transition Plan for high-wage applications.

High Application Volume Periods

ESDC processes LMIA applications in batches, and certain times of year see higher application volumes than others. The spring months tend to see elevated agricultural applications, while the fall often sees increases in applications from construction and hospitality sectors. Submitting a non-seasonal application during a peak volume period may mean it sits in a longer queue before an officer is assigned to review it.

Complex Labour Market Conditions

Applications for positions in occupations where ESDC has inconclusive or contradictory labour market data, or where the local unemployment rate for the specific occupation is difficult to assess, may require more officer time for the assessment itself. This is particularly common in hybrid or emerging occupations where the NOC code classification is itself somewhat contested, or where labour market data has not caught up with rapid industry changes.

Officer Caseload and Regional Differences

ESDC processes LMIA applications through regional Service Canada offices, and processing times can vary across regions depending on staffing levels and caseload. While ESDC aims for national consistency, employers in regions with higher application volumes or lower officer staffing may experience longer timelines than the national average suggests.

How to Speed Up Your LMIA Application

Submit a Complete Application the First Time

This advice sounds obvious, and it is, but the number of LMIA applications that arrive at ESDC with missing documents, incomplete forms, or inconsistent information is striking. The fastest path through LMIA processing is a complete, consistent, well-organized application. Before submitting, use a thorough checklist to verify that every required document is present, that all form fields are correctly completed, and that the information in the form is consistent with the information in the supporting documents.

Respond to RFIs Immediately

If you receive an RFI, treat it as the highest priority communication in your inbox. Read it carefully, understand exactly what is being asked, and prepare a complete and precise response. If the RFI is asking for specific documentation, provide that documentation plus any context that helps the officer understand why the evidence supports a positive decision. Do not provide partial responses with a promise to send more later; submit everything the officer needs in a single organized response.

Use the Global Talent Stream When Eligible

If your position qualifies for the Global Talent Stream, the two-week processing target is a compelling reason to pursue that stream over the standard TFWP streams, even if the standard stream would also apply. GTS requires that the position meet specific occupational and wage criteria, but for eligible tech employers, the processing time advantage is significant.

Plan Around Processing Realities, Not Best-Case Scenarios

The most effective way to manage LMIA processing time is not to try to shorten it, but to plan around it accurately from the beginning. If your best estimate for standard stream processing is 12 weeks, build your hiring plan around a 16-week timeline as your baseline and treat anything faster as a pleasant surprise. Employers who plan based on best-case scenarios consistently find themselves in difficult positions when reality diverges from optimism.

Maintain Advertising During the Processing Period

ESDC may ask, particularly in lengthy processing situations, whether the employer’s recruitment efforts remain current and whether there are now qualified Canadians available who were not available at the time of application. Maintaining ongoing recruitment activity during the processing period, and keeping records of that activity, positions the employer to respond credibly if this question is raised.

What Happens If Processing Takes Too Long: Practical Problems and Solutions

The most common problem caused by extended LMIA processing is a mismatch between the LMIA timeline and the foreign worker’s current immigration status. If a worker is in Canada on a study permit or a work permit that expires while the LMIA is being processed, their status becomes a pressing concern. Workers can sometimes maintain legal status through maintained status rules (formerly ‘implied status’) while their extension application is pending with IRCC, but this requires careful timing and planning.

Another problem arises when the employer has made firm business commitments based on a projected hire date that the LMIA timeline does not support. In these situations, employers sometimes ask whether there is any way to expedite the process. ESDC does not have a formal expedite request system for standard TFWP streams in the same way IRCC has for some visa applications. The GTS is the closest thing to a formal expedite mechanism, and it is only available for eligible positions.

One practical strategy is to pursue LMIA and work permit applications in parallel where possible. While a work permit cannot actually be submitted without the LMIA number, some preparatory steps for the work permit application can be completed in advance, meaning the worker can submit quickly once the LMIA is in hand rather than starting the work permit preparation process from scratch.

The Intersection of LMIA Processing and Express Entry Timelines

For workers in the Express Entry pool who are using an LMIA-backed job offer to boost their CRS score, processing timelines create a specific timing challenge. The LMIA must be obtained, and the employer must submit the job offer through the IRCC Employer Portal, before the job offer points are reflected in the Express Entry profile. If the Express Entry draw occurs before the LMIA is processed and entered in the system, the worker may miss a draw that they would have qualified for with the job offer points.

Express Entry profiles also expire after twelve months, and workers who receive an ITA have sixty days to submit a complete PR application. If the LMIA processing timeline is uncertain, workers and employers should map out these timelines carefully to ensure that the job offer points are available when they are needed, and that the PR application timeline is realistic given all the concurrent processing involved.

Officer Assessment Considerations That Affect Speed

Beyond the mechanical factors that affect processing time, there are assessment-related factors that influence how long an officer spends reviewing a particular file. Complex labour market conditions for the specific NOC in the specific region require more officer analysis time. Transition plans that are detailed and require careful review take longer to assess than simpler ones. Business legitimacy that requires verification through additional records takes time.

Applications that raise no questions and present evidence consistently and credibly are processed faster, not because ESDC gives them preferential treatment, but because the officer’s review is less time-intensive. This is another reason why application quality directly affects processing time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an LMIA take in Canada in 2026?

Standard LMIA applications (high-wage and low-wage streams) in 2026 are taking approximately 8 to 20 weeks for complete applications. The Global Talent Stream targets two weeks. Primary Agriculture applications follow seasonal timelines. These are estimates; actual processing depends on completeness, the specific stream, regional caseload, and whether the officer issues a Request for Information. Planning for longer timelines than the published estimates is prudent.

Is there a way to expedite an LMIA application?

The Global Talent Stream offers a two-week processing target for eligible technology and knowledge economy positions and is the closest formal mechanism to expedited processing. For standard TFWP streams, there is no formal expedite request process. The most reliable way to minimize processing time is to submit a complete, consistent, well-organized application that requires no RFI from the officer.

Why is my LMIA taking so long?

Common reasons for extended LMIA processing include an incomplete application requiring an RFI, high application volumes at the regional Service Canada office, complexity in the labour market assessment for the specific occupation and region, officer review time for the transition plan, or business legitimacy verification. If your application has been in process for longer than the published target, you can contact Service Canada to request a status update.

Does the Global Talent Stream really process in 2 weeks?

The Global Talent Stream targets a two-week processing time from receipt of a complete application. In practice, many complete GTS applications are processed within this window or close to it. However, applications that trigger an RFI due to incomplete information or officer questions can take significantly longer. The two-week target is real but conditional on a genuinely complete and compliant application.

When should I submit my LMIA application relative to the worker’s intended start date?

For standard TFWP streams, submit the LMIA application at least five to six months before the intended start date to allow for processing, work permit application by the worker, and any unforeseen delays. For GTS applications, three months is a more reasonable minimum. For agricultural applications under SAWP, follow the seasonal submission windows published by ESDC, which typically open several months before the season begins.
