On July 15, 2026, Canada paused new applications under the Parents and Grandparents Program, the permanent residence pathway many families had hoped to use. For thousands of Canadians who want their parents nearby, the super visa is now the main way to bring them to Canada for long stays. It allows parents and grandparents to stay for up to five years per entry, with a visa valid for up to 10 years.

This guide explains super visa requirements in 2026, who qualifies, how the income and insurance rules work, how the PGP pause affects your options, and how to prepare a strong application.

Quick Summary

The super visa lets parents and grandparents of Canadian citizens, permanent residents and registered Indians visit for up to five years per entry. Applicants need a letter of invitation from a child or grandchild who meets a minimum income threshold, private health insurance valid for at least one year from entry from a Canadian insurer or an approved foreign insurer, and an immigration medical exam. With PGP intake paused since July 15, 2026, the super visa is the main long-stay option for parents.

Who Can Apply?

You can apply if you are the parent or grandparent of a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or registered Indian. You must apply from outside Canada and be admissible. Your spouse or common-law partner can be included in your application, but dependent children cannot.

Host Requirements

Your child or grandchild in Canada, the host, must:

  • Be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident or registered Indian.
  • Be at least 18 years old and live in Canada.
  • Provide a signed letter of invitation that promises financial support for your entire visit.
  • Meet the minimum income threshold for their family size.

Understanding the Income Requirement

The host’s household income must meet at least the minimum necessary income, based on the Low Income Cut-Off (LICO), for the total number of people in the household, including the visiting parents or grandparents. Your host’s spouse or common-law partner can usually combine incomes. Provide proof such as a Notice of Assessment, T4 slips, employment letters and pay stubs. IRCC publishes the current income table with the application instructions, so always use the latest figures.

Health Insurance Requirements

Your insurance must:

  • Come from a Canadian insurance company or an approved foreign insurance company.
  • Be valid for at least one year from the date of entry.
  • Cover health care, hospitalization and repatriation.
  • Provide at least $100,000 in emergency coverage.
  • Be paid in full or in instalments, with a deposit where applicable.

Insurance is often the largest cost of a super visa, so compare policies carefully and read exclusions for pre-existing conditions.

Medical Exam

All super visa applicants must complete an immigration medical exam with an IRCC panel physician. Book the exam early, because results are valid for 12 months.

Step-by-Step: How to Apply

Step 1: Gather the host's documents

Include the invitation letter, proof of status in Canada, proof of income and proof of the family relationship.

Step 2: Buy qualifying insurance

Purchase insurance that meets every requirement and keep the policy documents.

Step 3: Complete the medical exam

Visit a panel physician and keep the confirmation.

Step 4: Submit the application online

Apply from outside Canada with all forms, documents and fees, and give biometrics when asked.

Step 5: Prepare for arrival

At the port of entry, the border officer decides your length of stay. Carry your insurance proof and invitation letter.

Extending a Super Visa Stay

If your parents want to stay beyond the period the border officer authorized, they can apply to extend their stay from inside Canada before their status expires. Extensions require proof of continued insurance coverage and financial support.

Common Super Visa Mistakes

  • Buying insurance that does not meet the one-year or $100,000 requirement.
  • Using outdated income tables.
  • Leaving out the host’s spouse’s income when it is needed.
  • Weak invitation letters missing household details.
  • Not explaining ties to the home country.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long can parents stay in Canada on a super visa?

Parents and grandparents can stay for up to five years per entry on a super visa, and the visa can be valid for up to 10 years or until the passport expires. The border officer decides the actual length of stay at entry, and visitors can apply to extend their stay from inside Canada.

What insurance do I need for a super visa?

You need private health insurance from a Canadian insurer or an approved foreign insurer, valid for at least one year from your entry date, covering health care, hospitalization and repatriation, with at least $100,000 in emergency coverage. You can pay in full or in instalments, subject to the policy terms.

How much income does the host need for a super visa?

The host must meet the minimum necessary income based on the Low Income Cut-Off for the total household size, including the visiting parents or grandparents. The host’s spouse or common-law partner can usually combine income. Check IRCC’s latest income table before applying, because thresholds change.

Can I still sponsor my parents for permanent residence in 2026?

Canada paused new applications under the Parents and Grandparents Program on July 15, 2026, and has not announced when intake will resume. Existing applications continue processing. For now, the super visa is the main option for parents and grandparents who want long stays in Canada.
