You know you sent it. You have the courier receipt, the upload confirmation, maybe even a photo of the envelope. Yet IRCC has just asked for the same document again, or your passport has not come back from the visa office. When IRCC loses your documents, panic is natural. A clear plan works better.

IRCC handles millions of applications and documents every year, and most arrive and return safely. But mistakes happen in mailrooms, scanning centres and visa offices. This guide explains the most common “lost document” situations, what to do in each, and how to protect yourself before you ever send another file.

Quick Summary

Stay calm and check your account first, because many “lost” documents are simply waiting to be matched to your file. Gather proof of what you sent, such as upload confirmations and courier tracking. Respond to any IRCC request on time, even if you sent the document before. For missing passports, contact the visa application centre and IRCC. To replace a lost immigration document, such as a work permit or confirmation of permanent residence, use IRCC’s application to verify status or replace an immigration document.

First: Is the Document Really Lost?

Before you assume the worst, check three things. First, review every message in your IRCC account, because IRCC may have confirmed receipt separately. Second, check whether your application or document is still in transit, especially for paper applications. Third, request your Global Case Management System (GCMS) notes if timing allows, because they can show whether IRCC received and scanned a document.

Scenario 1: IRCC Asks for a Document You Already Sent

This is the most common situation. An officer may not have seen your document because it was misfiled, poorly scanned or uploaded under the wrong category.

  • Do not argue or ignore the request. Treat it as a fresh deadline.
  • Re-submit the document exactly as requested, through the method the letter specifies.
  • Add a short, polite cover letter noting that you previously submitted it on a specific date, and attach your proof of submission.
  • Keep a copy of everything you send, plus your new confirmation.

Responding on time matters more than proving IRCC wrong. Missing a deadline can lead to refusal, even if the original error was IRCC’s.

Scenario 2: Your Passport Has Not Been Returned

If you submitted your passport for a visa or a confirmation of permanent residence and it has not come back:

  • Check the tracking service of the visa application centre (VAC) you used.
  • Contact the VAC directly with your tracking or reference number.
  • Submit an enquiry through IRCC’s official web form, explaining the dates and including your application number.
  • If the passport is truly lost, report it to your country’s passport authority and apply for a replacement, then inform IRCC of the new passport details.

Scenario 3: IRCC Lost Original Documents You Mailed

IRCC rarely asks for original documents, and you should send originals only when an instruction explicitly requires them. If IRCC requested originals and they have not come back, contact IRCC through the web form with your application number, the list of documents and your courier tracking details. Then begin replacing any document you cannot do without, such as birth or marriage certificates, through the issuing authority.

Scenario 4: You Lost an Immigration Document Issued by IRCC

If you lost a document IRCC issued to you, such as a work permit, study permit, visitor record or confirmation of permanent residence, you can apply to replace it using IRCC’s Application to Verify Status, or Replace an Immigration Document (IMM 5009). If you lost your PR card, apply for a replacement card instead, and include a police report or incident number if you have one, as described in IRCC’s PR card application guide.

How to Prove What You Sent

Your proof is your protection. Build these habits into every submission:

Submission methodProof to keep
Online portal or IRCC accountScreenshots of the upload page, the submission confirmation and the acknowledgement of receipt
Courier or registered mailTracking number, delivery confirmation with signature and date
Visa application centreReceipt, reference number and tracking printouts
Email or web formCopy of the sent message and IRCC’s automated confirmation

Also keep a complete digital copy of your application exactly as submitted, organized by IRCC’s checklist order. This makes resubmission fast if anything goes missing.

Prevent Problems Before They Start

  • Apply online whenever possible, because digital submissions reduce handling errors.
  • Name files clearly, for example “Applicant_Passport_Biopage.pdf”, and upload them under the correct category.
  • Check scans for readability before uploading; blurry or cut-off pages often trigger repeat requests.
  • Never send originals unless IRCC asks for them in writing.
  • Use trackable delivery for anything you mail.

When to Escalate

If IRCC does not respond to your enquiry and the issue affects a deadline, travel or your ability to work, escalate. Options include calling the IRCC Client Support Centre at 1-888-242-2100 (Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. local time, within Canada), contacting your Member of Parliament’s office, or asking a regulated consultant to intervene on your behalf.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if IRCC asks for a document I already submitted?

Respond before the deadline, even though you sent it before. Re-submit the document using the method in IRCC’s letter and include a short cover letter noting the original submission date, with proof such as an upload confirmation or courier tracking. Ignoring the request can lead to refusal, regardless of who made the error.

How do I find out if IRCC received my documents?

Check your online account for confirmations and messages, track paper submissions through your courier, and review your acknowledgement of receipt. For more detail, request your GCMS notes, which often show what IRCC received, when it was scanned and which documents an officer reviewed or requested.

What do I do if my passport is lost at the visa office?

Contact the visa application centre with your tracking number, then submit an IRCC web form enquiry with your application number and dates. If the passport is confirmed lost, report it to your passport authority, apply for a replacement and inform IRCC of the new passport number so it can update your file.

How do I replace a lost work permit or confirmation of permanent residence?

Use IRCC’s Application to Verify Status, or Replace an Immigration Document (IMM 5009) to replace documents such as work permits, study permits, visitor records or a confirmation of permanent residence. For a lost PR card, apply for a replacement PR card instead, including a police report or incident number if available.
