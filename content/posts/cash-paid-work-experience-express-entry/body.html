Many workers in Canada and abroad are paid partly or fully in cash, especially in restaurants, construction, trucking, retail and family businesses. For Express Entry, that creates a real problem. The work may be genuine, but if you cannot prove you were paid for it, an officer may not accept it as qualifying work experience. Immigration professionals have reported refusals in 2025 and 2026 where salary could not be traced through bank deposits or payroll records.

This guide explains what the law requires, why cash payment creates risk, what evidence helps and how to protect your application if some or all of your pay was in cash.

Quick Summary

Express Entry work experience must be paid work. Section 2 of the IRPR defines work as an activity for which wages are paid or commission is earned, and the program provisions in sections 75, 87.1 and 87.2 require qualifying work experience. Being paid in cash is not prohibited by law, but you must prove the work was real and paid. Officers increasingly look for traceable evidence, such as payroll records, tax slips and bank deposits. Reports of refusals for cash-paid experience reflect practice trends, not a published IRCC policy, so strong documentation is your best protection.

What the Law Requires

ProvisionRelevance to cash-paid work
IRPR s. 2Defines work as an activity for which wages are paid or commission is earned
IRPR s. 75(2)FSW: at least one year of continuous full-time paid work experience in an eligible occupation
IRPR s. 87.1(2) and (3)CEC: one year of full-time work in Canada; self-employment and unauthorized work excluded
IRPA s. 16(1)You must produce the evidence an officer reasonably requires
IRPA s. 40(1)(a)False or misleading evidence of employment can be misrepresentation

Nothing in IRPA or the IRPR says that cash wages do not count. The issue is proof. The burden is on you to show that the work was genuine, full-time or equivalent, in the claimed occupation and paid.

Why Cash Payment Raises Red Flags

  • No payroll records: Officers cannot confirm salary or hours.
  • No tax records: Missing T4 slips or tax filings make it harder to show legitimate employment.
  • Possible unauthorized work: For the Canadian Experience Class, paragraph 87.1(3)(b) of the IRPR excludes unauthorized work, and officers may look more closely at informal arrangements.
  • Credibility concerns: Untraceable salary combined with weak reference letters can lead an officer to doubt the experience altogether.

What the Reports Say

In October 2025, Moving2Canada reported a regulated consultant’s observation that IRCC had begun refusing PR applications where salary for claimed work experience was paid in cash, sometimes without a procedural fairness letter. Other practitioners report officers requesting bank statements showing salary deposits. These reports describe trends seen in practice; we have not found an official IRCC policy statement that cash-paid experience is excluded. Treat the risk as real, but do not assume a refusal is automatic.

For experience outside Canada, equivalent local documents such as tax filings, social security contributions and salary certificates can help.

What If Your Experience Was Entirely in Cash?

Speak with a licensed professional before you create your profile or accept an ITA. Options may include:

  • Relying on other periods of experience that are well documented.
  • Gathering every available form of corroboration, including tax filings made after the fact where lawful and appropriate.
  • Waiting to build documented, payroll-based experience before applying.

Never create documents that did not exist at the time, such as backdated pay stubs. Submitting false documents can lead to a misrepresentation finding under section 40 of IRPA and a five-year ban.

Advice for Workers Still Being Paid in Cash

  • Ask your employer to pay you through payroll with deductions.
  • Deposit every payment into your bank account promptly and keep records.
  • File your taxes and report your income.
  • Keep copies of schedules, messages and anything that shows your hours and duties.
  • Make sure your work is authorized under your work permit conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does cash-paid work count for Express Entry?

The law does not say cash-paid work is excluded. Express Entry work experience must be paid, and section 2 of the IRPR defines work as an activity for which wages are paid or commission is earned. The challenge is proof: you must show the work was genuine, paid and in the claimed occupation with strong documentation.

Is IRCC refusing applications for cash-paid work experience?

Immigration professionals have reported refusals where salary could not be traced, and some report officers requesting bank statements showing salary deposits. These are practice trends, not a published IRCC policy. The safest approach is to provide traceable evidence, such as tax slips, pay stubs and bank deposits.

What documents prove cash-paid work experience?

Helpful evidence includes T4 slips and Notices of Assessment, pay stubs, bank statements showing regular deposits, employment contracts, Records of Employment, detailed reference letters and signed payment receipts. The more independent records you have, the stronger your claim. Never create documents after the fact to fill gaps.

Can cash-paid work be considered unauthorized work?

Not automatically. Unauthorized work means work done without the required authorization, such as outside your work permit conditions. However, informal cash arrangements can lead officers to look more closely. For the Canadian Experience Class, paragraph 87.1(3)(b) of the IRPR excludes unauthorized work entirely.
