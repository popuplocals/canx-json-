Few things put more strain on a family than one partner waiting at home, unable to work, while a permanent residence application crawls through processing. The good news is that Canada offers several ways for the spouse or common-law partner of a PR applicant to work while the family waits. The challenge is that the rules changed sharply in January 2025, and many people still rely on advice that no longer applies.

This guide explains every main pathway to an open work permit for the spouse of a PR applicant in 2026, who qualifies, what changed, how to apply and how to avoid the mistakes that lead to refusals.

Quick Summary

Spouses and common-law partners sponsored from inside Canada can apply for an open work permit while their sponsorship application is in process, under a public policy extended through December 31, 2026. Spouses of economic PR applicants usually rely on the principal applicant’s status: if the principal holds or qualifies for a bridging open work permit, the spouse may qualify for a spousal open work permit, but only under the narrower rules in force since January 21, 2025. Every case depends on the principal applicant’s program, occupation and permit validity.

Pathway 1: Spouses Sponsored From Inside Canada

If your Canadian citizen or permanent resident spouse or common-law partner is sponsoring you, and you live together in Canada, you can usually apply for an open work permit while IRCC processes the sponsorship. Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) extended this public policy through December 31, 2026.

Key conditions generally include:

  • You are the sponsored spouse or common-law partner in a family class application.
  • You live at the same address as your sponsor in Canada.
  • You have valid temporary resident status, are on maintained status, or are eligible to restore your status.
  • You include the acknowledgement of receipt for the sponsorship application, or submit the work permit application with it.

This pathway did not change in the January 2025 restrictions, which makes it the most reliable route for sponsored spouses.

Pathway 2: Spouses of Economic PR Applicants

Many spouses of Express Entry and provincial nominee applicants assume they automatically qualify for an open work permit. They do not. In most cases, the spouse applies for a spousal open work permit based on the principal applicant’s own work permit and job.

Since January 21, 2025, a spouse of a foreign worker generally qualifies only if the principal worker:

  • Works in a TEER 0 or TEER 1 occupation, or in an eligible TEER 2 or 3 occupation in a priority sector such as health care, construction, natural and applied sciences, natural resources or education.
  • Has at least 16 months of validity remaining on their work permit when the spouse applies.

If your spouse holds a bridging open work permit while waiting for PR, review whether their occupation and remaining permit validity meet these rules. Dependent children of workers generally no longer qualify for open work permits under the 2025 changes.

Pathway 3: Spouses of International Students

If your spouse is studying, you may qualify only if your spouse is enrolled in a master’s program of at least 16 months, a doctoral program, or an eligible professional degree program. Since March 4, 2026, IRCC refuses these applications if the student is in their final academic term.

Documents You Will Usually Need

  • Proof of your relationship, such as a marriage certificate or evidence of at least one year of cohabitation for common-law partners.
  • A copy of your spouse’s work permit or study permit and, for workers, proof of employment, such as a job letter and recent pay stubs.
  • The acknowledgement of receipt for the PR or sponsorship application, where relevant.
  • Your passport and proof of current status in Canada.
  • Proof of payment for the work permit fee and the open work permit holder fee.

Fees and Processing

An open work permit application generally costs $255 in government fees, made up of the $155 work permit processing fee and the $100 open work permit holder fee, plus $85 for biometrics if required. Processing times vary, and inland family class work permits can take many months. Check the official IRCC processing times tool before you plan.

Common Reasons for Refusal

  • The principal worker’s occupation does not meet the January 2025 TEER and sector rules.
  • The principal’s work permit has less than 16 months of validity remaining.
  • Weak or inconsistent relationship evidence.
  • Applying after your own status expired without requesting restoration.
  • Missing proof of the principal’s employment or PR application.

Protect Your Status While You Wait

Apply before your current status expires. If you apply on time and stay in Canada, you can usually remain under maintained status while IRCC decides. Leaving Canada during processing can end maintained status, so plan travel carefully.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the spouse of a PR applicant get an open work permit in 2026?

Often, yes, but it depends on the pathway. Spouses sponsored from inside Canada can apply under a public policy extended through December 31, 2026. Spouses of economic PR applicants usually qualify only if the principal applicant’s work permit and occupation meet the spousal open work permit rules in force since January 21, 2025.

Does my spouse's Express Entry application automatically give me a work permit?

No. Submitting an Express Entry or provincial nominee application does not automatically give the spouse a work permit. The spouse generally needs to qualify for a spousal open work permit based on the principal applicant’s work permit, occupation and remaining validity, or qualify through another pathway.

What changed for spousal open work permits in January 2025?

Since January 21, 2025, spouses of foreign workers generally qualify only if the worker is in a TEER 0 or 1 occupation, or an eligible TEER 2 or 3 occupation in a priority sector, with at least 16 months left on their permit. Spouses of students qualify only for eligible master’s, doctoral or professional programs.

How much does a spousal open work permit cost?

Government fees are generally $255, which includes the $155 work permit processing fee and the $100 open work permit holder fee. You may also need to pay $85 for biometrics. Check IRCC’s current fee list before you pay, because incorrect payments can delay processing.
