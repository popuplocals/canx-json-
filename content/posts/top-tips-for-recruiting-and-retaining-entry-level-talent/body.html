Attracting and retaining entry-level talent is a growing challenge across industries, especially in sectors like hospitality, manufacturing, logistics, and retail where demand for frontline workers remains high. These positions often see high turnover and low application rates, leaving businesses struggling to build a stable workforce.

But with the right recruitment and retention strategies, employers can turn entry-level roles into career starting points for committed, motivated employees.

In this blog, we share practical tips to help Canadian businesses recruit smarter, retain longer, and reduce hiring costs by building a sustainable entry-level talent pipeline.

Why Entry-Level Hiring Needs a Rethink

Many businesses assume entry-level workers are easy to hire and quick to replace. But in reality, the competition for talent is fierce, and turnover is costly. Some key challenges include:

    • High competition for the same workers across sectors
    • Misalignment between candidate expectations and job realities
    • Poor onboarding or unclear career paths
    • Wage pressures and changing worker priorities

To overcome these challenges, employers must focus on both how they hire and how they keep employees engaged from day one.

Top Tips for Recruiting Entry-Level Workers

1. Write Clear, Inclusive Job Descriptions

Avoid vague titles like “general laborer” or “helper” without context. Instead, use simple language and include:

    • Responsibilities
    • Required skills (if any)
    • Physical or schedule expectations
    • Opportunities to grow or get trained

Make sure the tone is welcoming and inclusive, especially for youth, newcomers, or career changers.

2. Offer Competitive Entry-Level Compensation

While entry-level pay may be modest, offering a fair starting wage, shift premiums, or bonuses for attendance can set your job apart. Be transparent about wage structure from the start.

3. Recruit Where Entry-Level Workers Are

Don’t rely only on job boards. Instead:

    • Partner with local high schools, community colleges, and training centers
    • Join youth employment programs or provincial workforce initiatives
    • Use social media channels like TikTok and Instagram to reach younger candidates
    • Attend job fairs and community hiring events

4. Shorten and Simplify the Hiring Process

Entry-level candidates often juggle multiple job opportunities. If your process takes too long or asks for too much upfront, they’ll drop off.

    • Avoid requiring resumes if not needed
    • Use short application forms
    • Offer walk-in interviews or open house hiring events
    • Provide prompt follow-up and decisions

5. Promote Training and Growth Opportunities

Even entry-level workers are thinking about their future. Highlight any opportunities for:

    • On-the-job training
    • Promotions and raises
    • Certifications or trade paths
    • Cross-training within the company

This builds trust and shows your company is a place where people can grow.

Top Tips for Retaining Entry-Level Talent

1. Create a Strong Onboarding Experience

The first few days on the job set the tone. A structured, friendly onboarding helps new hires feel welcomed and informed.

    • Assign mentors or buddies
    • Offer hands-on training with patience
    • Review expectations and company culture early

2. Recognize and Reward Early Wins

Entry-level workers may not expect big promotions, but they value being noticed. A simple shout-out, gift card, or monthly recognition can make a difference.

3. Build a Feedback-First Culture

Regular check-ins help new employees feel heard and supported. Create open channels for feedback and suggestions, and act on them when possible.

4. Offer Flexibility Where You Can

Flexible shifts, scheduling input, and accommodations for life events can increase loyalty. Even small gestures like predictable hours matter.

5. Foster a Sense of Belonging

Involve entry-level employees in team meetings, training, or community efforts. Feeling part of the bigger picture helps people stay longer.

Conclusion

Recruiting and retaining entry-level talent isn’t just about filling roles. It’s about creating opportunities for people to enter the workforce and grow. With the right strategies, entry-level workers can become your most reliable long-term employees.
