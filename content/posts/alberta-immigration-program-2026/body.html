Alberta started 2026 with 6,403 provincial nomination spaces, and in August the federal government approved 200 more. That gives the Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP) 6,603 nominations to award this year, one of the larger provincial allocations in Canada. But more spaces do not mean an easier path. Alberta reported more than 45,000 eligible worker Expressions of Interest in January 2026, and it chooses carefully.

This guide explains every AAIP stream in 2026, how Alberta divides its nominations, which sectors it prioritizes, how the Worker Expression of Interest system works, and what you can do to improve your chances.

Quick Summary

The AAIP has 6,603 nomination spaces in 2026. More than half are reserved for the Alberta Opportunity Stream, which serves people already living and working in Alberta. Other options include the Rural Renewal Stream, the Tourism and Hospitality Stream, the Alberta Express Entry Stream with targeted pathways for tech, health care, law enforcement and priority sectors, and several entrepreneur streams. Alberta prioritizes health care, technology, construction, manufacturing, aviation and agriculture.

Alberta's Priority Sectors in 2026

Alberta focuses on occupations that support its economy and communities. In 2026, its priority sectors include health care, technology, construction, manufacturing (a new addition this year), aviation and agriculture, alongside a strong focus on rural communities.

The Worker Streams Explained

Alberta Opportunity Stream (AOS)

The AOS is Alberta’s largest stream. It is designed for temporary foreign workers who already live and work in Alberta. Candidates generally need a valid work permit, a full-time job offer from an Alberta employer in an eligible occupation, qualifying Alberta work experience, language scores that meet the threshold for their occupation’s TEER level, and at least a high school education. Some occupations are ineligible, so confirm your occupation before you submit an Expression of Interest.

Rural Renewal Stream (RRS)

The RRS supports rural communities that want to attract and keep newcomers. Candidates need a job offer from an employer in a designated rural community and an endorsement from that community. In 2026, Alberta introduced one-year endorsements, community caps and TEER-based eligibility rules.

Tourism and Hospitality Stream

This stream serves workers already living and working in Alberta’s tourism and hospitality sector, with a full-time job offer from an eligible employer in that sector. It has a small allocation, so competition can be strong.

Dedicated Health Care Pathways

Alberta runs dedicated pathways for health care professionals, including options linked and not linked to Express Entry. These pathways reflect Alberta’s ongoing need for physicians, nurses and allied health workers.

The Alberta Express Entry Stream

The Alberta Express Entry Stream lets Alberta nominate candidates from the federal Express Entry pool. You must have an active federal profile and qualify for the Federal Skilled Worker, Canadian Experience Class or Federal Skilled Trades program. A nomination adds 600 points to your Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score.

PathwayWho it targets
Accelerated Tech PathwayTech workers with an eligible job offer from an Alberta tech employer
Dedicated Health Care PathwayPhysicians, nurses and allied health professionals
Law Enforcement PathwayEligible police and law enforcement occupations
Priority SectorsCandidates in sectors such as construction, agriculture and aviation

Alberta discontinued its Family Connection and Occupation in Demand Express Entry pathways in March 2025.

How the Worker Expression of Interest System Works

For most worker streams, you do not apply directly. Instead, you submit a Worker Expression of Interest (EOI), and Alberta scores and ranks your profile. Alberta then invites selected candidates to apply in draws. Each draw can target a specific stream, pathway, sector or score range. Keeping your EOI accurate and up to date matters, because false or outdated information can lead to refusal.

Entrepreneur Streams

Alberta also offers business pathways, including the Rural Entrepreneur Stream, the Graduate Entrepreneur Stream for graduates of Alberta institutions, the Foreign Graduate Entrepreneur Stream for innovative start-ups, and the Farm Stream for experienced farm managers. Each has its own net worth, investment and business plan requirements, and the 2026 entrepreneur allocation is small.

Tips to Strengthen Your AAIP Chances

  • Confirm your occupation is eligible for your target stream before submitting an EOI.
  • Raise your language scores, which help your provincial and federal rankings.
  • Consider job opportunities in rural Alberta communities.
  • Keep your work permit valid throughout the process.
  • Keep a federal Express Entry profile active if you qualify.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many nominations does Alberta have in 2026?

Alberta started 2026 with 6,403 provincial nomination spaces. In August 2026, the federal government approved 200 additional spaces, bringing the total to 6,603. More than half of the original allocation was reserved for the Alberta Opportunity Stream, which serves people already living and working in Alberta.

Can I apply to the AAIP from outside Canada?

Some AAIP options can include candidates outside Canada, such as certain Alberta Express Entry pathways and entrepreneur streams. However, the Alberta Opportunity Stream and Tourism and Hospitality Stream are designed for people already living and working in Alberta. Check each stream’s eligibility before submitting an Expression of Interest.

Does Alberta's Express Entry stream require a job offer?

It depends on the pathway. The Accelerated Tech Pathway requires an eligible job offer from an Alberta tech employer, and some other pathways also expect Alberta ties. Every candidate needs an active federal Express Entry profile and must qualify for a federal Express Entry program to receive an Alberta Express Entry nomination.

Which occupations does Alberta prioritize in 2026?

Alberta’s 2026 priority sectors include health care, technology, construction, manufacturing, aviation and agriculture, with additional focus on rural communities. Draws can target specific sectors, pathways or occupations, so candidates in these fields generally have more opportunities, but every candidate must still meet the stream’s full eligibility rules.
